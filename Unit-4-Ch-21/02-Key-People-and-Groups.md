# Key People and Groups — Chapter 21 (4.8): Continuity and Change

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

> [!tip] Note
> Topic 4.8 is a synthesis chapter — the key "people and groups" here are the *analytical categories* that help you see change and continuity. Use this file to review how each major group experienced the period as a whole.

---

### European Maritime Powers (Spain, Portugal, Dutch, English, French)
- **Change:** Went from regional powers to global empires; from Silk Road customers to oceanic commercial dominators.
- **Continuity:** States continued to compete for power and resources; religion remained intertwined with political authority; mercantilist economic thinking was an evolution of older commercial practices.

---

### Indigenous American Peoples
- **Change:** Catastrophic demographic collapse (50–90% population loss); destruction of imperial states (Aztec, Inca); imposition of new labor systems, religions, and social hierarchies.
- **Continuity:** Indigenous cultures survived in syncretic forms; Indigenous peoples continued to resist and adapt; many pre-Columbian agricultural and administrative structures persisted under colonial overlay.

---

### Enslaved Africans and the African Diaspora
- **Change:** Creation of the largest forced migration in history; development of heritable racial chattel slavery; emergence of new Afro-American hybrid cultures.
- **Continuity:** Slavery existed in Africa, the Islamic world, and Europe before this period; African cultures were not destroyed but transformed; African resistance was constant.

---

### Asian Merchants and States (China, India, Ottomans)
- **Change:** European intrusion into Indian Ocean trade; increased flow of American silver into Asian economies; some states weakened by European commercial competition.
- **Continuity:** Asian states remained powerful through most of this period (Qing China, Mughal India, Ottoman Empire were at their height); Asian merchants remained active; European dominance in Asia came later (mostly 18th–19th century).

---

### West African Kingdoms
- **Change:** Insertion into Atlantic slave trade transformed political economies of many kingdoms; demographic losses; new commercial relationships with Europeans.
- **Continuity:** African states maintained political sovereignty; many kingdoms (Dahomey, Ashanti) grew *stronger* through participation in the slave trade; African cultural traditions persisted.

> [!exam-tip]
> For synthesis essays, practice stating **one change and one continuity** for each major group above. This gives you the analytical flexibility to respond to almost any CCOT prompt on Unit 4.
