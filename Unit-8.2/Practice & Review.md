# Practice & Review

**Topic 8.2 — The Cold War | AMSCO pp. 559–561**

## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: Truman Address to Joint Session of Congress, March 12, 1947 (AMSCO p. 559)*

> "When forces of liberation entered Greece, they found that the retreating Germans had destroyed virtually all the railways, roads, port facilities, communications, and merchant marine. More than a thousand villages had been burned. . . . Greece must have assistance if it is to become a self-supporting and self-respecting democracy. The United States must supply that assistance. . . . There is no other country to which democratic Greece can turn. No other nation is willing and able to provide the necessary support for a democratic Greek government . . . . If we falter in our leadership, we may endanger the peace of the world—and we shall surely endanger the welfare of our own Nation."
>
> — President Harry Truman, Address to Joint Session of Congress, March 12, 1947

**1. What was the context in the United States in which Truman issued this statement?**

A. People were increasingly supportive of a policy of open imperialism.
B. People feared the spread of Soviet communism in Europe.
C. People argued for a return to isolationism after the loss of so many lives in World War II.
D. People were calling for the United States to actively overthrow undemocratic governments.

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Truman's speech was the announcement of the Truman Doctrine — a direct response to Soviet pressure on Greece and Turkey. The dominant fear driving US policy was Soviet communist expansion into Western Europe. (A) is wrong — the US framed its policy as anti-imperialist. (C) was a real sentiment but was being *overcome*, not represented, by Truman's speech. (D) describes rollback, not the containment Truman actually advocated.

---

**2. Which individual most clearly showed through thoughts or actions that he held ideas similar to those expressed by Truman?**

A. U.S. diplomat George Kennan
B. U.S. Secretary of State George Marshall
C. UN Secretary General Trygve Lie
D. U.S. General Douglas MacArthur

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> George Kennan articulated the containment strategy that directly underlies Truman's speech — the idea that the US must actively resist Soviet expansion at every pressure point. Kennan's "Long Telegram" (1946) and "X Article" (1947) laid the intellectual foundation for the Truman Doctrine. Marshall (B) focused on economic aid; MacArthur (D) later argued for *rollback* (going further than Truman), not containment.

---

**3. What was the most important similarity between Truman's statement and the Marshall Plan?**

A. Both emphasized military aid to prevent the spread of communism.
B. Both created coalitions to finance rebuilding European infrastructure.
C. Both provided economic aid to rebuild nations after World War II.
D. Both required the implementation of democratic elections in order for a nation to obtain aid.

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Both the Truman Doctrine (for Greece and Turkey specifically) and the Marshall Plan provided economic assistance to rebuild war-damaged nations. The Truman Doctrine was not primarily military (A) — it included economic aid. The Marshall Plan did not require democratic elections (D) — it was offered to all European nations including the USSR. (B) is too specific and inaccurate.

## Short-Answer Questions

*Source: Belgrade Declaration, First Non-Aligned Movement Conference, 1961 (AMSCO p. 560)*

> "Imperialism is weakening. Colonial empires and other forms of foreign oppression of peoples in Asia, Africa and Latin America are gradually disappearing from the stage of history. Great successes have been achieved in the struggle of many peoples for national independence and equality . . . . The Governments of countries participating in the Conference resolutely reject the view that war, including the 'cold war,' is inevitable as this view reflects a sense both of helplessness and hopelessness and is contrary to the progress of the world."
>
> — Belgrade Declaration, Non-Aligned Movement Conference, 1961

**SAQ 1**

**A. Identify ONE way in which this passage reflected the context regarding self-determination in which the Non-Aligned Movement was formed.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Contextualization
> The passage reflects the wave of decolonization occurring in the late 1950s and early 1960s — when dozens of African and Asian nations gained independence from European colonial powers. The declaration's statement that "colonial empires . . . are gradually disappearing" directly references this context: the Non-Aligned Movement drew its membership primarily from newly independent states that had just thrown off colonial rule and were determined to exercise genuine sovereignty rather than trade European colonial dependence for superpower dependence.

**B. Identify ONE way in which this passage reflected the context regarding the superpowers in which the Non-Aligned Movement was formed.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Contextualization
> The passage reflects the context of Cold War superpower rivalry by explicitly rejecting the idea that the "cold war" is inevitable. This was a direct response to US and Soviet pressure on newly independent nations to align with one side or the other. The Non-Aligned Movement was formed precisely because member states refused to accept the Cold War's binary logic — they wanted independence from both superpower blocs at a moment when each was actively competing for their allegiance through aid, trade deals, and diplomatic pressure.

**C. Explain ONE way in which the Non-Aligned Movement was an example of other trends in international relations in the 20th century.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Continuity/Connection
> The Non-Aligned Movement exemplified the broader 20th-century trend of formerly colonized peoples asserting national sovereignty and self-determination on the world stage. Just as nationalist movements had challenged European empires throughout the century — from Indian independence (1947) to African independence movements in the 1950s-60s — the Non-Aligned Movement represented a collective assertion that the nations of Asia, Africa, and Latin America would determine their own political futures rather than accept domination by outside powers, whether colonial or Cold War superpowers.

**SAQ 2**

**A. Identify the response of the Soviet Union to the Marshall Plan.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Identification
> The Soviet Union refused to participate in the Marshall Plan and barred its Eastern European satellite states from doing so as well. Instead, it created the Council for Mutual Economic Assistance (COMECON) in 1947 to coordinate trade and economic policy within the Soviet bloc.

**B. Explain the policy of containment advocated by the United States after World War II.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Explanation
> Containment, articulated by diplomat George Kennan in 1946–1947, held that the Soviet Union was inherently expansionist and would keep pushing outward unless met with consistent, firm resistance. Rather than trying to roll back communism where it already existed, containment aimed to prevent its further spread — by supporting threatened governments (Truman Doctrine), rebuilding economic stability (Marshall Plan), and forming military alliances (NATO). The policy committed the US to permanent global engagement and justified intervention wherever communism appeared to be gaining ground.

**C. Explain how the arms race and the space race were similar.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Both the arms race and the space race were expressions of Cold War superpower competition in the technological domain. In both cases, the Soviet Union achieved early breakthroughs (atomic bomb 1949, Sputnik 1957) that shocked Americans and triggered massive US investment to close the gap. Both were driven by military implications as much as scientific ambition — Sputnik demonstrated ICBM capability just as surely as it demonstrated satellite technology. And both contributed to the doctrine of Mutual Assured Destruction: the arms race created the nuclear stockpiles, and the space race produced the delivery systems (ICBMs) that made MAD operational.
