# People, States & Movements

**Topic 8.2 — The Cold War | AMSCO pp. 554–561**

---

## Key Figures

**Harry Truman**
US President (1945–1953) who defined American Cold War policy in its critical early years. After succeeding FDR, Truman authorized the atomic bombings of Japan, confronted Soviet expansion at the Potsdam Conference, issued the Truman Doctrine (1947) pledging US support to countries resisting communist takeover, and oversaw the Marshall Plan. Truman's decisions transformed the US from a reluctant world power to the leader of a global anti-communist coalition.

**George Kennan**
US diplomat and chief architect of the containment strategy. Kennan served at the US Embassy in Moscow in the 1930s and again in 1946, where he developed a deep analysis of Soviet foreign policy. His 1946 "Long Telegram" and 1947 "X Article" argued that the Soviets were inherently expansionist but could be deterred through consistent, firm resistance at strategic pressure points. Containment became the foundational US Cold War doctrine for decades, though Kennan himself later criticized its overly militarized application.

**Winston Churchill**
British Prime Minister during WWII and leader of the Conservative opposition by 1946. His March 1946 speech in Fulton, Missouri — delivered with Truman present — coined the phrase "Iron Curtain" to describe Soviet domination of Eastern Europe. Though Churchill held no official power at the time, the speech effectively announced the Cold War to the public and galvanized Western opinion against Soviet expansion.

**Nikita Khrushchev**
Soviet leader (1953–1964) who succeeded Stalin and introduced a period of relative de-Stalinization. Khrushchev presided over some of the most dangerous Cold War moments, including the 1961 construction of the Berlin Wall and the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis — the closest the Cold War came to nuclear war. He also oversaw the Sputnik launch (1957) and the expansion of the Soviet space and missile program.

**Mao Zedong**
Leader of the Chinese Communist Party who won the Chinese Civil War and founded the People's Republic of China in 1949. China's "fall" to communism shocked the United States and intensified Cold War fears — critics asked "Who lost China?" Mao's alignment with the USSR (initially) created a massive communist bloc in Eurasia that shaped US strategy in Asia for decades.

**Kim Il-sung**
North Korean communist leader who launched the Korean War (1950) by invading South Korea. US-led UN forces intervened under the containment framework. The war ended in stalemate at the 38th parallel (1953), establishing the pattern of Cold War proxy conflicts — neither superpower fighting directly, but each backing its side. Kim's dynasty rules North Korea to the present.

## Key States

**United States**
Emerged from WWII as the world's leading economic and military power, the only nation with (initially) nuclear weapons. Organized the Western Cold War coalition through [[Concept Notes/NATO\|NATO]] (1949), implemented the Marshall Plan, and pursued containment globally. Its democratic-capitalist model was held up as the alternative to Soviet communism.

**Soviet Union (USSR)**
The other superpower, emerging from WWII with a massive land army and territorial gains in Eastern Europe. The USSR tested its first atomic bomb in 1949 (four years after the US), launched Sputnik in 1957, and established a network of satellite states that served as a buffer zone and ideological expansion. Led by Stalin until 1953, then Khrushchev.

**Eastern European Satellite States**
Bulgaria, East Germany, Hungary, Poland, and Romania were transformed into Soviet-aligned states after WWII. They adopted Soviet-style five-year plans, banned opposition parties, and were economically integrated into the Soviet bloc through COMECON. Their populations had little say — periodic uprisings (East Germany 1953, Hungary 1956, Czechoslovakia 1968) were crushed by Soviet force.

## Key Movements

**Non-Aligned Movement**
A coalition of newly decolonized African and Asian states that sought to avoid entanglement in the US-Soviet rivalry. Its intellectual foundation came from the 1955 Bandung Conference and it was formally organized in 1961 in Belgrade, Yugoslavia.

**Jawaharlal Nehru** (India) — First Prime Minister of independent India (1947–1964); a principal voice for non-alignment and a stronger UN. Opposed UN intervention in the India-Pakistan conflict over Kashmir — illustrating the tension between non-alignment principles and national interests.

**Kwame Nkrumah** (Ghana) — Led Ghana to independence from Britain in 1957; advocated Pan-African unity through the Organization of African Unity (OAU). One of the most respected African leaders of the postwar era and a prominent voice for non-alignment.

**Gamal Abdel Nasser** (Egypt) — President of Egypt (1954–1970); helped mediate the Bandung Conference and championed Pan-Arabism. His nationalization of the Suez Canal (1956) triggered an international crisis and demonstrated how non-aligned leaders could leverage Cold War competition for national goals.

**Sukarno** (Indonesia) — First President of Indonesia (1945–1967); organized and hosted the Bandung Conference. Sukarno critiqued both superpowers but accepted large amounts of aid from each — embodying the pragmatic opportunism that non-alignment often required in practice.

> [!exam-tip]
> For AP essays, Nehru, Nkrumah, and Nasser are the three Non-Aligned leaders most likely to appear. Know each one's specific contribution and country. The AP often asks you to use specific individuals as evidence.

> [!tip]
> **Kennan vs. Truman:** Kennan envisioned containment as primarily political and economic — limiting Soviet influence without arms buildup. Truman (and later NSC-68) militarized containment. This distinction matters for AP causation and continuity/change questions.

> Joseph McCarthy (the Red Scare) is covered in the Think as Historian section of 8.2 but is NOT one of the YAML key people. He's relevant context for domestic US politics but not a Cold War foreign policy actor. Don't confuse him with the policymakers above.
