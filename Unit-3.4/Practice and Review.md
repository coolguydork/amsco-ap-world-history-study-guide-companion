## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

Which of the following best explains why the Aztec Empire was more vulnerable to Spanish conquest than the Mughal Empire was to early European intrusion?

A) The Aztec Empire lacked gunpowder weapons, while the Mughals had adopted them fully  
B) The Aztec Empire relied on fear and tribute rather than direct governance, creating resentful subject peoples who joined the Spanish against the Aztecs  
C) The Aztec Empire had no professional military, while the Mughals maintained a large standing army  
D) The Aztec Empire had no religious ideology to unite its subjects, unlike the theocratic Mughal state

**Answer: B**
- A is partially true but not the best explanation — Aztecs lacked gunpowder but the Mughals had it; the key is political structure.
- **B is correct** — the Aztec tributary system created enemies among subject peoples who allied with Cortés.
- C is wrong — the Aztecs had a fierce warrior society and professional warrior castes.
- D is wrong — the Aztecs had a deeply religious state (human sacrifice, cosmic mythology).

**Question 2**

Both the Ottoman Janissaries and the Safavid Ghulams were similar in that both

A) were recruited from the hereditary aristocracy of their respective empires  
B) were recruited from minority religious or ethnic groups to ensure loyalty to the central government  
C) were organized primarily as naval forces to extend imperial control over maritime trade  
D) were required to practice the dominant religion of their empire before recruitment

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — both were *not* from the aristocracy; their outsider status was precisely what made them loyal.
- **B is correct** — Janissaries from Balkan Christians; Ghulams from Georgian, Armenian, Circassian peoples; both outsiders with nowhere else to go.
- C is wrong — both were land-based military forces; the Safavids had no significant navy.
- D is wrong — Janissaries *converted* to Islam after recruitment, not before.

**Question 3**

The primary cause of the Ottoman-Safavid conflict in the period 1450–1750 was

A) competition over the Indian Ocean spice trade  
B) disputes over European colonial territories in the New World  
C) both the Sunni-Shi'a religious divide and competition over border territories and trade routes  
D) Ottoman attempts to force Shi'a Muslims to convert to Sunni Islam in conquered territories

**Answer: C**
- A is wrong — neither empire was an Indian Ocean power in this way; it's about overland routes.
- B is wrong — neither empire had New World colonies.
- **C is correct** — AMSCO explicitly identifies both religious and economic causes (silk trade embargoes).
- D is wrong — this overstates the religious dimension and mischaracterizes the conflict.

**Question 4**

Which of the following is the best example of a land-based empire using art or architecture to legitimize its authority?

A) The Ottoman Empire's use of the devshirme system to create loyal bureaucrats  
B) The Mughal Empire's construction of the Taj Mahal to display imperial wealth and sophistication  
C) The Tokugawa shogunate's requirement that daimyo maintain residences in Edo  
D) The Aztec Empire's use of tribute lists to extract goods from subject peoples

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — devshirme is an administrative tool, not artistic legitimation.
- **B is correct** — the Taj Mahal is the canonical example of architecture demonstrating imperial power and cultural achievement.
- C is wrong — the Edo residence requirement was a political control mechanism, not an artistic statement.
- D is wrong — tribute lists are an economic/military tool.

**Question 5**

A historian studying the decline of land-based empires in the period 1450–1750 would most likely conclude that

A) these empires declined primarily because of external military conquest by European powers  
B) the decline of these empires was primarily caused by religious conflict between Islam and Christianity  
C) internal weaknesses — including weak leadership, overtaxation of peasants, and failure to modernize militaries — were the primary causes of decline  
D) the empires declined because they expanded too aggressively and stretched their military beyond sustainable limits

**Answer: C**
- A is wrong — European *military* conquest of the Gunpowder Empires came mostly in the 19th century, not 1450–1750. European *economic* penetration was more relevant in this period.
- B is wrong — Islamic Gunpowder Empires also fought *each other* (Sunni vs. Shi'a), not just Christians.
- **C is correct** — AMSCO specifically identifies weak sultans, harem politics, overtaxation, and failure to modernize.
- D is wrong — overextension was a factor (Aurangzeb's southward expansion drained the Mughal treasury) but not the primary cause across all empires.

## Short Answer Questions (Synthesis / Comparison)

**SAQ 1 — Comparison**

Briefly explain ONE similarity and ONE difference between the Aztec and Ottoman Empires in their methods of controlling subject populations.

*Sample Similarity:* Both the Aztec and Ottoman Empires used **tribute systems** to extract resources from subject populations. The Aztecs demanded yearly offerings of goods and people from conquered states; the Ottomans required tribute — technically one-fifth of conquered territories' wealth — including the Christian boys who became Janissaries through the devshirme system. In both cases, tribute was a form of ongoing submission and recognition of imperial authority.

*Sample Difference:* The Ottoman Empire governed its diverse population through a **centralized, professional bureaucracy** trained via the devshirme system, with administrators, diplomats, and soldiers integrated into a single imperial structure. The Aztec Empire, by contrast, governed through **minimal direct control** — it relied on fear, intimidation, and tributary relationships rather than direct administration. This distinction made the Aztec Empire far more vulnerable to collapse when its tributary states defected to the Spanish in 1519.

---

**SAQ 2 — Causation**

Explain ONE cause of the decline of the Gunpowder Empires in the period 1450–1750.

*Sample Response:* One major cause of the decline of the Gunpowder Empires was the **corruption of tax collection systems**, which both drained imperial revenue and alienated the peasant class. In the Mughal Empire, zamindars — originally salaried officials responsible for collecting taxes from peasants — were eventually given land grants and allowed to keep a portion of what they collected. Over time, zamindars began withholding increasing amounts and building personal armies, effectively becoming independent power brokers rather than agents of the central government. Similarly, in the Ottoman Empire, tax farmers paid a fixed sum to the sultan and kept whatever excess they could extract — creating incentives for ruthless exploitation of peasants rather than efficient revenue collection. This corruption simultaneously weakened the empires financially and generated peasant resentment that fueled uprisings like the White Lotus Rebellion in Qing China (1796–1804) and revolts against Aurangzeb in Mughal India.

## LEQ Practice Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which land-based empires in the period c. 1450–c. 1750 used similar methods to consolidate and maintain power."**

**Thesis approach:** These empires were *more similar than different* in their methods — all used gunpowder, centralized bureaucracy, taxation, religious legitimacy, and elite military forces. However, the *specific mechanisms* differed in important ways (civil service exam vs. devshirme vs. hereditary samurai). Your argument should acknowledge both the broad similarities *and* the meaningful differences.

**Suggested structure:**
1. **Thesis**: Land-based empires in this period used broadly similar methods (point of comparison) but differed in the *mechanisms* of each method (nuance).
2. **Body 1**: Military — all used elite forces; compare Janissaries (Ottoman) vs. Ghulams (Safavid) vs. samurai (Japan)
3. **Body 2**: Bureaucracy — all governed through elites; compare devshirme vs. civil service exam vs. zamindars
4. **Body 3**: Legitimacy — all used religion and architecture; compare Taj Mahal vs. Suleymaniye Mosque vs. Versailles (and their different religious frameworks)
5. **Complexity point**: The Aztec Empire as a contrasting case — *less* bureaucratic, *more* tributary, ultimately more vulnerable

## Quick Reference: 5 Things to Know for Every Empire

### Ottoman
1. Devshirme → Janissaries
2. Istanbul = Bosporus Strait trade hub
3. Suleiman I = peak; Battle of Lepanto (1571) = naval decline
4. Sunni Islam; tax farming; intendants-style corruption
5. Failed Siege of Vienna (1683) = turning point

### Safavid
1. Shi'a Islam = state religion → conflict with Sunni Ottomans
2. Shah Abbas I = peak; Ghulams = slave soldiers
3. Isfahan sacked 1722 = effective end
4. No navy, no natural defenses
5. "Men of the pen" = Persian bureaucracy

### Mughal
1. Babur (Tamerlane's descendant) → Akbar (peak) → Aurangzeb (decline)
2. Zamindars = tax collectors who became corrupt
3. Akbar = tolerance; Aurangzeb = intolerance + revolts
4. Taj Mahal = architectural legitimacy
5. British/French economic penetration precedes political control

### Aztec
1. Tributary empire — minimal direct control
2. Human sacrifice = religious/political legitimacy
3. Subject peoples joined Spanish = collapsed 1521
4. Tribute lists = main revenue source
5. Less bureaucratic than all Eurasian empires

### Inca
1. Mita = labor tribute
2. Federal provinces with loyal noble governors
3. Quipu = record-keeping (no writing)
4. Roads and messengers = administrative control
5. Collapsed under Spanish military + disease (see [[Unit-4.3/Spanish Empire in the Americas]])
