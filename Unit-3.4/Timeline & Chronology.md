# Timeline & Chronology — Comparison in Land-Based Empires

Topic 3.4 spans the entire period c. 1450–c. 1750 — the AP exam era of dominant land-based empires. (AMSCO pp. 177–188)

---

## Key Dates

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| c. 1450–c. 1750 | Era of dominant land-based empires: Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, Russian, Qing | All five empires share gunpowder technology, centralized bureaucracies, and monumental building programs — the core comparison frame for this topic |
| c. 1501 | Safavid Empire founded by Shah Ismail I; Shi'a Islam declared state religion | Sets up the defining Sunni–Shi'a split that drives Ottoman–Safavid wars |
| 1526 | Mughal Empire founded by Babur at the Battle of Panipat | Beginning of Muslim rule over a majority-Hindu population in South Asia |
| c. 1556–1605 | Reign of Akbar (Mughal) | Height of Mughal tolerance; abolition of jizya; attempted Din-i-Ilahi |
| c. 1591 | Morocco invades and destroys the Songhai Empire; sacks capital Gao | Shows how even powerful land empires could fall to external military force |
| c. 1600 | Ottoman army at its military height; leads world in gunpowder technology and scale | After Suleiman, Ottoman military discipline and technology began to lag |
| 1665–1666 | Ottoman emissaries introduce coffee to French and Austrian aristocratic courts | Example of cultural diffusion as imperial soft power; Pomeranz & Topik thesis |
| 1682–1725 | Peter the Great of Russia modernizes military, bureaucracy, and culture | Russia adapts European models to remain competitive; key case study in imperial adaptation |
| 1701 | Peter the Great's Decree on Western Dress mandates European clothing at court | Top-down cultural modernization as a tool of state centralization |
| 1714 | Peter the Great mandates mathematics education in Russian regions | State-directed education as centralization tool; parallel to Ottoman and Mughal reforms |

## Chronological Themes

**Early period (c. 1450–c. 1600): Rise and consolidation**
The [[Concept Notes/Gunpowder Empires|Gunpowder Empires]] expanded rapidly using superior military technology (cannons, firearms) and developed centralized bureaucratic systems to govern conquered territories. Suleiman I's reign (1520–1566) represented the Ottoman apex. The Safavid–Ottoman conflict became entrenched along the Sunni–Shi'a divide.

**Middle period (c. 1600–c. 1700): Stress fractures appear**
After c. 1600 the Ottoman military fell behind European innovation. Harem politics and succession crises weakened all three Gunpowder Empires. The Mughal Empire reached its greatest territorial extent under Aurangzeb (r. 1658–1707) but at the cost of religious tolerance, deepening Hindu–Muslim tensions that would accelerate Mughal decline.

**Late period (c. 1700–c. 1750): Adaptation and decline**
Peter the Great demonstrated that a land empire could modernize successfully by looking westward. The Safavid Empire collapsed in 1722. The Ottoman and Mughal empires survived but were increasingly under pressure from European maritime powers (Unit 4 connection). The Qing Empire in China remained strong through the mid-eighteenth century.

## AP Period Context

Topic 3.4 falls in **AP Period 2 (c. 1200–c. 1450)** by formal assignment, but the content covers 1450–1750. The period tag reflects the AP College Board's classification. The essential question and learning objective are fully within the 1450–1750 era (AP Period 4 in actual content terms). On the AP exam, be ready to apply this material to free-response questions on Unit 3 themes.
