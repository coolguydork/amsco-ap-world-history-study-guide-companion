## Essential Question Review

**By what methods did empires increase their societal and cultural influence from c. 1450–c. 1750?**

Three main methods: (1) **Military Might** — gunpowder weapons, elite enslaved soldiers (Janissaries, Ghulams), conquest; (2) **Centralized Bureaucracy** — civil service exams, devshirme, tax systems; (3) **Legitimacy** — religion (caliph, divine right), art (portraits, miniatures), monumental architecture (Taj Mahal, Versailles).

## DBQ Document Analysis — Peter the Great's Russia

The AMSCO textbook includes a set of primary source documents on Peter the Great (pp. 185–188) that model the type of sources you will encounter in a DBQ. Practice analyzing each document using HAPP (Historical situation, Audience, Purpose, Point of view).

---

### Document 3 — Peter I, Decree on the Invitation of Foreigners, 1702 (AMSCO p. 186)

> "Since our accession to the throne all our efforts and intentions have tended to govern this realm in such a way that all of our subjects should, through our care for the general good, become more and more prosperous. For this end we have always tried to maintain internal order, to defend the state against invasion, and in every possible way to improve and to extend trade. . . . To attain these worthy aims, we have endeavored to improve our military forces, which are the protection of our State, so that our troops may consist of well-drilled men, maintained in perfect order and discipline. In order to obtain greater improvement in this respect, and to encourage foreigners, who are able to assist us in this way, as well as artisans profitable to the State, to come in numbers to our country, we have issued this manifesto."

> [!tip] Document 3 Analysis
> **Historical situation:** Russia in 1702 is militarily weak compared to Western Europe; Peter is in the midst of the Great Northern War with Sweden.
> **Audience:** Foreign skilled workers and military experts.
> **Purpose:** Recruit Western expertise to modernize Russia's military and economy.
> **Point of view:** Peter presents his motivation as concern for the "general good" — but the actual urgency is military competition.
> **Key argument this document supports:** Land-based empires used deliberate state policy to modernize and maintain military influence.

### Document 4 — Peter I, Decree on Western Dress, 1701 (AMSCO p. 186)

> "Western dress shall be worn by all the boyars, members of our councils and of our court . . . gentry of Moscow, secretaries . . . provincial gentry, gosti [merchants], government officials, streltsy, members of the guilds purveying for our household, citizens of Moscow of all ranks, and residents of provincial cities . . . excepting the clergy and peasant tillers of the soil."

> [!tip] Document 4 Analysis
> **Historical situation:** Peter is forcibly Westernizing the Russian court elite.
> **Audience:** Russian nobles, merchants, and officials.
> **Purpose:** Mandate cultural Westernization as a tool of centralization and modernization.
> **Point of view:** The decree is coercive — it threatens enforcement. Resistance existed (as Document 6 shows).
> **Key argument:** Rulers used cultural policy (appearance, dress, customs) as tools of centralization — connecting to the broader 3.4 legitimacy theme.

---

### Document 5 — Statute for the College of Manufactures, 1723 (AMSCO p. 187)

> "His Imperial Majesty is diligently striving to establish and develop in the Russian Empire such manufacturing plants and factories as are found in other states, for the general welfare and prosperity of his subjects. . . . His Imperial Majesty gives permission to everyone, without distinction of rank or condition, to open factories wherever he may find suitable. . . . Factory owners must be closely supervised, in order that they have at their plants good and experienced [foreign] master craftsmen, who are able to train Russians in such a way that these, in turn, may themselves become masters, so that their produce may bring glory to the Russian manufactures."

> [!tip] Document 5 Analysis
> **Historical situation:** Russia needs to develop domestic manufacturing to reduce dependence on imports.
> **Purpose:** Create state-directed industrialization by inviting foreign expertise and transferring knowledge to Russian workers.
> **Key argument:** This is state-directed economic development — an example of how land-based empires used centralized bureaucracy to modernize and project power.

### Document 6 — Jean Rousset de Missy, Life of Peter the Great, c. 1730 (AMSCO p. 187)

> "The tsar labored at the reform of fashions, or, more properly speaking, of dress. Until that time the Russians had always worn long beards, which they cherished and preserved with much care, allowing them to hang down on their bosoms, without even cutting the moustache. With these long beards they wore the hair very short. . . . The tsar, in order to reform that custom, ordered that gentlemen, merchants, and other subjects, except priests and peasants, should each pay a tax of one hundred rubles a year if they wished to keep their beards. . . . Officials were stationed at the gates of the towns to collect that tax, which the Russians regarded as an enormous sin on the part of the tsar and as a thing which tended to the abolition of their religion."

> [!tip] Document 6 Analysis
> **Historical situation:** Peter's modernization program meets popular resistance, especially over the beard tax.
> **Audience:** French readers (Rousset de Missy is a foreign observer).
> **Purpose/Point of view:** An outside perspective on Peter's reforms — notes both the policy and the popular reaction. The foreign author may be sympathetic to Westernization.
> **Key argument:** Modernization from above created social tension. Older Russians connected their beards to religious identity; Peter's tax was experienced as an attack on religion. This shows how legitimacy and cultural policy can conflict.
> **Good for a DBQ point on:** Resistance to imperial centralization; the limits of top-down reform.

## SAQ Practice Frames

Use these prompts to practice written responses. Aim for 3–5 sentences per part (claim + evidence + explanation).

**SAQ 1 — Comparison**
a) Describe ONE method used by land-based empires to consolidate political power from c. 1450 to c. 1750.
b) Explain ONE similarity in the methods used by TWO different empires to legitimize their rule.
c) Explain ONE difference in the bureaucratic systems used by the Ottoman Empire and the Ming dynasty.

> [!tip] Model Response — SAQ 1b (Similarity)
> Both the Ottoman and Mughal empires used religion to legitimize imperial rule. The Ottoman sultans claimed the title of caliph — successor to the Prophet Muhammad — to project religious authority over the Muslim world alongside their political power. Similarly, Mughal emperors constructed mosques and madrasas across their empire to demonstrate piety and align the state with Islam. In both cases, religious legitimacy served to unite a religiously diverse empire around the ruler's authority.

**SAQ 2 — Causation**
a) Describe ONE cause of the decline of the Gunpowder Empires after c. 1600.
b) Explain how ONE internal factor contributed to the weakening of a specific land-based empire.
c) Explain how Peter the Great's reforms illustrate a method by which a land-based empire attempted to maintain its influence.

> [!tip] Model Response — SAQ 2c (Peter the Great)
> Peter the Great's reforms in Russia (r. 1682–1725) illustrate how a land-based empire used military modernization and state-directed cultural change to maintain influence. Peter reorganized the Russian army along European lines, invited foreign military experts and craftsmen, and established the College of Manufactures to develop domestic industry. These top-down reforms reflected the broader challenge facing land-based empires after c. 1600: European military technology was advancing rapidly, and empires that failed to adapt risked conquest or irrelevance. Unlike the Gunpowder Empires, which largely failed to modernize their military technology, Russia's adaptive reforms allowed it to remain a major power.

## LEQ Practice Prompt (Unit 3 Level)

**"Evaluate the extent to which land-based empires used similar methods to consolidate power during the period c. 1450–c. 1750."**

**Outline:**
- **Thesis**: Land-based empires used broadly similar methods — military force, centralized bureaucracy, and religion-based legitimacy — but differed significantly in how they implemented each, reflecting their distinct cultural, geographic, and political contexts.
- **Body 1 (Similarity)**: Gunpowder technology was universal among Eurasian land empires; all used elite, loyal military forces (Janissaries, Ghulams, professional armies).
- **Body 2 (Difference)**: Bureaucratic recruitment methods differed sharply — the Ottoman devshirme (enslaved loyalty) vs. the Ming civil service exam (merit-based loyalty) reflects different imperial structures.
- **Body 3 (Complexity)**: Legitimacy strategies diverged: Islamic empires used the caliphate title; European monarchs used divine right; Aztecs used human sacrifice. These differences reflect distinct religious and cultural foundations, not just administrative choices.
- **Conclusion**: The similarities show a common logic of empire-building; the differences show how geography, religion, and history shaped specific implementations.

## Quick Review — Comparison Tables

### Bureaucratic Recruitment Methods

| Empire | Method | Basis of Loyalty |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Ottoman | Devshirme (enslaved boys) | Owed everything to the sultan |
| Safavid | Ghulams + "men of the pen" | Slave-soldiers + Persian educated class |
| Ming/Qing | Civil service exams | Confucian merit; scholar-gentry class |
| Songhai | Madrasa scholars from Timbuktu | Islamic education + mansa loyalty |
| Inca | Provincial noble appointments | Hereditary loyalty to the emperor |
| Aztec | Tributary system (not bureaucratic) | Fear and military intimidation |

### Taxation Methods

| Empire | Method | Key Feature |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Ottoman | Tax farming | Private contractors; prone to exploitation |
| Mughal | Zamindars | State-appointed tax collectors; became local elite |
| Ming | Hard currency (silver) | Accelerated global silver trade |
| Aztec | Tribute lists | Goods + people; no direct state oversight |
