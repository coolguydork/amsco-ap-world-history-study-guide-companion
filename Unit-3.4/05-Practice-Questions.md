# Unit 3.4: Practice Questions — Comparison in Land-Based Empires

#apwh #unit-3 #period-2

## Multiple Choice Questions

**Question 1**
*"He [Akbar] abolished the jizya, the tax on non-Muslim subjects. He invited Hindu, Christian, Zoroastrian, and Jain scholars to his court. He created a new class of officials called mansabdars, ranked by the number of cavalry they could supply, drawn from all religious backgrounds."*
*(Adapted from Abu'l-Fazl, Akbarnama, c. 1590)*

Which of the following best explains Akbar's policies as described in the passage?

A) Akbar converted to Hinduism and abandoned Islam as the state religion
B) Akbar used religious pluralism as a deliberate strategy to consolidate power over a diverse empire
C) Akbar's policies reflected Mughal military weakness and the need to appease Hindu subjects
D) Akbar was following Ottoman policies that had been established by Suleiman the Magnificent

**Correct Answer: B**
*Akbar's pluralism was strategic: by incorporating Hindu Rajput elites into the military nobility and removing discriminatory taxes, he built a broader coalition of loyal subordinates — administrative pragmatism, not personal theology.*

**Question 2**
The Ottoman devshirme system and the Chinese civil service examination system were similar in that both:

A) Required candidates to demonstrate mastery of Islamic jurisprudence
B) Created a class of imperial servants whose loyalty was to the state rather than to hereditary elites
C) Were open to all male subjects regardless of social background or religion
D) Were abolished by reformers who saw them as obstacles to meritocracy

**Correct Answer: B**
*Both systems deliberately bypassed hereditary elites: devshirme recruited enslaved boys with no family ties; the civil service exam elevated educated men regardless of noble birth. Both created bureaucratic classes loyal to the emperor/sultan rather than to local lords.*

**Question 3**
Which of the following best explains why the Safavid Empire declared Shia Islam the state religion in the early 16th century?

A) The majority of the Iranian population had always been Shia and demanded official recognition
B) Shia Islam provided a theological justification for the Safavid claim to political authority, and distinguished the empire from its Sunni Ottoman rival
C) The Safavids converted to Shia Islam under pressure from the Mughal Empire
D) Shia scholars controlled the Persian economy and required state religious endorsement

**Correct Answer: B**
*Declaring Shia Islam state religion served two functions: it grounded Safavid political authority in the imam tradition, and it created a sharp religious distinction from the Ottoman Caliphate — building a Shia Persian national identity that mobilized subjects against the Sunni Ottoman threat.*

**Question 4**
The Qing Dynasty's practice of presenting the emperor as simultaneously a Confucian Son of Heaven, a Tibetan Buddhist patron, and a Mongol steppe khan is best described as:

A) Religious syncretism motivated by the emperor's personal theological interests
B) Strategic deployment of different cultural identities to legitimize rule over diverse subject populations
C) Evidence that the Qing had abandoned Manchu cultural traditions
D) A failed experiment that caused widespread rebellion among Confucian scholars

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Qing employed what historians call "pluralistic legitimation" — presenting the emperor through the cultural framework most meaningful to each subject group. This was deliberate political strategy, not personal belief.*

**Question 5**
Which of the following is the most significant difference between how the Ottoman Empire and the Mughal Empire managed religious diversity among their subjects?

A) The Ottomans forced all subjects to convert to Islam, while the Mughals permitted non-Muslim worship
B) The Ottomans used the millet system to allow non-Muslim communities self-governance under their own religious leaders, while the Mughal approach varied significantly by ruler
C) The Mughal Empire had no non-Muslim subjects, while the Ottoman Empire governed large Christian and Jewish populations
D) Both empires used identical administrative systems derived from the Abbasid Caliphate

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Ottoman millet system was a formal, institutionalized framework for religious pluralism; the Mughal approach changed dramatically between Akbar (tolerant pluralism) and Aurangzeb (reimposed jizya, religious restrictions) — demonstrating that policy varied by ruler rather than following a fixed institutional model.*

**Question 6**
The collapse of the Songhai Empire in 1591 most directly illustrates which of the following patterns?

A) The inability of West African states to develop sophisticated administrative systems
B) The decisive military advantage that gunpowder weapons gave to armies that possessed them over those that did not
C) The role of climate change in destabilizing West African political structures
D) The negative consequences of participation in the Trans-Saharan trade network

**Correct Answer: B**
*A Moroccan army using firearms crossed the Sahara and defeated the much larger Songhai cavalry force at Tondibi — a stark demonstration of gunpowder's force-multiplying effect. Songhai's collapse was technological, not organizational.*

**Question 7**
Russian expansion across Siberia in the 16th and 17th centuries was most similar to which of the following other imperial expansions in the same period?

A) Ottoman expansion into southeastern Europe through conquest and settlement
B) Qing expansion into Central Asia and Taiwan through military conquest and colonization of indigenous peoples
C) Mughal expansion into the Deccan through absorption of existing political structures
D) Safavid consolidation of the Iranian plateau through forced religious conversion

**Correct Answer: B**
*Both Russian Siberian expansion and Qing expansion into Central Asia and Taiwan involved military conquest using firearms superiority over indigenous populations, followed by settlement and resource extraction — patterns that prefigure later European colonial expansion.*

**Question 8**
Which of the following best explains why all six major land-based empires of c. 1450–1750 used gunpowder weapons?

A) A single inventor shared the technology simultaneously with all six courts
B) Gunpowder technology diffused outward from China through Central Asian trade networks, and states that adopted it gained decisive military advantages over those that did not
C) European missionaries introduced gunpowder to each empire as part of their religious diplomacy
D) All six empires independently invented gunpowder simultaneously

**Correct Answer: B**
*Gunpowder originated in China and diffused westward via the Silk Roads and Islamic world. States that adopted it (Ottomans, Mughals, Safavids, Qing) conquered those that did not (Songhai, various Central Asian polities) — a classic example of technology diffusion shaping political outcomes.*

**Question 9**
A historian argues that "land-based empires in the period c. 1450–1750 were more similar in their methods of expansion than in their methods of administration." Which of the following evidence best supports this argument?

A) All six empires used gunpowder weapons in conquest, but governed their diverse populations through very different administrative systems (devshirme, mansabdari, dyarchy, millet, etc.)
B) All six empires used identical tax-farming systems to extract revenue from subject populations
C) All six empires governed through the same religious framework derived from Islamic jurisprudence
D) All six empires expanded exclusively through diplomatic marriage alliances rather than military conquest

**Correct Answer: A**
*The convergence is in military technology (all used gunpowder); the divergence is in administration — each empire developed distinct solutions to the problem of governing diverse populations. This is the key "similar in some ways, different in others" framework for this comparison.*

---

**Question 10**
Which of the following best describes a continuity shared by ALL six major land-based empires of c. 1450–1750?

A) All six empires imposed a single religion on all subjects
B) All six empires relied on agricultural tax revenue as the foundation of their fiscal systems
C) All six empires used hereditary aristocracies exclusively to staff their administrative bureaucracies
D) All six empires maintained peaceful relations with neighboring European maritime empires

**Correct Answer: B**
*Regardless of religion, administrative model, or geography, all six empires extracted revenue primarily from taxing agricultural surplus — the universal fiscal foundation of pre-industrial states. This is the deepest structural continuity across the comparison.*

## Short Answer Questions

### SAQ 1

**Prompt:** Answer parts A, B, and C.

**A)** Explain ONE similarity in how any two of the following empires legitimized their political authority in the period c. 1450–1750: Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, Qing.

**B)** Explain ONE difference in how those same two empires managed religious diversity among their subjects.

**C)** Explain ONE reason why gunpowder technology was significant for land-based empire-building in the period c. 1450–1750.

**Top-score guidance:**
- A: Both Ottoman and Mughal used Islamic identity; both Qing and Ottoman used monumental architecture; both Safavid and Ottoman claimed religious supremacy — pick any pair with specific evidence
- B: Ottoman used formal millet system; Mughal varied by ruler (Akbar tolerant, Aurangzeb restrictive); Safavid imposed Shia homogeneity; Qing used cultural pluralism — be specific about the mechanism
- C: Firearms gave decisive advantage over cavalry-based nomads and fortified opponents; enabled states to expand into territories previously held by nomadic powers; explain cause-and-effect

### SAQ 2

**Prompt:** Answer parts A, B, and C.

**A)** Explain ONE way the Ottoman devshirme system and the Qing dyarchy system both served the goal of consolidating imperial power.

**B)** Explain ONE way the Mughal mansabdari system differed from the Ottoman devshirme system in how it recruited imperial servants.

**C)** Explain ONE continuity in how land-based empires organized their economies across the period c. 1450–1750.

**Top-score guidance:**
- A: Both created loyal administrative classes that bypassed hereditary local elites whose interests might conflict with imperial authority
- B: Mansabdars were free nobles ranked by cavalry provision; devshirme recruits were enslaved boys without family ties — very different origins and social positions, though both loyal to the emperor
- C: All relied on agricultural taxation as primary revenue; tax-farming was widespread; serfdom/coerced agriculture (Russia, Qing) persisted throughout

## Long Essay Question

**Prompt:** Compare the methods used by land-based empires to consolidate and maintain power in the period c. 1450–1750.

**Top-score guidance:**
- **Thesis:** Make a claim about the extent of similarity vs. difference. Strong option: "While land-based empires in c. 1450–1750 shared the use of gunpowder military technology and agricultural taxation as foundations of power, they differed significantly in how they managed religious diversity — ranging from the Ottoman millet system's formal pluralism to the Safavid imposition of Shia homogeneity."
- **Evidence (minimum 2 specific examples per category):**
  - Military (all): Ottomans at Constantinople; Mughals at Panipat; Qing conquest of Ming; Moroccan destruction of Songhai
  - Administration (varied): Ottoman devshirme; Mughal mansabdari; Qing dyarchy; Russian serfdom
  - Legitimation (varied): Ottoman caliphate claim; Safavid Shia; Akbar's pluralism/Din-i-Ilahi; Qing Confucian ritual
- **Complexity:** Address HOW gunpowder technology diffused from China through Islamic networks to all six empires — showing the global connection underlying the comparison; or address how the same technology produced different imperial outcomes based on geography and existing social structures
- **CCOT element:** Note that by 1750, European maritime empires were beginning to challenge land-based empires — the very gunpowder advantage that built these empires was being surpassed by European military-fiscal state innovations
