# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 4.2

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1394–1460 | Prince Henry the Navigator sponsors Portuguese exploration | First European monarch to systematically fund seafaring; begins Africa coast exploration and Atlantic slave trade |
| 1405–1433 | Zheng He's voyages (China) | State-sponsored maritime exploration across Indian Ocean; China withdrew afterward — key contrast with Europe |
| 1488 | Dias rounds Cape of Good Hope | Opened viable sea route around Africa; proved Atlantic and Indian Oceans connected |

## Chronological Patterns to Know

**Wave 1 — Portugal (1400s–1500s)**: Systematic state investment in exploration → Africa coast → Indian Ocean route → trading post empire. The pioneer nation, but overextended and vulnerable.

**Wave 2 — Spain (1490s–1560s)**: Columbus triggers; Aztec and Inca conquests fund it; Manila galleons globalize it. Spain builds a **territorial empire**, not just a trading post empire.

**Wave 3 — France, England, Netherlands (1534–1620s)**: All search for northwest passage; all fail; all find other value in the Americas. France → furs; England → settlement; Netherlands → trade networks and chartered companies.

**The Zheng He Gap (1433–1513)**: Between the end of Chinese maritime exploration and the arrival of Portuguese traders in China, roughly 80 years passed. This gap illustrates how the Ming court's political decision not to explore left China's coasts uncontested — until Portuguese ships arrived.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam sometimes asks you to sequence events or explain why one development came before another. Know the rough order: **Portugal → Spain → England/France/Netherlands**, and understand that each wave responded to and competed with the one before.
