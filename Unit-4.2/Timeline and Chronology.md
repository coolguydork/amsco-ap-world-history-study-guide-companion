# Topic 4.2 – Timeline and Chronology

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

> [!tip]
> For the AP exam, you don't need to memorize every date — but you need to know the **sequence** of events and be able to explain *why* each event mattered. Focus on the dates marked ⭐ as especially important for exam use.

## Major Milestones in European Exploration

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **1394–1460** | Prince Henry the Navigator sponsors Portuguese exploration | Establishes the model of state-sponsored exploration |
| **⭐ 1488** | Bartholomew Diaz rounds Cape of Good Hope | Proves a sea route from Europe to the Indian Ocean is possible |
| **⭐ 1492** | Columbus's first voyage; lands in Caribbean | Initiates European contact with the Americas; begins Columbian Exchange |
| **1494** | Treaty of Tordesillas | Spain and Portugal divide the world between them along a meridian |
| **1497** | John Cabot reaches North America for England | England establishes early claim to North America |
| **⭐ 1498** | Vasco da Gama lands in India | Portugal achieves direct sea route to Asia; Indian Ocean trade disrupted |
| **1500s** | French expeditions search for northwest passage | France enters competition for American territory and resources |
| **1507** | Portugal builds fort at Hormuz (Persian Gulf) | First link in Portugal's trading post empire chain |
| **1509** | Portugal defeats combined Muslim-Venetian fleet at Battle of Diu | Portugal establishes naval dominance in Indian Ocean |
| **1510** | Portugal builds fort at Goa, India | Key node in trading post empire |
| **⭐ 1511** | Portugal seizes Malacca; builds fort there | Controls Southeast Asian spice trade |
| **1514** | Portuguese traders arrive in China | First European-Chinese contact in this era |
| **⭐ 1521** | Cortés overthrows Aztec Empire | Spain gains enormous wealth and territory in Mesoamerica |
| **⭐ 1521–1522** | Magellan's fleet circumnavigates the globe | Proves earth can be sailed around; Spain claims Philippines |
| **1535** | Jacques Cartier sails St. Lawrence River | France claims territory in modern Canada |
| **1549** | Christian missionaries arrive in Japan | Japan initially tolerates, then suppresses Christianity |
| **1565** | Spain returns to Philippines; begins conquest | Manila becomes Spain's Pacific trade hub |
| **1582** | Matteo Ricci arrives in China | Jesuit missionaries attempt (and largely fail) to convert Chinese elites |
| **⭐ 1588** | England defeats Spanish Armada | England becomes a major naval power; enters Atlantic competition |
| **1600s** | Japan expels missionaries; restricts trade | Japan enters period of near-isolation (sakoku) |
| **1607** | Jamestown founded | England's first successful permanent colony in North America |
| **1608** | Quebec founded | France's main settlement in North America |
| **1609** | Henry Hudson explores Hudson River for the Dutch | Basis for Dutch claims in North America; leads to New Amsterdam |

## Chronological Patterns to Know

**The Portuguese were first.** Portugal led all European powers in systematic exploration. Their sequence — West Africa → Cape of Good Hope → India → Southeast Asia → Japan → China — unfolded over roughly 100 years (1430s–1550s).

**Spain was right behind.** Within four years of Diaz rounding Africa, Spain had sent Columbus west. Spain's path: Caribbean → Mesoamerica (Aztec) → South America (Inca) → Philippines. Spain built a territorial colonial empire, unlike Portugal's trading post empire.

**England, France, and the Dutch came later.** The defeat of the Spanish Armada (1588) was a turning point for England. By 1600, all three powers were actively competing in North America and challenging Portugal in Asia.

**Japan's closing** happened gradually. Portuguese traders arrived in the early 1500s; missionaries in 1549; full expulsion of foreigners by the 1630s. This is a story of increasing resistance to European influence.

## Causation Chain

Italian monopoly on Asian trade → Spain and Portugal seek new routes → New navigation technology enables ocean travel → State sponsorship provides financing → Voyages reveal new lands → Competition among European states accelerates exploration → Discovery of American silver transforms global economy

> [!exam-tip]
> Causation questions on the AP exam often ask you to explain what *caused* European exploration. The best answers connect: Italian monopoly (economic cause) + state sponsorship (political cause) + new technology (enabling cause) + religious motivation (cultural cause).
