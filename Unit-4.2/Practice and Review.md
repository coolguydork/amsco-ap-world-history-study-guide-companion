# Topic 4.2 – Practice and Review

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** Portugal's strategy of establishing a series of fortified trading posts along the coasts of Africa, India, and Southeast Asia is best described as building a

- A) territorial colonial empire similar to Spain's empire in the Americas
- B) trading post empire focused on controlling key chokepoints in maritime trade
- C) cultural empire based on the spread of Catholicism
- D) defensive network to protect against Ottoman naval expansion

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Portugal's trading post empire (Hormuz → Goa → Malacca) focused on controlling strategic ports and trade routes rather than governing large territories. This allowed a small nation to dominate trade without the resources needed for territorial administration.

---

**2.** Which of the following best explains why Spain's monarchs sponsored Columbus's 1492 voyage?

- A) They believed Columbus's geographical theories were scientifically proven
- B) They sought to break the Italian monopoly on Asian trade by finding a new western sea route
- C) They wanted to establish colonies in Africa to compete with Portugal
- D) They had no interest in trade and sponsored the voyage only for religious reasons

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Italian city-states controlled the lucrative overland/Mediterranean trade routes to Asia. Spain hoped a westward sea route would allow direct access to Asian goods and bypass the Italian middlemen.

---

**3.** The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) was significant primarily because it

- A) ended the war between Spain and Portugal over the Americas
- B) established a line dividing the world between Spanish and Portuguese spheres, giving Portugal Brazil
- C) granted Portugal control of the Indian Ocean spice trade
- D) allowed Spain to circumnavigate Africa en route to Asia

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Treaty of Tordesillas drew a north-south line through the Atlantic; Spain claimed all lands to the west, Portugal claimed all lands to the east. This is why Brazil is Portuguese-speaking while the rest of Latin America is Spanish-speaking.

---

**4.** The French approach to North American colonization differed most significantly from the Spanish approach in that the French

- A) primarily sought to establish permanent farming settlements
- B) converted indigenous peoples to Protestantism
- C) traded with native peoples for furs rather than displacing them for land
- D) built fortified trading posts rather than establishing any settlements

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The French relied on the fur trade and maintained relatively cooperative relations with Native Americans. The Spanish, by contrast, conquered, displaced, and enslaved indigenous populations to extract silver and agricultural wealth.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Question**: Briefly explain ONE cause of European maritime exploration in the period 1450–1600 AND provide ONE piece of specific evidence to support your explanation.

**Model Response (Economic cause)**: One major cause of European maritime exploration was the desire to break the Italian monopoly on trade with Asia. Before 1450, Italian city-states like Venice controlled the lucrative Mediterranean routes through which silk, spices, and luxury goods reached Europe. By controlling access, Italians set prices and reaped enormous profits, excluding other European powers from direct trade with Asia. Spain and Portugal responded by searching for alternative sea routes — Portugal going east around Africa (Vasco da Gama reached India in 1498) and Spain going west across the Atlantic (Columbus's 1492 voyage). The primary evidence for this economic cause is the explicit commercial motivation of the voyages: explorers were instructed to find new trade routes and claim valuable resources, not merely to map geography.

## Document-Based and LEQ Connections

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 4.2:**

- "Evaluate the extent to which state power drove European maritime expansion in the period 1450–1750."
- "Compare the methods of Portuguese and Spanish overseas expansion in the period 1450–1600."
- "Explain the causes of European maritime exploration in the period 1450–1600."

**Key arguments to be able to make:**

1. **States were essential**: Without royal financing, legal authority, and military protection, voyages were too expensive and risky. Portugal and Spain led because their governments most aggressively backed exploration.

2. **Multiple motives interacted**: Economic (break Italian monopoly, find silver), political (compete with rival states, expand territory), religious (convert non-Christians), and technological (new ships and navigation tools) causes all combined.

3. **Comparison — Portugal vs. Spain**: Portugal built a *trading post* empire focused on controlling chokepoints; Spain built a *territorial* empire involving conquest, settlement, and extraction of silver. Both relied on state sponsorship but with different strategies.

## Key Comparisons

| Explorer | Sponsoring State | Region | Goal | Legacy |
|----------|----------------|--------|------|--------|
| Columbus | Spain | Caribbean/Americas | Western route to Asia | Colonization of Americas; Columbian Exchange |
| Vasco da Gama | Portugal | Africa/India | Eastern route around Africa | Indian Ocean trading post empire |
| Magellan | Spain | South America/Philippines | Circumnavigation | Pacific trade; Spain claims Philippines |
| John Cabot | England | North America | Northwest passage | Early English claim to North America |
| Henry Hudson | Netherlands | North America | Northwest passage | Dutch claim to Hudson Valley; New Amsterdam |
| Cartier/Champlain | France | North America | Northwest passage/resources | Fur trade; Quebec; New France |

> [!tip]
> A great way to compare explorers is to ask: **What did they want? What did they find? What did their state do with it?** These three questions expose both similarities (everyone wanted a trade route to Asia) and differences (Spain colonized aggressively; France traded more peacefully).

## CCOT Prompt Practice

**Prompt**: Describe ONE continuity and ONE change in European maritime exploration strategies between c. 1450 and c. 1650.

**Continuity**: Throughout the period, European states remained centrally involved in sponsoring maritime voyages. From Portugal's sponsorship of Diaz and da Gama in the late 1400s through England's chartering of the East India Company in 1600, exploration was too expensive and risky for private individuals alone. States provided financing, legal authority to claim territory, and military backing — a pattern that persisted across all European powers throughout the period.

**Change**: The *goals* of exploration shifted from finding sea routes to Asia (primary goal, 1450–1510) toward establishing colonies and extracting resources from the Americas (dominant goal by 1550). Once Spain discovered the enormous silver deposits of Mexico and Peru in the early-to-mid 1500s, the American colonies became the primary economic focus of Spanish imperial policy, relegating the original goal of reaching Asian spice markets to secondary importance.
