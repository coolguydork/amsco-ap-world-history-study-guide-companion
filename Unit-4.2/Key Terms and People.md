# Topic 4.2 – Key Terms and People

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Key Economic and Political Terms

**Mercantilism**
An economic theory holding that a nation's wealth is measured by the amount of gold and silver it accumulates. To maximize wealth, governments pursued policies of exporting more than they imported, establishing colonies to extract raw materials, and creating trade monopolies. *Significance*: Provided the ideological justification for European colonization; required heavy state involvement in trade and exploration.

> [!warning]
> Mercantilism ≠ capitalism. Under mercantilism, states *controlled* trade. Capitalism (which developed later) relies on free markets and private investment. The AP exam tests this distinction.

**Trading Post Empire**
An empire built on a network of small, fortified commercial outposts at strategic coastal locations rather than on control of large territorial interiors. *Best example*: Portugal's chain of forts from Hormuz → Goa → Malacca. *Significance*: Allowed a small nation to control key trade chokepoints without the resources needed for territorial conquest. Compare to Spain's colonial empire, which involved large-scale settlement and territorial control.

**Manila Galleons**
Heavily armed Spanish ships that carried silver from Mexico across the Pacific to Manila in the Philippines, where it was exchanged for Chinese silk, spices, and luxury goods. *Significance*: Connected American silver to Asian markets; made Manila a key node in the first truly global trade network.

**Northwest Passage**
A hypothetical sea route through or around North America that would allow European ships to reach Asia more easily than going around South America. French, English, and Dutch explorers searched for it extensively in the 16th and 17th centuries. *Significance*: The search for a northwest passage drove much of the exploration of North America's coastline and interior.

**Monopoly**
Complete control over a market or trade route, allowing the controlling party to set prices. *Examples*: Italian city-states monopolized Mediterranean-Asian trade before 1450; Portugal tried to monopolize Indian Ocean spice trade via its trading post empire.

## Key People

**Christopher Columbus (1451–1506)**
Genoese navigator who, with Spanish sponsorship from Queen Isabella and King Ferdinand, sailed west from Europe in 1492 hoping to reach Asia. He landed in the Caribbean, initiating contact between Europe and the Americas. Never acknowledged that he had found lands unknown to Europeans — he believed until his death that he had reached Asian islands.

> [!exam-tip]
> Columbus matters for the AP exam primarily as the *catalyst* for European colonization of the Americas — not as a heroic discoverer. His voyages triggered the Columbian Exchange, the transatlantic slave trade, and the destruction of indigenous American civilizations. Frame him in terms of consequences.

**Prince Henry the Navigator (1394–1460)**
Portuguese prince who sponsored systematic expeditions along Africa's Atlantic coast. Never sailed far from shore himself. Under his leadership, Portugal began importing enslaved Africans by sea, replacing the overland slave trade. *Significance*: Represents the model of **state sponsorship** driving exploration. See also [[Unit-4.1/Key Terms and People]].

**Bartholomew Diaz**
Portuguese explorer who sailed around the Cape of Good Hope (southern tip of Africa) in 1488, proving a sea route to the Indian Ocean was possible. His crew threatened mutiny, forcing him to turn back before reaching India.

**Vasco da Gama**
Portuguese navigator who completed Diaz's route, landing in India in 1498. Established Portugal's foothold in Indian Ocean trade. His success made Portuguese expansion into Asia possible.

**Afonso de Albuquerque**
Portuguese admiral and governor of Portuguese India (1509–1515) who built Portugal's trading post empire in the Indian Ocean. Known for brutal methods — reportedly sent severed ears of conquered peoples back to Portugal as evidence of his victories. Seized Goa, Malacca, and other key ports.

**Ferdinand Magellan**
Portuguese explorer sailing for Spain who led the first circumnavigation of the globe. Died in the Philippines in 1522, but one of his ships completed the journey. *Significance*: Proved the world could be sailed around; Spain's claim to the Philippines grew out of his voyage.

**John Cabot**
Explorer sent by England in 1497 to find a northwest passage. Claimed lands from Newfoundland to the Chesapeake Bay for England. *Significance*: England's early stake in North America, though not followed up aggressively until the late 16th century.

**Henry Hudson**
Dutch-sponsored English explorer (1609) who explored the Hudson River and Bay in North America. His explorations gave the Dutch the basis for claiming the Hudson River Valley and founding New Amsterdam (later New York City).

**Jacques Cartier**
French explorer who sailed the St. Lawrence River in 1535, claiming much of modern Canada for France. Searched for a northwest passage; instead found fur-rich territory.

**Samuel de Champlain**
French explorer (1609–1616) who founded Quebec (1608) and expanded French presence in North America. Key figure in establishing the French fur trade and relationships with Native American nations.

**Matteo Ricci (arrived China 1582)**
Italian Jesuit missionary who tried to win converts among China's court elite by demonstrating European scientific and technical knowledge. *Significance*: Example of European missionaries using intellectual prestige rather than force; also evidence of early cultural contact between Europe and Ming China.

## Key Places

| Location | Significance |
|----------|-------------|
| Cape of Good Hope | Southern tip of Africa; rounding it opened the sea route to the Indian Ocean |
| Goa | Portuguese fort on India's west coast (built 1510); key Indian Ocean trading post |
| Malacca | Portuguese fort on the Malay Peninsula (built 1511); controlled access to Southeast Asian spice trade |
| Hormuz | Portuguese fort in the Persian Gulf (built 1507); gateway to the Middle East |
| Manila | Spanish commercial center in the Philippines; hub of Pacific silver trade |
| Jamestown (1607) | First permanent English settlement in North America |
| Quebec (1608) | First major French settlement in North America |
| New Amsterdam | Dutch settlement on Manhattan; later became New York City |
