# Practice Questions — Unit 4.2: (4.2): Exploration

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–3.**

> *"I, [the representative of the Spanish Crown], notify and make known to you, as best I can, that God Our Lord, One and Eternal, created Heaven and Earth, and one man and one woman, of whom you and we, and all the people of the world, were and are descendants… Therefore I request and require you… to acknowledge the Church as the mistress and superior of the whole world… If you do not, I will enter with force against you… and I will take your women and children, and I will make them slaves."*
> — The *Requerimiento*, Spanish legal document read to Indigenous peoples before conquest, c. 1513

---

**1.** The *Requerimiento* is best understood as evidence of which of the following?

- A) Spanish willingness to negotiate peacefully with Indigenous peoples before conquest
- B) The role of religious justification in legitimizing Spanish colonial violence
- C) The legal protections granted to Indigenous peoples under Spanish law
- D) Spanish respect for Indigenous political sovereignty

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Requerimiento provided a theological framework — requiring "consent" to Spanish authority — that gave legal and religious cover to conquest regardless of how Indigenous peoples actually responded.*

---

**2.** A historian studying the *Requerimiento* would most likely argue that it demonstrates:

- A) The sincerity of Spanish religious motives in the Americas
- B) That Spanish colonialism was primarily motivated by missionary Christianity rather than wealth
- C) The gap between legal/religious justifications and the actual practice of colonial violence
- D) That the Spanish Crown sought to minimize violence in its American colonies

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The Requerimiento was read in Spanish to peoples who didn't understand the language, then conquest proceeded regardless — it was a legal fiction that exposed the contradiction between stated ideals and colonial reality.*

---

**3.** Which of the following developments most directly resulted from the Spanish conquests the *Requerimiento* was used to justify?

- A) The establishment of Portuguese trading-post empires along the West African coast
- B) The development of the encomienda system and Indigenous demographic collapse
- C) The creation of the first transpacific trade route between Manila and Acapulco
- D) The founding of joint-stock companies to manage colonial enterprises

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Spanish conquest led directly to the encomienda system (coerced Indigenous labor) and the catastrophic demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans through disease, violence, and exploitation.*

---

**4.** Which of the following best explains why Portugal, not Spain, was the first European power to find a sea route to Asia?

- A) Portugal had a superior navy and more experienced sailors than Spain
- B) Spain was distracted by wars with France and could not fund exploration
- C) Portugal's earlier systematic exploration of the African coast gave it a geographic and institutional head start
- D) The Treaty of Tordesillas prohibited Spain from exploring eastern routes to Asia

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*Portugal had been systematically exploring the African coast since 1415 under Prince Henry's sponsorship — by 1488 they had rounded the Cape and knew the route to India was navigable, decades before Spain had comparable capability.*

---

**5.** The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) is best understood as evidence of which of the following?

- A) A cooperative European effort to share the benefits of exploration equally
- B) The early application of European legal frameworks to assert control over non-European territories
- C) The Catholic Church's opposition to Spanish and Portuguese colonial ventures
- D) An agreement between Spain, Portugal, and the Ottoman Empire over trade routes

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Tordesillas imposed a European-drawn line across the globe without consulting any of the peoples who lived there — an early assertion that European states and the Church had the right to divide up the world.*

---

**6.** The Portuguese trading-post empire differed most significantly from the Spanish territorial empire in that Portugal:

- A) Relied exclusively on missionary Christianity rather than military force
- B) Controlled commercial nodes along coastlines rather than conquering and administering large populations
- C) Was prohibited by the Treaty of Tordesillas from establishing colonies in the Americas
- D) Used joint-stock companies rather than royal sponsorship for its overseas ventures

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Portugal's *feitoria* model secured key ports and straits to control trade flows without the enormous cost of governing large interior territories — a fundamentally different imperial strategy from Spain's conquest and administration of Indigenous empires.*

---

**7.** Which of the following best describes a significant effect of Magellan's circumnavigation (1519–1522)?

- A) It demonstrated that the Americas were a separate continent from Asia
- B) It established Spain's claim to the Philippines and opened a Pacific trade route
- C) It proved that a Northwest Passage existed through North America
- D) It ended Portuguese dominance of the Indian Ocean spice trade

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The circumnavigation gave Spain geographic knowledge of the Pacific and established the foundation for the Manila Galleon trade (1565), the first transpacific commercial route.*

---

**8.** The primary economic motive driving Portuguese and Spanish exploration in the late 15th century was:

- A) The desire to establish plantation colonies using enslaved African labor
- B) Access to Asian spices and luxury goods without paying Ottoman and Venetian markups
- C) The need to find new agricultural land for Europe's growing population
- D) Competition with the Ming Dynasty for control of Indian Ocean trade

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The spice trade was enormously profitable; Ottoman control of overland routes gave intermediaries enormous leverage over European buyers, making a direct sea route to Asia the primary commercial goal.*

---

**9.** Which of the following represents the most significant long-term consequence of Columbus's 1492 voyage?

- A) The establishment of permanent Spanish settlements in the Caribbean
- B) The discovery of a viable westward route to Asia
- C) The integration of the Western Hemisphere into a global network of exchange for the first time
- D) The creation of the Treaty of Tordesillas dividing the Atlantic world

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*Columbus's most significant historical consequence was connecting two previously isolated hemispheres — triggering the Columbian Exchange, demographic collapse, and the Atlantic economy that defined Period 4.*

---

**10.** The rapid Spanish conquest of the Aztec and Inca Empires in the early 16th century was most directly enabled by:

- A) Superior Spanish military strategy and professional army training
- B) A combination of epidemic disease, Indigenous allies, and firearms
- C) The weakness and political disunity of Aztec and Inca leadership
- D) Spanish use of naval blockades to starve Indigenous populations

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Smallpox devastated populations before and during the conquests; Tlaxcalan and other Indigenous allies provided the bulk of military manpower; and Spanish firearms and horses provided psychological and tactical advantages — no single factor suffices.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Evaluate the most significant motivation behind European maritime exploration in the period 1450–1600."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE economic motivation for European exploration.
**Part B:** Identify ONE religious or political motivation for European exploration.
**Part C:** Explain which motivation you believe was most significant and provide evidence to support your argument.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Focus on the spice trade and bypassing Ottoman intermediaries — be specific, not vague.
> - **Part B:** Religious mission (*Reconquista* mentality, papal bulls) or political rivalry between Iberian crowns.
> - **Part C:** Strong arguments are **arguable** — you can choose economic *or* religious/political, but you must provide specific evidence. Avoid saying "all motivations were equally important."

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the Portuguese and Spanish approaches to building overseas empires in the period 1450–1600."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE similarity between Portuguese and Spanish overseas expansion.
**Part B:** Briefly describe ONE difference in how each empire was organized or operated.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why the two empires developed differently.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Similarity:** Both were Catholic, Iberian, state-sponsored, and used violence against non-European peoples.
> - **Difference:** Portugal = trading-post / coastal / commercial empire; Spain = territorial / interior / tribute-extracting empire.
> - **Reason:** Spain encountered wealthy, densely populated Indigenous empires worth conquering; Portugal encountered coastlines and existing trade networks better exploited through commercial nodes.
