# Timeline — Unit 4.2: (4.2): Exploration

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

> [!tip] Big Picture
> Topic 4.2 covers the explosive burst of European exploration between 1488 and 1600 that permanently connected all inhabited continents. Every event in this timeline was made possible by the technologies of 4.1 ([[Unit-4.1]]) and each one cascades into the economic, social, and demographic consequences explored in Topics 4.3–4.8.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1488 | Bartolomeu Dias rounds Cape of Good Hope | Southern Africa | Proves Atlantic-to-Indian Ocean route is navigable; launches final phase of Portuguese push to Asia |
| 1492 | Columbus's first voyage; lands in Caribbean | Americas | First sustained European contact with the Americas; triggers [[Columbian Exchange]] |
| 1493 | Pope issues *Inter Caetera* papal bull | Rome / Atlantic | Grants Spain rights over newly discovered western lands; obligates Christian missionary activity |
| 1494 | Treaty of Tordesillas | Atlantic | Spain and Portugal formally divide the non-European world; Portugal gets Brazil and Asia |
| 1497–1499 | Vasco da Gama sails to India | Indian Ocean | First European sea voyage to Asia; Portugal gains direct access to spice trade |
| 1500 | Pedro Álvares Cabral claims Brazil for Portugal | South America | Establishes Portuguese foothold in the Americas; later becomes center of sugar plantation economy |
| 1513 | Vasco Núñez de Balboa crosses Panama; sights Pacific | Central America | First European to see the Pacific from the Americas; establishes Spanish presence in the region |
| 1519–1521 | Cortés conquers Aztec Empire | Mesoamerica | Spain gains control of the most populous region of the Americas; sets template for conquest |
| 1519–1522 | Magellan-Elcano circumnavigation | Global | First voyage around the world; proves Earth's size and Pacific's extent |
| 1532 | Pizarro captures Atahualpa; conquest of Inca begins | Andes | Spain gains control of South America's richest silver-producing region |
| 1545 | Silver discovered at Potosí | Bolivia | World's largest silver deposit; transforms global trade and triggers [[Price Revolution]] |
| 1565 | Spanish establish permanent colony in Philippines (Manila) | Pacific | Enables Manila Galleon trade; first transpacific commercial route |
| 1577–1580 | Francis Drake circumnavigates globe | Global | England challenges Spanish dominance; signals European competition for global empire |
| 1607 | English establish Jamestown, first permanent English colony | North America | England enters the Americas seriously; begins competition with Spain and France |
| 1608 | French establish Quebec | North America | France's primary North American base; center of fur trade and missionary activity |

---

> [!warning] Continuity Note
> European exploration displaced, not replaced, existing trade networks. The Indian Ocean trade continued; Arab, Indian, and Chinese merchants remained active. What changed was European *insertion* into these networks — and their use of violence to extract favorable terms.
