# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 6.1 | AMSCO pp. 367–374**

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1600 | British East India Company (EIC) chartered | Corporate vehicle for British commercial and eventual political control of India; model of corporate imperialism |
| 1602 | Dutch East India Company (VOC) chartered | First major joint-stock colonial company; dominated Southeast Asian spice trade; model for later imperial corporate structures |
| 1788 | First British settlers arrive in New South Wales (Australia) | Beginning of British settler colonialism in the Pacific after loss of American colonies; nationalist empire-building |
| 1857–58 | Indian Rebellion (Sepoy Mutiny); British Crown dissolves EIC | Britain assumes direct rule of India; marks transition from corporate to formal imperial governance |
| 1859 | Darwin publishes *On the Origin of Species* | Theory of natural selection later misappropriated as Social Darwinism to justify racial hierarchy and imperial conquest |
| 1870–71 | Franco-Prussian War; German unification | France's defeat intensifies French nationalist drive for colonial empire as compensation; unified Germany enters imperial competition |
| c. 1870–1914 | "New Imperialism" and Scramble for Africa accelerate | Industrial nations compete intensively for territory in Africa and Asia; nationalist, cultural, and economic motives peak simultaneously |
| 1884–85 | Berlin Conference | European powers formalize rules for partitioning Africa; no African representatives present; shows imperialism as both nationalist competition and European diplomatic cooperation |
| 1894–95 | Sino-Japanese War | Japan defeats China; acquires Korea and Formosa (Taiwan); demonstrates that Asian industrial powers could be imperial aggressors, not only victims |
| 1899 | Kipling publishes "The White Man's Burden" | Iconic statement of cultural-paternalistic rationale for imperialism; frequent AP primary source |

> [!tip]
> Notice the causal sequence: industrialization (1760s–1870s) → intensified economic competition → nationalist rivalry → Scramble for Africa and Asia. Causation runs directly from Unit 5 into Unit 6. The timeline of the EIC (1600) and VOC (1602) also connects New [[Concept Notes/Imperialism\|Imperialism]] to the first wave of colonialism in Unit 4.
