# Timeline & Chronology

**AMSCO pp. 480–492**

> [!exam-tip]
> Notice the clustering: the early 1920s brought hyperinflation (Germany) and the NEP (USSR); the early 1930s brought the Depression's peak political fallout; the mid-to-late 1930s saw fascist aggression (Spain, Ethiopia) setting up WWII.

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1917–1922 | Russian Civil War | Devastated Soviet economy; prompted Lenin's NEP as emergency compromise |
| 1921 | Lenin introduces the New Economic Plan (NEP) | Pragmatic retreat from pure communism; allowed limited private trade to stabilize economy |
| 1922 | Mussolini takes power in Italy | First fascist government in Europe; pioneered corporatist economic model |
| 1922 | Formation of the U.S.S.R. | World's first communist state formally established; 15 Soviet republics under Bolshevik control |
| Early 1920s | Hyperinflation in Weimar Germany | Reparations payments printed away; middle-class savings wiped out; deepened resentment of Versailles |
| 1929 | Wall Street Crash; [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]] begins | Global economic catastrophe; mass unemployment; destabilized democratic governments worldwide |
| 1929 | PRI founded in Mexico | Post-revolutionary single-party consolidation; provides political stability through authoritarian control |
| 1928–1932 | Stalin's First Five-Year Plan | Forced rapid industrialization; state command economy replaces NEP; enormous human cost |
| 1929–1933 | Collectivization of Soviet agriculture | Peasant farms merged into kolkhozy; grain seized by state; Holodomor famine kills millions in Ukraine |
| 1931 | Spanish Republic established | King Alfonso XIII abdicates; democratic government includes liberals, socialists, regional autonomy movements |
| 1933 | Hitler appointed Chancellor of Germany | Depression + Versailles resentment + weak democracy enables Nazi rise; democracy dismantled within months |
| 1933 | FDR's New Deal begins | Keynesian response to Depression: relief, recovery, reform; preserves democracy while expanding state |
| 1934 | Lázaro Cárdenas elected president of Mexico | Land reform and economic nationalism; culminates in 1938 oil nationalization |
| 1935 | Italy invades Ethiopia | Mussolini's imperial expansion; League of Nations imposes sanctions but fails to stop aggression |
| 1936 | Spanish Popular Front wins election | Coalition of leftists wins; triggers Franco's military uprising months later |
| 1936 | Spanish Civil War begins | Franco's Nationalists vs. Republican Popular Front; proxy war between fascism and democracy |
| 1936–1938 | Stalin's Great Purges | Tens of thousands executed; millions sent to gulags; Soviet military officer corps decimated |
| April 1937 | Bombing of Guernica | Nazi Luftwaffe and Italian air force bomb Basque civilian town; first major aerial bombing of civilians |
| 1938 | Cárdenas nationalizes Mexican oil → PEMEX | Foreign oil companies expropriated; PEMEX created; key example of Latin American economic nationalism |
| April 1939 | Franco's Nationalists win Spanish Civil War | Spain becomes fascist dictatorship; Franco rules until 1975 |

## Causation Arrow: From Depression to WWII

```
WWI reparations → Weimar hyperinflation (1920s)
        ↓
Wall Street Crash (1929) → Great Depression → mass unemployment
        ↓
Political radicalization: Hitler (1933), expanded fascism in Italy/Spain
        ↓
Fascist aggression: Ethiopia (1935), Spanish Civil War (1936–39), Anschluss (1938)
        ↓
→ WWII (Topic 7.6)
```

> [!tip]
> The AP often asks: what were the long-term causes of WWII? The Great Depression is always a key answer — it enabled fascist governments that then pursued aggressive foreign policies leading to war.
