# Key Terms & Concepts

**AMSCO pp. 480–492**

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently uses primary sources from this era — Cárdenas's oil nationalization speech, Lenin's kulak telegram, FDR's inaugural address. Know the context behind each so you can deploy them as evidence.

## Economic Concepts

**inflation**
Rise in prices that erodes purchasing power. Weimar Germany experienced hyperinflation in the early 1920s as the government printed money to pay war reparations, destroying middle-class savings and destabilizing democracy. The Great Depression later reversed this into deflation. Both extremes fueled political radicalism.

**deficit spending**
Government policy of spending more than it collects in taxes, borrowing to make up the difference. [[John Maynard Keynes|Keynesian economics]] endorsed deficit spending as the tool to stimulate demand during recessions. FDR's [[New Deal]] and WWII mobilization used it to pull the U.S. out of the Depression.

**relief**
The first "R" of FDR's New Deal: immediate aid to suffering Americans — unemployment insurance, food assistance, welfare programs — to prevent starvation and homelessness during the Great Depression.

**recovery**
The second "R" of FDR's New Deal: policies to restart the economy — public works (CCC, WPA), agricultural price supports, banking reform — to reduce unemployment and restore consumer spending.

**reform**
The third "R" of FDR's New Deal: structural changes to prevent another depression — regulation of banks and stock markets (SEC, FDIC), labor protections (Wagner Act), Social Security. Permanently expanded the federal government's economic role.

**corporatism**
The economic doctrine underlying Italian Fascism. The state organizes the economy into sector-based corporations integrating employers, trade unions, and government officials. Theoretically eliminates class conflict by subordinating all economic actors to state direction; in practice it suppressed independent labor unions and served big business.

**collectivize**
Stalin's forced policy (1929–1933) merging millions of privately owned peasant farms into large state-run collective farms. Intended to modernize agriculture and feed industrial workers. Met fierce peasant resistance — Stalin responded by seizing grain, causing the Holodomor famine in Ukraine (est. 3.5 million deaths).

**kolkhoz**
A Soviet collective farm. Peasants were forced to pool land, livestock, and tools into communally managed operations with state-set production quotas. The system caused massive disruption, famine, and millions of deaths in the early 1930s.

**deficit spending** *(see above)*

**hypernationalism**
Extreme nationalism placing the nation above all other values, combined with militarism, xenophobia, and claims of cultural or racial superiority. A defining feature of interwar fascist movements in Europe and Japan. Used by Mussolini, Hitler, and Portugal's Salazar to mobilize mass support and justify conquest.

## Economic Events & Policies

**[[Concept Notes/Great Depression|Great Depression]]**
Global economic crisis (1929–1939) triggered by the U.S. stock market crash. Mass unemployment, bank failures, and collapsing prices spread worldwide. Destabilized democratic governments and drove populations toward authoritarian alternatives — fascism on the right, communism on the left.

**New Deal**
FDR's sweeping package of programs and reforms (1933–1939) to bring relief, recovery, and reform during the Great Depression. Programs included the CCC (public works for young men), WPA (arts and infrastructure jobs), TVA (regional development), Social Security Act, FDIC (bank deposit insurance), and SEC (stock market regulation). Inspired by Keynesian economics; permanently enlarged the federal government.

**New Economic Plan (NEP)**
Lenin's pragmatic 1921 retreat from pure communism after the Russian Civil War devastated the Soviet economy. Allowed small-scale private trade and farmers to sell surplus grain on the market. Stabilized the Soviet economy through the 1920s until Stalin abandoned it in 1928–29 in favor of forced collectivization and Five-Year Plans.

**Five-Year Plan**
Stalin's centralized economic blueprints setting mandatory production quotas for Soviet industry and agriculture in five-year cycles. The first (1928–32) prioritized heavy industry — steel, coal, machinery — rapidly industrializing the USSR at enormous human cost (forced labor, famine, purges). Subsequent plans continued the pattern; used as a model by other authoritarian states.

**PEMEX** (Petróleos Mexicanos)
State-owned oil company created in 1938 when President [[Lázaro Cárdenas]] nationalized Mexico's oil industry, expropriating mostly U.S. and British companies. Became one of the world's largest state-owned enterprises. Key AP example of economic nationalism and state-led development in Latin America.

## Political Structures

**totalitarian state**
A political system in which the government seeks to control all aspects of public and private life — economy, culture, education, press, religion — through censorship, surveillance, propaganda, and terror. Exemplified by Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy, and Stalinist USSR. Distinguishes 20th-century authoritarian regimes from earlier dictatorships that tolerated civil society.

**[[Concept Notes/Fascism|fascism]]**
Authoritarian ultranationalist ideology rejecting both liberal democracy and communism. Emphasizes the supremacy of the nation-state, a single-party dictator, military expansion, and racial/cultural hierarchy. First took power in Italy (Mussolini, 1922), then Germany (Hitler, 1933). Thrived in conditions of economic crisis and national humiliation.

**Politburo**
The Political Bureau of the Communist Party's Central Committee — the top decision-making body of the Soviet Union. Stalin seized control of it after Lenin's death, using it to consolidate one-man rule. Members faced execution if they crossed Stalin, especially during the Great Purges (1936–1938).

**Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI)**
Mexican political party formed in 1929 that dominated the country's politics for over seven decades. Under the PRI, all presidents were party members until 2000. Provided political stability after the Mexican Revolution while maintaining an authoritarian grip through patronage and electoral manipulation. A post-revolutionary single-party model common across the Global South.

**gulag**
Soviet system of forced labor camps. Millions of political prisoners, ethnic minorities, and perceived regime opponents were imprisoned; death rates were high due to brutal conditions. A defining feature of Stalinist totalitarianism and a key AP example of state terror.

## The Spanish Civil War (1936–1939)

**Spanish Republic**
The Second Spanish Republic (1931–1939), established after King Alfonso XIII abdicated. A democratic government including liberals, socialists, and regional autonomy movements. Its Popular Front government was overthrown by Franco's Nationalist military uprising in 1936, sparking the Spanish Civil War.

**Popular Front**
Coalition of leftist parties — communists, socialists, liberals — formed to oppose fascism. Won Spain's 1936 elections but was overthrown by Franco's uprising. Also formed governments in France and Chile. Represented the Comintern's strategy of anti-fascist alliances across party lines.

**Nationalists**
Franco's rebel coalition in the Spanish Civil War, comprising conservative monarchists, the Catholic Church, landowners, the Falange (Spanish fascists), and much of the military. Received crucial military support from Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy. Defeated the Republican government by April 1939.

**Republicans / Loyalists**
Defenders of Spain's elected democratic government (the Second Spanish Republic) in the Civil War. Included socialists, communists, anarchists, and liberals. Supported by the Soviet Union, Mexico, and international volunteers. Defeated by Franco's Nationalists in 1939.

**Guernica**
Basque town in northern Spain bombed by Nazi Germany's Luftwaffe and Fascist Italy's air force in April 1937. One of the first deliberate aerial bombings of a civilian population. Shocked world opinion. Immortalized by Picasso's painting. Became a global symbol of fascist brutality.

**Luftwaffe**
Germany's air force under the Nazi regime. Gained combat experience bombing Guernica in the Spanish Civil War. Became the spearhead of WWII Blitzkrieg campaigns. Its failure to defeat the RAF in the Battle of Britain (1940) was Hitler's first major strategic setback.

**Basque region**
Autonomous region in northern Spain with a distinct language and culture. The Basque people supported the Spanish Republic. The bombing of Guernica — a Basque cultural center — became the war's defining atrocity.

> [!warning]
> Don't mix up Republicans/Loyalists (they're the same side — defenders of the elected government) with the Nationalists (Franco's rebel coalition). The naming is counterintuitive: the "Nationalists" were the rebels.

## Soviet Geography

**Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (U.S.S.R.) / Soviet Union**
Communist federal state formed in 1922 after the Bolshevik consolidation of power. Comprised Russia and surrounding republics spanning Eurasia. Under Stalin it industrialized rapidly through coercive Five-Year Plans while millions died in purges, gulags, and forced famine. A major Allied power in WWII; dissolved in 1991.

**Russian Civil War**
Armed conflict (1917–1922) between the Bolshevik Red Army and anti-Bolshevik White forces, foreign interventionists, and nationalist movements. Devastated the economy. Ended with Bolshevik victory and formation of the USSR. Prompted Lenin to introduce the pragmatic NEP to stabilize the country.

## Italian Imperialism

**Libya / Italian Somaliland**
Territories Italy colonized in the late 19th–early 20th centuries. Mussolini used Italian Africa to justify imperial prestige. Italian Somaliland was the launching point for the invasion of Ethiopia (1935), which exposed the weakness of League of Nations collective security.
