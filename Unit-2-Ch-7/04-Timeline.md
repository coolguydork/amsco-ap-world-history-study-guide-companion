# Chapter 7: Timeline — The Silk Roads

> [!tip]
> **Big Picture:** Chapter 7 sits within AP Period 1 (c. 1200–1450), a period defined by the *intensification* of existing trade networks and their transformation by Mongol conquest and plague. The Silk Roads did not begin or end in this period — they were ancient before 1200 and continued (in maritime form) after 1450 — but the Mongol era represents their peak volume and their most catastrophic consequence (the Black Death).

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| c. 100 BCE–200 CE | Han Dynasty China and Roman Empire linked via Silk Roads | Eurasia | Silk Roads established as a sustained network; silk moves west, glassware and wool move east — **CONTINUITY**: routes themselves ancient, persist to this period |
| 7th–8th century CE | Rise of Islam; Muslim merchants begin dominating Silk Road trade | Middle East, Central Asia | Islam spreads along trade routes; Muslim merchants displace Sogdians as primary middlemen |
| 1206 | Temujin unifies Mongol tribes; becomes Genghis Khan | Central Asia | Beginning of the Mongol conquests that will reshape Silk Road commerce |
| 1215 | Mongols sack Beijing (Zhongdu); begin conquest of northern China | East Asia | Demonstrates Mongol military reach; disrupts northern Chinese commerce temporarily |
| 1220–1221 | Mongols destroy Samarkand, Bukhara, and other Silk Road oasis cities | Central Asia | Short-term catastrophic disruption of Silk Road infrastructure; long-term: Mongols rebuild and profit from trade |
| 1258 | Mongols sack Baghdad; end of Abbasid Caliphate | Middle East | Removes the most powerful Islamic state; the *Pax Mongolica* begins to knit the resulting Mongol-controlled territory into a trade network |
| c. 1250s–1340s | *Pax Mongolica* — relative peace across Mongol-unified Eurasia | Eurasia | Peak era of Silk Road commerce; Marco Polo's journey made possible; plague also travels these routes |
| 1271–1295 | Marco Polo travels to China and returns; eventually dictates *Travels* | Eurasia | Primary source for Silk Road conditions under Pax Mongolica; evidence of Chinese wealth that fuels European desire for direct trade — **CHANGE**: European awareness of Asia intensifies |
| 1304–1368 | Ibn Battuta's travels across the Islamic world and beyond | Africa, Middle East, Asia | Primary source for Islamic trade networks, Silk Road conditions in the 14th century, and the reach of Muslim merchant communities |
| 1330s | Bubonic plague originates in Central Asian rodent populations | Central Asia | Beginning of the pandemic that will travel Silk Roads westward |
| 1346–1353 | Black Death sweeps across the Middle East, North Africa, and Europe | Eurasia, N. Africa | 25–50% mortality in affected regions; collapses labor forces, destabilizes Mongol rule, ends the *Pax Mongolica* — **CHANGE**: single biggest demographic event of the period |
| 1347 | Plague arrives in Sicily via Genoese trading ships from Caffa (Crimea) | Europe | Illustrates how Silk Roads and maritime routes were interconnected vectors of disease spread |
| 1368 | Yuan Dynasty collapses; Ming Dynasty founded | East Asia | End of Mongol rule in China; overland Silk Road loses key eastern anchor — **CHANGE**: Ming initially restricts foreign trade, weakening overland networks |
| c. 1370s–1405 | Timur (Tamerlane) briefly reunifies Central Asian Silk Roads | Central Asia | Last major restoration of overland trade; Timurid Renaissance (Samarkand as cultural capital) — short-lived *continuity* |
| c. 1400–1450 | Maritime routes increasingly replace overland routes as dominant Silk Roads | Indian Ocean, Southeast Asia | **CHANGE**: The overland Silk Road declines; maritime networks rise — setting stage for Portuguese entry into Indian Ocean trade in Unit 3 |
