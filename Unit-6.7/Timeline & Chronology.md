# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 6.7 — Effects of Migration | AMSCO pp. 429–438**

---

## Core Dates

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1833 | Britain abolishes slavery in its empire | Creates immediate demand for replacement labor → launches large-scale Indian indentured servitude to colonies |
| c. 1848–1855 | California gold rush | Major pull factor for Chinese migration to the U.S.; later triggers nativist backlash and anti-Chinese legislation |
| 1853 | Argentine Constitution guarantees civil rights to foreigners | Key pull factor for massive Italian (and other European) immigration to Argentina |
| 1855 | Chinese Immigration Act (Victoria, Australia) | First major anti-Chinese immigration law in Australia; limits Chinese per ship |
| c. 1850s–1860s | Australian gold rushes | Chinese population reaches ~50,000; triggers violent nativist attacks and legal restrictions |
| 1860–1861 | White miner attacks on Chinese goldfields in New South Wales | Immediate cause of the Chinese Immigration Regulation and Restriction Act (1861) |
| 1861 | Chinese Immigration Regulation and Restriction Act (NSW) | Escalation of legal discrimination; sets precedent for White Australia Policy |
| 1879 | California constitution revised to target Chinese residents | Prohibits Chinese from public employment, denies citizenship, encourages segregation |
| 1881 | Influx of Chinese Restriction Act (NSW) | Entrance tax; further escalation of anti-Chinese policy in Australia |
| 1882 | Chinese Exclusion Act (USA) | First U.S. federal law targeting a specific ethnic group; suspended Chinese laborer immigration |
| 1886–1901 | ~32,000 Indian workers go to Kenya for railroad construction | Shows scale of Indian labor migration across the British Empire; few chose to remain |
| 1893 | Gandhi arrives in Pretoria, South Africa | Experiences discrimination; begins transformation into activist |
| 1894 | Gandhi founds Natal Indian Congress | First formal Indian civil-rights organization; prototype for later anti-colonial movements |
| 1902 | Chinese Exclusion Act made permanent | Demonstrates entrenched racial exclusion in U.S. immigration law |
| 1901 | Australian federation; White Australia Policy adopted | Culmination of decades of anti-Chinese legislation; restricts non-British immigration until mid-1970s |
| 1914 | Gandhi returns to India | Transitions from South Africa activist to leader of Indian nationalist movement |

## Chronological Patterns to Know

**The escalation pattern in Australia:** Each decade brought stricter restrictions — 1855 (ship limits) → 1861 (colony-wide restriction) → 1881 (entrance tax) → 1901 (national policy). The AP may ask you to trace this escalation as an example of CCOT in state immigration policy.

**The labor substitution sequence:** 1833 abolition → indentured servitude → kangani system. Each step shifted the form of coerced labor while maintaining the underlying economic structure of plantation and colonial labor exploitation.

**Gandhi's trajectory as chronological causation:** 1893 arrival in South Africa → 1894 Natal Indian Congress → years of nonviolent resistance → 1914 return to India → 1947 Indian independence. The AP may ask you to explain how migration experiences shaped later political movements.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP does not require you to memorize every year, but you should know the **Chinese Exclusion Act (1882)** and the **White Australia Policy (1901)** as major milestones of state discrimination, and **Gandhi's Natal Indian Congress (1894)** as a migration-caused political organization with long-term consequences.
