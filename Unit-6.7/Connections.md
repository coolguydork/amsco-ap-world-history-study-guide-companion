# Connections

**Topic 6.7 — Effects of Migration | AMSCO pp. 429–438**

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-6.6/Causes of Migration in an Interconnected World Overview\|Causes of Migration in an Interconnected World Overview]]**
Topic 6.6 establishes the push and pull factors that drove 19th-century migration — poverty, famine, labor demand, abolition of slavery, colonial recruitment systems. Topic 6.7 follows directly: the migrations *caused* by those factors produced the ethnic enclaves, nativist backlash, discrimination laws, and political organizations covered here. Never discuss effects without grounding them in the causes from 6.6.

---

## Leads To

**Newly Independent States [[Unit-8.6/Newly Independent States Overview|Newly Independent States Overview]]**
Gandhi's experience organizing against anti-Indian discrimination in South Africa (Natal Indian Congress, 1894) was the formative chapter of his political life. When he returned to India in 1914, he brought the tactics of nonviolent resistance that would define the independence movement. Topic 6.7 is thus a *causal origin* of 20th-century decolonization movements.

## Compare With

**Topic 1.3 — African Societies**
The demographic disruption caused by the [[Concept Notes/Atlantic Slave Trade\|Atlantic Slave Trade]] and the later 19th-century Indian and Chinese migration flows are both cases of large-scale coerced or semi-coerced migration reshaping populations. The key difference: the slave trade created diasporas with *no* connection to homeland, while 19th-century indentured laborers often maintained ties and sometimes returned.

**Topic 6.6 — Causes of Migration**
The companion topic. 6.6 = causes; 6.7 = effects. They are frequently tested together. Know how to move from one to the other in an essay without blending them.

## Cross-Topic Connections

**[[Concept Notes/Labor Unions\|Labor Unions]]**
Irish immigrants played a foundational role in the American labor movement, promoting unions and workers' rights. Denis Kearney's Workingmen's Party simultaneously advanced labor organizing *and* anti-Chinese nativism — a contradiction that the AP may ask students to analyze. Labor organizing is both an effect of Irish migration and a cause of subsequent discrimination against Chinese migrants.

**[[Concept Notes/Indian National Congress\|Indian National Congress]]**
Gandhi's political education in South Africa — resisting discrimination against Indian migrants through the Natal Indian Congress — directly shaped his leadership of the Indian National Congress in India. The Congress, founded in 1885, became the primary vehicle for Indian independence. The connection: migration experiences produced the activist who led decolonization.

**[[Concept Notes/Pan-Africanism\|Pan-Africanism]]**
The experience of diaspora communities — Indian in South Africa, African in the Caribbean — being discriminated against in receiving societies contributed to Pan-African and anti-colonial consciousness. The Natal Indian Congress is an early prototype of diaspora political organizing against racial discrimination.

## AP Comparison Opportunities

**Compare Chinese immigrant experiences in the U.S. vs. Australia:**
Both countries used gold rushes as the trigger for nativist backlash; both produced escalating anti-Chinese laws; both ultimately implemented exclusionary national policies. Key difference: in Australia, the exclusion became national policy at federation (1901) and was explicit. In the U.S., the Chinese Exclusion Act (1882) was technically aimed at *laborers* only and was eventually repealed (1943). The White Australia Policy is the more durable and comprehensive example.

**Compare Indian and Chinese diaspora experiences:**
Both faced nativist discrimination in receiving societies. Key difference: Chinese immigrants were more dispersed (SE Asia, Americas, Australia) and faced legislative exclusion; Indian immigrants under the British Empire were often labor-recruited by the colonial government itself, creating a different power dynamic. Gandhi's activism in Natal is an Indian diaspora response that has no clear Chinese parallel in this period.

**Compare effects of voluntary vs. coerced migration:**
Italian immigrants to Argentina were largely voluntary economic migrants; they faced less discrimination and achieved greater economic mobility, eventually comprising 55%+ of the population. Indian indentured servants had little legal choice; their mobility was severely constrained during the indenture period. The type of migration clearly influenced the nature of the experience in the receiving society.

> [!exam-tip]
> The most common LEQ comparison this topic supports: "Compare the experiences of TWO migrant groups in their receiving societies in the period 1750–1900." Strong pairs: Chinese in U.S. vs. Australia; Indian in Africa vs. Caribbean; Irish in U.S. vs. Italians in Argentina.
