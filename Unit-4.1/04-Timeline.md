# Timeline — Unit 4.1: (4.1): Technological Innovations

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

> [!tip] Big Picture
> Topic 4.1 focuses on the *tools* that made Period 4 possible. These technologies were not new in 1450 — most had existed for centuries — but their combination and state-sponsored deployment in Atlantic Europe between 1415 and 1500 triggered a global transformation. This chapter sets up every subsequent Unit 4 topic.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| c. 850 | Gunpowder invented in China | China | First use of explosive chemical mixture; spreads westward over centuries |
| c. 1040 | Magnetic compass used for navigation in China | China | Directional navigation without stars; later adopted in Europe via Islamic world |
| c. 1200s | Lateen sail and dhow design spread across Indian Ocean | Arab world / Indian Ocean | Allows sailing into the wind; later adapted by Portuguese shipbuilders |
| c. 1300 | Portolan charts developed in Mediterranean | Italy / Spain | Detailed coastal navigation maps; enable systematic charting of new coastlines |
| c. 1350 | Stern rudder widely adopted in European ships | Europe | Improved maneuverability; superior to side-mounted rudders for ocean voyaging |
| 1415 | Portuguese capture Ceuta; Prince Henry begins sponsoring exploration | Portugal / North Africa | Institutional launch of systematic maritime exploration |
| c. 1430–1440 | Caravel design perfected in Portugal | Portugal | Lightweight, maneuverable ship that enables Atlantic exploration |
| c. 1440 | Gutenberg develops movable-type printing press | Germany | Rapid diffusion of maps, navigation manuals, and geographic knowledge |
| 1488 | Bartolomeu Dias rounds Cape of Good Hope | Southern Africa | Proves Atlantic-to-Indian Ocean sea route is navigable; validates Portuguese investment |
| 1494 | Luca Pacioli publishes first accounting textbook (double-entry bookkeeping) | Italy | Codifies financial tools needed to manage large commercial ventures |
| 1497 | Vasco da Gama reaches India using combined navigational technologies | Indian Ocean | First successful European sea voyage to Asia; direct result of 4.1 innovations |
| 1519–1522 | Magellan-Elcano expedition circumnavigates the globe | Global | Definitive proof of Earth's shape and the scale of oceanic distances |
| 1600 | British East India Company uses joint-stock model | England | Financial innovation enables sustained imperial commerce |
| 1602 | VOC founded as world's first publicly traded company | Netherlands | Joint-stock model scales to handle continent-spanning commercial empire |

---

> [!warning] Continuity Note
> Many of the technologies listed here predate 1450 by centuries. The key *change* is their **systematic combination and state-sponsored deployment** in Atlantic Europe — a qualitative shift, not just incremental improvement.
