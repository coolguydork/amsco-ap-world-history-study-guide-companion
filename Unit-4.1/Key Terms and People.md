# Topic 4.1 – Key Terms and People

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Navigation Technology Terms

**Magnetic Compass**
Originally invented in China for fortune telling; adapted by Chinese and later European mariners in the 12th century for directional navigation. Works because Earth's magnetic field aligns freely moving magnets along the north-south axis. *Significance*: Allowed sailors to determine direction in cloudy weather or at night — essential for open-ocean travel.

**Astrolabe**
An instrument improved by Muslim navigators in the 12th century that measures the angle of the sun or a star above the horizon. Used to calculate **latitude** (distance north or south of the equator). *Significance*: Without knowing latitude, open-ocean navigation was extremely dangerous.

**Cartography**
The art and science of mapmaking. Improved dramatically during this period through more accurate astronomical charts, better instruments, and the accumulation of voyage data. *Significance*: Better maps reduced navigational error and allowed sailors to plan routes with confidence.

**Astronomical Chart**
Any map of the stars and galaxies used by mariners to determine direction and location. Used since antiquity by Babylonian, Chinese, and Greek astronomers. Telescopes improved star-chart accuracy after 1609.

**Lateen Sail**
A triangular sail used by Arab sailors in the Indian Ocean. Unlike the ancient square sail (which only worked with wind directly behind the ship), the lateen could catch wind from *either* side, allowing ships to sail into the wind. *Significance*: Combined with a square sail, the lateen enabled transoceanic travel for the first time.

**Caravel**
A small, three-masted Portuguese sailing ship developed in the 15th century that combined square and lateen sails. Maneuverable, sturdy in storms, and capable of long voyages. *Significance*: The primary ship used by Portuguese and Spanish explorers; enabled European maritime expansion.

**Carrack**
A larger sailing ship with 3–4 masts. Used for long ocean voyages at speed by the Portuguese and Spanish. Successor to the caravel for major trade routes.

**Fluyt**
A Dutch cargo ship with a wide hold and small crew requirements. Economical and efficient. *Significance*: Helped make Dutch commercial trade highly profitable in the 17th century.

**Gunpowder**
Chinese invention that, when mounted on ships as cannons, gave European vessels devastating military firepower. *Significance*: Allowed Europeans to dominate existing trade networks through force; also adopted by pirates (e.g., Dutch Sea Beggars).

**Primogeniture**
Legal inheritance system giving the entire estate to the eldest son. *Significance*: Pushed younger sons of European nobles to seek fortune through exploration, conquest, and colonization.

> [!warning]
> Students often think primogeniture caused exploration by itself. It's one *push factor* in a complex mix that also includes population pressure, religious motivations, commercial rivalries, and technological capability.

**Maritime Empires**
Empires based primarily on sea power and control of trade routes rather than on large land territories. Spain, Portugal, Britain, France, and the Netherlands all became maritime empires. Contrast with the land-based empires of the Ottomans and Mughals.

**Omani-European Rivalry**
Ongoing competition between the Omani Sultanate (based in the Arabian Peninsula) and European powers — especially Portugal — for control of Indian Ocean trade routes. One motivation for Columbus's search for a westward route to Asia.

## Key People

**Alexander Neckham (1157–1217)**
English scholar who wrote one of the earliest European descriptions of the magnetic compass, describing how sailors touched a needle with a magnet to find north. *Exam relevance*: Often quoted in primary source documents as early evidence of compass use in Europe.

**Prince Henry the Navigator (1394–1460)**
Portuguese prince who sponsored systematic maritime exploration along the West African coast, though he himself never sailed far out to sea. He financed expeditions, supported cartographic research, and began the Portuguese practice of importing enslaved Africans by sea — replacing the overland slave trade. *Exam relevance*: Key figure showing how **state sponsorship** drove European exploration.

> [!exam-tip]
> Henry the Navigator is a great example of state involvement in exploration. He didn't sail himself, but he organized the **infrastructure** — funding, mapmaking, ship design — that made Portuguese expansion possible. Use him to argue that states were essential to transoceanic exploration.

**Tsar Peter the Great of Russia**
Visited Western Europe in 1697 to observe military and naval technology. Hired German and other European technicians to help modernize Russia's military and naval power. *Significance*: Shows that maritime technology continued to spread cross-culturally well into the 17th century, reaching even Russia.
