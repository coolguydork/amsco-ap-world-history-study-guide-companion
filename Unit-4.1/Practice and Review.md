# Topic 4.1 – Practice and Review

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The magnetic compass, astrolabe, and lateen sail all contributed most directly to which of the following developments in the period 1450–1750?

- A) The expansion of Mongol trade networks across Central Asia
- B) European maritime exploration and the opening of transoceanic trade routes
- C) The spread of Islam through the Indian Ocean trade network
- D) The development of land-based gunpowder empires in Asia

**Answer: B** — All three technologies helped European sailors navigate open oceans safely. The compass gave direction, the astrolabe gave latitude, and the lateen sail gave flexibility in wind direction.

---

**2.** The navigational technologies used by European explorers in the 15th and 16th centuries were primarily the result of which of the following?

- A) European innovation during the Renaissance
- B) Cross-cultural exchange among Chinese, Islamic, and European traditions
- C) The rediscovery of ancient Greek navigational methods
- D) Portuguese investment in original scientific research

**Answer: B** — The compass came from China, the astrolabe was improved by Muslim navigators, and the lateen sail was used by Arab sailors. European explorers synthesized these traditions rather than inventing them from scratch.

---

**3.** Prince Henry the Navigator's most significant contribution to European maritime expansion was his

- A) personal leadership of voyages to the Indian Ocean
- B) invention of the caravel and lateen sail
- C) systematic state sponsorship of expeditions along the African coast
- D) negotiation of trade agreements with the Ottoman Empire

**Answer: C** — Henry organized and financed exploration rather than sailing himself. He represents the model of **state sponsorship** driving maritime exploration.

---

**4.** Which of the following best explains why European states became centrally involved in sponsoring maritime exploration?

- A) Individual merchants had enough capital to finance voyages independently
- B) Voyages were too expensive for individuals alone, and states had political and economic motives for expansion
- C) The Catholic Church required states to fund missionary voyages
- D) European law prohibited private individuals from organizing overseas expeditions

**Answer: B** — Voyages required enormous capital investment. States had both the resources and the motives (wealth, territory, religious expansion, rival competition) to sponsor them.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Question**: Using specific evidence, explain ONE way in which cross-cultural interactions contributed to changes in maritime technology between 1200 and 1500.

**Model Response**: One way cross-cultural interactions contributed to changes in maritime technology was the adoption and improvement of the astrolabe in Europe. Originally a Greek instrument, it was refined by Muslim navigators in the 12th century and became an essential navigational tool that allowed sailors to calculate their latitude — how far north or south of the equator they were. European sailors learned of this improved instrument through contact with Islamic scholars, particularly through Al-Andalus (Islamic Spain), where Islamic and European intellectual traditions intersected. Without the ability to calculate latitude, sailors could not safely navigate open oceans, so this cross-cultural technological transfer was essential to later European maritime expansion.

## Document-Based and Long Essay Connections

**For DBQs**: Topic 4.1 often appears in documents related to the causes of European exploration or the role of technology in enabling empire. Look for:
- Primary source descriptions of navigation instruments
- State charters or royal orders sponsoring voyages
- Comparisons between European and Asian maritime technologies

**For LEQs**: Common prompts connected to 4.1:
- "Evaluate the extent to which cross-cultural exchange drove European maritime expansion in the period 1450–1600."
- "Compare the role of technology in enabling the Mongol Empire (c. 1200–1350) with the role of technology in enabling European maritime empires (c. 1450–1600)."

> [!exam-tip]
> When writing about technology and exploration, always make the *causal* argument explicit: technology created the **capability** for ocean travel; states and merchants provided the **motivation** and **financing**. Both are needed to explain European expansion.

## Key Comparisons to Know

| Feature | Chinese Navigation (to c. 1430) | European Navigation (1450–1600) |
|---------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Key ships | Treasure ships (enormous) | Caravels (small, nimble) |
| State support | Strong initially, then withdrawn after Zheng He | Strong and sustained |
| Purpose | Display power, collect tribute | Find trade routes, claim territory, spread Christianity |
| Long-term result | China withdrew from ocean trade | European maritime empires dominated global trade |

> [!warning]
> Don't confuse the Chinese abandonment of ocean exploration (after Zheng He's final voyage, c. 1430) with European expansion. The AP exam frequently uses this contrast to test understanding of **why** China and Europe made different choices. China's decision was political (opposition from Confucian scholar-gentry); Europe's expansion was driven by commercial rivalry and state competition.

## CCOT Prompt Practice

**Prompt**: Describe a continuity AND a change in maritime trade networks between c. 1200 and c. 1600.

**Continuity**: The Indian Ocean remained a central hub of global trade throughout this period. Arab and Indian merchants continued trading across the Indian Ocean after European arrival, maintaining familiar patterns of paying port fees and using religious/ethnic networks.

**Change**: The entry of European maritime powers — especially Portugal — fundamentally altered who controlled trade routes. By the early 1500s, the Portuguese used superior naval firepower to take over key Indian Ocean trading posts (like Malacca), disrupting the established Muslim merchant networks and imposing licensing fees at gunpoint.
