# Practice Questions — Unit 4.1: (4.1): Technological Innovations

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–2.**

> *"He [Prince Henry] had also a wish to know the land that lay beyond the isles of Canary and that Cape called Bojador, for that up to his time, neither by writings, nor by the memory of man, was known with any certainty the nature of the land beyond that Cape."*
> — Gomes Eanes de Zurara, *Chronicle of the Discovery of Guinea*, c. 1450

---

**1.** The passage best illustrates which of the following factors driving European maritime expansion?

- A) Religious crusading impulse against Islam
- B) State-sponsored curiosity and desire for geographic knowledge
- C) Competition with Chinese maritime expeditions
- D) Demand for enslaved labor in Portuguese sugar colonies

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Zurara emphasizes Henry's desire for knowledge of unknown lands — a state-sponsored intellectual and commercial drive that characterized the early Portuguese exploration model.*

---

**2.** Which of the following technological developments most directly made the exploration described in the passage possible?

- A) The invention of the printing press
- B) The development of the joint-stock company
- C) The perfection of the caravel and lateen sail
- D) The discovery of gunpowder by European alchemists

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The caravel's ability to navigate coastal shallows and sail into the wind made systematic exploration of the African coast feasible; without it, venturing beyond Cape Bojador was extremely dangerous.*

---

**3.** The magnetic compass used by European explorers in the 15th century was most directly derived from which of the following?

- A) Ancient Greek mathematical models
- B) Islamic astronomical instruments developed in Baghdad
- C) Chinese navigational technology diffused through the Islamic world
- D) Indigenous Pacific Islander wayfinding techniques

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The compass originated in Song Dynasty China and reached Europe via Arab intermediaries — a classic example of cross-cultural technology diffusion.*

---

**4.** The development of joint-stock companies in the 16th and 17th centuries most directly addressed which obstacle to European overseas expansion?

- A) The lack of trained sailors willing to undertake dangerous voyages
- B) The high cost and financial risk of long-distance commercial voyages
- C) The inability of European states to build large ocean-going vessels
- D) The resistance of the Catholic Church to commercial activity

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Joint-stock companies spread financial risk across many investors, making it viable to fund expensive voyages whose ships might be lost — without ruining any single investor.*

---

**5.** The decision by the Ming Dynasty to end Zheng He's voyages after 1433 most directly demonstrates which of the following?

- A) China lacked the navigational technology to sustain oceanic exploration
- B) Political and cultural factors, not just technology, determine whether states pursue maritime expansion
- C) China's economy was too weak to fund further exploration
- D) Zheng He's voyages had produced no commercially valuable results

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*China had superior ships but Confucian court culture viewed merchants unfavorably and saw no strategic value in continued exploration — demonstrating that technology alone does not determine historical outcomes.*

---

**6.** Which of the following best explains why Portuguese navigators, rather than those of other European states, led early oceanic exploration?

- A) Portugal possessed a larger population and more sailors than Spain or England
- B) Portugal's location and Prince Henry's institutional support gave it a head start in maritime technology
- C) Portugal had conquered Muslim Spain and gained access to Islamic navigational knowledge
- D) Portuguese explorers were more willing to accept financial risk than other Europeans

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Portugal's Atlantic coastline, its early engagement with West Africa, and Prince Henry's state-sponsored navigation school gave it a structural advantage over other European competitors.*

---

**7.** The printing press most directly contributed to European maritime expansion by:

- A) Enabling sailors to communicate with each other across long distances
- B) Accelerating the spread of geographic knowledge, maps, and navigation manuals
- C) Allowing European states to publish propaganda encouraging emigration
- D) Providing a cheaper alternative to manuscript books for navigation

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The press made books and maps affordable and widely available, rapidly diffusing geographic discoveries and practical navigation knowledge among European sailors, merchants, and rulers.*

---

**8.** Gunpowder weapons gave European maritime powers which of the following advantages in establishing trading-post empires in Asia?

- A) The ability to travel faster than Asian merchant vessels
- B) Superior diplomatic skills in negotiating with Asian rulers
- C) The capacity to bombard coastal fortifications and overpower local naval resistance
- D) Access to Asian markets that had previously been closed to outside trade

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*Portuguese cannon-equipped ships could destroy coastal defenses and force local rulers to grant trading rights — a coercive advantage that enabled the establishment of feitorias (trading posts) around the Indian Ocean rim.*

---

**9.** Which of the following represents a significant continuity between the technological developments of Unit 3 (1200–1450) and those of Unit 4 (1450–1750)?

- A) European states continued to dominate the development of new navigational technologies
- B) Technologies continued to spread through cross-cultural contact and trade networks
- C) Gunpowder weapons continued to be used exclusively by Chinese armies
- D) The joint-stock company model continued unchanged from its medieval origins

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Technology diffusion through trade and cultural exchange was a defining feature of the Mongol-era Silk Roads (Unit 2–3) and continued in Unit 4 — what changed was the *direction* and *recipients* of diffusion.*

---

**10.** The development of double-entry bookkeeping in Renaissance Italy is best understood as an example of which of the following AP World History themes?

- A) Political transformation driven by new forms of governance
- B) Economic innovation enabling more complex and large-scale commercial networks
- C) Cultural exchange between European and Islamic intellectual traditions
- D) Environmental adaptation in response to agricultural surplus

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Double-entry bookkeeping is a financial/economic innovation — it made large, multi-party commercial ventures manageable, directly supporting the expansion of long-distance trade networks.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain how technological innovations enabled European maritime expansion in the period 1450–1600."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE specific technology that enabled European oceanic exploration.
**Part B:** Explain how that technology was developed or acquired by Europeans.
**Part C:** Explain ONE consequence of European maritime expansion that resulted from the use of this technology.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A/B:** Strong answers name a *specific* technology (caravel, astrolabe, compass) and trace its origins — don't say Europeans "invented" the compass or astrolabe.
> - **Part C:** Connect directly to a Unit 4 consequence — Columbian Exchange, Atlantic slave trade, or trading-post empires. Vague answers ("it helped them explore") won't earn full credit.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the approach to maritime technology and exploration of Ming China and Portugal in the 15th century."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE similarity between Chinese and Portuguese maritime capabilities around 1400–1430.
**Part B:** Briefly describe ONE difference in how each state used its maritime technology after 1430.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why the outcomes of Chinese and Portuguese maritime activity differed so dramatically.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Both had large, capable ocean-going vessels and state sponsorship at some point.
> - **Part B:** China withdrew after 1433; Portugal accelerated down the African coast.
> - **Part C:** Best answers focus on *political/cultural* factors (Confucian skepticism of merchants, Ming court politics) rather than just technology differences.
