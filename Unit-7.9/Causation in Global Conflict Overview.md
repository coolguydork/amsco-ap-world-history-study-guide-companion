# Causation in Global Conflict

**AP Period 4 | Printed pp. 531–543 | AP Skill: Causation**

> [!exam-tip]
> Topic 7.9 is a **synthesis and causation** chapter — the AP will ask you to evaluate the *relative significance* of causes. Know how to rank underlying vs. immediate causes, and how one effect becomes a cause for a subsequent event (chain causation).

## The Big Picture

The 20th century's global conflicts reshaped political power worldwide. At the century's start, Western European empires dominated. By 1945, power had shifted decisively to the United States, the Soviet Union had emerged as a rival superpower, and colonial empires were dissolving. Three great empires — Ottoman, Russian, and Qing — collapsed entirely. Understanding *why* these conflicts happened is the core AP skill for this topic.

## Political Causes of Global Conflict

The roots of WWI lay in four intertwined political forces: a shifting **alliance system** that turned a regional Balkan crisis into a continental war; **nationalism** (especially Serbian nationalism, the "spark in the powder keg"); an **arms race** that made all parties confident in war; and **imperial rivalry** among European powers, Russia, and Japan for territory and resources.

WWI's settlement planted the seeds of WWII. The [[Concept Notes/Fascism\|Fascism]] of Germany and Italy was extreme nationalism weaponized as state ideology. Fascist regimes exploited resentment of the Treaty of Versailles and the failure of Western democracies to oppose early German aggression (appeasement). Japan's imperial ambitions in Asia drove the Pacific theater of WWII.

## Economic Causes of Global Conflict

The primary economic driver was competition over **markets and resources**. As industrialization spread globally, access to raw materials and consumer markets became strategic. This fueled imperialism in Africa and Asia throughout the 19th century — the same tensions that contributed to WWI.

The U.S. oil and steel embargo against Japan (in response to Japanese expansion in Asia) directly triggered the Pearl Harbor attack (December 7, 1941), bringing the U.S. into WWII. The [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]] (1929–1939) deepened economic grievances across Europe and enabled the rise of leaders like Hitler who promised economic revival.

## Effects of Global Conflict

**Human cost:** WWI and WWII together killed approximately 85 million people. Civilian deaths soared in WWII due to aerial bombardment, genocide, mass starvation, and the atomic bomb — marking a fundamental change: modern warfare killed as many civilians as soldiers.

**Political transformation:** WWI toppled the Ottoman and Russian empires. The Russian Revolution produced a communist totalitarian state; the Ottoman collapse eventually produced a secular Turkish republic. Treaty of Versailles resentment fueled [[Concept Notes/Fascism\|Fascism]] in Germany and Italy, setting up WWII.

**Decolonization:** Both wars weakened colonial powers economically and ideologically. Wilson's **[[Concept Notes/Self-Determination\|self-determination]]** principle after WWI legitimized independence movements. After WWII, exhausted European powers could no longer sustain their empires. India gained independence in 1947 but was partitioned into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan — a division that produced ongoing conflict.

**Power shift:** The U.S. emerged from both wars relatively unscathed compared to European powers and became the dominant Western power. The Soviet Union became its superpower rival, setting the stage for the Cold War (Unit 8).

## Relative Significance Framework — AP Skill

When evaluating relative significance of causes, ask:

- **Underlying vs. immediate:** [[Concept Notes/Imperialism\|Imperialism]] and nationalism are long-term; the assassination of Franz Ferdinand is immediate. Both matter — underlying causes explain *why* war was possible; immediate causes explain *why now*.
- **Chain causation:** Treaty of Versailles → economic hardship → rise of fascism → WWII. Each effect becomes a new cause.
- **Equal vs. dominant:** Were causes roughly equal (WWI: MAIN — militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism), or did one dominate (WWII economic cause: Japan's resource hunger → Pearl Harbor)?

> [!tip]
> AMSCO organizes causes as **political** and **economic**. AP rewards answers that distinguish categories, then argue which mattered most — don't just list causes.
