# Key Terms & Concepts

> [!exam-tip]
> Topic 7.9 is a synthesis/causation chapter — AMSCO provides no KEY TERMS BY THEME box. The vocabulary here is derived from the narrative and the AP skill framework for Causation. These are the analytical concepts the exam tests on this topic.

**Causation**
The historical reasoning skill of identifying and explaining why events happen. The AP distinguishes between *underlying* causes (long-term structural forces) and *immediate* causes (specific events or decisions that trigger an outcome). Relative significance asks which causes mattered *most* and why.

**Relative significance**
The ability to compare and rank causes by importance rather than just listing them. AP prompts routinely ask students to "evaluate the extent to which" a cause or effect was significant — this requires argument and evidence, not enumeration.

**Chain causation**
When an effect becomes the cause of a subsequent event. Key chain for Unit 7: nationalism → WWI → Treaty of Versailles → economic grievances → rise of fascism → WWII → decolonization → Cold War superpower rivalry.

**Total war**
A conflict in which a nation mobilizes all economic, social, and military resources. WWI was the first total war; WWII escalated the scale further, with industrial killing of civilians through aerial bombing, genocide, and atomic weapons. See [[Concept Notes/Total War\|Total War]].

**Alliance system**
The network of mutual defense treaties among European powers (Triple Alliance: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy; Triple Entente: France, Russia, Britain) that transformed the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand into a continental war. A key political cause of WWI.

**Arms race**
Competitive military buildup among great powers before WWI — naval rivalry (especially Britain vs. Germany) and land army expansion. Increased both the capability and likelihood of war.

**Imperial rivalry**
Competition among Western powers, Russia, and Japan for territory, markets, and resources in Africa and Asia. An economic-political overlap cause of both WWI tensions and Japanese expansion leading to WWII.

**[[Concept Notes/Nationalism\|Nationalism]]**
The belief that a people sharing language, culture, or ethnicity deserve their own sovereign state. In WWI: Serbian nationalism sparked the Balkan crisis; in WWII: extreme nationalism became [[Concept Notes/Fascism\|Fascism]]; after WWII: nationalism drove decolonization movements worldwide.

**[[Concept Notes/Fascism\|Fascism]]**
An extreme form of ultranationalist, authoritarian ideology that rejected liberal democracy and communism. In Germany ([[Concept Notes/Nazism\|Nazism]]) and Italy, fascist governments exploited resentment of the Treaty of Versailles and economic depression to seize power and pursue expansionist war.

**Appeasement**
The policy of Western democracies (Britain and France) of conceding to Hitler's territorial demands (e.g., Munich Agreement, 1938) to avoid war. Failed to stop German expansion and is now understood as a key immediate cause of WWII by enabling Hitler's aggression.

**[[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]]**
The global economic collapse of 1929–1939 triggered by the U.S. stock market crash. Mass unemployment and economic desperation across Europe enabled the rise of populist and fascist leaders who promised recovery. An economic cause of WWII.

**Self-determination**
The principle, advocated by U.S. President Woodrow Wilson after WWI, that peoples should have the right to choose their own government. Inspired anti-colonial independence movements but was inconsistently applied — rarely extended to non-European peoples at the Paris Peace Conference.

**Decolonization**
The process by which colonial territories gained independence, accelerating after WWII when colonial powers were economically exhausted. Former colonies with small settler populations typically gained independence peacefully; those with large settler populations often experienced violent transitions.

**Partition of India (1947)**
The division of British India into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan (which included East Pakistan, now Bangladesh) upon independence. Religious and ideological differences between Hindus and Muslims produced a violent partition and ongoing military conflicts between the two countries.

**Superpower rivalry**
The emergence of the United States and the Soviet Union as the two dominant global powers after WWII, displacing Western Europe. Sets the context for the Cold War (Unit 8) and represents a major political effect of 20th-century global conflict.

> [!warning]
> MAIN (Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism) is a useful mnemonic for WWI causes — but AP prompts usually ask you to go beyond listing. You need to argue which of these was most significant and support it with evidence.
