# Connections

## Within Unit 7 — This Topic Synthesizes All of Them

**[[Unit-7.1/Shifting Power Overview\|Shifting Power Overview]]**
7.9 is the synthesis payoff for 7.1. The power shift from Western Europe to the U.S. and the emergence of the Soviet Union that 7.1 introduces is the major political *effect* that 7.9 explains through causation. Know how imperialism and nationalism in 7.1 fed the causes of both world wars.

**[[Unit-7.2/Causes of World War I Overview\|Causes of World War I Overview]]**
The detailed political causes (MAIN: Militarism, Alliances, [[Concept Notes/Imperialism\|Imperialism]], Nationalism) in 7.2 are the specific content that supports 7.9's framework. When 7.9 asks about WWI causes, 7.2 provides the evidence.

**[[Unit-7.3/Conducting World War I Overview\|Conducting World War I Overview]]**
The [[Concept Notes/Total War\|total war]] concept, colonial soldiers, and the scale of WWI death detailed in 7.3 is evidence for 7.9's claim about unprecedented effects on civilian populations.

**[[Unit-7.4/Economy in the Interwar Period Overview\|Economy in the Interwar Period Overview]]**
The [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]] as an economic cause of WWII is introduced in 7.4. Use 7.4 data (unemployment rates, bank collapses, falling trade) to support the economic cause argument in any 7.9 LEQ.

**[[Unit-7.5/Unresolved Tensions After World War I Overview\|Unresolved Tensions After World War I Overview]]**
The Versailles resentment chain — a key link in 7.9's chain causation argument — is detailed here. Treaty of Versailles → humiliation → rise of fascism is the through-line.

**[[Unit-7.6/Causes of World War II Overview\|Causes of World War II Overview]]**
The political causes of WWII (fascism, appeasement, Japanese expansion) covered in 7.6 are 7.9's content for the second world war. Know the connections between Versailles, the Depression, and Hitler's rise.

**[[Unit-7.7/Conducting World War II Overview\|Conducting World War II Overview]]**
The scale of WWII destruction — aerial bombing, Holocaust, Pacific theater, atomic bomb — documented in 7.7 supports 7.9's argument about effects of global conflict (unprecedented civilian deaths, change in warfare).

**[[Unit-7.8/Mass Atrocities Overview\|Mass Atrocities Overview]]**
The Holocaust, Armenian Genocide, and other atrocities detailed in 7.8 are the most extreme *effects* of 20th-century global conflict that 7.9 asks students to explain causally. What conditions made mass atrocities possible?

## Cross-Unit Connections

**Unit 6 — Industrial Revolution and Imperialism**
The imperial rivalry underlying WWI grew directly from industrialization and 19th-century imperialism. Topics 6.1–6.3 (Industrial Revolution) and 6.5–6.7 (Imperialism) provide the long-term economic context for 7.9's causes. AMSCO explicitly prompts this connection: "Compare the motivating factors for wars fought in the 20th century with wars fought in the 19th century."

**Unit 8 — Cold War**
7.9's effects — superpower rivalry, decolonization, and new international institutions (UN) — set up Unit 8 directly. The Cold War is the consequence of 20th-century global conflict's power shift. India-Pakistan tensions (7.9 effect) also continue through Unit 8.

**Unit 9 — Globalization**
Decolonization (7.9 effect) produced the new states that participate in Unit 9's global economy and governance structures. The UN and international cooperation frameworks that 7.9 introduces are foundations of the post-1980 order.
