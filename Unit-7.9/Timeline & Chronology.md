# Timeline & Chronology

## Master Timeline — Causes and Effects of Global Conflict

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 19th century (ongoing) | Imperial rivalry: Western powers, Russia, and Japan compete for territory and markets in Asia and Africa | Long-term economic cause underlying both world wars |
| 1914 | Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo; WWI begins | Immediate cause of WWI; alliance system escalates regional conflict to continental war |
| 1914–1918 | WWI fought as first [[Concept Notes/Total War\|total war]]; ~85M total deaths in both world wars | Scale unprecedented; industrial weapons (machine guns, poison gas, artillery) redefined warfare |
| 1917 | Russian Revolution; Romanov dynasty collapses; Bolsheviks seize power | Political effect of WWI: totalitarian communist state replaces tsarist empire |
| 1919 | Treaty of Versailles; Wilson's Fourteen Points and self-determination principle | Immediate cause of WWII resentment; also sparks anti-colonial independence movements |
| 1910s–1920s | Ottoman Empire collapses; Turkey becomes secular republic under Atatürk | Political effect of WWI: major empire replaced by Western-style democracy |
| 1929–1939 | [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]] spreads globally; mass unemployment, economic collapse | Economic cause of WWII: enables rise of fascist populist leaders including Hitler |
| 1930s | [[Concept Notes/Fascism\|Fascism]] rises in Germany (Nazism) and Italy; appeasement by Western democracies | Political cause of WWII: resentment of Versailles + economic crisis + ideology of extreme nationalism |
| 1931–1937 | Japan expands into Manchuria and China; U.S. imposes oil/steel embargo on Japan | Economic cause of Pacific theater of WWII; resource scarcity drives Japanese expansion |
| 1939–1945 | WWII; civilians targeted at mass scale; atomic bombs dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki | Major change: civilian deaths (50M+) surpass military; new weapons of mass destruction |
| 1945 | United Nations founded | Political effect: attempt to prevent future wars through international cooperation |
| 1945 | U.S. and Soviet Union emerge as rival superpowers | Power shift away from Western Europe; sets stage for Cold War (Unit 8) |
| 1947 | India and Pakistan gain independence; Partition of India | Effect of WWII weakening colonial powers; religious partition produces mass violence and ongoing conflict |
| Post-1945 | Decolonization accelerates across Africa and Asia | Effect: exhausted colonial powers cannot maintain empires; nationalism drives independence movements |

## Cause-and-Effect Chain

```
19th-century imperialism & nationalism
        ↓
WWI (1914–1918)
        ↓
Treaty of Versailles (1919) — resentment + self-determination
        ↓
Great Depression (1929) — economic collapse
        ↓
Rise of fascism (1930s) — Germany, Italy
        ↓
WWII (1939–1945)
        ↓
Superpower rivalry (U.S. vs. USSR) → Cold War
Decolonization → new independent states
```

> [!tip]
> The AP frequently asks you to explain how 20th-century global conflicts were connected across time. This chain from imperial rivalry → WWI → WWII → Cold War is the backbone of Unit 7. Memorize the chain, not just individual dates.
