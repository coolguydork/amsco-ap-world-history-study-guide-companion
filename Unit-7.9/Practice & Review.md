# Practice & Review

## Long Essay Questions

**LEQ 1 — Causation (core prompt)**
From 1900 to the present, political and economic events have triggered global conflicts. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which different causes of global conflict are significant in the period 1900 to the present.

> [!tip] Approach — LEQ 1
> This is the central 7.9 prompt. Your thesis should take a clear position on *which* category of cause was most significant — political (nationalism, alliance system, fascism) or economic (imperial rivalry, resource competition, Great Depression) — and defend it with specific evidence across both world wars. The complexity point is easiest to earn here by acknowledging that causes shifted between WWI and WWII: nationalism dominated WWI while economic collapse was more decisive for WWII's timing.
>
> **Sample thesis:** "While nationalism was the most immediate political cause of both world wars, economic competition — particularly the drive to control markets and resources and the devastation of the Great Depression — was the deeper structural cause that made large-scale global conflict in the 20th century nearly inevitable."
>
> **Evidence to use:** WWI: alliance system, Serbian nationalism (Balkans as "powder keg"), arms race, imperial rivalry. WWII: Treaty of Versailles resentment → Great Depression → rise of Hitler; Japan's resource scarcity → Pearl Harbor. Decolonization as the long-term political effect of both conflicts.

**LEQ 2 — Continuity and Change**
The Paris Peace Conference after World War I had even more profound effects on the world than the war did. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which the 1919 Paris Peace Conference after World War I was a success or a failure.

> [!tip] Approach — LEQ 2
> Structure your argument around the conference's stated goals vs. actual outcomes. Wilson's Fourteen Points (self-determination, League of Nations, fair peace) vs. what Versailles actually delivered (punitive reparations, territorial dismemberment, no self-determination for non-European peoples, U.S. Senate rejecting the League).
>
> **Failure argument (stronger for this prompt):** War guilt clause and reparations fueled German resentment → economic instability → Hitler's rise → WWII. The chain causation from Versailles to WWII is the best evidence for "failure."
>
> **Success qualification (complexity point):** Some new nation-states emerged in Eastern Europe based on self-determination; the League did resolve some minor disputes. But these successes were overwhelmed by the consequences of excluding Germany from genuine peace.

**LEQ 3 — Continuity (anti-Semitism)**
Adolf Hitler's campaign of terror against Jews in the 1930s and 1940s had its roots in earlier discrimination against Jews in Europe. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which the nature of anti-Semitism in Europe from the Middle Ages through World War II shows continuities or changes over time.

> [!tip] Approach — LEQ 3
> **Continuity:** Religious anti-Semitism (Jews as Christ-killers) persisted from medieval Europe through the early modern period; legal restrictions, ghettos, and pogroms were recurring features. **Change:** 19th-century racial "science" transformed anti-Semitism from religious prejudice into pseudo-biological racism — Jews categorized as a dangerous race rather than a misguided religion. Nazi ideology took this racial anti-Semitism to genocide. The **change** from religious to racial framing is the most important transformation to highlight; the continuity of persecution as a pattern across time is the other key argument.

**LEQ 4 — Continuity and Change (territory)**
Conflicts over territory helped cause two world wars in the 20th century, but they also led to self-rule for many peoples worldwide in the 20th and 21st centuries. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which territorial holdings from 1900 to the present show continuity or change over time.

> [!tip] Approach — LEQ 4
> **Continuity:** Great powers still competed for territory at the start of the 20th century (imperialism → WWI) and through WWII (Japanese expansion, German annexations). **Change:** After WWII, the era of formal colonial empire effectively ended — decolonization produced dozens of new independent states. The change from a world of empires (1900) to a world of nation-states (post-1945) is a fundamental transformation. Complexity: some territorial disputes from decolonization (India-Pakistan, Israel-Palestine) created new conflict, so change in the form of territorial control did not produce change in the existence of territorial conflict.

## Document-Based Question

**DBQ — July Crisis, 1914**
Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which world leaders failed to prevent a regional conflict between Austria-Hungary and Serbia after the assassination of Franz Ferdinand from becoming a general European war.

The documents provided are the **Kaiser-Tsar correspondence** (July 29–August 1, 1914): letters and telegrams between Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany and Tsar Nicholas II of Russia as war approached.

> [!tip] DBQ Document Analysis — HAPP Framework
> **Document 2 (Tsar to Kaiser, July 29):** *Purpose* — Nicholas appeals to family friendship to restrain Germany's ally Austria-Hungary. *Historical Situation* — mobilization had not yet begun; diplomatic channels were still open. *Point of View* — a monarch caught between personal friendship and state obligation.
>
> **Document 3 (Kaiser to Tsar, July 28 and 29):** *Purpose* — Wilhelm tries to frame Austrian action as justified (responding to Serbian terrorism) while asking Nicholas not to mobilize. *POV* — Kaiser is simultaneously defending his ally and genuinely trying to mediate, revealing the tension between alliance loyalty and peace.
>
> **Documents 4–5 (late July–August 1):** Show deterioration: once Russian mobilization began (for defensive reasons against Austrian mobilization), the logic of military timetables overtook diplomacy. *Historical Situation* — once any great power mobilized, others had to respond within days or risk strategic disadvantage. This is the **best evidence** that the *alliance system and mobilization logic*, not personal failures, drove escalation.
>
> **Outside evidence to add:** The alliance system (Triple Alliance vs. Triple Entente) as structural cause; assassination of Franz Ferdinand as trigger; Austrian ultimatum to Serbia; German "blank check" to Austria-Hungary.
>
> **Thesis direction:** World leaders *tried* but ultimately *failed* — not because they were indifferent to peace but because the structural features of the alliance system and military mobilization timetables made de-escalation nearly impossible once the crisis began. This nuanced argument (partial failure, structural cause) earns complexity.

## Key Skill Review — Relative Significance

**The AP rubric rewards arguments that evaluate significance, not just describe causes.** Use this checklist when writing any 7.9 essay:

- [ ] Does my thesis make a claim about *which* cause(s) were most significant? (Not just "there were many causes")
- [ ] Do I explain the *mechanism* — how did the cause produce the effect?
- [ ] Do I address *relative* weight — why was one cause more important than another?
- [ ] Do I use **chain causation** (WWI → Versailles → Great Depression → WWII) to show how effects became causes?
- [ ] Do I earn the complexity point by qualifying my argument (acknowledging a counterargument or demonstrating a nuanced relationship)?
