# Causes of World War I

**AP Period 4** | Unit 7: Global Conflict | Printed pp. 461–468
**AP Themes:** GOV, ECN, CDI | **Skill:** Causation
**Essential Question:** What combination of long-term and short-term factors caused World War I?

---

## The Big Picture

World War I (1914–1918) — the "Great War" — was the product of decades of mounting tension in Europe suddenly detonated by a single act of political violence. No previous war had drawn in so many nations from so many parts of the world, or produced such staggering casualties. The war shattered the European order, toppled four empires, redrawn borders, and set the conditions for an even more catastrophic conflict a generation later.

Understanding WWI requires separating the **immediate cause** (the assassination that lit the fuse) from the **long-term structural causes** (the pile of gunpowder). Historians use the acronym **MAIN** — Militarism, Alliances, [[Concept Notes/Imperialism\|Imperialism]], [[Concept Notes/Nationalism\|Nationalism]] — to organize those long-term forces.

---

## Immediate Cause: Assassination in Sarajevo

On June 28, 1914, **Gavrilo Princip**, a Serbian nationalist and member of the [[Concept Notes/Black Hand|Black Hand]] secret society, assassinated **Archduke Franz Ferdinand** — heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne — and his wife Sophie in Sarajevo, Bosnia. The Black Hand aimed to end Austro-Hungarian control over the Balkans and unite South Slavs under Serbian leadership.

Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and issued a harsh ultimatum. When Serbia rejected its terms, Austria-Hungary declared war on July 28, 1914. Within weeks the alliance system did the rest: Germany declared war on Russia and France; Britain declared war on Germany; Japan entered the conflict. A regional dispute became a world war in under five weeks.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP frequently tests whether students can distinguish **immediate** vs. **underlying** causes. The assassination is the *spark*; MAIN (Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism) are the *powder*. Be ready to explain both, and to argue which long-term cause was most significant.

---

## Long-Term Causes: MAIN

**Militarism** — European powers competed for dominance partly by investing in massive armies and navies. Germany and Britain in particular poured resources into military buildup and naval construction. Industrial mass production made new weapons abundant. A militaristic culture glorified war and led publics to expect a quick, almost festive conflict — "over by Christmas," as one British soldier recalled.

**Alliances** — Seeking security and leverage, European powers formed a web of binding mutual-defense pacts, many of them secret. The two main blocs were the **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy — 1882) and the **Triple Entente** (France, Russia, Britain — 1907). These agreements meant any two-power conflict could cascade: Russia mobilized for Serbia, Germany mobilized against Russia, France was automatically in, Britain followed when Germany violated Belgian neutrality to execute the Schlieffen Plan.

**Imperialism** — European rivalry for colonies in Africa and Asia produced friction for decades before 1914. France resented Germany over the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71) and the loss of Alsace-Lorraine. Britain and France competed with Germany for African colonies. These imperial grudges hardened the alliance blocs and gave powers concrete strategic interests to defend.

**Nationalism** — Ethnic nationalism was destabilizing multinational empires. Serbs, Croats, Czechs, Arabs, and others wanted **[[Concept Notes/Self-Determination\|self-determination]]** — the right to govern themselves — and chafed under Ottoman or Austro-Hungarian rule. Princip's assassination was an act of Serbian nationalist resistance; the Austrian ultimatum was an attempt to crush it. Nationalism turned a great-power rivalry into a moral crusade.

---

## From Triple Alliance to Central Powers

When war began, the alliance map shifted. **Italy** remained neutral in 1914 (arguing its obligation was defensive, not offensive) and switched to the Allied side in 1915. The **Ottoman Empire** and **Bulgaria** joined Germany and Austria-Hungary, forming the **Central Powers**. Meanwhile the Triple Entente expanded into the **Allies**, eventually including Italy, Japan, the United States, China, and over 20 nations total.

---

## Consequences of the Great War

The war's results were transformative and mostly catastrophic:

- Four empires collapsed: **Russian, Austro-Hungarian, German, and Ottoman**
- Maps of Europe and the Middle East were redrawn; new nations created from former Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian territory
- Germany lost all overseas colonies; former Ottoman provinces (Iraq, Palestine, Syria, Lebanon) came under British or French mandate
- New technologies — machine guns, poison gas, tanks, aircraft — made WWI the deadliest war in history to that point
- Economic disruption across Europe; massive shift of power toward the **United States**
- Rise of **communism** (Russia 1917) and eventually **fascism** (reaction to instability)
- Harsh peace terms at Versailles — Germany forced to accept sole war guilt and pay massive reparations — planted the seeds of WWII

> [!warning]
> A common misconception: WWI was *not* caused solely or even primarily by German aggression. Austria-Hungary started the actual shooting war with Serbia. Germany's blank-check support escalated it. The alliance system generalized it. AMSCO specifically notes Germany was blamed unfairly for the full war at Versailles — the AP may ask about the *consequences* of that unfair assignment.
