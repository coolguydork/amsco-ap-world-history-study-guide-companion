## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Questions 1–3 refer to the image of New Zealand Rifle Brigade soldiers (Indian troops fighting for Britain) during WWI. (AMSCO p. 466)*

**1. Which of the following causes of World War I BEST explains the image of colonial soldiers fighting for Britain?**

A. Militarism
B. Alliances
C. Imperialism
D. Nationalism

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Colonial soldiers serving in WWI reflect **imperialism** — Britain had colonized India, Australia, New Zealand, and other territories, giving it a vast pool of non-European soldiers to recruit. The image shows that Britain's global empire gave it a military manpower advantage over Germany. Militarism (A) refers to the arms race, not colonial recruitment. Alliances (B) explains why nations joined but not why they had colonial troops. Nationalism (D) explains motivations of some colonial soldiers but not the structural reason they were available to Britain.

**2. As a result of colonization, these soldiers fought for which group?**

A. The Allies
B. The Triple Alliance
C. The Black Hand
D. The neutral states

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Britain was part of the Allied Powers (the expanded Triple Entente). Colonial subjects — whether willing or compelled — served under the Allied banner. The Triple Alliance (B) was Germany + Austria-Hungary + Italy, none of whom controlled India or New Zealand. The Black Hand (C) was a Serbian nationalist group, not a military alliance.

**3. The photo reflects that one advantage Britain and France had over Germany in WWI was that they:**

A. Had more colonial subjects to recruit as soldiers
B. Had a stronger sense of nationalism
C. Were more unified by shared religious beliefs
D. Were more willing to make alliances

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Britain and France both had massive colonial empires in Africa, Asia, and the Pacific, giving them millions of potential recruits beyond their home populations. Germany lost its colonies to the Allies at Versailles partly because it had fewer and smaller colonies, which had provided fewer advantages during the war. Nationalism (B) was stronger in Germany in some respects; religion (C) is irrelevant; both sides made alliances (D).

## Short-Answer Questions

### SAQ 1 — German Military Culture (Isabel Hull, *Absolute Destruction*, 2005)

*The passage describes German military culture as involving risk-taking, conviction that annihilation was war's sole goal, offensive tactics, disregard of logistics, and acceptance of self-destruction.*

**(A) Identify ONE way in which imperialism influenced social structures after 1900.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Imperialism created new social hierarchies that assigned racial inferiority to colonized peoples. European powers justified colonial rule through Social Darwinist ideologies, establishing legal, economic, and social systems that privileged European settlers over indigenous populations — a structure that persisted in former colonies long after decolonization. Examples: apartheid in South Africa, racial classification systems in French Indochina.

**(B) Explain ONE way that the German state influenced German culture in the period after 1900.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The German state cultivated a militarist culture through compulsory military service, public celebrations of military victories, and state investment in arms production. This shaped civilian culture to view the military as the highest expression of national identity and war as a legitimate, even desirable tool of statecraft — the "It'll be over by Christmas" mentality that Hull's passage describes as "romantic ruthlessness." Prussia's military traditions, absorbed into the unified German state after 1871, reinforced this orientation.

**(C) Explain ONE historical situation after 1900 in which the German state reflected the characteristics listed by Hull.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Germany's execution of the Schlieffen Plan in August 1914 reflects Hull's "minutely technical planning" and "disregard of logistics." The plan called for a rapid offensive through neutral Belgium to knock out France in six weeks before turning east to fight Russia — a high-risk, all-or-nothing gambit that prioritized tactical brilliance over diplomatic consequences. When Belgian resistance and French counter-attacks delayed the advance, the plan's inflexibility (no contingency for partial success) contributed to the Western Front stalemate that Germany had intended to avoid.

### SAQ 2 — Causes of WWI (No stimulus)

**(A) Identify ONE way that territorial or regional conflicts caused World War I.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Austria-Hungary's dispute with Serbia over control of Bosnia-Herzegovina created the immediate context for WWI. Serbian nationalists sought to unite Slavic peoples of the Balkans under Serbian rule, directly threatening Austro-Hungarian territorial integrity. When the Black Hand assassinated Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo (Bosnian capital), Austria-Hungary used this act of Serbian nationalism as justification for an ultimatum that began the war.

**(B) Explain ONE way that imperial competition for resources caused World War I.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Imperial competition between France and Germany hardened into a deep mutual antagonism that made alliance formation against Germany automatic for France. Germany's 1870–71 defeat of France and annexation of the mineral-rich Alsace-Lorraine region created lasting French resentment. When colonial competition in Africa (Moroccan Crises of 1905 and 1911) further pitted France and Germany against each other, France readily allied with Russia and Britain to counter German expansion — producing the Triple Entente that became the Allied Powers.

**(C) Explain ONE way that alliances after 1900 escalated global conflict.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The Triple Entente's commitment to mutual defense meant that when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia in July 1914, Russia felt obligated to mobilize in Serbia's defense as a fellow Slavic Orthodox nation. Germany then declared war on Russia to defend Austria-Hungary, triggering France's obligation to Russia, and Germany's invasion of Belgium triggered Britain's entry. What began as a two-power dispute between Austria-Hungary and Serbia escalated to a six-power conflict within two weeks — entirely through alliance cascade.

## Rapid Review

**MAIN Causes of WWI:**
- **M**ilitarism — arms race, glorification of military, mass-produced weapons
- **A**lliances — Triple Alliance vs. Triple Entente; automatic war cascade
- **I**mperialism — colonial rivalry hardened hostilities between European powers
- **N**ationalism — self-determination movements destabilized multinational empires; Serbian nationalism the immediate trigger

**Immediate cause:** Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand by Gavrilo Princip (June 28, 1914)

**Alliance sides:**
- **Allied Powers:** France, Britain, Russia (Triple Entente) + Italy (1915), US (1917), Japan, others
- **Central Powers:** Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria

**Key consequences:** Collapse of 4 empires; redraw of Middle East and European borders; rise of communism; harsh Versailles terms → seeds of WWII
