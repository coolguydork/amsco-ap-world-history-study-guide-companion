# Key Terms & Concepts

Topic 7.2 — Causes of World War I | Printed pp. 461–468

---

**Great War**
Common name for World War I (1914–1918), reflecting its unprecedented scale — the first conflict to involve nations from across the globe and kill tens of millions of soldiers and civilians. The war fundamentally weakened European powers, redrew borders, and set the conditions for WWII's even more destructive successor. On the AP, "Great War" and "World War I" are interchangeable; know both and recognize them in documents.

**Gavrilo Princip**
Serbian nationalist who assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his wife Sophie in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914, triggering the chain of alliance obligations that ignited World War I. A member of the [[Concept Notes/Black Hand|Black Hand]], he represents how extreme nationalism and political assassination could destabilize the entire European alliance system. The AP frequently uses his assassination as an SAQ or DBQ document prompt.

**Archduke Franz Ferdinand**
Heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne whose assassination by Gavrilo Princip in Sarajevo (June 28, 1914) served as the **immediate trigger** of World War I. His death prompted Austria-Hungary to issue an ultimatum to Serbia, activating the alliance systems that drew the major powers into war within weeks. Distinguish him from the long-term MAIN causes — he is the spark, not the underlying conditions.

**militarism**
Policy of building a strong military and glorifying its use; one of the four MAIN long-term causes of WWI. Germany and Britain in particular engaged in a naval arms race before 1914. Mass production from the Industrial Revolution made military buildup faster and cheaper than ever. A militaristic culture led European publics to expect war to be quick and glorious — a fatal miscalculation.

**secret alliances**
The network of binding military agreements among European powers formed before 1914, often negotiated in secret, committing nations to defend allies automatically if attacked. The system transformed a two-power dispute (Austria-Hungary vs. Serbia) into a continental and then world war within weeks. The AP asks students to explain how alliances caused WWI — emphasize the cascade effect and loss of diplomatic flexibility.

**Triple Alliance**
Pre-WWI defensive alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy (formed 1882); the nucleus of what became the Central Powers. When war broke out, Italy remained neutral then switched to the Allies in 1915, and the Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria joined to fill its place. The Triple Alliance contrasted with the Triple Entente — together the two blocs divided Europe into armed camps.

**Triple Entente**
Military alliance formed by 1907 among France, Russia, and Britain, in response to the Triple Alliance. France joined for revenge after the Franco-Prussian War; Russia joined as a fellow Slavic protector of Serbia; Britain joined to counter German naval buildup. Became the core of the Allied Powers in WWI. The Entente's formation meant Europe was split into two armed, mutually suspicious blocs by 1907.

**Allies**
The coalition of nations that fought against the Central Powers in World War I, expanding from the original Triple Entente (France, Russia, Britain) to include Italy, Japan, the United States, and over 20 others. Their combined colonial and industrial resources ultimately overwhelmed the Central Powers. By war's end, 28 "Allied and Associated Powers" had signed the armistice.

**Central Powers**
WWI coalition of Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, and Bulgaria. Named because these nations lay at the geographic center of Europe. Formed from the former Triple Alliance after Italy defected. Defeated by the Allies in November 1918. Key AP connection: the harsh terms imposed on the Central Powers — especially Germany — at the Paris Peace Conference directly caused WWII instability.

**Black Hand**
Serbian nationalist secret society that sought to unite all South Slavs by ending Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman control in the Balkans. Organized and facilitated the 1914 assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand. From Serbia's perspective a patriotic resistance group; from Austria-Hungary's perspective a terrorist organization. Illustrates how extreme nationalism could trigger interstate war.

**self-determination**
The principle that peoples sharing an ethnicity, language, and culture have the right to form their own independent state. A key driver of nationalism that destabilized multinational empires before 1914 and shaped Wilson's 14 Points after the war. Both Serbian and Arab nationalists fought for the Allies partly because they hoped victory would bring self-determination for their peoples. The Paris Peace Conference applied it selectively — mostly to European peoples, not colonial ones.

> [!exam-tip]
> MAIN acronym for the AP: **M**ilitarism, **A**lliances, **I**mperialism, **N**ationalism. These are the four long-term causes of WWI. Practice writing a sentence for each that explains the causal mechanism — not just what it is, but *how* it caused the war.

> [!tip]
> Triple Alliance vs. Triple Entente — a common mix-up. **Triple Alliance = Germany + Austria-Hungary + Italy** (eventually Central Powers). **Triple Entente = France + Russia + Britain** (Allies). Mnemonic: the "Entente" *fought* (the Allies were the "good guys" in Western memory).
