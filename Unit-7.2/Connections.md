# Connections

Topic 7.2 — Causes of World War I | Printed pp. 461–468

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-7.1/Shifting Power Overview\|Shifting Power Overview]]**
The power shifts among industrializing European states — Germany's rapid rise, Russia's instability, Britain's anxiety about its naval supremacy — created the competitive environment in which alliances and arms races developed. Topic 7.1 establishes why European powers were anxious and rivalrous; Topic 7.2 shows how that anxiety produced a specific war. The alliance system was a direct product of the shifting-power dynamics 7.1 describes.

**[[Unit-6.2/State Expansion Overview\|State Expansion Overview]]**
New imperialism — the European scramble for African and Asian colonies from the 1880s onward — is one of the four MAIN long-term causes of WWI. Imperial competition intensified rivalries between France and Germany (Alsace-Lorraine, Moroccan Crises) and between Germany and Britain (naval buildup, colonial competition). 6.2 is the foundation for understanding why "I" in MAIN matters.

## Leads To

**Conducting World War I** [[Unit-7.3/Conducting World War I Overview|Conducting World War I Overview]]
Topic 7.2 establishes *why* WWI started; Topic 7.3 covers *how* it was fought — trench warfare, new technologies (poison gas, tanks, aircraft), total war mobilization, and the global dimensions of the conflict. The structural causes from 7.2 explain the war's scale: because all major European powers were committed by alliances, the fighting was industrial, continental, and prolonged.

## Compare / Contrast

**Causes of World War II** [[Unit-7.6/Causes of World War II Overview|Causes of World War II Overview]]
The AP frequently asks students to compare WWI and WWII causes. Key parallels: both involved alliance systems, nationalism, and great-power rivalry. Key differences: WWI's causes were more diffuse and structural (MAIN); WWII's causes are more directly traceable to specific policy failures (appeasement, Hitler's expansionism) and the *consequences* of WWI (Versailles resentment, [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]]). The through-line — Versailles → instability → WWII — is a critical AP causation argument.

## Thematic Connections Across Units

**[[Concept Notes/Nationalism\|Nationalism]] as a cause of war** — The same nationalism that drove Serbian irredentism in 1914 also drove anti-colonial movements globally after WWI. When WWI weakened European empires and Wilson proclaimed [[Concept Notes/Self-Determination\|self-determination]], colonized peoples demanded it too. Connection to Topic 7.4 (anti-colonial nationalism) and Units 8–9 (decolonization).

**Alliance systems and collective security** — The WWI alliance system's failure to prevent war led directly to Woodrow Wilson's proposal for a **League of Nations** — an attempt to replace balance-of-power alliances with collective security. The League's weaknesses then contributed to WWII [[Unit-7.6/Causes of World War II Overview|Causes of World War II Overview]]. This cycle (alliance failure → collective security attempt → failure → next war) is a recurring AP theme.

**Imperial competition as a cause of conflict** — The connection between imperialism and war recurs across AP World History. European imperial rivalry contributed to both WWI (competition for colonies) and eventually to decolonization (colonial subjects demanding the self-determination WWI had promised). Link: [[Unit-6.2/State Expansion Overview\|State Expansion Overview]] → 7.2 → Topic 7.4.

> [!exam-tip]
> A classic AP LEQ question type: "Evaluate the extent to which [nationalism / imperialism / alliances] caused World War I." Practice writing a thesis that argues *relative significance* — e.g., "While all four MAIN factors contributed to WWI, the alliance system was most directly responsible because it transformed a regional dispute into a world war within five weeks."
