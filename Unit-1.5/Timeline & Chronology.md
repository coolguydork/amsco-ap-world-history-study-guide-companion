# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 1.5

*AMSCO pp. 43–52 | Period 1: c. 1200–c. 1450*

## Chronological Overview

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| c. 4th c. BCE – 10th c. CE | **Bantu migration** expands across sub-Saharan Africa | Spreads agriculture, Bantu languages, and kin-based governance across most of the continent |
| c. 5th c. CE | **Kingdom of Ghana** founded | First great West African trading kingdom; controls gold-salt exchange |
| c. 330 CE | **Ezana of Axum** converts to Christianity | Axum becomes one of world's first Christian kingdoms; foundational for Ethiopia's unique religious identity |
| c. 700s CE | Bantu-Arabic contact produces **Swahili language** | Cultural synthesis on the East African coast; a linguistic product of Indian Ocean trade |
| c. 750–1076 | **Ghana at peak power** | Controls trans-Saharan gold-salt trade; centralized monarchy; iron-equipped army |
| c. 800s CE | **Kin-based networks** dominate sub-Saharan interior | Hausa Kingdoms form in Nigeria; stateless governance widespread |
| c. 869–883 CE | **Zanj Rebellion** | Enslaved East Africans hold Basra for a decade — one of history's largest slave revolts |
| c. 1000 CE | Most of sub-Saharan Africa adopts **agriculture** | Triggers need for more complex governance; population growth enables larger political units |
| c. 11th–12th c. | **Ghana permanently weakened** by warfare | Mali rises in Ghana's place |
| c. 12th c. | **Great Zimbabwe** kingdom rises | East African power based on gold and Indian Ocean trade; begins major stone construction |
| c. 12th c. | **Ethiopian rock-hewn churches** built | 11 monumental churches cut from rock; symbol of Ethiopian Christian monarchy's power |
| c. **1235** | **Sundiata Keita** founds Mali Empire | Gold-salt trade continues under new leadership; griots preserve Sundiata's story |
| c. 14th c. | **Islam** reaches Hausa Kingdoms via missionaries | Trans-Saharan trade routes carry Islam as well as goods into the interior |
| **c. 1324** | **Mansa Musa's hajj** to Mecca | Most dramatic display of West African wealth in the medieval period; reportedly causes gold inflation in Cairo and the Middle East |
| **c. 1352** | **Ibn Battuta** visits Mali | Detailed account of Malian society, Islamic practice, and persisting pre-Islamic customs; key primary source |
| c. **1390** | **Kingdom of Kongo** founded | Central African kingdom; will become important in Topic 6.6 (Atlantic slave trade) |
| c. late **1400s** | **Great Zimbabwe** abandoned | Overgrazing destroys the surrounding environment; population moves elsewhere |
| **1464–1492** | **Sunni Ali** rules Songhai | Conquers Timbuktu (1468); Songhai displaces Mali as West Africa's dominant power (Topic 2.4) |

## Key AP Period 1 Focus (c. 1200–c. 1450)

During the AP exam's Period 1 window, the key African developments are:
- **Mali at its peak** (founded 1235; Mansa Musa's hajj 1324; Ibn Battuta's visit 1352)
- **Great Zimbabwe at its height and decline** (12th–late 15th c.)
- **Ethiopian Christianity** as a stable, isolated Christian monarchy
- **Stateless kin-based societies** continuing throughout sub-Saharan Africa

> [!exam-tip]
> **Mansa Musa's hajj (1324)** is the single most important date in Topic 1.5. It places Mali within Period 1, demonstrates the wealth from the gold-salt trade, shows the connection between Islam and West African kingship, and provides excellent SAQ/DBQ evidence. Memorize this date.

> [!tip]
> **Memory anchor — West Africa:** "Ghana → Mali → Songhai" — the three great trans-Saharan trading empires in sequence. Ghana peaks 750–1076; Mali rises c. 1235; Songhai takes over after 1468 (Topic 2.4). They occupy the same geographic zone in succession, each built on the same gold-salt trade.
