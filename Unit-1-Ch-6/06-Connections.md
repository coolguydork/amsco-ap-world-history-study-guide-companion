# Cross-Chapter Connections — Chapter 6: Developments in Europe (c. 1200–1450)

## 🔙 What This Chapter Builds On (Earlier Concepts)

- **[[Unit-1-Ch-1]] — East Asia:** Contrast European political fragmentation with Song China's centralized bureaucracy and civil service exam. Both show *different responses* to the challenge of governance. Also: Chinese agricultural surplus → population growth → vulnerability parallels Europe pre-plague.

- **[[Unit-1-Ch-2]] — Dar al-Islam:** The Islamic world's preservation and expansion of Greek learning directly fed European [[Scholasticism]]. The [[Crusades]] were Europe's military response to Islamic expansion — understand Islamic strength to understand why the Crusades were ultimately unsuccessful.

- **[[Unit-1-Ch-3]] — South and Southeast Asia:** Indian Ocean trade networks connected to Mediterranean trade that enriched Italian city-states. The interconnectedness of Eurasian trade is a recurring theme — the same networks that brought silk and spices brought the [[Black Death]].

- **[[Unit-1-Ch-5]] — Africa:** Mali and Great Zimbabwe's trade wealth contrast with Europe's more subsistence-based economy. Africa's gold fueled Mediterranean commerce that enriched European merchants.

## 🔜 What This Chapter Sets Up (Later Concepts)

- **[[Unit-2-Ch-7]] — Silk Roads:** The [[Black Death]]'s travel along Eurasian trade routes illustrates the danger of connectivity — a theme revisited in the Age of Exploration when European diseases devastate the Americas.

- **[[Unit-2-Ch-8]] — Mongol Empire:** The Mongols enabled the Pax Mongolica trade network that inadvertently spread plague to Europe. The fall of [[Byzantine Empire]] under Ottoman pressure (itself connected to Mongol disruption of the Islamic world) sent scholars to Italy.

- **[[Unit-3]] — Land-Based Empires (c. 1450–1750):** The fall of Constantinople (1453) opened space for Ottoman expansion and pushed Europeans to seek alternative trade routes — directly motivating the Age of Exploration. The Portuguese and Spanish crowns that funded Columbus and da Gama emerged from the [[Reconquista]].

- **[[Unit-4]] — Transoceanic Interconnections:** Everything in Ch. 6 points toward 1492. Weakened feudalism → mobile labor and capital → merchant investment in exploration. The [[Reconquista]] completed in 1492 freed Iberian monarchies to project power overseas. Universities trained navigators and cartographers.

- **[[Unit-5]] — Revolutions:** The erosion of Church authority (Western Schism, Black Death response failures) created the conditions for the Protestant Reformation (1517). The rise of vernacular literature and national identity prefigures Enlightenment nationalism.

## 🌍 Comparative Civilizations and Contrasts

| European Development | Comparable Elsewhere | Key Contrast |
|---------------------|---------------------|--------------|
| [[Feudalism]] — fragmented, land-based political order | Japan's feudalism (daimyo/samurai system) | **Similarity:** both decentralized, warrior-noble class; **Difference:** Japan's system more stable, lasted until 1868 |
| [[Catholic Church]] as dominant institution | Islamic Caliphates / ulama scholars | Both religion + politics intertwined; Islam had no single pope-equivalent — more decentralized religious authority |
| [[Black Death]] in Europe | Plague's impact along [[Silk Roads]] globally | China's Yuan Dynasty also devastated; population losses comparable, but China's recovery led to Ming nativism, while Europe's sparked capitalism |
| [[Universities]] and scholasticism | Islamic madrasas (House of Wisdom, Baghdad) | Islamic scholarship preceded European; Crusades transferred this knowledge westward |
| [[Magna Carta]] / Parliament | Aztec *tlatoani* councils, Mongol kurultai | All show forms of elite consultation limiting one-man rule — but very different in scope and legality |
| [[Reconquista]] | Crusades in the Middle East | Both show religion-motivated military expansion; Reconquista succeeded, Crusades ultimately failed |
