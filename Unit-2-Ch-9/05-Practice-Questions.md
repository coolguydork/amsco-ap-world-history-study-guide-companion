# Chapter 9: Practice Questions — Indian Ocean Exchange

> [!exam-tip]
> Indian Ocean questions frequently use artifacts (Chinese porcelain found in African sites, mosque architecture, Arabic words in Swahili) as stimulus material. These physical/archaeological sources are evidence of cultural exchange — practice reading them as historians do.

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Question 1**

*"The inhabitants of Kilwa are for the most part Muslims. Their sultan at the time of my arrival was Abu'l-Muzaffar Hasan, who was noted for his gifts and his generous qualities. He often made raids on the infidels of the mainland and attacked them, and for this reason he was given the title of Warrior of the Faith."*
— Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*, c. 1331

Which of the following conclusions about East African society is best supported by the passage above?

A) The Swahili city-states were politically dominated by Arab merchants from the Arabian Peninsula
B) Islam had spread to East Africa and was practiced by the ruling class of Swahili city-states
C) East African city-states used warfare against their neighbors primarily to acquire slaves for the Indian Ocean trade
D) The Swahili city-states had converted to Islam only recently under pressure from Mongol expansion

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The passage shows a Muslim sultan at the head of Kilwa's government, using Islamic legitimizing language ("Warrior of the Faith"). This is evidence that Islam had spread to East African ruling elites via Indian Ocean trade networks. Answer A is incorrect — Swahili rulers were of African origin, not Arab immigrants.

**Question 2**

Chinese porcelain found in the archaeological remains of Swahili city-states like Kilwa and Mombasa is best understood as evidence of:

A) Chinese political dominance over East African trade through tributary relationships
B) The integration of East African city-states into the Indian Ocean commercial network
C) The cultural influence of Confucian civilization on East African governance
D) A direct trade relationship between China and East Africa that bypassed Arab intermediaries

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The porcelain didn't require direct Chinese-African contact — it traveled through layers of intermediaries (Chinese → Southeast Asian or Indian → Arab → Swahili). Its presence is evidence of East Africa's participation in a wider Indian Ocean commercial network, not necessarily direct Chinese-African contact.

**Question 3**

The Indian Ocean trade network differed from the overland Silk Roads primarily in that:

A) Indian Ocean trade was controlled by a single imperial power throughout the period c. 1200–1450
B) Indian Ocean trade was driven by natural wind patterns rather than political stability, making it more resilient to political disruption
C) Indian Ocean trade carried primarily agricultural goods rather than luxury items
D) Indian Ocean trade was restricted to Muslim merchants who controlled all ports in the network

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This is a high-frequency comparison question. The key distinction: Silk Roads needed political stability (the Pax Mongolica); Indian Ocean trade ran on monsoon winds that didn't care about empires. This made Indian Ocean trade more resilient — it continued even as the Abbasid Caliphate collapsed and the Mongols invaded.

**Question 4**

The conversion of the Malaccan ruler to Islam around 1400 is best explained by which of the following?

A) Military pressure from the expanding Ottoman Empire forced Southeast Asian states to convert
B) Malaccan rulers converted to gain commercial advantages from Muslim merchant networks and political legitimacy within the Islamic world
C) Zheng He's voyages introduced Islam to Malacca as part of China's tributary system
D) Malaccan merchants adopted Islam after intermarrying with the Arab communities that controlled the spice trade

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Malaccan conversion is a model example of voluntary conversion for commercial and political reasons — Muslim merchants preferred to do business with Muslim rulers; conversion also gave Malaccan rulers access to the political prestige and diplomatic network of the Islamic world.

**Question 5**

Zheng He's voyages (1405–1433) and their subsequent discontinuation by the Ming Dynasty most directly illustrate which of the following historical patterns?

A) That maritime technology in the early 15th century was insufficient for sustained long-distance ocean travel
B) That state policy can dramatically shape a society's participation in global trade networks
C) That Chinese merchants lacked the commercial motivation to engage in Indian Ocean trade
D) That the Indian Ocean network was dominated by Islamic powers hostile to Chinese participation

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Zheng He's voyages demonstrate Chinese capability for massive maritime power; their discontinuation demonstrates that political decisions (the Confucian court's opposition to "foreign entanglements" and maritime trade) can override commercial and technological factors. State policy, not capability, determined China's withdrawal.

**Question 6**

Which of the following best explains why Islam spread more effectively through Indian Ocean trade routes than through overland Silk Road routes in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Muslim merchants were more numerous in the Indian Ocean region than in Central Asia
B) The Indian Ocean network was more decentralized, allowing Muslim merchants to establish permanent commercial diasporas in port cities without state interference
C) The Buddhist and Christian populations along the Silk Roads were more resistant to Islamic conversion than the animist and Hindu populations of the Indian Ocean rim
D) The Mongol Empire's sponsorship of Buddhism along the Silk Roads actively suppressed Islamic missionary activity in Central Asia

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The decentralized nature of Indian Ocean trade meant Muslim merchant communities could establish themselves in virtually every port city; their commercial role gave them social influence; their willingness to perform Islamic rituals publicly (building mosques, praying) made Islam visible and available to convert to.

**Question 7**

The prosperity of the Swahili city-states depended most directly on which of the following?

A) Agricultural surpluses from East African interior regions that provided food for export
B) Their position as intermediaries connecting East African gold and ivory to Indian Ocean maritime trade networks
C) Military conquest of interior African kingdoms that provided enslaved labor for coastal plantations
D) Chinese diplomatic recognition granted by Zheng He's expeditions that legitimized their rulers

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Swahili city-states were commercial entrepôts — they didn't produce what they traded, they facilitated its movement. Gold from Great Zimbabwe, ivory from elephant-rich interior Africa, and enslaved people flowed to the coast; Indian textiles, Chinese porcelain, and Islamic luxury goods flowed inward.

**Question 8**

*"[The treasure ships] were 444 feet long and 180 feet wide. Each ship could contain several thousand barrels of goods. They sailed to the countries of the Western Ocean."*
— Chinese historical account of Zheng He's treasure ships, 15th century

The passage is best used as evidence for which of the following?

A) China's intention to establish permanent colonies in the Indian Ocean region
B) The technological and organizational capabilities of the Ming Dynasty in the early 15th century
C) The commercial dominance of Chinese merchants throughout the Indian Ocean network
D) The superiority of Chinese naval technology over Portuguese ships that arrived later in the century

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The passage is evidence of Ming China's extraordinary maritime capability — the scale of Zheng He's fleet demonstrates sophisticated shipbuilding and organizational capacity. It does NOT prove Chinese commercial dominance (the voyages were diplomatic, not commercial) or colonial intent.

**Question 9**

Which of the following developments most directly contributed to the transformation of Southeast Asia's religious landscape between c. 1200 and 1500?

A) The Mongol Yuan Dynasty's promotion of Buddhism as the official religion of its tributary states
B) Christian missionaries traveling Indian Ocean routes from the Byzantine Empire
C) The conversion of the Malaccan ruler to Islam and the subsequent spread of Islam through Muslim merchant networks
D) The conquest of Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms by armies from the Abbasid Caliphate

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation:* Islam's spread into Southeast Asia was commercial and voluntary, not military. The Malaccan model — royal conversion for commercial reasons, followed by spread through merchant networks — was replicated across the Malay Archipelago.

**Question 10**

Which of the following pairs best represents the *continuity* and *change* in Indian Ocean trade during the transition from Period 1 (c. 1200–1450) to Period 2 (c. 1450–1750)?

A) Continuity: dominance of Muslim merchants / Change: the Mongol conquest of Indian Ocean port cities
B) Continuity: the use of monsoon winds for navigation / Change: the entry of European merchants who disrupted Muslim commercial dominance
C) Continuity: Chinese state sponsorship of maritime trade / Change: the end of Zheng He's voyages
D) Continuity: the role of the Swahili city-states / Change: the destruction of Malacca by the Ming Dynasty

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The monsoon winds remained constant (natural infrastructure doesn't change); the entry of Portuguese merchants after 1498 disrupted the Muslim-dominated trade system — this is the key change marking the transition from Period 1 to Period 2 Indian Ocean history.

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"The Indian Ocean trade network was the most effective mechanism for the spread of Islam in the period c. 1200–1450."**

**Part A:** Briefly explain ONE piece of evidence that supports this claim.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE piece of evidence that challenges or complicates this claim.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which the process by which Islam spread via Indian Ocean trade differed from the process by which it spread via Silk Road overland trade.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Swahili city-states' Muslim rulers; Malaccan conversion and subsequent spread into SE Asia; Ibn Battuta's accounts of Muslim communities throughout Indian Ocean ports
> - Part B: Islam also spread via the overland Silk Roads (Ch7); Silk Road trade spread Islam into Central Asia and eventually into China; consider also military conquest (Delhi Sultanate)
> - Part C: Indian Ocean spread was primarily through merchant diasporas establishing communities in port cities → commercial incentives for local rulers to convert; Silk Road spread was more through Sufi missionaries and traders in interior cities — both voluntary, but different social mechanisms

---

### SAQ 2

**"The Ming Dynasty's decision to end Zheng He's voyages represents one of the most consequential policy reversals in world history."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe the purpose and scale of Zheng He's voyages.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE reason why the Ming court decided to end the voyages.

**Part C:** Explain ONE long-term consequence of this policy reversal for the history of the Indian Ocean and global trade.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Seven voyages (1405–1433), hundreds of ships, diplomatic/tribute purpose, reached East Africa; the scale exceeded anything Europeans would launch for decades
> - Part B: Confucian officials opposed maritime trade as "un-Confucian" (merchants low status); cost of voyages; death of the Yongle Emperor removed patronage; internal threats more pressing than external prestige
> - Part C: Withdrawal created a power vacuum in the Indian Ocean → Muslim merchants retained dominance → Portuguese entered uncontested → Age of Exploration and European commercial dominance of Indian Ocean in Unit 3

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline

### DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which Indian Ocean trade transformed the societies of East Africa and Southeast Asia in the period c. 1000–1450."**

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**

**Thesis:** "Indian Ocean trade fundamentally transformed East African and Southeast Asian societies by creating new political hierarchies based on commercial wealth, spreading Islam as the common religious-legal framework of merchant communities, and producing urban port cultures that blended indigenous, Islamic, and broader Asian influences — while leaving significant interior regions largely untouched by these transformations."

**Possible Groupings:**
- Group 1: Political transformation (documents showing rulers gaining power through taxing trade, converting to Islam for commercial advantage)
- Group 2: Religious/cultural transformation (documents showing mosque construction, Arabic loanwords in Swahili, Islamic art in SE Asia)
- Group 3: Limits of transformation (documents showing continuity of interior regions, non-commercial populations, pre-Islamic cultural traditions)

**Outside Evidence:**
- Kilwa as example of East African transformation (stone architecture, gold trade, Muslim rulers)
- Malacca as example of Southeast Asian transformation (voluntary conversion, spread of Islam through islands)
- Chinese porcelain in African archaeological record as evidence of network reach
- Zheng He's voyages as Chinese state engagement with Indian Ocean network

**Complexity Point Options:**
- Argue transformation was **geographically uneven**: coastal city-states transformed dramatically; interior regions much less so
- Argue transformation was **selective**: rulers adopted Islam for political reasons while populations retained local practices → syncretism rather than replacement
- **Compare East Africa vs. Southeast Asia**: East African transformation was more about commercial wealth and political identity; Southeast Asian transformation included broader Islamization of populations over time
