# Chapter 9: Timeline — Indian Ocean Exchange

> [!tip]
> **Big Picture:** Indian Ocean trade is one of the most enduring networks in human history — it predates and postdates the AP World History course period. In the context of Period 1 (c. 1200–1450), the key developments are the **intensification** of Muslim merchant dominance, the rise of the **Swahili city-states** and **Malacca**, and the dramatic **Ming maritime expeditions** followed by their equally dramatic withdrawal. The Portuguese arrival (1498) falls in Period 2 and is the hinge point between these two eras.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| c. 800–900 CE | Swahili city-states emerge on the East African coast | East Africa | Evidence of early integration of East Africa into Indian Ocean network; Muslim Arab merchants establish communities |
| c. 1000–1200 | Great Zimbabwe emerges as gold-producing state in interior Africa | Southern Africa | Source of gold that flows to Swahili coast and into Indian Ocean trade; **CONTINUITY**: gold trade sustained for centuries |
| c. 1100–1300 | Malacca Strait increasingly dominant as SE Asian trade chokepoint | Southeast Asia | Whoever controls the Strait controls access between Indian Ocean and South China Sea |
| c. 1200 | Swahili city-states reach peak prosperity (Kilwa dominant) | East Africa | Stone mosque construction begins; Chinese porcelain found in archaeological record; Islamic cultural integration deepens |
| c. 1200–1300 | Song Dynasty China as major exporter of silk and porcelain via maritime routes | East Asia | Chinese goods reach East Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia via Indian Ocean trade; Chinese demand for spices creates commercial incentive |
| 1258 | Mongol sack of Baghdad | Middle East | Abbasid Caliphate destroyed; but Muslim merchants continue to dominate Indian Ocean trade — demonstrates network's resilience independent of political centers |
| 1290s–1340s | Ibn Battuta's Indian Ocean travels (East Africa, South Asia, Southeast Asia, China) | East Africa, Asia | Primary source for Indian Ocean trade in this period; describes Kilwa as one of the world's finest cities; documents the spread of Islamic civilization via maritime routes |
| c. 1350–1400 | Black Death reaches and affects Indian Ocean port cities | Middle East, South Asia | Disrupts trade temporarily; demographic impact less severe than in Europe but still significant |
| c. 1400 | Founding of the Malacca Sultanate | Southeast Asia | **CHANGE**: Malacca rapidly becomes dominant Southeast Asian commercial hub; ruler converts to Islam, connecting city to Muslim merchant network |
| 1405–1407 | Zheng He's First Voyage | Southeast Asia, India | **CHANGE**: Ming China directly enters Indian Ocean commercial space with massive naval fleet; asserts tributary relationships |
| 1407–1433 | Zheng He's voyages 2–7 | Indian Ocean, East Africa | Fleet reaches East Africa, Arabia, Persian Gulf; brings back giraffes and other exotica to China; largest maritime expeditions in world history at the time |
| c. 1430s | Ming court reverses maritime policy; Zheng He voyages discontinued | East Asia | **CHANGE**: China withdraws from active Indian Ocean participation; Confucian officials prevail in internal debate; fleet records reportedly burned |
| c. 1440–1450 | Malacca at peak power; Islam spreading into SE Asian islands | Southeast Asia | Islam becomes dominant in Malay Peninsula and begins spreading into Java and Sumatra — **CHANGE**: religious landscape of SE Asia transforming |
| 1488 | Bartolomeu Dias rounds Cape of Good Hope (southern Africa) | Africa | Portuguese probe the path that will connect Atlantic to Indian Ocean — **CHANGE**: European maritime power approaching Indian Ocean from outside |
| 1498 | Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut, India via Indian Ocean | India | Portuguese enter the Indian Ocean — beginning of European disruption of Muslim-dominated trade system; Unit 3 begins |
