# Unit 1: The Global Tapestry (c. 1200–c. 1450)

> [!summary] Unit at a Glance
> Unit 1 covers the period **c. 1200–c. 1450**, which AP World History calls **Period 1**. This is the "baseline" unit — it establishes the political, economic, cultural, and social structures that the rest of the course will build on, transform, or disrupt.

## The Big Picture

Between 1200 and 1450, several large empires and states emerged or consolidated across Eurasia, Africa, and the Americas. Three mega-trends shaped the period:

**1. The Revival and Rise of Powerful States.** The [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty and East Asia|Song Dynasty in China]] was the world's wealthiest and most technologically innovative state. The [[Unit-1.2/Dar al-Islam Overview|Islamic world]] stretched from Spain to Southeast Asia. New states emerged in [[Unit-1.5/Africa Overview|Africa]], [[Unit-1.3/South and Southeast Asia Overview|South and Southeast Asia]], and [[Unit-1.4/The Americas Overview|the Americas]]. [[Unit-1.6/Medieval Europe Overview|Europe]] was decentralized and feudal but beginning to change.

**2. The Mongol Moment.** The [[Concept Notes/Mongols|Mongols]] — nomadic pastoralists from Central Asia — built the largest land empire in history, connecting much of Eurasia under a single political umbrella. Conquest brought devastation, but also the **Pax Mongolica**: a period of relative peace and trade that accelerated the exchange of goods, ideas, and disease.

**3. Religion as the Glue of Civilization.** Across virtually every region, religion shaped governance, social structure, art, and intellectual life. [[Concept Notes/Islam|Islam]], [[Concept Notes/Buddhism|Buddhism]], [[Concept Notes/Confucianism|Confucianism]], [[Concept Notes/Hinduism|Hinduism]], and [[Concept Notes/Christianity|Christianity]] all spread, adapted, and competed during this period.

## AP Themes Present in Unit 1

- **Governance** — How do states legitimize and expand authority? (Song bureaucracy, Islamic caliphates, Aztec theocracy, European feudalism)
- **Economic Systems** — How does trade drive development? (Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, trans-Saharan trade)
- **Cultural Developments & Interactions** — How do religions and ideas spread and blend? (Sinification, Bhakti/Sufi parallels, al-Andalus)
- **Social Structures** — How is society organized by class, gender, and kinship? (Caste, foot binding, griots, serfs)
- **Technology & Environment** — What innovations enabled growth? (Champa rice, compass, printing, three-field system)
- **Human Migration** — How do nomadic and settled peoples interact? (Mongols, Bantu migrations, Turkic conquests)
