# Chapter 11: Connections

#apwh #unit-3 #connections

---

## Builds On (Earlier Chapters)

- [[Unit-1-Ch-1/00-Overview|Unit 1, Ch 1 — Islamic Caliphates]]: The Ottoman and Mughal empires inherited the tradition of Islamic governance; the Abbasid Caliphate (Unit 1) established the model of a universal Islamic empire that Suleiman and the Mughals consciously evoked
- [[Unit-1-Ch-2/00-Overview|Unit 1, Ch 2 — Mongol Empire]]: The Mongol legacy is direct — Babur descended from Timur and Genghis Khan; the Timurid cultural tradition heavily influenced Mughal court culture; post-Mongol power vacuums across Eurasia created the space gunpowder empires filled
- [[Unit-1-Ch-3/00-Overview|Unit 1, Ch 3 — Song/Yuan/Ming China]]: The Qing Dynasty built on Ming administrative structures (civil service examination system, Confucian bureaucracy); the Great Wall expanded under the Ming was a defensive response to the same steppe threats the Qing eventually absorbed
- [[Unit-2-Ch-7/00-Overview|Unit 2, Ch 7 — Silk Roads]]: Ottoman control of overland Eurasian trade routes was a direct cause of European maritime exploration (Unit 4) — the Silk Road connection links Chapters 7 and 11 directly
- [[Unit-2-Ch-8/00-Overview|Unit 2, Ch 8 — Indian Ocean Trade]]: The Mughal Empire's wealth derived partly from its position as a subcontinent connecting Indian Ocean trade; Persian Gulf trade under Safavid control

## Sets Up (Later Chapters)

- [[Unit-3-Ch-12/00-Overview|Unit 3, Ch 12 — Administering Empires]]: The empires introduced in Ch 11 are analyzed in depth in Ch 12 — their bureaucratic systems, architecture, and methods of cultural legitimation
- [[Unit-3-Ch-13/00-Overview|Unit 3, Ch 13 — Belief Systems]]: The Ottoman-Safavid Sunni-Shia divide, the Protestant Reformation (which happened while Suleiman besieged Vienna), and the Mughal religious accommodation policies all feed directly into Ch 13
- [[Unit-4/00-Overview|Unit 4 — Transoceanic Expansion]]: Ottoman control of eastern Mediterranean trade routes is frequently cited as a *cause* of Portuguese and Spanish exploration; the Qing later faced European contact pressure via the Canton System
- [[Unit-5/00-Overview|Unit 5 — Revolutions]]: The later decline of Mughal, Ottoman, and Qing empires sets up the context for colonialism and revolution in Unit 5–6

## Comparative Civilizations and Contrasts

| Compare | Contrast |
|---|---|
| Ottoman devshirme vs. Chinese examination system | Both created loyal officials; devshirme used enslaved converts, examination used educated elites |
| Mughal Akbar vs. Qing Kangxi | Both were "great" rulers who expanded territory and patronized culture; Akbar promoted religious pluralism, Kangxi promoted Confucian orthodoxy |
| Safavid Shah Abbas vs. Ottoman Suleiman | Both built brilliant capitals (Isfahan vs. Istanbul); both reformed their militaries; divided by Sunni/Shia |
| Russian expansion into Siberia vs. European colonial expansion (Unit 4) | Similar pattern of gunpowder-armed state subjugating indigenous peoples for resources; different geography (land vs. ocean) |
| Songhai Empire vs. later West African states (Unit 4–5) | Songhai's fall opened West Africa to increased trans-Saharan and eventually Atlantic contact |
