## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Question 1**

*Use the following passage to answer the question.*

> "The Sultan ordered that the great church of the Christians [Hagia Sophia] be opened and that the Islamic call to prayer be proclaimed there. And he himself prayed there."
> — Ottoman chronicle, c. 1453

The action described in the passage most directly reflects which of the following Ottoman strategies?

A) Using religious symbolism to legitimize imperial power and signal a break with Byzantine rule
B) Forcing Christians to convert to Islam under penalty of death
C) Establishing the millet system to protect Christian minorities
D) Promoting interfaith dialogue between Muslim and Christian scholars

**Correct Answer: A**
*The conversion of Hagia Sophia was a deliberate symbolic act — a statement that the Ottoman Empire, not Christian Byzantium, now controlled Constantinople. It was about legitimation through religious symbolism, not forced conversion.*

---

**Question 2**

Which of the following best explains why the Ottoman Empire emerged as the dominant power in the eastern Mediterranean after 1453?

A) The Ottomans discovered new silver deposits that funded a massive army
B) Gunpowder technology allowed the Ottomans to defeat cavalry-based armies and breach city walls
C) European Christian states deliberately weakened Byzantium so the Ottomans could take control
D) The Mongol Empire's expansion into Anatolia created a power vacuum that Ottomans filled

**Correct Answer: B**
*The siege of Constantinople relied on massive bronze cannons (including the famous Basilica cannon) to breach walls that had stood for over 1,000 years. This is the textbook example of gunpowder technology enabling imperial expansion.*

**Question 3**

The Mughal Emperor Akbar's decision to abolish the jizya tax most directly served to

A) Increase imperial revenue by taxing Muslim merchants instead
B) Win the loyalty of Hindu nobles whose military support was essential to Mughal power
C) Comply with a new interpretation of Islamic law promoted by Sufi scholars
D) Satisfy European merchants who objected to discriminatory taxation

**Correct Answer: B**
*Akbar's religious accommodation policies were fundamentally strategic: the Hindu Rajput warrior nobility controlled key territories and armies. Removing the discriminatory jizya was a calculated move to secure their cooperation.*

**Question 4**

*Use the map below (imagine a map showing Qing China's territory including Inner Asia, Tibet, and Manchuria) to answer the question.*

The Qing Dynasty's territorial expansion beyond China's traditional boundaries is best explained by

A) The Qing's adoption of European military technology acquired through Jesuit missionaries
B) The Kangxi Emperor's personal ambition to surpass the achievements of the Han Dynasty
C) Manchu military traditions that prioritized territorial conquest combined with gunpowder weaponry
D) Chinese merchants' desire for direct access to Central Asian trade routes

**Correct Answer: C**
*The Qing conquered Inner Asia (Xinjiang, Mongolia, Tibet) by combining traditional Manchu cavalry effectiveness with Chinese gunpowder artillery — a synthesis of military traditions from both their Manchu origins and the Chinese state they had conquered.*

**Question 5**

The Ottoman devshirme system and the Chinese examination system were similar in that both

A) Recruited military officers exclusively from conquered foreign populations
B) Created pathways for individuals from non-elite backgrounds to gain imperial power
C) Required candidates to demonstrate mastery of Islamic or Confucian religious texts
D) Were designed to prevent hereditary noble families from monopolizing government positions

**Correct Answer: D**
*Both systems served the same political purpose: breaking the ability of established noble families to dominate government. The devshirme brought in enslaved converts; the examination system tested merit. Both gave emperors loyal officials not beholden to powerful dynasties.*

**Question 6**

The Safavid Empire's forcible conversion of Persia to Shia Islam in the early 1500s had which of the following most significant long-term consequences?

A) It triggered a crusade by European Christian states to liberate Persia
B) It created an enduring sectarian and geopolitical rivalry between the Ottoman and Safavid empires
C) It caused the majority of Persians to emigrate to Ottoman territory
D) It led to the collapse of Sunni Islam as a political force in the Middle East

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Ottoman-Safavid rivalry — Sunni vs. Shia, Turk vs. Persian — became one of the defining geopolitical fault lines of the early modern Middle East. The two empires fought multiple wars over this divide.*

**Question 7**

The Songhai Empire's defeat by a Moroccan army at the Battle of Tondibi (1591) best illustrates which of the following historical patterns?

A) The inherent military superiority of North African states over sub-Saharan empires
B) The decisive role of gunpowder technology in military conflicts of the early modern period
C) The collapse of trans-Saharan trade routes in the late 16th century
D) The spread of Islamic radicalism from North Africa into West Africa

**Correct Answer: B**
*Morocco's army, though far smaller than Songhai's, carried firearms and cannons. The battle is a stark illustration of how gunpowder technology could negate traditional military advantages in numbers and cavalry.*

**Question 8**

Ivan IV's creation of the streltsy infantry units and his use of Cossacks for eastward expansion most closely parallels which other empire's strategy?

A) The Mughal use of mansabdars to administer provinces
B) The Ottoman use of the devshirme to create a loyal military force
C) The Qing use of the Eight Banners system to organize military power
D) The Safavid use of a slave army to reduce dependence on tribal leaders

**Correct Answer: B**
*Both Ivan IV and the Ottomans created professional infantry forces armed with firearms (streltsy = janissaries) that were loyal to the ruler rather than to hereditary noble families — the same political-military logic.*

**Question 9**

Which of the following best describes a similarity between the Ottoman millet system and the Mughal policy of religious accommodation under Akbar?

A) Both required non-Muslims to convert to Islam within three generations
B) Both allowed religious minorities to maintain their own legal and communal institutions
C) Both were inspired by Sufi Islamic traditions of mysticism and tolerance
D) Both were abandoned by successors who imposed stricter religious uniformity

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Ottoman millet system and Akbar's policies both permitted religious minorities to govern their own personal and religious affairs. (Note: D is partially true for the Mughal case — Aurangzeb reversed Akbar's policies — but is not true for the Ottoman millet, which persisted much longer.)*

**Question 10**

The Russian Empire's expansion across Siberia in the 16th–17th centuries most closely resembles which of the following patterns in AP World History?

A) The Mongol Empire's conquest of China — a nomadic people conquering a settled civilization
B) The Ottoman Empire's expansion into the Balkans — a Muslim empire absorbing Christian populations
C) European maritime expansion into the Americas — a technologically superior power conquering indigenous peoples for resources
D) The Qing Empire's expansion into Inner Asia — a centralized state absorbing frontier territories through military force

**Correct Answer: C**
*Russian expansion into Siberia shares key features with European colonial expansion: a state with gunpowder weapons subduing indigenous peoples with less advanced military technology, extracting resources (fur in Siberia, as opposed to silver and gold in the Americas), and establishing settler colonies.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Land-based empires of the period 1450–1750 used a variety of strategies to administer ethnically and religiously diverse populations."**

**(a)** Describe **ONE** specific strategy used by the Ottoman Empire to administer non-Muslim subjects.

**(b)** Describe **ONE** specific strategy used by the Mughal Empire to administer its non-Muslim (Hindu) subjects.

**(c)** Explain **ONE** similarity OR difference between the Ottoman and Mughal approaches to governing religious minorities.

> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) The millet system — allowing Christians and Jews to govern themselves in personal/religious matters through their own community leaders; or the devshirme; or the jizya tax structure
> - (b) Akbar's abolition of jizya; mansabdar system incorporating Rajput Hindu nobles; Din-i-Ilahi; marriage alliances with Rajput families
> - (c) Both used tolerance as a pragmatic tool to maintain stability and loyalty among large non-Muslim populations; OR: the millet system was more institutionalized while Mughal tolerance depended heavily on the individual ruler's personality (Akbar vs. Aurangzeb)

---

### SAQ 2

**(a)** Briefly explain **ONE** way in which gunpowder technology contributed to the expansion of a specific land-based empire in the period 1450–1750.

**(b)** Briefly explain **ONE** way in which gunpowder technology contributed to the *decline or defeat* of a specific state or empire in this period.

**(c)** Explain **ONE** limitation of "gunpowder technology" as a complete explanation for why land-based empires expanded in this period.

> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Ottoman siege of Constantinople using massive cannons; OR Babur's use of artillery at Panipat to defeat a larger cavalry force
> - (b) Songhai Empire's defeat by Morocco at Tondibi (1591); OR Byzantine Empire's fall to Ottoman cannons
> - (c) Administrative strategies, geographic location, religious legitimacy, or economic control of trade routes also explain expansion — technology alone is insufficient; e.g., the Mughals needed mansabdars to hold territory, not just guns to take it

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which land-based empires in the period 1450–1750 used religion as a tool of political legitimation."**

> **Outline for a top-scoring response:**

**Thesis:** State a historically defensible claim that addresses the extent (not just "yes" or "no"). Example: *While all major land-based empires used religion to legitimate their authority, the degree to which religion was enforced vs. managed as a tool of accommodation varied significantly based on the religious demographics of each empire.*

**Body Paragraph 1 — Strong use of religion for legitimacy:**
- Ottoman: Mehmed II converting Hagia Sophia; claiming title of Caliph after 1517; Friday prayers in the emperor's name
- Safavid: Shah Ismail converting Persia to Shia; claiming descent from Ali; Safavid shahs as both religious and political leaders

**Body Paragraph 2 — Religion as accommodation/pragmatism:**
- Mughal under Akbar: abolishing jizya; Din-i-Ilahi; marriages with Rajput families
- Qing: maintaining Confucian bureaucracy and rites while promoting Manchu Buddhism and shamanism

**Body Paragraph 3 — Counterargument/Complexity (for a 7/7 essay):**
- Religious policies changed over time within the same empire (Akbar → Aurangzeb; Kangxi → Yongzheng's anti-Christianity edicts)
- Practical needs (military, administrative, economic) sometimes overrode religious ideology
- For document sourcing, apply: historical situation (context), audience, purpose, and point of view (POV)

**Contextualization:** The Sunni-Shia split as the theological backdrop; the role of the Crusades and prior Islamic empires in shaping how these new empires thought about religious legitimacy.
