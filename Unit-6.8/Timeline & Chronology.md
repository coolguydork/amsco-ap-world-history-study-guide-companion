# Timeline & Chronology

> [!exam-tip]
> Topic 6.8 covers the entire 1750–1900 period synthetically. Rather than a narrow event sequence, master the arc: industrialization → overproduction → imperial expansion → resistance → migration. Being able to sequence these cause-and-effect chains is more important than memorizing exact years.

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1770s–1820s | First Industrial Revolution (Britain) | Root cause: industrial capitalism creates overproduction and demand for raw materials and markets |
| 1776 | American Independence | First successful anti-colonial revolt; model for later independence movements |
| 1800s–1820s | Latin American independence movements | Spanish/Portuguese colonies break away; demonstrates fragility of older colonial empires |
| 1819–1852 | Real wages roughly double in Britain | Living standards improve for some; wealth gap widens simultaneously; measures change in ECN |
| c. 1820–1900 | Exports as % of GDP of industrialized nations surge | Shift from mercantilist to capitalist trade; foreign markets become essential |
| 1838–1839 | Trail of Tears (U.S.) | Settler colonialism in North America; forced removal of Cherokee and other nations |
| c. 1840s–1900s | Scramble for Africa | Most of Africa colonized by European powers; direct rule replaces indigenous governance |
| 1842 | Treaty of Nanjing | Ends First Opium War; China cedes Hong Kong; beginning of "unequal treaties" era |
| c. 1850s–1900 | Mass indentured labor migration | Workers from South Asia, China, Southeast Asia, Europe move to Americas, Africa, Australia |
| 1857 | Indian Rebellion (Sepoy Mutiny) | Anti-colonial uprising → Company dissolved → British Raj begins 1858; nascent independence movement |
| 1858 | British Raj established | Crown replaces East India Company; India under direct rule; pivotal governance shift |
| 1858 | Moshoeshoe's letter to Sir George Grey | Documents British/Boer land seizure; African perspective on settler colonialism |
| 1860s–1890s | Second Industrial Revolution | New industries (steel, chemicals, electricity); intensifies resource demand and imperial competition |
| c. 1870s–1900 | European powers partition Africa at Berlin Conference (1884–85) | Formalizes colonialism; Africa divided with minimal indigenous input |
| 1885 | Fukuzawa Yukichi, "Good-bye Asia" | Japanese intellectual advocates Westernization; Japan as unique case of self-directed modernization |
| 1894–1895 | First Sino-Japanese War | Japan defeats China; signals new imperial power in Asia; accelerates Chinese reform efforts |
| 1899–1901 | Boxer Rebellion (China) | Anti-Western uprising suppressed; China forced to pay reparations; Qing Dynasty weakened |
| c. 1900 | Seeds of anti-colonial nationalism | Resistance movements across Asia/Africa lay groundwork for 20th-century independence |

## The Causal Chain in Visual Form

```
Industrial capitalism
        ↓
Increased manufacturing / overproduction
        ↓
Need for foreign markets + raw materials
        ↓
Imperial expansion (Africa, Asia, Americas)
        ↓
├── Dependent colonial economies / global inequality
├── Mass migration (indentured labor, settlers)
├── Resistance movements (Sepoys, Boxers, Moshoeshoe)
└── European political rivalries → seeds of WWI
```

> [!tip]
> The causal chain above is the backbone of any LEQ or SAQ on this topic. Start from industrial capitalism, trace the logic to imperialism, then branch out to the effects. A thesis that captures the whole chain earns higher scores than one that begins mid-chain ("imperialism caused migration...").

## Continuities vs. Changes (CCOT)

| Category | Continuity | Change |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| Trade systems | Long-distance trade continues | Shifts from mercantilist to capitalist model; scale explodes |
| Colonial power | European colonialism ongoing since 1400s | Dramatically accelerates; reaches Africa, East Asia |
| Labor coercion | Forced labor systems persist | Chattel slavery → indentured labor → wage labor (in theory) |
| Indigenous resistance | Resistance to empire ongoing | Now global in scale; seeds of organized nationalism |
| Living standards | Poverty in non-industrial regions | Divergence widens sharply between industrial/non-industrial |
| Migration | Human migration ongoing throughout history | Scale, speed, and global reach of migration unprecedented |
