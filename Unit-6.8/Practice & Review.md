# Practice & Review

## Long Essay Questions (LEQ)

These prompts are drawn directly from AMSCO pp. 445–449. The suggested writing time is 40 minutes each.

---

**LEQ 1:** In the period 1750 to 1900, some European powers suffered a decrease in influence, and new empires emerged — one in the Americas and one in Asia. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which the global balance of power shifted during that era.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation/Argumentation
> **Thesis (line of reasoning):** The global balance of power shifted significantly between 1750 and 1900, as the United States and Japan emerged as major imperial powers while traditional empires (Spanish, Portuguese, Ottoman, Qing) declined — a shift driven primarily by industrialization, which gave early adopters decisive military and economic advantages.
>
> **Contextualization:** Before 1750, Spain and Portugal dominated the Atlantic colonial world, while the Ottoman Empire and Qing China remained powerful regional hegemonies. The Industrial Revolution, beginning in Britain c. 1760, introduced new productive, military, and logistical capacities that would reshape the global order.
>
> **Evidence cluster 1 (U.S. as new power):** The American Revolution (1776) dismantled British colonial authority in North America. By the mid-19th century the U.S. was an industrial power, exerting Monroe Doctrine hegemony over Latin America and beginning Pacific expansion (Hawaii, Philippines, 1898).
>
> **Evidence cluster 2 (Japan as new power):** The Meiji Restoration (1868) modernized Japan along Western lines. Japan defeated China in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) and Russia in the Russo-Japanese War (1905), becoming the first non-Western power to defeat a European state in a modern war.
>
> **Counterargument/qualification:** The shift was real but uneven. Britain remained the dominant imperial power through 1900. The emergence of the U.S. and Japan did not immediately challenge European dominance in Africa or South Asia — it created a more multipolar competition, not a complete hegemonic transfer.

**LEQ 2:** From 1750 to 1900, nationalist movements emerged throughout South America, Africa, and Asia as resistance to imperialist powers increased. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which *internal* factors influenced the process of state building during that era.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> **Thesis:** Internal factors — particularly pre-existing political structures, religious identity, and educated indigenous elites — significantly shaped state-building processes in colonized regions, though external imperial pressures were equally, if not more, determinative.
>
> **Evidence for internal factors:** In India, the Rebellion of 1857 drew on Mughal legitimacy (Bahadur Shah as symbolic leader) and united Hindu and Muslim grievances — both internal cultural resources. In the Americas, Creole elites (internal economic class) led independence movements against Iberian rule. Japan's Meiji reformers used existing imperial institutions and samurai-class leadership to drive state modernization.
>
> **Evidence qualifying internal factors:** External triggers — the spread of Enlightenment ideologies, the demonstration effect of the American and French Revolutions, European military pressure — were equally essential. The Boxer Rebellion in China, despite tapping internal nationalist sentiment, was provoked by *external* Western and Japanese intrusion. Many independence movements explicitly modeled themselves on European political forms (constitutions, nation-states) borrowed from the colonizers.
>
> **Complexity:** Internal and external factors were not separate — imperial rule *created* internal conditions (educated middle class, unified ethnic identity, anti-colonial ideology) that then drove anti-colonial nationalism. This interdependence means the internal/external distinction is analytical, not historical.

**LEQ 3:** European powers divided up much of Africa and India from 1750 to 1900, but indigenous peoples had their own opinions about imperialism. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which the responses of native people to imperialism were *similar* in India and Sub-Saharan Africa between 1750 and 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> **Thesis:** Indigenous responses to imperialism in India and Sub-Saharan Africa were more similar than different, both characterized by initial armed resistance followed by pragmatic accommodation, though the scale of organized political resistance was greater in India due to its pre-existing administrative structures.
>
> **Similarity 1 — Armed resistance:** Both regions produced major armed uprisings. The Indian Rebellion of 1857 mobilized tens of thousands of soldiers and civilians. In Africa, the Asante Kingdom resisted British expansion repeatedly (Asante-British Wars), the Zulu defeated British forces at Isandlwana (1879), and Moshoeshoe of the Basotho used both diplomacy and military force to defend territory.
>
> **Similarity 2 — Invocation of legitimacy and rights:** Firoz Shah's 1857 proclamation appealed to justice and Hindu-Muslim unity. Moshoeshoe's 1858 letter to the British governor appealed to treaty law and royal authority. Both used the imperial power's own legal and moral frameworks to challenge their dispossession.
>
> **Difference — Scale and organization:** India's resistance could draw on the existing Mughal imperial legacy as a unifying symbol. Sub-Saharan Africa lacked a pan-African political structure; resistance was organized at the kingdom or ethnic level. This made African resistance more geographically fragmented, though no less fierce locally.

**LEQ 4:** As the economy grew increasingly global from 1750 to 1900, working people in Eurasia, the Americas, Australia, and Africa began moving into urban centers and across borders, but many had the same motivations their ancestors had. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which the migration patterns from 1750 to 1900 show *changes or continuities* over time.

> [!tip] Model Response — CCOT
> **Thesis:** Migration patterns from 1750 to 1900 show significant continuity in motivation (economic opportunity, escape from coercion) but represent a fundamental change in scale, global reach, and the structural role of states and corporations in organizing and directing migration.
>
> **Continuity — motivations:** Throughout history, people have migrated to escape poverty, violence, or coercion and to seek economic opportunity. Indentured laborers in 1850 were motivated by the same push-pull factors as medieval rural-urban migrants. Settler colonists sought land and opportunity, as had colonists since the 1500s.
>
> **Change — scale:** The volume of migration from 1750–1900 was unprecedented. Millions of Europeans emigrated to the Americas and Australia; millions of indentured workers crossed oceans; urbanization moved millions from rural to urban areas within countries. Steam-powered transportation compressed time and distance.
>
> **Change — state and corporate organization:** Pre-industrial migration was largely voluntary and uncoordinated. 19th-century migration was heavily shaped by state policy (land grants encouraging settlers, immigration restrictions targeting Asian workers) and corporate labor contracts (indentured indenture through plantation companies). The state and capital played active roles in directing human movement.

## Document-Based Question (DBQ)

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which imperialism influenced state formation and contraction around the world in the period 1750 to 1900. *(AMSCO p. 446)*

**Document Summary:**

| Doc | Source | Perspective |
|-----|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Andrew Jackson, message to Congress, 1835 | U.S. President justifies Cherokee removal ("Trail of Tears") — settler colonialism as state formation at indigenous expense |
| 2 | Treaty of Nanjing, 1842 | British legal instrument forcing China to cede Hong Kong — state *contraction* through unequal treaty |
| 3 | Map of Ottoman Empire, 1800–1914 | Visual evidence of Ottoman state contraction — territory lost in stages to European powers |
| 4 | Firoz Shah proclamation, Delhi Gazette, 1857 | Indian resistance voice — Hindu-Muslim unity against British; example of anti-colonial state formation attempt |
| 5 | Moshoeshoe letter to Sir George Grey, 1858 | African chief's perspective on treaty violation and land seizure — state contraction through settler pressure |
| 6 | Fukuzawa Yukichi, "Good-bye Asia," 1885 | Japanese intellectual endorsing Westernization — Japan as example of *successful* state transformation to avoid contraction |

> [!exam-tip]
> **Thesis strategy:** Don't just list "imperialism affected state formation in many ways." Make a *directional argument*: "Imperialism accelerated state formation in industrializing nations while simultaneously causing state contraction in colonized regions, creating a fundamentally asymmetrical global political order."
>
> **Sourcing three documents (required):**
> - **Doc 1 (Jackson):** *Purpose* — justifying a politically popular policy of land seizure through paternalistic racial language. *Historical situation* — Jacksonian America's settler expansion westward, enabled by military force and legal manipulation.
> - **Doc 4 (Firoz Shah):** *Point of view* — a Mughal prince appealing to Hindu-Muslim unity; his credibility rests on Mughal dynastic legitimacy, not popular electoral authority. *Audience* — Indian soldiers and civilians willing to fight, not British authorities.
> - **Doc 5 (Moshoeshoe):** *Point of view* — a sophisticated African diplomat who knows British legal frameworks and uses them to appeal for justice. The fact that he *knows* the treaties and cites them makes his account credible and damning.
>
> **Outside evidence to add:** Berlin Conference (1884–85) formalized African partition; Opium Wars (1839–42, 1856–60) show systematic British state-building through military coercion; Meiji Restoration (1868) as a counter-example of voluntary state transformation to resist colonization.

## SAQ Practice

**SAQ:** Using specific evidence, explain how industrialization contributed to imperial expansion in the period 1750–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Industrialization contributed to imperial expansion in the period 1750–1900 primarily by creating overproduction that exceeded domestic market capacity, driving industrialized nations to seek foreign markets and raw material sources.
>
> The manufacturing capacity of industrialized nations increased dramatically due to automation, the division of labor, and the assembly line. This efficiency produced more consumer goods (textiles, home furnishings, porcelain) than domestic markets could absorb, creating an overproduction crisis. To solve it, industrial economies looked overseas — both to sell surplus goods and to import the raw materials (coal, iron, rubber, copper, tin) that industry required. Western European nations competed intensely for foreign markets and colonial territories, particularly in Africa and Asia. By 1900, most of Africa had been partitioned among European powers, and large portions of South and Southeast Asia were under British and French colonial rule.
>
> Additionally, industrialization provided the *means* to enforce imperial expansion. Advances in military technology (Maxim gun, breech-loading rifles), steam-powered warships, and quinine-based malaria treatment gave industrialized nations decisive advantages over the non-industrialized regions they sought to control. The technological gap created by the Industrial Revolution was thus both a motive for and an enabler of 19th-century imperialism.

## Reflect on the Essential Question

**Q:** What was the relative significance of the effects of imperialism from 1750 to 1900?

Use these four criteria to rank the effects and build an argument:

| Effect | Depth of change | # of people | Duration | Importance |
|--------|----------------|-------------|---------|------------|
| Rising living standards (industrial nations) | Moderate (consumer goods, real wages) | Millions in Europe/U.S. | Decades | Improved quality of life but also inequality |
| Global economic inequality | High (restructured entire economies) | Hundreds of millions globally | 150+ years | Persisting into 21st century |
| Seeds of independence/nationalism | High (new political identities) | Billions eventually | 100+ years | Led to 20th-century decolonization |
| Mass migration | High (reshaped demographics) | Tens of millions | Permanent (diaspora communities) | Transformed populations of multiple continents |

> [!exam-tip]
> A defensible argument could prioritize any of these — what matters is that you *pick one, defend it with evidence, and acknowledge the others as less significant by comparison*. Avoid the trap of saying "all effects were equally significant" — that is description, not argument.
