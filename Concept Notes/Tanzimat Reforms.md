# Tanzimat Reforms

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**Tanzimat** (Arabic/Ottoman Turkish: "reorganization") refers to the sweeping series of Ottoman reforms carried out between **1839 and 1876**. They represent the Ottoman state's attempt to modernize — and survive — in an era of European industrial and imperial dominance.

---

## Core Changes

The Tanzimat occurred in two overlapping waves:

**Under Mahmud II (1808–1839) — Pre-Tanzimat foundation:**
- Abolished and massacred the Janissaries (1826)
- Abolished feudal tax-farming (1831), centralizing revenue
- Built roads, postal service, European-style government ministries
- Rebuilt army on European model

**Tanzimat proper (1839–1876):**
- Rooted out corruption in central government
- Created secular primary and secondary schools (replacing ulama control of education)
- Established secular colleges (military, engineering, translation, civil service)
- Codified Ottoman law: commercial code (1850), penal code (1858)
- Issued the **Hatt-i Humayun** (1856) — declared legal equality for all men regardless of religion or ethnicity
- Regulated the **millets** (separate religious courts) within the new legal framework

---

## What the Tanzimat Did NOT Do

- Did not extend meaningful rights to women — the 1839 edict didn't mention women; Mahmud II's secular reforms actually reduced some women's property rights under previous shariah protections
- Did not abolish the sultanate or create a constitutional monarchy
- Did not resolve ethnic nationalism threatening the empire's diverse population

---

## Reactions and Limits

The Hatt-i Humayun (1856) provoked contradictory reactions:
- **Christians in the Balkans** feared losing the autonomy they had under the millets system
- **Conservative Muslims** protested the break with Islamic tradition and law

Sultan **Abdulhamid II** (r. 1876–1909) initially accepted a constitution but became increasingly authoritarian — exiling the Young Turks reform movement and presiding over the **Hamidian Massacres** (1894–1896) in which 100,000–250,000 Armenians were killed. He earned the nickname "the Red Sultan."

## AP Significance

The Tanzimat is the AP exam's primary example of **Ottoman modernization** — and also of its **limits**. It shows how a state can attempt reform without changing its fundamental power structure, and how reform can be inconsistently applied (benefiting men, not women; benefiting some minorities while excluding others).
