# Spanish-American War

1898 war between the United States and Spain in which U.S. victory gave America control of Guam, Puerto Rico, Cuba, and the Philippines — launching the United States as a Pacific and Caribbean imperial power. The war grew out of Cuban independence struggles against Spain, U.S. economic interests in Cuba, and "yellow journalism" inflaming American public opinion. The acquisition of the Philippines especially provoked the Anti-Imperialist League and a debate about whether republican values were compatible with overseas empire.

> [!exam-tip]
> The Spanish-American War marks the U.S. joining the European imperial powers in the scramble for overseas colonies — a sharp break from its earlier Monroe Doctrine posture. On LEQ/DBQ essays about imperialism, use it to show that imperialism was not exclusively European and that economic motives (Cuban sugar, Pacific trade routes) drove American expansion just as Social Darwinism and the "civilizing mission" drove European expansion.

> [!warning]
> Cuba gained nominal independence after the war, but the Platt Amendment (1901) gave the U.S. the right to intervene in Cuban affairs — making it a de facto U.S. protectorate, not truly independent. This nuance matters for essays about the limits of self-determination.
