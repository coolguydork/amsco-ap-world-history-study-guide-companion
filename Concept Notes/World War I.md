# World War I

**World War I (1914–1918)** was a global conflict rooted in European nationalist rivalries, imperial competition, and a system of interlocking alliances. It is covered in depth in Unit 7 (not yet generated). The connections below trace the causal roots developed in Unit 5.

---

## Causal Roots in Unit 5 (c. 1750–1900)

### From Topic 5.2 — Nationalism and Revolutions
Nationalist movements reshaped the European map in the 19th century, creating the conditions that led to WWI:

- **German and Italian unification** created new great powers that fundamentally upset the balance of power established at the Congress of Vienna (1815). Germany's rise made older powers — especially Britain, France, and Russia — anxious. *(Source: [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism and Revolutions Overview]])*
- **Alsace-Lorraine**: Germany seized this French territory in the Franco-Prussian War (1870), creating a lasting French grievance that would fuel revanchism leading into WWI. *(Source: [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism and Revolutions Overview]])*
- **Balkan nationalism**: As the Ottoman Empire declined, Balkan peoples (Greeks, Serbs, Bulgarians) asserted independence. The Balkans became a powder keg of competing nationalisms, great-power rivalries (Austria-Hungary vs. Russia), and Ottoman collapse — the direct context for the assassination of Franz Ferdinand in 1914. *(Source: [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism and Revolutions Overview]])*

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP exam, WWI causation questions often ask you to trace *long-run* causes (nationalism, imperialism, alliance systems) vs. *short-run* triggers (the assassination). Topic 5.2 gives you the long-run causes — know how Balkan nationalism and German unification created the structural conditions for war.
