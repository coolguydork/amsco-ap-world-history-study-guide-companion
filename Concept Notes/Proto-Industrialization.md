# Proto-Industrialization

## What Is Proto-Industrialization?

**Proto-industrialization** describes a set of economic changes in which people in **rural areas** produce more goods than they need for local consumption, using **simple, home-based or community-based equipment** — rather than large factories with complex machinery.

It is called "proto" because it preceded and helped set conditions for the full Industrial Revolution, which came in the 18th–19th centuries.

## Proto-Industrialization in Song China

China experienced proto-industrialization **centuries before Western Europe**. The Song Dynasty (960–1279) is the key example:

- Rural artisans produced **steel** in widely dispersed smelting facilities under government supervision
- **Porcelain** and **silk** were manufactured at community scale and sold through expanding trade networks
- These goods were not consumed locally — they were exported via the Grand Canal and maritime routes

From [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty and East Asia]]: "China experienced proto-industrialization, a set of economic changes in which people in rural areas made more goods than they could sell locally... artisans produced steel and other products in widely dispersed smelting facilities... Artisans also manufactured porcelain and silk that reached consumers through expanding trade networks, especially by sea."

## Significance for AP

Proto-industrialization in Song China is evidence of:
1. **China as the world's most commercially sophisticated economy** in this period
2. **Continuity and precursor**: Sets up the later Industrial Revolution (Topic 5) by showing that industrialization has deep roots
3. **Technology**: China's coal use, steel-making, and production scale were unmatched before the 18th century
