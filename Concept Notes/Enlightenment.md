## Definition

The **Enlightenment** was an 18th-century European intellectual movement that applied **reason and scientific thinking** to questions of governance, society, and human nature. It grew directly from the **Scientific Revolution** of the 17th century and challenged the traditional bases of authority — divine right of kings, Church doctrine, aristocratic privilege.

---

## Root: The Scientific Revolution (AMSCO pp. 172–173)

The Scientific Revolution (early 1600s onward) laid the intellectual groundwork:
- **Francis Bacon (1620)**: empiricism — knowledge must rest on observable evidence
- **Isaac Newton (1687)**: *Principia* — gravitational force; showed the universe operates by rational, discoverable laws
- Royal Academies of Science in France and England formalized scientific exchange

Key insight: if natural laws governed the physical world, then **natural laws might also govern human society and government**. This leap from physics to politics was the birth of the Enlightenment.

## Key Enlightenment Ideas (preview — full treatment in [[Unit-5.1/Enlightenment and Political Revolutions]])

- **Natural rights** — humans are born with inherent rights (life, liberty, property) — John Locke
- **Social contract** — government derives its legitimacy from the consent of the governed — Locke, Rousseau
- **Separation of powers** — government should be divided to prevent tyranny — Montesquieu
- **Religious toleration** — reason, not faith, should guide public policy — Voltaire

- **Unit 3**: Scientific Revolution (Topic 3.3) → challenge to traditional authority
