# Shinto

## Definition

**Shinto** is Japan's indigenous religion, centered on reverence for **kami** — spirits or sacred forces present in natural phenomena (mountains, rivers, trees, storms) and in ancestors. Unlike Buddhism or Confucianism, Shinto has no founder, no single sacred text, and no systematic theology. It is rooted in Japan's own landscape and community life, practiced through rituals, shrines, and festivals that honor the kami and maintain harmony between humans and the natural world.

## Shinto and the Sinification of Japan

Shinto's most important role in AP World History is as a marker of **Japanese cultural resistance to sinification**. When Prince Shotoku Taishi (574–622) promoted Buddhism and Confucianism in Japan, he did so *alongside* Shinto — not in place of it. This coexistence reflects Japan's broader pattern of selective cultural adoption: borrowing from China while preserving native identity.

From [[Unit-1.1/Japan Korea Vietnam|Japan, Korea & Vietnam]]:
> "Japan's Prince Shotoku Taishi (574–622) promoted Buddhism and Confucianism along with Japan's traditional Shinto religion." — AMSCO p. 9

Over time, Buddhism and Shinto developed a syncretic relationship in Japan — Buddhist figures were identified with Shinto kami, and many Japanese practiced both traditions simultaneously. This blending is another example of religious syncretism in East Asia, comparable to Chan Buddhism's fusion of Buddhism and [[Daoism]] in China.

## Why It Matters for the AP Exam

Shinto appears in AP World History primarily as evidence that:

1. **Japan selectively adopted Chinese culture** — it absorbed Buddhism and Confucianism but maintained its native religious tradition, demonstrating agency in the face of sinification pressure
2. **Religious syncretism** — the coexistence and eventual blending of Shinto and Buddhism in Japan is an East Asian parallel to Chan Buddhism and Neo-Confucianism in China
3. **Cultural identity and resistance** — Shinto symbolizes the distinctiveness that Japan preserved even as it emulated Chinese political and cultural forms

> [!exam-tip]
> You don't need deep knowledge of Shinto's theology for the AP exam. Know it as Japan's **native religion** that survived alongside imported Buddhism and Confucianism — evidence of Japan's selective, autonomous approach to sinification.
