# Anti-Semitism

Hostility, prejudice, and discrimination against Jewish people. Anti-Semitism has deep roots in medieval European society (Jews blamed for the Black Death, expelled from England in 1290 and Spain in 1492), but reached its most extreme form in Nazi Germany, where Hitler weaponized it into state ideology — culminating in the Holocaust. Nationalist and economic anxieties in the late 19th and early 20th centuries intensified anti-Semitic movements across Europe.

> [!exam-tip]
> Anti-Semitism bridges Unit 5 (Enlightenment-era nationalism and exclusion) and Units 7–8 (Nazi ideology and the Holocaust). For DBQ/LEQ essays about the Holocaust or World War II, anti-Semitism is the *precondition* — it doesn't explain the Holocaust alone, but without it, the Nuremberg Laws, ghettoization, and genocide make no sense.

> [!warning]
> Don't treat anti-Semitism as uniquely German. The AP expects awareness that it was widespread across Europe before 1933 — the Dreyfus Affair in France, pogroms in Russia, and exclusionary nationalism throughout Eastern Europe all provide comparative context.
