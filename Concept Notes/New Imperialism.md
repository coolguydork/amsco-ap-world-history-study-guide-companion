# New Imperialism

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**New Imperialism** refers to the aggressive wave of European (and later American and Japanese) colonial expansion in the late 19th and early 20th centuries (roughly 1870–1914), distinguished from earlier colonialism by its industrial technological base, its speed and scale, and its near-total partition of Africa and Asia. The technologies of the [[Concept Notes/Industrial Revolution|Industrial Revolution]] — railroads, steamships, the telegraph, and industrial weapons — made this expansion possible.

## How Industrialization Enabled New Imperialism

**From [[Unit-5.5/The Two Industrial Revolutions]] (Topic 5.5):**
> "Industrial nations could extract resources from distant colonies efficiently, then sell finished goods back — the technological foundation of New Imperialism."

The mechanism was direct: industrial technology gave European states overwhelming military advantages over non-industrialized peoples, while railroads and steamships made it economically viable to extract raw materials from deep interior regions that had previously been inaccessible.

**Technological drivers:**
- **Steamships** — reliable, scheduled ocean transport; no longer dependent on wind
- **[[Concept Notes/Coaling stations|Coaling stations]]** — strategic control points (e.g., [[Concept Notes/Cape Colony|Cape Colony]], [[Concept Notes/Aden|Aden]]) that gave industrial navies global reach
- **Railroads** — opened interior Africa and Asia to resource extraction
- **Telegraph** — enabled real-time coordination of military campaigns across continents
- **Industrial weapons** — repeating rifles, machine guns, artillery created overwhelming military asymmetry

## Who Colonized Whom

By 1914, European powers controlled approximately **85% of the earth's land surface**. The partition of Africa was essentially complete by 1900. Asia was carved into spheres of influence. Only a handful of states maintained independence (Japan, Ethiopia, Thailand/Siam, Liberia).

**Key imperial powers:** Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Italy, the Netherlands, and by 1900, the United States and Japan.

## AP Themes

- **Governance:** Colonial governments imposed foreign rule, undermined local political systems
- **Economic Systems:** Colonies deindustrialized (see [[Concept Notes/colonialism|colonialism]]) and became raw-material exporters
- **Technology:** Industrial weapons and transportation infrastructure were both the tools and the symbols of imperial power
