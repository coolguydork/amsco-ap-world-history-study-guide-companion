# Nonaggression Pact

Also known as the **Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact** (after the two foreign ministers who signed it).

**August 23, 1939** — Secret agreement between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union pledging mutual non-aggression, trade cooperation, and the secret division of Eastern Europe into spheres of influence.

## Key Provisions

Public: Germany and the USSR pledged not to attack each other and to remain neutral if either was attacked by a third party.

Secret protocol: Divided Eastern Europe between German and Soviet spheres — Finland, Estonia, Latvia, and eastern Poland to the USSR; western Poland and Lithuania to Germany. Romania's Bessarabia was also assigned to the Soviet sphere.

## Why It Mattered

**For Hitler:** The pact freed Germany from the two-front war that had destroyed Imperial Germany in WWI. Hitler could now invade Poland (September 1939) without fear of Soviet retaliation, triggering WWII. It also shocked Western democracies that had hoped the USSR would counterbalance Germany.

**For Stalin:** The pact bought time to rebuild the Red Army (still recovering from Stalin's purges of its officer corps) and to expand Soviet territory westward as a buffer zone. Stalin disastrously misjudged Hitler's intentions and ignored intelligence of an impending invasion.

**For Japan:** When the Nonaggression Pact removed the threat of German pressure on the USSR, Japan could no longer count on Germany to distract the Soviets. Japan had been considering expansion into Soviet Siberia; the pact forced Japan to look south instead — toward Southeast Asia and the U.S.-held Philippines — setting the stage for Pearl Harbor.

## Violation

Germany violated the pact on **June 22, 1941** by launching Operation Barbarossa, the largest land invasion in history. The USSR, caught unprepared, suffered catastrophic initial losses before eventually turning the tide.
