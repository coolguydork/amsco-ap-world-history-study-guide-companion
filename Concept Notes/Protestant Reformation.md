# Concept Note: Protestant Reformation

> [!summary] Source-built concept note

## Definition

The **Protestant Reformation** was a 16th-century religious movement that **split Western Christianity** and ended the Roman Catholic Church's monopoly over Christian faith in Europe. Sparked by Martin Luther's challenge to Church corruption in 1517, it gave rise to Lutheranism, Calvinism, and Anglicanism — and triggered decades of religious warfare.

## Key Causes

1. **Church corruption** — sale of indulgences, simony, clerical immorality
2. **Political ambitions** of centralizing monarchs seeking Church lands
3. **Babylonian Captivity (1309–1377)** — papacy in France; undermined papal independence
4. **Gutenberg printing press (c. 1450)** — allowed rapid spread of reform ideas
5. **Theological dissatisfaction** — Wycliffe, Hus had already challenged the Church

## Key Outcomes

- **Religious pluralism** in Europe — Catholicism, Lutheranism, Calvinism, Anglicanism
- **Wars of Religion**: Schmalkaldic War, French Wars of Religion, Thirty Years' War (1618–1648)
- **Peace of Augsburg (1555)** — each German state chooses its religion
- **Peace of Westphalia (1648)** — three choices permitted; states gain autonomy
- **Counter-Reformation** — Catholic response via Inquisition, Jesuits, Council of Trent
- **Social changes**: women's literacy increased (Luther); but convent leadership ended for Protestants

---

## Connection to the Scientific Revolution

The Reformation and the Scientific Revolution emerged from the same period of intellectual ferment. The Reformation's challenge to Church authority loosened the grip of Church doctrine on all intellectual life. Within a century, **empiricism** (Bacon, 1620) and **Newton's *Principia*** (1687) offered a new framework — reason and evidence rather than faith — for understanding the world. This set the stage for the **[[Concept Notes/Enlightenment|Enlightenment]]** (see [[Unit-5.1/Enlightenment and Political Revolutions]]).

## AP Significance

The Protestant Reformation is high-frequency because it:
- Explains European religious geography for the next 400 years
- Drove the **Wars of Religion** that reshaped European political borders
- Enabled the **Counter-Reformation** and Jesuit missionary expansion
- Created the **social context** for increased literacy, print culture, and eventually Enlightenment
