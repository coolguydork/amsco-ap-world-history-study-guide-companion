# Capitalism

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**Capitalism** is an economic system in which private individuals own the means of production — factories, land, and capital — and make decisions based on market forces (supply and demand) rather than government direction. It replaced [[mercantilism]] as the dominant economic framework of the industrial era.

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 325–328)

> "In the Wealth of Nations (1776), Adam Smith, arguing that humans are naturally transactional, provided a foundational text in support of capitalism and the establishment of private entrepreneurship and shaped the economics and politics of the industrial age and the centuries to follow. Mercantilism, a system of economic protectionism, was replaced by a laissez-faire policy that promoted minimal governmental involvement in commerce."

## Key Distinctions

| System | Who owns production? | Who sets prices? | Government role |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Mercantilism** | State/chartered companies | Government | Heavy — controls trade |
| **Capitalism** | Private individuals | Market (supply & demand) | Minimal (laissez-faire) |
| **Socialism** | Collective/state | Government/collective | Central |

## AP Exam Significance

Capitalism is central to Period 3 (1750–1900). Know: (1) how it replaced mercantilism, (2) the role of [[Adam Smith]] in justifying it, (3) how it enabled the [[Unit-5.3/Industrial Revolution Begins Overview|Industrial Revolution]], and (4) the critiques it generated from [[Unit-5.8/Karl Marx & Socialism|Karl Marx]] and others.
