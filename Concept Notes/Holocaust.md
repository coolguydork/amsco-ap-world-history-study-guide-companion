# Holocaust

Nazi Germany's systematic genocide of approximately 6 million Jews and 5 million others (Roma, disabled, political prisoners, gay people, Soviet POWs) during WWII (1941–1945). The Holocaust is the paradigmatic case of 20th-century genocide and the primary reference point for international human rights law.

## Key Facts

- **Perpetrator:** Nazi Germany, led by Adolf Hitler; implemented by Heinrich Himmler's SS
- **Scale:** ~6 million Jews (two-thirds of European Jewry) + ~5 million others = ~11 million total
- **Method:** Escalating persecution → ghettos → deportation → death camps (Auschwitz, Treblinka, Dachau); industrial technology (trains, gas chambers, crematoria) made mass murder systematic
- **Legal foundation:** Nuremberg Laws (1935) stripped Jews of citizenship; enabled escalating persecution
- **Final Solution:** Coordinated at the Wannsee Conference (January 1942); death camps assigned quotas for extermination

## Key Consequences

The Holocaust's exposure transformed international law. The **Nuremberg Trials** (1945–46) established that leaders could be criminally accountable for crimes against humanity. The **UN Genocide Convention** (1948) obligated states to prevent and punish genocide. The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** (1948) codified universal rights. Israel's founding (1948) was directly accelerated by the Holocaust refugee crisis and the moral case for a Jewish homeland.

> [!exam-tip]
> The most common AP comparison: **Armenian Genocide ↔ Holocaust**. Both: state-organized, target minority framed as a threat, wartime cover, international inaction. Key difference: method (deportation/exposure vs. industrial death camps) and that Hitler explicitly cited the Armenians as precedent.
