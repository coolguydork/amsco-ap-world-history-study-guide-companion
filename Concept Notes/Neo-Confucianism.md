# Neo-Confucianism

## What Is Neo-Confucianism?

**Neo-Confucianism** is a syncretic philosophy that evolved in China between **770 and 840 CE**. It combined:
- **Confucian ethics** (social hierarchy, filial piety, loyalty to rulers, moral self-cultivation)
- **Buddhist metaphysics** (meditation, inner cultivation, the nature of the mind)
- **Daoist naturalism** (harmony with the natural world)

The result was a system that emphasized **ethics** and **social order** rather than the mysteries of God or nature — making it highly practical for governance.

## Significance

Neo-Confucianism became the **dominant intellectual and governmental ideology** of East Asia. It spread from China to:
- **Korea**: Adopted by educated elite; used to run a Chinese-style centralized government
- **Japan**: Adopted alongside feudal structures; used by shoguns to justify authority
- **Vietnam**: Adopted in scholar-official classes, though Vietnamese officials often remained village-loyal rather than emperor-loyal

It justified the **civil service exam** system: if government is about ethics, then rulers who demonstrate ethical and intellectual mastery (through exams) are legitimate.

It also reinforced **patriarchal social structures**: filial piety (subordinating oneself to the male head of household) was extended as a metaphor for loyalty to the ruler.
