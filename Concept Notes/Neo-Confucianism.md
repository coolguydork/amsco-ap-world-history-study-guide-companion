## What It Is

**Neo-Confucianism** is a syncretic intellectual and philosophical system that emerged in China between c. 960 and 1280 CE. It fused three traditions:

- **Confucian ethics** — social harmony, filial piety, loyalty, proper hierarchy
- **Daoist metaphysics** — naturalistic philosophy, the "way" (dao), harmony with nature
- **Buddhist concepts** — self-cultivation, meditation, spiritual introspection

The result emphasized **ethics and moral self-improvement** over theology or the mysteries of God and nature. It became the dominant intellectual tradition across East Asia for centuries.

---

## Why It Matters for the AP Exam

Neo-Confucianism is one of the most exam-tested concepts in AP World History. It appears as:
- Evidence of **cultural diffusion** (China → Korea, Japan, Vietnam)
- Evidence of **religious syncretism**
- A framework for understanding **governance** (civil service exam, scholar-gentry)
- A tool for explaining **patriarchal social structures** (filial piety, women's roles)
- A comparison point for other intellectual traditions (Islamic rationalism, European scholasticism)

> [!exam-tip]
> When the AP asks about how Chinese cultural traditions affected neighboring societies, Neo-Confucianism is almost always the strongest evidence. Be specific: name it, explain its syncretic origins, and give a regional example (Japan adopting it via the civil service exam; Korean elites studying Confucian classics).

## Topic 1.1 — Developments in East Asia (AMSCO pp. 9–10)

Neo-Confucianism developed during the Song Dynasty as Confucian scholars encountered Buddhist texts made widely available through woodblock printing. Rather than rejecting Buddhism, Song scholars absorbed its ideas of self-cultivation and Daoist naturalism into a reinvigorated Confucian framework. This new synthesis became **immensely popular** in China and spread to Japan, Korea, and Vietnam — the countries in China's political and cultural orbit.

Key social effects in China:
- Reinforced **filial piety** (duty to male head of household and ruler)
- Strengthened **patriarchal norms**, including foot binding among aristocratic families
- Provided ideological backing for the **scholar-gentry** class and the civil service exam
