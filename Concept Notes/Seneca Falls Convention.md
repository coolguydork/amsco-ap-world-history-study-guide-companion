# Seneca Falls Convention

> [!summary] Source-built concept note

## Quick Definition

The **Seneca Falls Convention** (July 1848) was the first organized women's rights convention in the United States. Held in Seneca Falls, New York, approximately 300 people gathered to discuss and advocate for women's equality. The convention produced the *Declaration of Sentiments*, drafted by **Elizabeth Cady Stanton**, which applied Enlightenment natural rights language to women's political and legal exclusion.

**Key line from the Declaration of Sentiments:** "We hold these truths to be self-evident: that all men and women are created equal."

## Context: Why 1848?

1848 is a significant year across multiple topics:
- In Europe: the [[Unit-5.8/Reactions to the Industrial Economy Overview|Revolutions of 1848]] swept European cities, demanding democratic government
- *The Communist Manifesto* was also published in 1848
- The Seneca Falls Convention occurred in the same year — part of a broader 19th-century wave of political reform movements applying Enlightenment ideas to new social questions

---

## Key Figures

- **Elizabeth Cady Stanton** — primary author of the *Declaration of Sentiments*; central organizer
- **Lucretia Mott** — Quaker abolitionist and co-organizer; had been denied a seat at an anti-slavery convention because she was a woman
- **Frederick Douglass** — the only prominent Black man to attend; argued in favor of women's suffrage
