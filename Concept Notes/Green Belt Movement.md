# Green Belt Movement

**Founded:** 1977 by Wangari Maathai in Kenya
**Type:** Environmental and women's empowerment NGO
**Method:** Organizing rural women to plant trees, conserve soil, and protect water sources

The Green Belt Movement was a direct response to the environmental degradation caused by colonial-era deforestation and land misuse in Kenya. Rural women reported drying streams and unpredictable food supplies. Maathai organized them to plant trees — over 51 million trees planted by 2004 — restoring soil, watersheds, and food security.

The movement also had a deeper social mission: it demonstrated to rural women that they had the capacity to change their environment through collective political action, rather than leaving decisions to government officials. This was a form of grassroots democratic empowerment.

**Wangari Maathai** won the **Nobel Peace Prize in 2004** — the first African woman to do so. The Nobel Committee's decision to award a *peace* prize for *environmental* activism was significant: it validated the argument that environmental rights are human rights, and that environmental degradation is a source of conflict.
