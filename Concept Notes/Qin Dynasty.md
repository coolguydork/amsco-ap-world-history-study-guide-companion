# Qin Dynasty

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

## Definition

The **Qin Dynasty** (221–207 BCE) was the first dynasty to unify China under a single emperor. Though it lasted only fourteen years, it established foundational institutions that defined Chinese governance for over two millennia — most importantly, the **imperial bureaucracy**: a centralized system of appointed officials carrying out state policy.

## Historical Significance

The Qin Dynasty matters in AP World History not for its own longevity (it collapsed quickly under the strain of its own harsh rule) but for what it *established*:

- **Imperial bureaucracy** — the Qin created the model of a centralized administrative state governed by appointed officials rather than feudal lords. This structure persisted through the Han, Tang, Song, Ming, and Qing dynasties.
- **Standardization** — the Qin standardized weights, measures, currency, and written script across China, enabling economic and cultural integration of a vast territory.
- **The "First Emperor"** — Qin Shi Huangdi was the first ruler to take the title *huangdi* (emperor), centralizing power in a way that became the template for all subsequent Chinese dynasties.

## Why It Matters for the AP Exam

The Qin Dynasty appears in AP World History primarily as the **origin point of the imperial bureaucracy** — a *continuity* that runs through all later Chinese dynasties covered in the course.

From [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty|Song Dynasty]]:
> "The bureaucracy had been a feature of Chinese government since the Qin dynasty (221 B.C.E.–207 B.C.E.). It represented a continuity across centuries and dynasties." — AMSCO p. 4

When AP essays ask you to analyze continuity in Chinese governance, the Qin Dynasty is the starting point of the thread.

> [!exam-tip]
> You don't need deep knowledge of the Qin Dynasty itself for AP World History Modern — the course starts at c. 1200. But you should be able to cite the Qin as the origin of the bureaucratic tradition that continued through the Song and beyond.
