# Balkan Nationalism

Nationalist movements among the peoples of the Balkan Peninsula — Serbs, Bulgarians, Greeks, Romanians, Croats — seeking independence from or expansion at the expense of the declining Ottoman and Austro-Hungarian Empires. By the early 20th century, the Balkans had become the most explosive fault line in European politics, earning the label "the powder keg of Europe."

## Historical Background

The Balkans were a patchwork of ethnic and religious groups under foreign imperial control. The Ottoman Empire's long retreat from Europe ("the Eastern Question") created a vacuum that Austria-Hungary and Russia competed to fill. Russia promoted Pan-Slavism — the idea that Slavic peoples (Serbs, Bulgarians, Russians) shared a common identity and destiny — as both a genuine ideology and a tool of imperial expansion. Austria-Hungary feared that Serbian nationalism would inspire its own South Slav minorities to break away.

**Key events:**
- **Balkan Wars (1912–1913):** Serbia, Bulgaria, Greece, and Montenegro drove the Ottomans out of most of their remaining European territory in the First Balkan War, then fought each other over the spoils in the Second Balkan War. Serbia emerged enlarged and emboldened.
- **Black Hand:** A Serbian secret nationalist society (formally "Union or Death") linked to the army's officer corps that promoted Greater Serbia by any means, including assassination. Its network reached Bosnian nationalist Gavrilo Princip.
- **Assassination of Franz Ferdinand (June 28, 1914):** Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, was assassinated in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip, a Bosnian Serb nationalist with ties to the Black Hand. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and issued an ultimatum that triggered the alliance system, pulling Europe into World War I.

## AP Significance

Balkan nationalism is the **proximate cause** of WWI in AMSCO's framing — the spark that ignited the underlying tinder of alliance rivalries, imperial competition, and an arms race. On the AP exam, students must distinguish between underlying causes (MAIN: Militarism, Alliance systems, Imperialism, Nationalism) and the immediate trigger (the assassination). Balkan nationalism is the clearest example of how **GOV** theme tensions — the struggle over who governs multiethnic territories — can escalate into global conflict.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP frequently asks students to evaluate the **relative significance** of causes of WWI. Balkan nationalism is the immediate trigger but NOT the root cause — make sure to situate Princip's act within the broader framework of alliance systems and imperial rivalry. Blaming WWI solely on the assassination is a common AP essay error.
