# All Quiet on the Western Front

**All Quiet on the Western Front** (*Im Westen nichts Neues*, 1929) is an anti-war novel by German author **Erich Maria Remarque**, based on his own experience as a young soldier in WWI. It depicts trench warfare on the Western Front from the perspective of a German soldier named Paul Bäumer, following his psychological disintegration as the war destroys his generation.

## Historical Significance

- Published in 1929 — a decade after the war ended — it became one of the fastest-selling books in history
- Adapted into a celebrated 1930 film
- Remarque was a young German soldier during WWI (born 1898); his experience directly informs the narrative
- The Nazis banned and burned the book in 1933 — its anti-war message was antithetical to their militarist ideology
- Represents the cultural response to industrialized warfare: the "Lost Generation" thesis that WWI destroyed young men's faith in civilization, progress, and the state

AMSCO references the novel to illustrate the soldier's experience of trench warfare (p. 470). The novel is evidence for the SIO theme: how total war shattered the idealistic nationalism that had sent young men to enlist in 1914.

## Exam Angle

Use *All Quiet on the Western Front* as evidence for cultural and social effects of WWI — specifically the **disillusionment** of the generation that fought. It contrasts with the pre-war propaganda and patriotic enthusiasm that preceded the conflict, showing the gap between what governments promised and what soldiers experienced.

> [!tip]
> The novel pairs well with Wilfred Owen's poem "Dulce et Decorum Est" (British perspective) — both are anti-war primary sources that the AP uses to illustrate the gap between propaganda's glorification of war and the soldier's actual experience.
