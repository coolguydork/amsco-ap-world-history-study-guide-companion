# Berlin Conference

Meeting held in Berlin (1884–85), hosted by German Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, at which the major European powers negotiated the division of Africa among themselves. No African representatives were invited or consulted. The conference established rules for the "orderly" colonization of Africa — including the principle that a power had to effectively occupy territory to claim it — and guaranteed European nations free navigation on Africa's major rivers (Niger, Congo).

## AP Significance

The Berlin Conference is the defining moment of the **Scramble for Africa**. For AP essays, it represents:

1. **European agency and African exclusion:** The most explicit example of imperial powers treating the African continent as territory to be divided at will, with no acknowledgment of existing African political authority, sovereignty, or boundaries.

2. **Artificial borders:** The conference created colonial borders that cut across ethnic, linguistic, and political lines — dividing unified societies and combining rival groups into single colonies. These borders became sources of conflict when African nations achieved independence in the 20th century.

3. **Great-power competition as a cause of imperialism:** Bismarck hosted the conference to prevent European war over Africa. The conference shows that imperial expansion was driven partly by interstate competition — powers colonized to deny territory to rivals, not only for direct economic benefit.

> [!exam-tip]
> A common AP error: students say "the Berlin Conference *colonized* Africa." It did not. Colonization was already underway; the conference *regulated and formalized* the division to prevent European war. Make sure your essays reflect this distinction.
