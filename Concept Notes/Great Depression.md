# Great Depression

Global economic crisis (1929–1939) triggered by the U.S. stock market crash. Mass unemployment, bank failures, and collapsing commodity prices spread worldwide. Destabilized democratic governments and drove populations toward authoritarian alternatives — [[Concept Notes/Fascism|fascism]] on the right, communism on the left.

## Key Facts

- **Trigger:** U.S. Wall Street Crash, October 1929; credit contraction spread globally
- **Scale:** U.S. unemployment peaked ~25%; similar rates across industrialized world
- **Duration:** 1929–1939; only truly ended with WWII rearmament spending
- **Global reach:** Devastated both industrial economies (Europe, U.S.) and commodity-export colonial economies (Latin America, Africa, Asia)

## Political Consequences

The Depression's most important AP consequence is what it *enabled politically*. In countries with weak democratic traditions, economic despair empowered authoritarian movements:
- **Germany:** Weimar hyperinflation (1920s) + Depression unemployment → Nazi rise (1933)
- **Italy:** Depression deepened Mussolini's already-established fascist state (since 1922)
- **Spain:** Economic and political crisis → Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) → Franco's dictatorship
- **Japan:** Military faction took control; pursued imperial expansion in Asia

In the **United States**, strong democratic institutions and the New Deal preserved democracy — the key contrasting case the AP uses.

## Government Responses

| Country | Response | Key Feature |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| United States | [[Concept Notes/Keynesian Economics\|New Deal]] (FDR) | Deficit spending, public works, banking reform; preserved democracy |
| Soviet Union | Five-Year Plans (Stalin) | Command economy; forced collectivization; rapid industrialization at enormous human cost |
| Italy / Germany | Fascist autarky + rearmament | State-directed economy; military spending ended unemployment; suppressed labor |
| Mexico | Economic nationalism (Cárdenas) | PEMEX oil nationalization; state-led development against foreign imperialism |
