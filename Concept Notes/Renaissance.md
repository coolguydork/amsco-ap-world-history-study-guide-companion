## Definition

The **Renaissance** (French for "rebirth") was a cultural, artistic, and intellectual movement that emerged in Italy in the 14th century and spread across Europe through the 15th and 16th centuries. It was characterized by a renewed interest in classical Greek and Roman thought, a growing emphasis on human potential and reason (*humanism*), and extraordinary achievements in art, literature, science, and philosophy.

## The Renaissance as a Product of Global Exchange

The Renaissance did not emerge from Europe in isolation. In AP World History, it is understood as the **downstream consequence of centuries of cross-cultural knowledge transfer**:

**The chain of transmission:**
1. **Ancient Greece** produced foundational works in philosophy, science, and mathematics (Aristotle, Euclid, etc.)
2. **Islamic scholars** in Baghdad's [[Concept Notes/House of Wisdom|House of Wisdom]] translated and preserved these works in Arabic, then made original contributions (astronomy, trigonometry, medicine)
3. **Al-Andalus (Islamic Spain)** became the conduit: scholars like **Ibn Rushd (Averroes)** wrote Aristotle commentaries that influenced **Maimonides** (Jewish) and then **Thomas Aquinas** (Christian)
4. **Paper-making** (Chinese technology transmitted through the Islamic world) gave Europeans the medium to record and spread ideas
5. **The printing press** (Gutenberg, 1439) — itself enabled by paper — multiplied the reach of Renaissance thought

From [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty|Song Dynasty]]:
> "Chinese farmers... [developed] woodblock printing... Europeans with such diverse skills would be called 'Renaissance men.'" — AMSCO p. 7

## Why It Matters for the AP Exam

The Renaissance sits at the intersection of multiple AP World History themes:

- **Cultural diffusion** — it demonstrates how ideas travel across civilizations over centuries
- **Causation** — the Renaissance *caused* new scientific thinking that enabled the [[Concept Notes/Scientific Revolution|Scientific Revolution]] and eventually European exploration
- **Continuity** — Greek knowledge survived in Islamic libraries when it had been lost in Western Europe
- **Cross-topic connections** — it links Topics 1.1 (Song technology), 1.2 (Islamic Golden Age), and Unit 3 (European state-building and expansion)

> [!exam-tip]
> If an AP essay asks you to explain a cause of European global expansion after 1450, the Renaissance → Scientific Revolution → navigational technology chain is a high-value argument. Trace it back to the Islamic Golden Age and Song Dynasty innovations.
