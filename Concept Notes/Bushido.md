# Bushido

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**Bushido** ("the way of the warrior") was the Japanese samurai code of conduct, emphasizing frugality, loyalty, martial arts mastery, and honor unto death. It governed the behavior and values of Japan's warrior class during and after the feudal period.

---

## Core Values

- **Frugality** — rejection of luxury and excess
- **Loyalty** — absolute devotion to one's lord (daimyo)
- **Martial excellence** — constant cultivation of fighting skill
- **Honor unto death** — preferring death (ritual suicide, *seppuku*) over the dishonor of surrender or defeat

---

## AP Context

Bushido is most important on the AP exam as a **comparison point** with European chivalry. The two warrior codes arose in similar feudal contexts — decentralized societies dependent on a military elite — but reflect distinct cultural priorities.

| Feature | Bushido (Japan) | Chivalry (Europe) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary loyalty | To one's daimyo / lord | To God, king, and countrymen |
| Tone | Austere, martial, ascetic | More romantic; includes "courtly love" |
| Honor | Death before dishonor | Defense of the weak and defenseless |
| Religion | Influenced by Zen Buddhism and Shinto | Grounded in Christianity |

> [!exam-tip]
> When comparing Japanese and European feudalism, always pair the warrior class comparison (samurai vs. knights) with the code comparison (Bushido vs. chivalry). Both reflect the values of the civilization that produced them.
