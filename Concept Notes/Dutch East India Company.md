# Dutch East India Company

Dutch trading company (VOC — *Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie*; chartered 1602) that dominated the Southeast Asian spice trade and established colonial control over much of Indonesia (the Dutch East Indies). The VOC was the world's first multinational corporation and joint-stock company, raising capital from private investors and operating its own armies, navies, courts, and treaty-making authority — effectively acting as a state. At its peak it was the most valuable company in history.

> [!exam-tip]
> The VOC is the AP's premier example of a *joint-stock company* as an instrument of imperialism. It shows how private capital funded colonial expansion, blurring the line between commercial enterprise and state power. On essays about the Age of Exploration or economic imperialism, the VOC demonstrates that European empires were often built by corporations, not just monarchies.

> [!warning]
> Don't confuse the VOC (Dutch East India Company) with the EIC (English/British East India Company). Both were joint-stock companies operating in Asia, but the VOC focused on Southeast Asia (Indonesia, spice islands) while the EIC focused on South Asia (India). Both are valid evidence for the same AP arguments.
