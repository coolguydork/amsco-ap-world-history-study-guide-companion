# Encomienda

## Definition

A Spanish colonial labor system granting a landowner (**encomendero**, typically a conquistador) the legal right to compel indigenous labor in exchange for food, shelter, and Christian instruction. In practice, the system functioned as forced labor with no meaningful compensation and was notorious for extreme brutality. *(AMSCO p. 4.4 Key Terms)*

## How It Worked

After conquest, the Spanish crown distributed encomienda grants to conquistadors as rewards. An encomendero could demand a set number of labor days from indigenous people in his district. Theoretically, in return he provided food, lodging, and religious instruction. In practice, the system was widely and brutally abused — indigenous workers were worked to death in mines, on plantations, and in construction projects.

## The Adapted Mit'a

Spain also transformed the Inca **mit'a** — a communal labor obligation in which villages contributed a portion of labor to public works — into coerced mine labor at Potosí. Villages were required to send a percentage of adult men to work in the silver mines for minimal wages and in lethal conditions. This is a classic AP example of how Europeans **adapted existing indigenous institutions** for colonial exploitation rather than creating systems from scratch. *(AMSCO p. 4.4 Key Terms)*

## Criticism and the New Laws

**Bartolomé de las Casas** (Dominican friar, 1484–1566) documented encomienda abuses in *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1552). His lobbying produced the **New Laws of 1542**, which theoretically limited the encomienda — prohibiting its use as outright slavery and restricting inheritance of encomienda grants. In practice, abuses continued, especially on the northern frontier of New Spain. Las Casas is the AP's primary sourcing figure for indigenous rights advocacy and the limits of colonial reform. *(AMSCO p. 236)*

## Encomienda vs. Other Labor Systems

| System | Region | Who was coerced | Duration | Racial basis |
|--------|--------|-----------------|----------|--------------|
| Encomienda | Spanish Americas | Indigenous Americans | Theoretically limited | Not explicitly racial |
| Mit'a (adapted) | Andes/Peru | Indigenous Americans | Cyclical village obligation | Not explicitly racial |
| Chattel slavery | Americas (plantations) | Enslaved Africans | Lifetime; hereditary | Explicitly racial |
| Indentured servitude | Primarily N. America | Europeans | 5–7 years | Not racial |
| Hacienda system | Spanish Americas | Indigenous Americans | Long-term debt peonage | Not explicitly racial |

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently asks you to compare coercive labor systems. The key distinctions: **encomienda** = indigenous labor, theoretically limited, Spanish Americas; **chattel slavery** = African labor, hereditary, racially defined, all plantation colonies; **indentured servitude** = European labor, time-limited, primarily British North America. Know these three cold.

## Why Encomienda Gave Way to Chattel Slavery

Indigenous workers knew the local terrain and could escape. Epidemic disease killed them faster than they could be replaced. The New Laws (1542) limited encomienda legally. Indentured servants became free after their contracts expired. Enslaved Africans could be held permanently, their children were born enslaved under chattel slavery, and their skin color made escape into European-dominated society harder. The logic was brutal economics. *(Topic 4.4)*

## Long-Term Legacy

Encomienda created the hacienda system — large agricultural estates in which landowners held indigenous workers in **debt peonage** (workers borrowed money from the hacienda store and were legally prevented from leaving until debts were repaid). Debt peonage persisted in parts of Latin America into the 20th century. The racial and economic inequalities of Latin American societies today trace directly to this period.

## AP Exam Threads

**Causation:** Columbian Exchange disease → indigenous population collapse → encomienda labor insufficient → Atlantic slave trade. This is the central causal chain of Topic 4.4.

**Comparison:** Encomienda vs. Ottoman devshirme (both extract labor from subject populations, but devshirme created loyal soldiers/administrators while encomienda created resentment and resistance).

**Continuity:** Coercive labor systems persisted across all periods: serfdom (Unit 1, Unit 4.7) → encomienda/mit'a/chattel slavery (Unit 4) → indentured servitude/debt peonage (Unit 5, 6).

**Evidence for LEQ/DBQ:** Encomienda is strong evidence for arguments about how European states used existing institutions, how colonial economies operated, and how religious justifications were used to mask economic extraction.
