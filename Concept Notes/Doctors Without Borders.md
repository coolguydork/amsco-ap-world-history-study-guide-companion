# Doctors Without Borders

**Also known as:** Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF)
**Founded:** 1971, Paris, France
**Nobel Peace Prize:** 1999

---

## What It Is

Doctors Without Borders (MSF) is an international humanitarian medical organization that provides emergency healthcare in conflict zones, epidemic areas, and regions underserved by state health systems. It operates independently of governments and is funded primarily through private donations, allowing it to work in politically sensitive areas without government interference.

---

## AP Significance

MSF represents the post-1945 proliferation of **non-governmental organizations (NGOs)** as major actors in global humanitarian response — one of the defining features of Period 4 globalization. Where states and intergovernmental organizations cannot or will not act, NGOs like MSF fill critical gaps.

In the context of **Topic 9.2 (Disease)**, MSF treated approximately 1.7 million malaria patients annually in the early 21st century and provided frontline medical care during Ebola outbreaks in West and Central Africa. MSF is the primary example of an NGO operating in global public health.

## Key Characteristics

- Operates in conflict zones where state health systems have collapsed
- Refuses funding from governments whose actions conflict with its humanitarian principles
- Advocates publicly on behalf of affected populations ("témoignage" / bearing witness)
- Provides care regardless of ethnicity, religion, nationality, or political affiliation
