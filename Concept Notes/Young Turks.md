# Young Turks

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

The **Young Turks** (formally the Committee of Union and Progress, or CUP) were a coalition of Ottoman reformers and intellectuals who advocated for constitutional government, modernization, and a stronger, unified Ottoman state in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

---

## Background

Sultan **Abdulhamid II** (r. 1876–1909) initially accepted the Ottoman constitution of 1876 but quickly suspended it and ruled as an autocrat. Fearful of reform movements as threats to his power, he drove the Young Turk reformers into exile — where they continued to organize and publish.

---

## The 1908 Revolution

The Young Turks staged a successful revolt in 1908, forcing Abdulhamid to restore the constitution. When he attempted a counter-coup in 1909, they deposed him entirely and installed a pliant sultan while the CUP governed from behind the scenes. This was the end of the Tanzimat era and the beginning of a new — though short-lived — constitutional period.

---

## Significance

The Young Turks represent a second, more radical wave of Ottoman reform — driven not by sultans from above but by secular nationalist reformers from below. Their movement shows that the limits of the [[Concept Notes/Tanzimat Reforms|Tanzimat]] reforms eventually produced an organized political opposition.

The CUP governed the Ottoman Empire during World War I and was responsible for the **Armenian Genocide (1915–1916)** — a continuation, at a far greater scale, of the ethnic violence that began under Abdulhamid's Hamidian Massacres.
