# Universal Declaration of Human Rights

**Adopted:** December 10, 1948 by the United Nations General Assembly
**Drafted by:** UN Commission on Human Rights, chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt
**Scope:** 30 articles articulating fundamental rights for all people regardless of race, sex, language, religion, political opinion, national origin, or social status

The UDHR established for the first time that human rights were an **international responsibility**, not merely a domestic matter. It was a direct response to the Holocaust and WWII atrocities — an attempt to ensure "never again" through a binding normative framework.

**Core rights articulated:**
- Right to life, liberty, and security of person
- Freedom from torture and slavery
- Right to a fair trial
- Freedom of thought, conscience, and religion
- Freedom of expression and association
- Right to education and participation in cultural life
- Right to social security and an adequate standard of living

**AP Significance:** The UDHR is the foundational document of the modern human rights era. It gave activists worldwide a shared language and legal standard with which to challenge their governments. It directly enabled: anti-apartheid pressure on South Africa, women's rights conventions (CEDAW, 1979), and the creation of international courts (ICJ, ICC).
