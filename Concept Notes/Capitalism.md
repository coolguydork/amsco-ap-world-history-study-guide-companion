# Capitalism

An economic system in which private individuals or companies own the means of production and compete in open markets for profit. Replaced [[Concept Notes/Mercantilism|Mercantilism]] as the dominant economic framework in Western Europe during the Industrial Age (c. 1750–1900).

**Intellectual foundation:** Adam Smith, *Wealth of Nations* (1776) — argued that individuals pursuing private profit in competitive markets produce general prosperity through the "invisible hand." Smith's ideas justified reducing state control over trade and production.

**Key features:** private property, free markets, wage labor, profit motive, reinvestment of capital.

**AP significance:** Capitalism is the central economic *change* of Unit 5. The shift from mercantilism (state-regulated, surplus-focused) to capitalism (market-driven, profit-focused) restructured who held economic power and how goods were produced. Industrial capitalists who owned factories replaced landed aristocrats at the top of the social hierarchy.

**Critiques:** Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels (*The Communist Manifesto*, 1848) argued that capitalism structurally exploited the proletariat, concentrating wealth in the hands of the bourgeoisie. Their analysis inspired socialism, labor unions, and 20th-century communist revolutions.
