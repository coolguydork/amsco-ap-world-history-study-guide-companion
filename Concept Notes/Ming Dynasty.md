# Ming Dynasty

Chinese dynasty (1368–1644) founded by Zhu Yuanzhang after expelling the Mongol Yuan rulers; restored Confucian governance, rebuilt the bureaucracy, and constructed much of the Great Wall. The early Ming sponsored Zheng He's massive maritime expeditions (1405–1433) into the Indian Ocean — seven voyages reaching Arabia and East Africa — before abruptly withdrawing from oceanic exploration and turning inward. Ming China was the world's largest economy for most of this period, attracting massive silver inflows from the Americas via the Manila galleon trade.

> [!exam-tip]
> The Ming appear in at least three AP essay contexts: (1) post-Mongol recovery and Neo-Confucian restoration; (2) Zheng He's voyages as evidence of early Chinese maritime capability — and their cancellation as a pivot point in world history; (3) the silver trade, where American silver flowed to China to pay for silk and porcelain, integrating the Ming economy into the first global trade network.

> [!warning]
> Don't confuse the cancellation of Zheng He's voyages with Chinese isolation. The Ming continued extensive land-based and coastal trade; they simply chose *not* to build an overseas empire. This is a deliberate policy choice, not geographic isolation.
