# Nationalism

## Definition

Nationalism: the belief that people who share a common culture, language, ethnicity, or history constitute a "nation" and deserve political self-governance. One of the most powerful forces in modern world history; both created and destroyed empires in the 19th–20th centuries.

## Types of Nationalism (AP Comparison Framework)

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Liberal nationalism** | Self-determination + democratic governance | Indian National Congress (early), Korean Declaration of Independence |
| **Ethnic/racial nationalism** | Nation defined by blood/race; often exclusionary | Nazi Germany, Japanese hypernationalism |
| **Cultural nationalism** | Nation defined by shared culture/language | Pan-Arabism, Négritude, Turkish nationalism under Atatürk |
| **Communist nationalism** | Anti-imperial revolution + Marxist state | CCP (China), Ho Chi Minh (Vietnam) |
| **Religious nationalism** | Nation defined by faith community | Muslim League / Pakistan, Zionism |

## AP Key Tensions

**Self-determination vs. empire:** WWI's aftermath showed that self-determination was applied selectively — to white Europeans but not to colonized peoples. This hypocrisy fueled anti-colonial nationalism.

**Unity vs. partition:** Nationalism often fractures rather than unites. Indian independence produced Hindu-India and Muslim-Pakistan. Arab nationalism coexisted with distinct Syrian, Egyptian, Iraqi national identities. Chinese nationalism split into CCP and KMT.

**Nationalism and violence:** Nationalist movements were not inherently nonviolent. Gandhi's nonviolent approach was exceptional; most nationalist movements involved armed struggle at some point.

## AP Essay Angle

Nationalism is almost always testable as a **cause** (of WWI, WWII, decolonization, partition) or as a **response** (to imperialism, to economic crisis, to perceived humiliation). Know your examples by region and type so you can deploy them flexibly on any LEQ or DBQ.
