# Corporation

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

A **corporation** is a business entity chartered by the government as a legal "person" — it can own property, make contracts, and be sued, independent of its individual owners. Its defining innovation was **limited liability**: stockholders can lose only what they invested, not their personal assets. Corporations became the dominant form of business organization during the Industrial Revolution.

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 325–328)

> "A corporation is a business chartered by a government as a legal entity owned by stockholders (individuals who buy partial ownership directly from the company when it is formed or later through a stock market). Stockholders might receive sums of money, known as dividends, from a corporation when it makes a profit. If a corporation experiences a loss or goes bankrupt, the stockholders are not liable for the losses. The most that stockholders can lose is what they paid for the stock in the first place."

> "A corporation differs from [sole proprietorship and partnership] in that a corporation is a more flexible structure for large-scale economic activity. It replaced the traditional system of a single entrepreneur engaging in high-risk business endeavors with a system of larger companies, collectively engaging in lower-risk efforts. By spreading risk, investments became much safer and more attractive."

## Four Key Features (AMSCO p. 326)

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Limited Liability** | Investors risk only their investment; not personally liable for losses |
| **Transferability of Shares** | Ownership (stock) can be bought and sold freely; attracts more investors |
| **Legal Personality** | The corporation can make contracts, be sued, and hold property as an independent entity |
| **Perpetual Existence** | The corporation survives changes in ownership |

## Why It Matters for AP

The corporation's limited liability structure is the mechanism that made the Industrial Revolution's scale possible. Without it, no individual investor would risk personal ruin on a transcontinental railroad or a global mining operation. With it, thousands of small investors could collectively fund projects of enormous scale — and some corporations grew large enough to form [[monopoly|monopolies]] and operate as [[transnational companies]].

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP exam, always connect the corporation's *mechanism* (limited liability → pooled capital → lower risk) to its *consequence* (industrial-scale enterprises, transnational companies, economic imperialism). The corporation is not just a business form — it is the institutional engine of 19th-century global capitalism.
