# Manchu

Tungusic ethnic group from northeastern China (Manchuria) who conquered the Ming Dynasty to establish the Qing Dynasty (1644–1912), the last imperial dynasty of China. The Manchu were a minority ruling over a vast Han Chinese majority, which shaped their governance strategy: they maintained the Confucian bureaucratic system and Chinese administrative structures, but reserved top military positions for Manchu elites and required Han men to wear the queue (a Manchu hairstyle) as a sign of submission.

> [!exam-tip]
> The Manchu/Qing conquest is a great AP comparison case for how outside conquerors governed large empires. Compare them to the Mongols (who also ruled China but were far less willing to adopt Chinese customs) — the Manchu's selective adaptation of Chinese culture is a key reason the Qing lasted nearly three centuries while the Yuan lasted less than one.

> [!tip]
> "Manchu" refers to the ethnic group; "Qing" refers to the dynasty they founded. Use both terms correctly — the AP does.
