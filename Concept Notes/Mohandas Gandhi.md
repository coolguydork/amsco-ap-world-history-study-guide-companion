# Mohandas Gandhi

## Who He Was

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948): Indian lawyer, activist, and leader of the independence movement against British rule. Known as *Mahatma* ("great soul") by Indians. Developed his philosophy of nonviolent resistance (*satyagraha*) in South Africa before returning to India.

## Where He Appears

- **Topic 6.3 (Indigenous Responses to State Expansion)** — Gandhi's South Africa years (1893–1914): fought racial discrimination as a lawyer; founded the Natal Indian Congress; first developed satyagraha as a method
- **[[Unit-7.5/Unresolved Tensions After World War I Overview\|Topic 7.5]]** — India independence movement: leads Indian National Congress as a mass movement; Amritsar Massacre (1919) radicalizes India; Salt March (1930); opposed partition with Pakistan

## Core Concepts

**Satyagraha ("devotion-to-truth"):** Nonviolent resistance to unjust laws; participants willingly accept arrest and punishment to expose colonial injustice to the world. The strategy assumes that the oppressor has a conscience that can be stirred, and that international public opinion matters.

**Civil disobedience:** Breaking unjust laws openly, accepting consequences, refusing to cooperate with an unjust system. Gandhi's genius was making this a *mass* movement, not just an elite intellectual stance.

**Boycott of British goods:** Economic pressure combined with cultural assertion — wearing homespun Indian cloth instead of British-manufactured textiles was both protest and identity statement.

## AP Significance

Gandhi represents the most studied example of nonviolent anti-colonial resistance on the AP exam. Key arguments to know:

1. **Method matters:** Gandhi's approach was not passive — it required enormous courage and discipline. It worked by making British repression visible and internationally embarrassing.
2. **Limits of nonviolence:** Gandhi could not bridge the Hindu-Muslim divide; Jinnah's two-state solution prevailed, producing partition and mass communal violence in 1947.
3. **Global influence:** Gandhi's methods directly influenced the U.S. Civil Rights Movement (MLK), anti-apartheid movements, and other 20th-century liberation struggles.

## AP Essay Angle

Use Gandhi as your primary example for: (1) nonviolent anti-colonial resistance; (2) the role of charismatic leadership in nationalist movements; (3) the tension between unity and partition in decolonization; (4) cross-cultural transfer of resistance methods (satyagraha → MLK).
