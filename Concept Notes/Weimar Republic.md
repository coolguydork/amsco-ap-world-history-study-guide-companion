# Weimar Republic

Germany's democratic government established after WWI (1919–1933). Named for the city of Weimar where its constitution was drafted. It replaced the monarchy of Kaiser Wilhelm II following Germany's defeat.

**Why it matters for AP:** The Weimar Republic is the bridge between WWI and WWII — its instability explains how Hitler legally came to power. The AP exam frequently asks about the conditions that enabled fascism to rise in democratic states.

## Key Facts

- Accepted the Treaty of Versailles (1919), making it politically toxic from day one — many Germans associated it with national humiliation and the "stab in the back" myth
- Required to pay enormous war reparations; the resulting hyperinflation (early 1920s) devastated the middle class
- The Great Depression (1929–) caused mass unemployment; by 1932 over 6 million Germans were jobless
- Political extremism grew on both left (Communists) and right (Nazis) as the Republic appeared unable to govern
- The Nazis won enough seats in the 1932 elections for President Hindenburg to appoint Hitler chancellor in January 1933 — a legal appointment, not a coup
- Once in power, the Nazis used the Reichstag fire (February 1933) to suspend civil liberties and ban opposition parties
- The Weimar Republic was formally dissolved when Hitler merged the offices of chancellor and president in 1934
