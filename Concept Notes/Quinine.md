# Quinine

Medicine derived from the bark of the cinchona tree (native to South America) used to treat malaria. Its widespread adoption by European armies and colonizers in the 19th century dramatically reduced mortality from malaria in tropical regions, especially sub-Saharan Africa. Before quinine became widely available, the African interior — where malaria was endemic — was effectively off-limits to European colonizers, who died in vast numbers from the disease. Quinine removed this biological barrier, making the Scramble for Africa's rapid interior penetration possible.

## AP Significance

Quinine is a high-value **enabling cause** of 19th-century imperialism. On the AP exam, it appears in causation questions: "Explain ONE reason why European colonization of Africa accelerated in the late 19th century." Quinine + steamships + industrial weapons = the technological package that made the Scramble for Africa possible at unprecedented speed.

It also illustrates the **irony of colonial medicine**: a medicine derived from South American indigenous knowledge (cinchona bark was used by Andean peoples) enabled the European conquest of Africa. The Americas' biological contribution to European imperialism parallels the earlier exchange in which American crops fueled European population growth.
