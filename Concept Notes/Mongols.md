## Who Were the Mongols?

The Mongols were a group of nomadic pastoralists from the **steppes of Central Asia**. Between 1206 (unification under Genghis Khan) and the mid-13th century, they built the **largest land empire in human history**, stretching from Central Europe to the Pacific Ocean.

## Role in Unit 1 (c. 1200–c. 1450)

**Destruction**: The Mongols conquered and destroyed the **Abbasid Caliphate** in **1258**, burning Baghdad. They ended Seljuk rule in the Middle East. They devastated Kievan Rus in the 13th century.

**Stopped**: The Mamluks stopped the Mongol advance at **Egypt**. The Japanese repelled two Mongol naval invasions (1274, 1281). The Delhi Sultanate resisted Mongol conquest.

**Connection**: After conquest, the **Pax Mongolica** (Mongol Peace) allowed unprecedented trade and communication across Eurasia. Chinese technologies (gunpowder, paper, printing) spread westward. Marco Polo traveled to China. First direct contacts between Europe and China since the classical period occurred.

**Song Dynasty**: The Mongols conquered Song China in 1279, ending that dynasty. They established the Yuan Dynasty (1271–1368), later replaced by the **Ming Dynasty**.

## AP Significance

The Mongols are one of the most frequently tested subjects in AP World History. Key themes:
- **Destruction and connectivity**: How does conquest lead to cultural and commercial exchange?
- **Nomadic vs. settled**: The Mongol empire represents the last great expansion of nomadic power; after 1450, settled states with gunpowder begin to dominate
- **Technology transfer**: Gunpowder from China spread westward via Mongol networks
