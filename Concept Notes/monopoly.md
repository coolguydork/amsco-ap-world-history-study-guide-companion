# Monopoly

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

A **monopoly** exists when a single [[corporation]] controls a specific industry or market, eliminating competition. Industrial-era monopolies grew from corporations that leveraged superior technology, capital, and scale to drive out all rivals. They concentrated enormous wealth and political power in the hands of a single firm.

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 325–328)

> "Some corporations became so powerful that they could form a monopoly, control of a specific business and elimination of all competition. For example, Alfred Krupp of Essen, Germany, ran a gigantic company that used the Bessemer process, a more efficient way to produce steel, gaining a monopoly in the German steel industry. In the United States, John D. Rockefeller created a monopoly in the oil industry."

> "With their growth, corporations gained great economic and political power. For example, the decision by a corporation about where to build a new factory could create thousands of new jobs for a community."

## Key Examples

| Monopolist | Country | Industry | Method |
|-----------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Alfred Krupp** | Germany | Steel | Bessemer process; military contracts |
| **John D. Rockefeller** | United States | Oil (Standard Oil) | Horizontal integration; price warfare |
| **Cecil Rhodes / De Beers** | Britain / South Africa | Diamonds | Colonial land control; state backing |

> [!warning]
> A monopoly is control of a *specific* market — not the entire economy. Rockefeller monopolized oil refining; Krupp monopolized German steel. Competition continued in other sectors. Don't overstate monopoly as the elimination of all market activity.

## AP Exam Significance

Monopolies appear on the AP exam as examples of how industrial capitalism concentrated power, fueled economic imperialism (De Beers), and generated political backlash (labor movements, socialist critiques). They are also linked to [[transnational companies]], since the most powerful monopolists often operated across national borders.
