## What Is Sufism?

**Sufism** is a mystical movement within Islam that emphasizes **direct inner experience of the divine** over intellectual study of scripture. Sufis believed in truths that could not be understood through formal learning alone — only through introspection, meditation, and devotion.

Sufism may have originated as a reaction to the perceived luxury of early **Umayyad rulers**, who Sufis saw as having strayed from the spiritual simplicity of Islam's origins.

## Why Sufis Were Effective Missionaries

Sufis played a critically important role in the **spread of Islam** across Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia. Their effectiveness rested on several strategies:

1. **Cultural adaptation**: Sufis tended to adopt local customs and weave local religious elements into Islamic practice. Converts could become Muslims without feeling they had abandoned their traditions.
2. **Emotional accessibility**: Sufi practice emphasized devotional emotion and personal spiritual experience — more accessible than formal scholarship.
3. **Egalitarianism**: Sufism appealed to people of all social classes, including those at the margins of society.

## Comparison: Sufism and the Bhakti Movement

Sufism and the **[[Concept Notes/Bhakti Movement|Bhakti Movement]]** in Hinduism are a classic AP comparison pair:

| Feature | Sufism | Bhakti Movement |
|---------|--------|-----------------|
| Religion | Islam | Hinduism |
| Focus | Mystical inner experience; divine love | Emotional devotion to a personal deity |
| Social accessibility | Open to all classes | Did not discriminate by caste or gender |
| Role in spreading religion | Key missionary movement | Helped spread Hinduism |
| Famous figure | ʿĀʾisha al-Bāʿūniyya (poet) | Mira Bai (poet) |

Both movements were mystical, egalitarian, and effective at spreading their faiths — partly *because* they were not rigidly orthodox.
