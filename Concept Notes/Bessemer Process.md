# Bessemer Process

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

The **Bessemer Process** (1856) was a revolutionary steelmaking technique that made cheap mass production of steel possible for the first time, launching the **Second Industrial Revolution**. Invented by Henry Bessemer, the process involved blasting molten iron with air — burning off impurities and producing steel, an alloy of iron and carbon that is both stronger and more workable than iron.

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 311–312)

> "The mass production of steel, an alloy of iron and carbon, became possible with the introduction of the Bessemer Process in 1856. This process involved blasting the molten metal with air as a means of removing impurities as well as helping keep the metal from solidifying. Over the years, Bessemer's innovation was refined and improved, allowing steel to become the strong and versatile backbone of the industrial society."

## From the Vault

**From [[Unit-5.5/The Two Industrial Revolutions]] (Topic 5.5):**
> "The Bessemer Process (1856) was the breakthrough. By blasting molten iron with air, impurities burned off and the metal remained workable while becoming enormously stronger. Steel is an alloy of iron and carbon — it combines iron's strength with workability that cast iron lacked."

> "Results of cheap steel: Railroads — steel rails lasted far longer than iron rails and could support heavier loads. Skyscrapers — steel frames allowed buildings to rise many stories. Battleships — steel-hulled warships replaced wooden and iron-hulled ones. Bridges — steel enabled longer, stronger spans (Brooklyn Bridge, 1883)."

> "Steel production became a measure of national industrial power. By 1900, Germany and the United States had both surpassed Britain in steel output — a signal of shifting global dominance."

## Why It Matters

Steel was to the Second Industrial Revolution what coal/steam was to the First. The Bessemer Process made steel cheap enough to use everywhere — it became the structural material of modern civilization.

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP exam, don't confuse iron (First Industrial Revolution) with steel (Second Industrial Revolution). Iron production was the backbone of Britain's early industrial dominance. Steel production, made possible by the Bessemer Process, was where Germany and the United States overtook Britain. This shift in steel leadership foreshadows the shift in global industrial dominance heading into the 20th century.
