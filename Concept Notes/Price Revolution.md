# Price Revolution

## Definition

The sustained high inflation that swept Europe during the 16th and early 17th centuries, caused primarily by the massive influx of American silver from Potosí (Bolivia) and Zacatecas (Mexico) into European markets, compounded by population growth. *(AMSCO p. 233)*

## Causes

*(Topics 4.4, 4.5)*

1. **American silver flood** — Potosí alone produced enormous quantities of silver from the 1540s onward; Spanish galleons transported it to Seville and from there into European trade circuits
2. **Population growth** — 16th-century European population recovery after the Black Death increased demand for goods without a corresponding increase in supply
3. **Mercury amalgamation technology** — dramatically increased silver extraction efficiency at Potosí, accelerating the bullion flood

## Mechanism

More silver in circulation meant each unit of silver bought less. Prices for grain, land, and manufactured goods rose sharply across Europe — in some areas tripling or quadrupling over the century. This is the classic inflation dynamic applied to an entire continent.

## Effects

| Group | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| Spanish crown | Short-term windfall; long-term weakening as military costs outpaced silver revenue |
| European merchants | Profits from price arbitrage; incentive to invest in trade ventures |
| Peasants and wage-earners | Real wages fell as prices outpaced wages |
| Landlords with fixed rents | Lost real income; some were ruined |
| Investors in joint-stock companies | Benefited from commercial expansion funded by circulating silver |

**Spain's paradox:** Spain received the most American silver but benefited least in the long run — it spent the silver on military campaigns and imported luxury goods rather than investing in domestic industry, leaving it economically weaker by 1600 than the Netherlands or England.

## Connection to Global Silver Trade

American silver did not stay in Europe. It flowed along two major routes:

1. **East across the Pacific (Manila Galleon trade)** — Spanish ships from Acapulco carried silver to Manila, where it was traded for Chinese silk, porcelain, and spices; China's tax system demanded silver, making it the ultimate destination
2. **East through Levantine trade** — silver moved through Ottoman and Mughal commercial networks

This is why the Price Revolution is a global phenomenon, not just a European one — American silver linked the hemispheres economically for the first time. *(Topic 4.8 — CCOT)*

> [!tip]
> The Price Revolution is evidence for the AP argument that the period 1450–1750 saw the creation of the **first truly global economy**: American silver connected the Western Hemisphere to China via the Pacific, and to Europe and the Islamic world via the Atlantic.

## AP Exam Threads

**Causation:** Spanish silver extraction (Topic 4.4) → Price Revolution → incentive for commercial innovation (joint-stock companies, financial instruments) → Commercial Revolution (Topic 4.5).

**Unintended consequences:** Spain did not intend to inflate European prices or accelerate Dutch and English commercial development. The Price Revolution demonstrates how economic forces produce outcomes no actor planned.

**CCOT:** Before 1492, European economies were relatively self-contained. The Price Revolution is the first major sign of American economic integration with Afro-Eurasia.
