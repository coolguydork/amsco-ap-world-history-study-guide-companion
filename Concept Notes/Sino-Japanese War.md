# Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895)

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

The **Sino-Japanese War** (1894–1895) was fought between Qing China and Meiji Japan over influence in Korea. Japan's decisive victory shocked the world — a non-Western, recently modernized state had defeated a large, historically dominant empire. The war's consequences reshaped East Asian politics and triggered a crisis in Chinese reform efforts.

---

## Background

Both China and Japan had interests in Korea, which was nominally a Chinese tributary state. After a Korean rebellion gave both powers an excuse to send troops, war broke out. Japan's modernized military — rebuilt along Prussian lines after 1868 — proved dramatically superior to China's forces, which had been "strengthened" by the [[Concept Notes/Self-Strengthening Movement]] but not fundamentally restructured.

---

## Key Outcomes

- **Treaty of Shimonoseki (1895):** China ceded Taiwan and the Liaodong Peninsula to Japan, paid a massive indemnity, and recognized Korean independence (ending Chinese tributary influence there)
- Japan emerged as Asia's first modern industrial-military power
- China's humiliation energized reformers: the **[[Concept Notes/Hundred Days of Reform]]** (1898) followed directly

---

## Why This Matters for the AP Exam

The Sino-Japanese War is the **clearest evidence** of the consequences of reform vs. non-reform. Japan had restructured its institutions (Meiji Restoration, 1868); China had only modernized its technology (Self-Strengthening Movement). The war's outcome proved that technology without institutional reform was insufficient.

> [!exam-tip]
> Use the Sino-Japanese War as **causation evidence** in essays: it caused the Hundred Days of Reform (1898), which failed, which contributed to the Boxer Rebellion (1900) and ultimately the 1911 Revolution.

---

## Forward Connections

- **1895 → Hundred Days of Reform (1898)** — reformers galvanized by defeat
- **1895 → Japanese imperial expansion** — Japan gains Taiwan, expands into Pacific
- **1905 → Russo-Japanese War** — Japan defeats Russia (another shock to European powers)
- **1931 → Japanese invasion of Manchuria** — seeds of aggression planted here
- **1937–1945 → Second Sino-Japanese War** — the 19th-century rivalry continues in catastrophic form
