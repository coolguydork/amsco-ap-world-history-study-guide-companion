# Socialism

Political-economic system advocating collective or government ownership of the means of production to eliminate exploitation and reduce inequality. Broader than communism: socialism includes a spectrum from democratic socialism (gradual reform through elections) to Marxist socialism (revolutionary overthrow of capitalism). Key thinkers include Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels (*Communist Manifesto*, 1848), who argued capitalism's contradictions would inevitably produce a working-class revolution.

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP, socialism appears as a *reaction* to industrialization — the answer to the question "what did workers and intellectuals do in response to the suffering of the Industrial Revolution?" Use it alongside labor unions, strikes, and anarchism as evidence that industrialization generated organized resistance, not just passive suffering.

> [!tip]
> Know the spectrum: utopian socialism (early idealists like Owen, Saint-Simon) → Marxist socialism (scientific, revolutionary) → democratic socialism (reformist, parliamentary) → communism (Marxist-Leninist state ownership). The AP distinguishes between these, especially when comparing early socialist movements with 20th-century Soviet communism.
