# Meiji Restoration

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

The **Meiji Restoration** (1868) was the political revolution in Japan that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate and "restored" the emperor to power — in practice launching Japan's systematic top-down modernization program. Named for Emperor Meiji ("Enlightened Rule"). It is one of the most tested events on the AP World History exam.

---

## Key Facts (AMSCO pp. 319–321)

- **Trigger**: U.S. Commodore Matthew Perry sailed into Edo (Tokyo) Bay in **1853**, forcing Japan to open trade. The display of industrial military power shocked Japan's leaders, who feared the fate China had suffered after the [[Opium War]].
- **Political change**: Reform-minded samurai (from Satsuma and Choshu domains) overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate in **1868** and declared imperial rule restored under Emperor Meiji.
- **Founding document**: The **Charter Oath (1868)** formally abolished feudalism, declared equality before the law, and committed Japan to seeking knowledge from around the world.
- **Government structure**: Constitutional monarchy modeled on **Prussia** — a strong emperor ruling through an oligarchy of elder statesmen (**genro**).
- **Duration of transformation**: Japan went from feudal isolation to industrial great power in roughly **40 years** — the fastest modernization in modern history.

---

## Reform Program at a Glance

| Sector | Reform |
|---|---|
| Military | Reorganized on Prussian model; universal conscription; modern navy |
| Education | New national school system; emphasis on technical fields; students sent abroad |
| Economy | Government subsidized key industries (tea, silk, weaponry, shipbuilding, sake) |
| Infrastructure | Railroads and roads built nationwide; telegraph networks |
| Finance | High **agricultural tax** funded all of the above |
| Social | Feudalism abolished; samurai class legally dissolved (1871) |
| Business | Rise of [[zaibatsu]] — family conglomerates that partnered with the state |

---

## Results

- **Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895)**: Japan defeated China — proof that Meiji modernization had succeeded where China's Self-Strengthening Movement had failed.
- **Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905)**: Japan defeated Russia — the first time a non-Western nation defeated a European great power in modern history. Settled by the **Treaty of Portsmouth** (mediated by President Theodore Roosevelt).
- Japan became the **first non-Western industrial great power** and built its own empire (Taiwan, later Korea).

---

## Social Costs

The Meiji Restoration also replicated industrial society's problems (AMSCO p. 320):
- Female textile workers were exploited in ways mirroring British mill workers
- Peasants bore the heaviest tax burden
- The samurai class — Japan's dominant social group for centuries — was dismantled. Some transitioned to genro roles; others resisted violently. The last samurai battle was fought in the 1870s.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam loves the comparison: **Japan (Meiji) vs. China (Self-Strengthening Movement)**. Japan succeeded because it pursued *comprehensive institutional reform* — not just technology, but government, military, education, and social structure. China tried to graft technology onto an unreformed system. The Meiji Restoration is also a strong example for **causation** (Perry's arrival → Meiji) and **CCOT** (feudal → industrial in 40 years).
