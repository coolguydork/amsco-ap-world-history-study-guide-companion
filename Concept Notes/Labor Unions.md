# Labor Unions

Worker associations that used collective action — strikes, collective bargaining, work stoppages — to win better wages, shorter hours, and improved working conditions from factory owners. Emerged in industrializing Britain in the early 19th century and spread throughout industrializing nations by 1900.

**AP significance:** Labor unions represent a key social and political *change* of the Industrial Age. For the first time, wage laborers organized across individual workplaces to challenge the power of industrial capitalists as a class. Their formation demonstrates that industrialization produced organized resistance, not just passive suffering.

**Key mechanism — collective bargaining:** Unions negotiate wages, hours, and conditions on behalf of all workers in a workplace or industry. Individual workers had no bargaining power; organized workers could threaten production shutdowns.

**Political impact:** Labor unions became the organizational basis for labor parties (Britain's Labour Party, Germany's SPD) that advocated for minimum wages, shorter workdays, paid leave, and social insurance — contributing to the political reforms of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

**Limitation:** Union membership was largely restricted to male workers throughout this period; women's labor, though essential to industrial production, was often excluded from union organizing.
