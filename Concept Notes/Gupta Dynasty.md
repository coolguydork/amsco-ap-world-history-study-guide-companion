# Gupta Dynasty

> [!summary] Source-built concept note

## Overview

The Gupta Dynasty (c. 320–550 CE) ruled much of northern India and is often called India's "Golden Age" or "Classical Era." It represented one of the rare moments of relative political unity in South Asian history. Under Gupta rule, Hinduism and Sanskrit culture flourished, and significant advances were made in mathematics (the concept of zero, the decimal system, algebra), astronomy, literature, and medicine.

The Gupta collapse in **550 CE** — caused by internal decline and invasions by the Huna (Hephthalite) peoples — ended this period of unity and triggered over 1,000 years of political fragmentation that defines the conditions of **Topic 1.3**.

## Why It Matters for the AP Exam

The Gupta Dynasty serves primarily as **contextualization** — the "before" that explains the decentralized, contested political landscape of Period 1 (1200–1450). When AP prompts ask you to contextualize developments in South Asia, the Gupta collapse and the cultural legacy it left behind is the standard starting point.

**Key legacy items to know:**
- Caste system (*jati*) as a social organizing force — carried forward through all subsequent eras
- Hinduism and Sanskrit as cultural unifiers across a politically fragmented region
- Indian mathematics and science — transmitted to the Islamic world and eventually to Europe via Arab scholars (referenced in Topic 1.3)
