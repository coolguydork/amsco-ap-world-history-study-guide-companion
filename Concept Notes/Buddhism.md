# Buddhism

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**Buddhism** is a religion and philosophy originating in northern India in the 5th–4th century BCE, founded on the teachings of Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha, "the Enlightened One"). It spread across Asia via the [[Unit-2.1/Silk Roads Overview|Silk Roads]] and Indian Ocean networks to become one of the world's major religions.

---

## Core Teachings

- **Four Noble Truths**: (1) Life involves suffering; (2) suffering stems from desire/craving; (3) eliminating desire leads to liberation; (4) the Eightfold Path is the way to liberation
- **Eightfold Path**: right speech, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and related ethical commitments
- **Nirvana**: liberation from the cycle of rebirth (samsara); the goal of Buddhist practice
- **No permanent self (anatta)**: contrast with Hindu belief in an eternal soul (atman)

---

## Three Major Forms in AP World History

| Form | Focus | Strongest Region |
|---|---|---|
| **Theravada** ("Way of the Elders") | Personal spiritual growth through silent meditation and self-discipline | Southeast Asia (Topic 1.3) |
| **Mahayana** ("Greater Vehicle") | Spiritual growth for all beings; emphasizes community and service | China, Korea (Topic 1.1) |
| **Tibetan Buddhism** | Chanting and ritual; merged with local Bon religion | Tibet |

> [!exam-tip]
> Know which form spread where. Theravada → Southeast Asia. Mahayana → East Asia (China, Korea, Japan). This distinction appears on AP comparison and continuity/change questions.

## Spread and Cultural Diffusion

Buddhism spread through deliberate missionary activity, trade networks, and royal patronage:

- **India → Central Asia → China**: via the Silk Roads during the Han and Tang dynasties
- **Tang Dynasty monk Xuanzang** (7th century) traveled from China to India to retrieve Buddhist texts — a famous example of religious pilgrimage driving cultural diffusion
- **India → Southeast Asia**: via Indian Ocean trade routes; Theravada became dominant in mainland Southeast Asia
- **China → Korea → Japan**: Buddhist texts and monks traveled with tributary and trade contacts

---

## Buddhism and Cultural Synthesis

Buddhism's ability to adapt to local traditions made it a powerful force for **syncretism**:

- In China, Buddhist missionaries translated *dharma* as *dao* ("the way"), connecting Buddhism to [[Concept Notes/Daoism|Daoism]]. Over time this fusion produced **Chan Buddhism** (called Zen in Japan)
- **Neo-Confucianism** absorbed Buddhist metaphysical ideas while emphasizing Confucian ethics → see [[Concept Notes/Neo-Confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]]
- In Japan, Buddhism coexisted with [[Concept Notes/Shinto|Shinto]] rather than replacing it; Prince Shotoku Taishi promoted both

---

## AP Context: Religion as a Unifying Force

In Period 1 (c. 1200–c. 1450), Buddhism functioned as a **trans-regional civilizational glue**, similar to the role of Islam in Dar al-Islam and Christianity in Europe:

- Provided a shared moral framework and cultural identity across diverse polities
- Buddhist monasteries served as centers of learning, art, and economic activity
- Buddhist networks connected merchants, monks, and rulers across Asia
