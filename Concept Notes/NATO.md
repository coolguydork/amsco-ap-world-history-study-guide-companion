# NATO

**North Atlantic Treaty Organization** — Western military alliance formed April 1949; collective defense commitment (attack on one = attack on all); the first permanent US peacetime military alliance and cornerstone of Cold War Western strategy.

## Origin and Purpose

NATO was formed by 12 Western nations (Belgium, Canada, Denmark, France, Great Britain, Iceland, Italy, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, US) in response to Soviet domination of Eastern Europe after WWII. It institutionalized the US commitment to European security — a permanent break from the pre-WWII tradition of US isolationism.

Its central mechanism: Article 5, the collective defense clause, treating an attack on any member as an attack on all. This deterred Soviet conventional aggression against Western Europe throughout the Cold War.

## Cold War Role

NATO was the institutional backbone of the US **containment** strategy. It provided the military framework for stationing US troops in Europe, coordinating Western defense planning, and incorporating West Germany (1955) into the Western alliance — a move the Soviets answered by founding the **Warsaw Pact** (1955).

France withdrew from NATO's integrated military command in 1966 (though remaining a political member), reflecting tensions within the alliance over US dominance.

## AP Exam Significance

NATO is the AP's primary example of a **Cold War military alliance** and **US containment** in action. Common exam angles:
- Why did the US form NATO? (containment; end of isolationism; Soviet threat to Western Europe)
- How did the Warsaw Pact respond to NATO? (mirror alliance; Soviet control of Eastern Europe)
- Why were SEATO and CENTO less effective than NATO? (less cohesive membership; no equivalent Article 5 automaticity)
