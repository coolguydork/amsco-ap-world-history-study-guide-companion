# Bourgeoisie

In Marxist analysis, the capitalist class who own the means of production (factories, land, capital) and exploit the labor of the proletariat (working class) for profit. More broadly, the bourgeoisie refers to the urban middle class — merchants, professionals, and industrialists — who rose to prominence in medieval European towns and became the dominant political and economic force during the Industrial Revolution.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP uses "bourgeoisie" in two contexts: (1) pre-industrial — the merchant middle class whose wealth challenged aristocratic power and helped fuel Enlightenment liberalism and democratic revolutions; (2) industrial — Marx's exploiting capitalist class whose conflict with the proletariat drives his theory of class struggle. Make sure you know which era an essay is asking about.

> [!tip]
> Marx predicted the bourgeoisie would eventually be overthrown by the proletariat in a communist revolution. This prediction becomes important evidence in essays about socialist and communist movements in Units 5–8.
