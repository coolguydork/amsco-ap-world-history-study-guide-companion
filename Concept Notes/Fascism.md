# Fascism

Authoritarian ultranationalist ideology that rejects both liberal democracy and Marxist communism. Emphasizes the supremacy of the nation-state, a single charismatic leader, military expansion, suppression of opposition, and cultural/racial hierarchy. First took power in Italy (Mussolini, 1922), then spread to Germany (Hitler, 1933), Spain (Franco, 1939), and Portugal (Salazar).

## Core Features

- **Totalitarian state:** Government controls all aspects of public and private life — economy, culture, press, religion, education
- **Single-party dictatorship:** All opposition abolished; one leader (Il Duce, Führer) embodies the nation
- **Hypernationalism:** Nation as supreme value; extreme xenophobia; racial/cultural hierarchy
- **Rejection of class struggle:** Fascism claims to transcend capitalism vs. communism by subordinating both to state power
- **Militarism and imperial expansion:** War glorified; national greatness achieved through conquest
- **Anti-communism:** Fascism rose partly as a response to fear of communist revolution among middle classes and elites

## Key Fascist Regimes

| Country | Leader | Key Feature |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| Italy | Mussolini (1922–1943) | First fascist state; corporatism; expansionism in Africa |
| Germany | Hitler (1933–1945) | Racial fascism (Nazism); Holocaust; WWII |
| Spain | Franco (1939–1975) | Catholic-conservative variant; stayed neutral in WWII |
| Portugal | Salazar (1932–1968) | *Estado Novo*; hypernationalism; colonial empire preserved |

## Fascism vs. Communism vs. Liberal Democracy

| Feature | Fascism | Communism (Stalin) | Liberal Democracy |
|---------|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Private property | Preserved (state-supervised) | Abolished | Protected |
| Political parties | One (fascist) | One (communist) | Multiple |
| Economy | Corporatist autarky | Command (Five-Year Plans) | Regulated capitalism |
| Class | Denied; nation supersedes class | Class struggle is history's engine | Classes coexist |
| Racism | Central (esp. Nazism) | Official rejection (but ethnic purges) | Formal equality |

> [!exam-tip]
> AP loves to ask you to compare fascism and Stalinism. Both are totalitarian states that suppressed opposition and expanded government control. Key difference: fascism preserved private property and allied with conservative elites; Stalinism abolished private property and attacked old elites. Different ideologies; similarly brutal methods.

## Historical Context: Why Did Fascism Rise?

1. **Economic crisis:** The [[Concept Notes/Great Depression|Great Depression]] (and Weimar hyperinflation) created mass unemployment and despair
2. **WWI humiliation:** Germany's defeat and Versailles reparations created national resentment; Italy felt "cheated" of promised territory
3. **Fear of communism:** Middle classes and elites funded fascist parties as a bulwark against communist revolution
4. **Weak democracies:** Weimar Germany and liberal Italy lacked deep democratic traditions
