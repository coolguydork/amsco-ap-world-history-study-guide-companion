# Philosophes

French Enlightenment intellectuals (c. 1680–1789) who applied reason and empirical observation to society, religion, and government. Key figures include Voltaire (religious tolerance, free speech), Montesquieu (separation of powers), Rousseau (social contract, popular sovereignty), and Locke (natural rights, limited government). Their ideas spread through salons, coffeehouses, and the *Encyclopédie*, directly influencing the American and French Revolutions.

> [!exam-tip]
> Philosophes are the intellectual *cause* behind Atlantic revolutions. On any LEQ about the origins of the American or French Revolution, include philosophes as evidence of how Enlightenment ideas challenged monarchical and Church authority. The AP also expects you to explain the *mechanism* of diffusion — salons, print culture, the *Encyclopédie* — not just the ideas themselves.

> [!tip]
> "Philosophes" is specifically French, but the broader Enlightenment included British thinkers (Locke, Newton) and Scottish ones (Hume, Smith). When writing, use "Enlightenment thinkers" for the broader movement and "philosophes" only when referring to the French circle.
