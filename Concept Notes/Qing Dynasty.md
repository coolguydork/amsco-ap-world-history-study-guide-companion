# Qing Dynasty

Last imperial dynasty of China (1644–1912), founded by the [[Concept Notes/Manchu\|Manchu]] people who conquered the Ming. The Qing expanded Chinese territory dramatically — absorbing Tibet, Xinjiang, and Mongolia — while maintaining the Confucian bureaucratic system to govern their Han Chinese majority. In the 19th century, the Qing faced a perfect storm of internal rebellions ([[Concept Notes/Taiping Rebellion\|Taiping Rebellion]]) and external imperial pressure (Opium Wars, treaty ports, spheres of influence) that fatally weakened the dynasty, culminating in the 1911 Revolution and the establishment of the Republic of China.

> [!exam-tip]
> The Qing trajectory — powerful empire to "sick man of Asia" — is one of the AP's most important arcs. For LEQ/DBQ essays, you can use the Qing to argue that internal weakness plus external pressure is what caused 19th-century Chinese decline, or to compare how different states responded to Western imperialism (Japan modernized; China resisted then struggled to adapt).

> [!tip]
> The Qing's Self-Strengthening Movement (1860s–1890s) shows that China tried selective Westernization — adopting Western military technology without Western political institutions. Compare this directly with Japan's Meiji Restoration for a strong comparative essay.
