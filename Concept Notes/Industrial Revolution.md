# Industrial Revolution

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

The **Industrial Revolution** was a fundamental transformation in how goods were produced, beginning in Britain around 1760 and spreading globally through the 19th century. It replaced hand production and human/animal muscle with machine power, factory organization, and fossil fuel energy. Its consequences reshaped population, urbanization, global trade, social structures, and the balance of world power.

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 297–313)

> "The Industrial Revolution. The rigid structure of early factory work described by Adam Smith is one of the most enduring images of the Industrial Revolution. Industrialization, the increased mechanization of production, and the social changes that accompanied this shift, had their roots in several influences. Among these were the Columbian Exchange and rise of maritime trading empires, increased agricultural productivity, and greater individual accumulation of capital."

> "As the Industrial Revolution spread from Great Britain to Europe and North America, and then to the world, it reshaped society, increasing world population, shifting people from farm to city, and expanding the production and consumption of goods."

## Two Waves

Historians distinguish two phases:

**First Industrial Revolution (c. 1760–1850)** — centered in Britain; driven by coal, steam, and iron. Key technologies: spinning jenny, water frame, [[Concept Notes/James Watt|James Watt]]'s steam engine, interchangeable parts, iron production.

**Second Industrial Revolution (c. 1850–1914)** — spread to the United States, Germany, France; driven by steel (the [[Concept Notes/Bessemer Process|Bessemer Process]]), petroleum ([[Concept Notes/Fossil Fuels|fossil fuels]]), and electricity. Key figures: [[Concept Notes/Alexander Graham Bell|Alexander Graham Bell]] (telephone), [[Concept Notes/Guglielmo Marconi|Guglielmo Marconi]] (radio).

## AP Significance

- The Industrial Revolution is the **central economic event of Period 3** — almost every other topic in Unit 5 either causes it or results from it.
- It enabled **New Imperialism** (Unit 6): industrial military technology let a small number of European powers dominate vast territories.
- It created new social classes (factory workers, industrial capitalists), new ideologies ([[Concept Notes/capitalism|capitalism]], [[Concept Notes/socialism|socialism]]), and new global trade patterns.
- States that failed to industrialize risked colonization; states that succeeded gained imperial power. This is the central argument of [[Unit-5.6/Government's Role in Industrialization Overview]].
