# Confucianism

## Definition

**Confucianism** is a philosophical and ethical system founded by the Chinese thinker Kongzi (known in the West as Confucius, c. 551–479 BCE). It emphasizes proper social relationships, moral cultivation, loyalty to rulers, and **filial piety** — the duty of family members to subordinate their desires to the male head of household and, by extension, to the ruler. Confucianism became the foundational ideology of Chinese governance for over two thousand years.

## Core Principles

- **Filial piety** — respect and deference to parents, elders, and rulers; the glue of the social hierarchy
- **Proper relationships** — five key relationships (ruler/subject, parent/child, husband/wife, elder/younger sibling, friend/friend) each defined by specific duties
- **Moral cultivation** — the ideal ruler and official govern through virtue and example, not force
- **Merit through learning** — the educated, virtuous man (the *junzi* or gentleman) is the ideal social figure
- **Social hierarchy** — a stable, ordered society requires clear ranks and roles

## Why It Matters for the AP Exam

Confucianism underpins nearly every major AP World History topic involving East Asia:

- **Civil service exam** — based on mastery of Confucian texts; created the [[Concept Notes/Civil Service Exam|meritocracy]] of the [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty|Song Dynasty]]
- **Scholar gentry** — the class of Confucian-educated officials who dominated Chinese society
- **Filial piety** — reinforced patriarchal family structure and political loyalty across dynasties
- **Low merchant status** — Confucian ideology ranked merchants last because they didn't produce goods; this contrasts sharply with Islamic merchant culture
- **Sinification** — Confucianism was the primary ideology exported through [[Concept Notes/Sinification|sinification]] to Japan, Korea, and Vietnam
- **[[Concept Notes/Neo-Confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]]** — the Song-era synthesis of Confucianism with Buddhist and Daoist ideas
