# Civil Service Exam (Keju)

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

## Definition

China's **civil service exam** (known in Chinese as the *keju*) was a system of competitive written examinations used to select government officials. Based on knowledge of [[Confucianism|Confucian texts]], it allowed men who scored well to earn prestigious bureaucratic positions regardless of their family background. Because positions were awarded on merit rather than birthright, the system created a **meritocracy**.

## Historical Context

The exam system had roots in earlier dynasties but was dramatically expanded under the [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty|Song Dynasty]] (960–1279). Emperor Song Taizu extended access to young men of the lower economic classes — a significant change from the aristocratic domination of earlier periods.

> "By scoring well, a young man could obtain a highly desired job in the bureaucracy. These exams were based on knowledge of Confucian texts. Because officials obtained their positions by demonstrating their merit on these exams, China's bureaucratic system was known as a meritocracy. Though the poor were vastly underrepresented in the bureaucracy, the Chinese system allowed for more upward mobility than any other hiring system of its time." — AMSCO p. 4

## Comparative Variations

| Region | Civil Service Exam? | Open to Peasants? |
|---|---|---|
| **Song China** | Yes | Yes (though underrepresented) |
| **Korea** | Yes | **No** — aristocracy blocked access |
| **Vietnam** | Yes | Yes, but officials owed loyalty to villages, not emperor |
| **Japan** | No equivalent | N/A |

Korea's exam exclusion is a key comparative point: the Korean aristocracy used their political power to prevent commoners from using the exam to enter the bureaucracy — undermining the meritocratic principle. See [[Unit-1.1/Japan Korea Vietnam|Japan, Korea & Vietnam]].
