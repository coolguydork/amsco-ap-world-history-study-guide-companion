# Bolsheviks

The Bolsheviks were the radical Marxist faction of the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party, led by Vladimir Lenin, that seized power in the October Revolution of 1917 and established the Soviet state. Their name means "majority" in Russian — a label adopted after a 1903 party split, though they were not actually the majority. The Bolsheviks reshaped the 20th century by creating the world's first communist state and inspiring revolutionary movements globally.

## Ideology

Lenin adapted classical Marxism — which predicted revolution would arise spontaneously from the industrial working class — into a theory of **vanguard revolution**: a disciplined, centralized party of professional revolutionaries would lead the masses to power rather than waiting for conditions to ripen. This became **Marxism-Leninism**, the theoretical foundation of Soviet communism and later of communist parties in China, Cuba, Vietnam, and elsewhere.

Key doctrines:
- **Dictatorship of the proletariat:** A transitional period of party rule before the state "withers away" into true communism. In practice, it became permanent one-party dictatorship.
- **Democratic centralism:** Lower party bodies must follow decisions made above; internal debate is permitted before decisions, but not after.
- **Imperialism as capitalism's highest stage:** Lenin's 1917 pamphlet argued that WWI was driven by capitalist competition for colonies, making anti-imperialist nationalism a potential ally of communist revolution.

## Rise to Power

Russia's defeats in WWI, the February Revolution (March 1917) that toppled the tsar, and the Provisional Government's decision to continue the war created the opening. Lenin returned from exile in April 1917 with his **April Theses** — demanding immediate withdrawal from WWI, land redistribution, and "All power to the Soviets." The Bolsheviks grew rapidly, promising "Peace, Land, Bread."

The **October Revolution (November 7, 1917, New Style):** The Bolsheviks' Red Guard seized key buildings in Petrograd and overthrew the Provisional Government almost bloodlessly. Lenin formed the **Council of People's Commissars** as the new government.

## Consolidation of Power

- **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918):** Russia exited WWI at enormous cost — ceding Poland, Finland, the Baltic states, and Ukraine to Germany. Lenin accepted this humiliation to buy time.
- **Russian Civil War (1918–1921):** The Red Army (built by Leon Trotsky) fought the White Army (counterrevolutionary forces supported by Britain, France, Japan, and the United States). The Bolsheviks won.
- **Red Terror:** Systematic use of political violence, executions, and imprisonment to eliminate opponents. The tsar and his family were executed in July 1918.
- **War Communism:** Emergency economic policy nationalizing industry and requisitioning peasant grain — effective militarily but caused catastrophic famine.
- **New Economic Policy (NEP, 1921):** Lenin's tactical retreat — allowed small-scale private trade and farming to revive the economy after War Communism's failures.

## After Lenin (1924)

Lenin died in January 1924. Joseph Stalin outmaneuvered rivals (including Trotsky) to consolidate control by 1929, ending the NEP and launching forced collectivization and rapid industrialization. The Bolshevik Party became the **Communist Party of the Soviet Union (CPSU)**.

> [!warning]
> The AP frequently conflates the terms "Bolshevik," "Communist," and "Soviet." Be precise: Bolsheviks → the party's name during 1917–1918; the party renamed itself the Communist Party in 1918; "Soviet" refers to the system of elected councils that the Communists ultimately controlled. Stalin was a Communist, not formally a "Bolshevik" by that point.
