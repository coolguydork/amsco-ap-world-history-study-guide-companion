# Socialism

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**Socialism** is the economic and political philosophy that advocates collective or government ownership of the means of production, distribution, and exchange. It arose in the 19th century as a direct critique of industrial [[capitalism]]'s inequalities. Its most influential theorist was [[Unit-5.8/Karl Marx & Socialism|Karl Marx]].

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 333–335)

> "Karl Marx (1818–1883) was a German scholar and writer who argued for socialism. Unlike utopian socialists, whom he scorned because he thought they wanted to escape problems rather than confront them, he wanted to look at how the world actually operated. He called his approach to economics 'scientific socialism.'"

> "For Marx, socialism would replace capitalism. It, then, would later be replaced by a final stage of economic development, communism, in which all class distinctions would end."

## From the Vault

**From [[Unit-5.7/Key Terms & Concepts|Key Terms & Concepts]] (Topic 5.7):**
> Socialism advocates collective or government ownership of the means of production, distribution, and exchange. Associated with Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels (*The Communist Manifesto*, 1848; *Das Kapital*, 1867). Socialism held that capitalism exploited workers (the proletariat) for the benefit of owners (the bourgeoisie).

**From [[Unit-5.8/Karl Marx & Socialism|Karl Marx & Socialism]] (AMSCO pp. 333–335):**
> Marx argued the inevitable sequence: workers will recognize their shared class interests → socialist revolution → the proletariat takes control of the means of production → wealth shared fairly → eventually communism (no class distinctions, state withers away).

## Two Varieties of Socialism in This Period

| Type | Approach | Key figures | Outcome |
|------|---------|------------|---------|
| **Utopian socialism** | Create ideal communities as proof-of-concept alternatives to capitalism | Robert Owen, Charles Fourier | Mostly failed; small-scale experiments |
| **Marxist ("scientific") socialism** | Analyze capitalism's internal contradictions; predict inevitable revolution | Marx, Engels | Inspired 20th-century communist revolutions |

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam distinguishes **utopian socialists** (wanted to escape capitalism by creating model communities) from **Marxists** (wanted to replace capitalism through class struggle and revolution). Marx was contemptuous of utopian socialists — he called his approach "scientific" because it was grounded in historical analysis, not wishful idealism.

## Global Impact (20th Century)

Socialism remained a fringe idea in the 19th century but became enormously consequential afterward:
- **Russia 1917**: Bolshevik Revolution applied Marxist socialism → Soviet Union
- **China 1949**: Communist Revolution → People's Republic of China
- **Decolonization movements**: Marxist analysis of imperial exploitation shaped independence movements worldwide
