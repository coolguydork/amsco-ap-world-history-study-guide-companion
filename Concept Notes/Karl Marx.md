# Karl Marx

> [!summary] Source-built concept note
> This note collects relevant excerpts from existing study-vault notes and the structured AMSCO reference system. Use it as a quick concept hub, then return to the full topic notes for complete context.

**Karl Marx** (1818–1883) was a German scholar and political philosopher who developed the theory of scientific socialism — a comprehensive critique of capitalism and a blueprint for replacing it. His ideas became the intellectual foundation of socialist and communist movements worldwide, with enormous consequences for the 20th century.

## AMSCO Reference (pp. 333–335)

> "Karl Marx (1818–1883) was a German scholar and writer who argued for socialism. Unlike utopian socialists, whom he scorned because he thought they wanted to escape problems rather than confront them, he wanted to look at how the world actually operated. He called his approach to economics 'scientific socialism.'"

> "In 1848, Karl Marx and his wealthy supporter Friedrich Engels published a pamphlet (now called the Communist Manifesto) that summarized their critique of capitalism. According to Marx, capitalism was an advance on feudalism because it produced tremendous wealth, but it also produced needless poverty and misery. This contradiction between wealth and poverty occurred because capitalism divided society into two basic classes."

> "The proletariat was essentially the working class, working in factories and mines, often for little compensation. The bourgeoisie included the middle class and investors who owned machinery and factories where workers produced goods."

> "Marx said that market competition drove the bourgeoisie to exploit the proletariat for the sake of higher profits... Marx exhorted the proletariat to recognize their shared interest as a class and take control of the means of production and share the wealth they created fairly."

> "For Marx, socialism would replace capitalism. It, then, would later be replaced by a final stage of economic development, communism, in which all class distinctions would end."

## Key Ideas

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| Proletariat | Working class; sell their labor in exchange for wages |
| Bourgeoisie | Capitalist class; own factories, machines, land |
| Class struggle | History driven by conflict between economic classes |
| Surplus value | Profit comes from exploiting workers' labor |
| Scientific socialism | Marxism as an empirical analysis, not idealistic dreaming |
| Communism | Final stage: classless, stateless society; collective ownership |

## AP Significance

- Marx is the **primary intellectual origin** of socialism and communism — ideologies that shaped revolutions in Russia (1917), China (1949), Cuba (1959), and beyond.
- On AP essays, Marx appears as a **reaction to industrialization**: he's evidence of how industrial capitalism created social disruption and ideological opposition.
- The Communist Manifesto (1848) is a frequently cited document in AP DBQs about industrialization, reform, and ideology.
- Key contrast: Marx vs. [[Concept Notes/capitalism|capitalism]] / [[Concept Notes/laissez-faire|laissez-faire]] — one camp said markets should be free; Marx said markets structurally exploit workers and must be replaced.

> [!warning]
> Don't conflate Marx's theory with 20th-century communist states. Marx never saw a communist revolution in his lifetime, and 20th-century regimes (Soviet Union, Maoist China) diverged significantly from his written theory. For AP purposes, focus on Marx as an *ideological response to industrial capitalism*, not as a description of any actual government.
