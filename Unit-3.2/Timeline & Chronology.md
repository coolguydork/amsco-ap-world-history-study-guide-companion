# Timeline & Chronology — Empires: Administration

*AMSCO pp. 155–166 | AP Period 2: c. 1450–c. 1750*

---

## Chronological Overview

Topic 3.2 covers how the land-based empires established in [[Unit-3.1/European, East Asian & Gunpowder Empires Expand Overview|Topic 3.1]] actually governed their territories. The key dates below trace the development of administrative systems — bureaucracies, military forces, legal codes, and architectural projects — across Eurasia and Africa in the period c. 1450–c. 1750.

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1265 | English Parliament established under Simon de Montfort | Early precedent for limiting monarch's authority; contrasts with absolute monarchy developing elsewhere in this period |
| 1485–1603 | Tudor dynasty rules England | Tudors centralize power *through* Parliament, royal courts, and control of the Church of England — creating England's distinctive limited monarchy |
| 1493–1528 | Askia the Great rules Songhai | Uses Islamic piety and pilgrimage to Mecca to legitimize rule; builds efficient bureaucracy; Songhai becomes largest kingdom in West Africa |
| 1534 | Henry VIII breaks with Rome; establishes Church of England | Religious authority becomes a tool of political centralization; monarch becomes head of both church and state |
| 1547–1584 | Ivan IV ("Ivan the Terrible") rules Russia | First formal tsar; creates oprichnina paramilitary; suppresses boyar power; expands Russia east |
| 1556–1605 | Akbar rules the Mughal Empire | Greatest Mughal administrator: abolishes jizya, institutes zamindars system, employs Hindus in government, pursues religious tolerance |
| 1568 | Oda Nobunaga captures Kyoto, begins unifying Japan | First of three daimyo to unify Japan; uses Portuguese muskets to defeat traditional samurai forces |
| 1582 | Toyotomi Hideyoshi continues Japanese unification | Conducts sword hunts and census to fix social hierarchy; completes territorial consolidation after Nobunaga's assassination |
| 1600 | Battle of Sekigahara — Tokugawa Ieyasu defeats rivals | Final battle of Japanese unification; leads directly to the Tokugawa shogunate and the Edo "Period of Great Peace" |
| 1603 | Tokugawa Ieyasu appointed shogun | Establishes Tokugawa Shogunate; implements sankin-kōtai hostage system to control daimyo; Japan unified under centralized military rule until 1868 |
| 1610–1643 | Louis XIII rules France; Cardinal Richelieu as chief minister | Richelieu develops the intendant system; French absolutism takes institutional form |
| 1613 | Romanov Dynasty takes power in Russia | Follows the "Time of Troubles" after Ivan IV's death; Romanovs bring stability but maintain autocratic rule |
| 1628–1658 | Shah Jahan rules the Mughal Empire | Builds Taj Mahal and Red Fort; monumental architecture as imperial legitimation; prosperous but costly reign |
| 1643–1715 | Louis XIV rules France ("the Sun King") | Apex of European absolute monarchy: "L'état, c'est moi"; builds Versailles; requires nobility at court; weakens feudal lords |
| 1661–1722 | Emperor Kangxi rules the Qing Dynasty | Masterful combination of Confucian legitimacy and Manchu identity; employs Han officials; extends Qing control over Tibet |
| 1682 | Louis XIV moves court to Versailles | Versailles becomes a political instrument: nobles required to attend, kept from building independent power in home provinces |
| 1682–1725 | Peter the Great rules Russia | Forcible westernization; builds St. Petersburg; subordinates Orthodox Church; creates salaried provincial administration; introduces head tax (1718) |
| 1689 | English Bill of Rights signed | Guarantees individual civil liberties; requires parliamentary consent for taxation and army; confirms England's path toward limited (not absolute) monarchy |
| 1718 | Peter the Great institutes head tax in Russia | Replaces land tax with per-person tax; increases revenue but deepens peasant burden and serfdom |

> [!tip]
> Long reigns (Akbar, Kangxi, Louis XIV) tend to correlate with administrative success — stability allowed administrative systems to mature and embed. Short or chaotic reigns often signal administrative breakdown (e.g., the "Time of Troubles" in Russia after Ivan IV).

## Chronological Patterns

**Sequence within empires:** Notice how each major empire follows a similar arc — expansion [[Unit-3.1/European, East Asian & Gunpowder Empires Expand Overview|European, East Asian & Gunpowder Empires Expand Overview]] → administrative consolidation [[Unit-3.2/Empires: Administration Overview|Empires: Administration Overview]] → peak prosperity → gradual weakening [[Unit-4.6/Internal & External Challenges to State Power Overview|Internal & External Challenges to State Power Overview]]. The administrative methods of Topic 3.2 are what made the peak sustainable, and their corruption or failure explains the later decline.

**Simultaneity across empires:** The mid-to-late 17th century was a peak period for administrative consolidation *across* the globe simultaneously: Louis XIV at Versailles (1682), Kangxi's Qing golden age (1661–1722), Peter the Great's Russia (1682–1725), and the Tokugawa shogunate all reaching maturity in roughly the same generation.

**England's exceptional path:** While the continent moved toward absolute monarchy, England moved the opposite direction — from Tudor centralization to the English Bill of Rights (1689). This divergence from the rest of Europe foreshadows the political revolutions of Topic 5.
