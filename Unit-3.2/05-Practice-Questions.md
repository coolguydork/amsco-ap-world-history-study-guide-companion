**Question 1**

*Use the image description below to answer the question.*

> An architectural complex features a large central dome flanked by four minarets, reflected in a long rectangular pool lined with cypress trees. The building is made of white marble inlaid with semi-precious stones.

The structure described above most directly reflects which of the following?

A) The Ottoman practice of converting Christian holy sites into Islamic spaces
B) The Mughal synthesis of Persian-Islamic and Indian architectural traditions as a symbol of imperial power
C) The Safavid investment in Shia Islamic religious institutions as a tool of governance
D) The Qing emphasis on Confucian aesthetic principles in imperial construction

**Correct Answer: B**
*The description matches the Taj Mahal — white marble, four minarets, reflecting pool — which blends Persian garden design and Islamic dome architecture with Indian craftsmanship. It is the definitive symbol of Mughal imperial culture.*

**Question 2**

The Ottoman timar system and the Mughal mansabdar system were similar in that both

A) Required officials to be Muslim converts trained in the imperial capital
B) Linked the right to collect taxes from a region to the obligation to provide military service
C) Created hereditary noble classes that became powerful rivals to the emperor
D) Were introduced by the same ruler to solve the same administrative problem

**Correct Answer: B**
*Both systems fused taxation and military service: timar holders collected taxes from their lands and brought soldiers to war; mansabdars were ranked officials who funded cavalry from their tax revenues. Both solved the problem of funding armies without a standing payroll.*

**Question 3**

The "Sultanate of Women" in the Ottoman Empire and the political role of Nur Jahan in the Mughal Empire are best understood as evidence that

A) Islamic law required women to serve as regents when sultans or emperors were incapacitated
B) Women in early modern Islamic empires could exercise significant political power through informal household and family networks
C) Feminist movements in the 17th century influenced political structures across Eurasia
D) The Ottoman and Mughal empires had formal legal provisions for female rule

**Correct Answer: B**
*Both cases demonstrate that women could exercise real political power not through formal legal rights but through their positions within the household structures that were central to imperial politics — as mothers, wives, and regents.*

**Question 4**

The Qing Dynasty's imposition of the queue (hairstyle) requirement on Han Chinese men most directly served to

A) Spread Manchu cultural practices among the Han Chinese population as a form of assimilation
B) Signal Han Chinese submission to Manchu rule and mark the political hierarchy visually
C) Distinguish Manchu soldiers from Han civilians during military campaigns
D) Fulfill a religious obligation of the Qing imperial family's Buddhist faith

**Correct Answer: B**
*The queue order was primarily a political tool: a visible, daily reminder of Manchu dominance. Resistance was punishable by death, making it an enforcement mechanism for the social order the Qing imposed.*

**Question 5**

Which of the following most accurately describes a key difference between the Ottoman devshirme system and the Chinese civil service examination system?

A) The devshirme recruited based on academic merit, while the examination system recruited based on ethnic origin
B) The devshirme created officials who were legally enslaved to the sultan, while the examination system created officials who were free Confucian scholars
C) The devshirme was used to staff provincial governments, while the examination system was limited to the imperial capital
D) The devshirme favored Muslim applicants, while the examination system was open to all religious groups

**Correct Answer: B**
*This is the fundamental distinction: devshirme recruits were technically slaves of the sultan (which paradoxically gave him total control over them), while examination officials were free scholars who owed their position to their mastery of Confucian classics.*

**Question 6**

*Consider the following statement from a Mughal court chronicle:*

> "His Majesty is of the opinion that no man should be interfered with on account of religion, and that anyone is to be allowed to go over to a religion that pleases him."

This statement most directly reflects which of the following Mughal policies?

A) The universal adoption of the Din-i-Ilahi faith at court
B) Akbar's pragmatic approach to governing a religiously diverse empire by accommodating rather than suppressing difference
C) A formal legal guarantee of religious freedom enshrined in Mughal imperial law
D) The influence of Jesuit missionaries on Akbar's religious thinking

**Correct Answer: B**
*Akbar's tolerance was a governance strategy as much as a personal belief: religious accommodation was the most practical way to maintain loyalty among a Hindu-majority population ruled by a Muslim emperor.*

**Question 7**

The Safavid state's monopoly on Persian silk production and trade is best understood as an example of

A) Free-market economic policies that attracted European merchants
B) State-directed economic control to fund military and cultural programs
C) Islamic prohibition on private merchants trading in luxury goods
D) Safavid competition with Mughal cotton textile exports

**Correct Answer: B**
*The silk monopoly gave the Safavid state direct revenue from one of the world's most valuable commodities, which Shah Abbas used to fund his ghulam military reforms and the construction of Isfahan — a direct link between economic control and state power.*

**Question 8**

Aurangzeb's reversal of Akbar's religious tolerance policies in the Mughal Empire is historically significant primarily because it

A) Led immediately to a popular revolution that ended the Mughal dynasty
B) Demonstrated that rulers' individual religious beliefs had no effect on imperial stability
C) Contributed to internal fragmentation by alienating Hindu, Sikh, and regional elites, undermining imperial unity
D) Was required by a new interpretation of Islamic law issued by the Sheikh al-Islam in Istanbul

**Correct Answer: C**
*Aurangzeb's orthodoxy (reinstating jizya, destroying temples, suppressing non-Muslims) alienated key constituencies — especially the Rajput nobility and Sikh community — that Akbar's inclusionary policies had brought into the imperial system. The Maratha confederacy grew into a major military challenge during his reign.*

**Question 9**

The construction of grand imperial capitals — Isfahan (Safavid), Istanbul (Ottoman), Agra/Delhi (Mughal) — in the period 1450–1750 most directly reflects which of the following patterns?

A) Rulers used architectural patronage to relocate populations away from frontier regions
B) Cultural and architectural achievement served as a form of political legitimation, communicating imperial power and divine favor
C) Trade networks centered on capital cities generated the tax revenue needed for military expansion
D) Capital cities were designed primarily as defensive fortifications against rival empires

**Correct Answer: B**
*In all three cases, the physical beauty and grandeur of the capital communicated the same message: our empire is blessed, our ruler is chosen, and our civilization surpasses all rivals. Architecture was imperial propaganda in stone.*

**Question 10**

The Qing "dyarchy" — appointing both Manchu and Han officials to administer each province — is best understood as a strategy for

A) Spreading Manchu language and culture throughout China by embedding Manchu officials at every level
B) Governing a large, culturally distinct majority population while maintaining Manchu political control
C) Ensuring that Confucian governance principles were upheld by having Han scholars check Manchu decisions
D) Fulfilling a diplomatic obligation to Han Chinese elites who had helped the Manchu conquer China

**Correct Answer: B**
*The dyarchy solved a fundamental problem: the Manchu were a tiny minority ruling a vast Han majority. By pairing Manchu and Han officials, the Qing maintained surveillance and control at the provincial level while preserving the administrative expertise of the existing Han bureaucracy.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Land-based empires in the period 1450–1750 used a variety of cultural and artistic methods to legitimate their political authority."**

**(a)** Describe **ONE** specific architectural or artistic achievement that reflected the political power of a land-based empire in this period.

**(b)** Explain **ONE** way in which this achievement served as a tool of political legitimation (not just religious expression).

**(c)** Briefly explain **ONE** similarity in how two different empires used architecture or art to legitimate their authority.

> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Taj Mahal (Mughal imperial wealth/divine favor); Süleymaniye Mosque (Ottoman piety + power); Isfahan (Safavid royal identity); Forbidden City (Confucian hierarchy)
> - (b) These structures communicated divine favor, imperial wealth, and cultural superiority to both subjects and rivals — they were designed to be *seen* and to inspire awe
> - (c) Both Ottoman mosques and Mughal mausoleums communicated Islamic piety and imperial patronage; OR both the Forbidden City and the Naqsh-e Jahan Square organized space hierarchically to express the emperor's central position in the cosmos

### SAQ 2

**(a)** Briefly explain **ONE** way in which land-based empires used military institutions to consolidate political power.

**(b)** Briefly explain **ONE** way in which these same military institutions became a threat to imperial stability over time.

**(c)** Explain **ONE** way in which one empire's response to this challenge differed from another empire's.

> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Ottoman Janissaries gave sultans a loyal professional army independent of noble cavalry; Qing Eight Banners gave the Manchu military dominance; Mughal mansabdars linked military service to administrative rank
> - (b) Janissaries blocked reform and became kingmakers; banner soldiers grew ceremonially useless but demanded stipends; mansabdars became hereditary and independent
> - (c) Shah Abbas reformed by creating a new ghulam slave army to replace tribal forces; the Qing responded by building the Green Standard Han Chinese army alongside the banners; the Ottomans attempted reform repeatedly (succeeding only under Mahmud II in 1826)

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which the administrative systems of land-based empires in the period 1450–1750 effectively consolidated imperial power."**

> **Outline for a top-scoring response:**

**Thesis:** Develop a historically defensible claim that addresses *extent* — e.g., *Administrative systems effectively consolidated power in the short term by creating loyal, centralized bureaucracies and separating military power from hereditary nobles, but many systems contained structural weaknesses that undermined long-term stability.*

**Body Paragraph 1 — Evidence for effectiveness:**
- Ottoman devshirme created loyal officials/soldiers without hereditary interests
- Mughal mansabdar system centralized tax collection and military in one structure
- Qing dyarchy maintained Manchu control while utilizing Han administrative expertise

**Body Paragraph 2 — Evidence of limits/failures:**
- Janissaries became political obstacles and blocked modernization
- Mansabdars became hereditary under weak Mughal rulers after Aurangzeb
- Qing banner forces became financially burdensome and militarily obsolete

**Body Paragraph 3 — Complexity/Nuance:**
- Administrative effectiveness depended heavily on individual rulers: Akbar vs. Aurangzeb; Suleiman vs. later weak sultans
- External challenges (European maritime competition, internal rebellion) stress-tested these systems in ways their designers did not anticipate

**Contextualization:** Connect to the Mongol legacy — these empires were designed partly in reaction to the Mongol pattern of conquest without lasting administration; the goal was to build something that would outlast any single ruler.
