## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

The Ottoman devshirme system was primarily designed to

A) convert the entire Christian population of conquered territories to Islam  
B) create a loyal, professionally trained bureaucracy and military force independent of local power brokers  
C) reduce the tax burden on Muslim subjects of the Ottoman Empire  
D) provide enslaved labor for the construction of Ottoman architectural monuments

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — only the recruited boys were converted; it was selective, not wholesale.
- **B is correct** — the system's purpose was loyalty to the sultan above family or ethnic/regional ties.
- C is wrong — it had no direct relationship to Muslim tax rates.
- D is wrong — the boys became elite military and civil servants, not construction laborers.

**Question 2**

Which of the following best describes a key difference between English and French political development in the period 1450–1750?

A) England moved toward absolute monarchy while France developed a constitutional system  
B) France developed a powerful parliament while England relied on royal intendants  
C) England developed limits on royal authority through Parliament while France moved toward royal absolutism  
D) Both England and France adopted identical systems of divine right monarchy

**Answer: C**
- A is wrong — these are reversed.
- B is wrong — these are reversed (England had Parliament, France had intendants).
- **C is correct** — Parliament (especially the English Bill of Rights, 1689) limited the crown; Louis XIV epitomized absolutism.
- D is wrong — their systems diverged significantly.

**Question 3**

Peter the Great's replacement of the Russian land tax with a head tax in 1718 is best understood as

A) a progressive reform that reduced the burden on large landowners  
B) an effort to increase state revenue by expanding the taxpayer base to include all individuals  
C) a policy designed to encourage migration from rural to urban areas  
D) a response to the demands of the boyar class for tax relief

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — the head tax actually shifted burden to peasants, not away from it.
- **B is correct** — taxing individuals (heads) rather than land captured more revenue from the large peasant population.
- C is wrong — it accelerated serfdom, not urbanization.
- D is wrong — Peter consistently worked *against* boyar privileges.

**Question 4**

The Tokugawa shogunate's requirement that daimyo maintain residences both in their home territories and in the capital (Edo) was primarily intended to

A) encourage the daimyo to invest in Japan's urban infrastructure  
B) prevent the daimyo from forming independent power bases that could challenge the shogunate  
C) ensure that samurai had multiple employment opportunities across Japan  
D) promote cultural exchange between regional and national elites

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — the policy was political, not infrastructural.
- **B is correct** — families as effective hostages and required residence in Edo kept daimyo subordinate.
- C is wrong — samurai employment was tied to their daimyo, not the shogunate's residence requirements.
- D is wrong — cultural exchange may have been a side effect, but was not the purpose.

**Question 5**

The decline of the Mughal zamindar system over time illustrates which of the following historical patterns?

A) The inevitability of democratic reform in agricultural societies  
B) The tendency of local tax officials to accumulate personal power at the expense of central authority when poorly supervised  
C) The incompatibility of Islam and Hinduism in governing a shared territory  
D) The superiority of salary-based systems compared to tribute-based revenue collection

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — the zamindar decline led to fragmentation, not democracy.
- **B is correct** — zamindars began keeping more taxes and building private armies as central oversight weakened.
- C is wrong — the Hindu-Muslim conflict under Aurangzeb is a related but separate issue.
- D is wrong — the zamindars *were* initially salaried; the problem was the shift to land grants.

## Short Answer Questions

**SAQ 1 — Comparison**

Briefly explain ONE similarity and ONE difference between the Ottoman devshirme system and the Ming/Qing civil service examination system as tools of administrative control.

*Sample Similarity:* Both the Ottoman devshirme and the Chinese civil service exam were designed to create a **loyal, meritocratic bureaucracy** independent of hereditary aristocratic power. In both cases, officials were selected for ability (military and administrative in the Ottoman case, scholarly in the Chinese case) rather than noble birth, and they owed their positions to the central government — the sultan or the emperor.

*Sample Difference:* The devshirme system was **coercive and religiously transformative** — boys were forcibly taken from Christian families, converted to Islam, and cut off from their origins to ensure loyalty. The civil service exam, by contrast, was **voluntary and open** to members of the scholar-gentry class who chose to study Confucian texts. The Ottoman system used social rupture to guarantee loyalty; the Chinese system used scholarly identity and ambition.

**SAQ 2 — Causation**

Explain ONE cause of the increased use of monumental architecture in Period 2 land-based empires.

*Sample Response:* One cause was rulers' need to **legitimize their authority over diverse, multiethnic populations**. Empires like the Ottoman, Mughal, and French kingdoms governed millions of people who owed their loyalty to local lords, religious institutions, or ethnic groups rather than to the central state. Monumental architecture — the Suleymaniye Mosque in Istanbul, the Taj Mahal in Agra, the Palace of Versailles in France — projected imperial wealth, divine favor, and cultural sophistication in a way that text or proclamations could not. A ruler who could build on a massive scale demonstrated that God (or fate) had blessed his rule, making subjects more likely to accept his authority as legitimate.

---

**SAQ 3 — CCOT**

Explain ONE continuity and ONE change in the methods rulers used to consolidate power between the period c. 1200–c. 1450 (Unit 1/2) and c. 1450–c. 1750 (Unit 3).

*Sample Continuity:* **Religion as a source of legitimacy** continued as a key tool of governance. Just as Mansa Musa of Mali used a pilgrimage to Mecca to legitimate his rule in the 1300s (Topic 2.4), Askia the Great of Songhai used the same strategy after 1493, and the Mughal emperor Akbar used religious tolerance (and later Aurangzeb used Islamic piety) to justify Mughal authority. Divine sanction for rulers remained constant across cultures and centuries.

*Sample Change:* The **professionalization of bureaucracy** became more sophisticated. Earlier medieval states relied primarily on personal loyalty and feudal obligation. In Period 2, empires created structured civil service systems (China's exams), specialized military-bureaucratic corps (Ottoman Janissaries), salaried provincial officials (France's intendants, Peter's Russian reforms), and codified laws of appeal (Akbar's courts). Government became more institutional and less personal.

## LEQ Guidance

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 3.2:**

- "Evaluate the extent to which rulers in the period 1450–1750 used similar methods to consolidate power."
- "Evaluate the extent to which the administrative methods of land-based empires in the period 1450–1750 were effective in maintaining central authority."

**Strong comparative pairings:**
- Ottoman devshirme vs. Chinese civil service exam (both create loyal bureaucrats; different methods)
- France's absolutism vs. England's constitutional monarchy (same period, opposite directions)
- Peter the Great vs. Louis XIV (both centralized dramatically; different cultural styles)
- Tokugawa Japan vs. Mughal India (both used landholding elites; different control mechanisms)

**Common mistakes:**
- Claiming the Ottoman devshirme was purely slavery — it also provided upward mobility
- Forgetting that the English Bill of Rights (1689) is the *result* of a constitutional struggle, not the beginning
- Confusing intendants (France) with justices of the peace (England)
- Missing that the zamindar system *started* as a success under Akbar and only deteriorated later

## Quick Summary: Comparing Administrative Systems

| Region | Key System | Strength | Long-term Weakness |
|--------|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| France | Absolutism / intendants | Centralized decision-making | Resentment → Revolution |
| England | Parliament / justices of peace | Shared authority, legitimacy | Slower to centralize |
| Russia | Autocracy / provinces | Military modernization | Oppressive serfdom |
| Ottoman | Devshirme / Janissaries | Ultra-loyal bureaucracy and military | Janissaries later became kingmakers |
| Mughal | Zamindars | Efficient initially | Local corruption, fragmentation |
| China | Civil service exam | Merit-based, stable | Bureaucracy became corrupt in late Qing |
| Japan | Tokugawa shogunate / hostage system | Peace and stability | Isolation; fell behind technologically |
