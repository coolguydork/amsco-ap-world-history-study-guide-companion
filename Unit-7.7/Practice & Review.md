# Practice & Review

## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: AMSCO pp. 517–518 | Questions 1–3 refer to a political cartoon (Library of Congress)*

The cartoon depicts the German invasion of the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa, 1941).

**1. Which of the following set the stage for the event represented in the cartoon?**

- A) The creation of the Nonaggression Pact in 1939
- B) The formation of the pro-Nazi Vichy government in 1940
- C) The signing of the Destroyers-for-Bases Agreement in 1940
- D) The acceptance of the Atlantic Charter in 1941

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact (1939) temporarily allied Germany and the USSR, dividing Poland between them. Hitler then violated the pact by invading the Soviet Union (Operation Barbarossa) in June 1941. The cartoon depicts this betrayal. B, C, and D all postdate the Nonaggression Pact and are unrelated to the German-Soviet relationship that the cartoon represents.

**2. What development during World War II does the cartoon represent?**

- A) The alliance between Hitler and the Soviet Union
- B) The surprise Nazi invasion of the Soviet Union
- C) The successful Soviet defense of Stalingrad
- D) The rise of Soviet communism during the war

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Operation Barbarossa (June 1941) was a massive surprise invasion of the USSR after years of supposed non-aggression. A is the Nonaggression Pact itself (1939), not what the cartoon depicts. C (Stalingrad) was a Soviet victory in 1943. D is not an event depicted in this cartoon.

**3. The importance of the event depicted in the cartoon was that it was:**

- A) A direct response to Germany's invasion of Poland
- B) The battle that caused Britain and France to enter the war
- C) The last Allied victory in Europe, ending the European part of the war
- D) An Allied victory that was crucial in defeating Germany

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Operation Barbarossa opened the Eastern Front, where the Soviet Union eventually absorbed and destroyed the majority of German military strength. Soviet victories — especially Stalingrad and Kursk — were the most decisive Allied contribution to defeating Germany. A is wrong (Barbarossa came after the invasion of Poland); B is wrong (Britain and France entered the war over Poland in 1939, not due to this event); C is wrong (V-E Day ended the European war).

## Short-Answer Questions

*Source: AMSCO p. 518 | SAQ 1 refers to an excerpt from "American Women in World War II," The National World War II Museum*

The passage describes ~50,000 American women who served in uniform in WWII, performing roles from office work to aircraft testing to front-line nursing, while General Eisenhower credited women's contributions as essential to D-Day.

**SAQ 1A: Identify ONE short-term result from the service of women during World War II.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> One short-term result of women's wartime service was the expansion of women's employment opportunities in traditionally male fields, including manufacturing, engineering, and military support roles. With millions of men in the armed forces, women proved they could perform jobs previously considered impossible for them, and government propaganda ("Rosie the Riveter") actively celebrated this shift.

**SAQ 1B: Identify ONE long-term result from the service of women during World War II.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Continuity and Change
> One long-term result was the erosion of the argument that women were inherently unsuited for skilled, technical, or dangerous work. Women's demonstrated competence during WWII contributed to the postwar women's rights movements of the 1950s–60s, which challenged legal and social restrictions on women's roles in the workforce and public life.

**SAQ 1C: Explain ONE way in which social structures changed OR stayed the same during World War II.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Continuity and Change
> Social structures changed in that wartime necessity temporarily broke down gender barriers in the workforce: women operated machinery, flew aircraft, and served near front lines. However, this change was largely reversed after the war when returning soldiers reclaimed industrial jobs and women were encouraged to return to domestic roles — suggesting the change was contextual rather than permanent. The tension between wartime disruption and post-war reversion illustrates that social change driven by emergency does not automatically persist.

## SAQ 2: WWI vs. WWII

**SAQ 2A: Identify ONE piece of evidence that supports the argument that WWI caused WWII.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed harsh reparations and war-guilt clauses on Germany, creating the economic devastation and national humiliation that Hitler exploited to rise to power. The Great Depression further destabilized Weimar Germany, making Hitler's extreme nationalism appealing to desperate voters. Without the punitive peace of WWI, the conditions enabling Hitler's rise likely would not have existed.

**SAQ 2B: Explain ONE continuity in the types of warfare used in WWI and WWII.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Continuity
> Both wars were "total wars" in which governments mobilized entire national populations and economies. In WWI, states introduced rationing, conscription, women in industry, and propaganda on a large scale for the first time. WWII continued and intensified all of these practices — the U.S. produced war material at unprecedented scale, the USSR mobilized every resource against Germany, and Britain used rationing and propaganda extensively. Total war as a governing principle is a direct continuity linking both conflicts.

**SAQ 2C: Explain ONE change in the types of warfare used in WWI and WWII.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Change
> The nature of combat changed dramatically from static to mobile warfare. WWI was defined by trench stalemate: both sides dug in and exchanged artillery barrages with minimal territorial gains at enormous human cost. WWII, by contrast, featured the German Blitzkrieg — fast-moving tank columns supported by coordinated air strikes that conquered France in six weeks. The Pacific theater introduced carrier-based naval air warfare in which ships never came within sight of each other. These mobile, technologically-driven forms of warfare represented a decisive break from WWI's static attritional model.

## Essential Question Response

**Explain similarities and differences in how governments used a variety of methods to conduct war.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> All major WWII belligerents employed total war strategies mobilizing civilian populations, but they did so with very different effectiveness. The United States used its industrial advantage and efficient state coordination to produce war material at unprecedented scale, while propaganda drew women into the workforce. The Soviet Union accepted catastrophic human losses through ruthless authoritarian mobilization, directing every resource toward defeating Germany. By contrast, Germany counter-productively relied on coerced foreign labor, which produced low output, and Japan emphasized ideological loyalty over practical mobilization, refusing even to deploy women in industry.
>
> A key difference was the role of new technology. The United States and Japan both demonstrated the dominance of aircraft carriers in the Pacific; Germany perfected Blitzkrieg to achieve rapid land conquests. The defining moment, however, was the United States' use of atomic bombs — a weapon of mass destruction with no WWI parallel that instantly killed tens of thousands and made the nuclear age the defining reality of post-war international relations.
