# Timeline & Chronology

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Sept 1939 | Germany invades Poland; WWII begins | Blitzkrieg proves devastating; Nonaggression Pact divides Poland between Germany and USSR |
| Apr–Jun 1940 | Germany conquers Denmark, Norway, France (6 weeks) | Speed of French defeat shocks world; demonstrates Blitzkrieg effectiveness |
| Jun 1940 | Vichy France established | Collaborationist regime; spectrum of responses to occupation (collaboration vs. resistance) |
| Jul–Oct 1940 | Battle of Britain | First German defeat; RAF and radar deny Luftwaffe air superiority; Hitler abandons invasion |
| Sept 1940 | Destroyers-for-Bases Agreement | U.S. breaks neutrality; 50 destroyers to Britain for naval base leases |
| Mar 1941 | Lend-Lease Act | U.S. supplies war materials to Allies; pretense of neutrality ends |
| Jun 1941 | Operation Barbarossa — Germany invades USSR | Largest land invasion in history; 27 million Soviets will die; opens Eastern Front |
| Aug 1941 | Atlantic Charter | Roosevelt-Churchill declaration of post-war goals; foundation for UN |
| 1941–1944 | Siege of Leningrad | ~900 days; ~1 million deaths; exemplifies Eastern Front's human cost |
| Dec 7, 1941 | Japan attacks Pearl Harbor | U.S. enters WWII; war becomes truly global |
| Dec 1941 | Hitler declares war on USA | U.S. now at war in both Pacific and European theaters |
| May 1942 | Battle of the Coral Sea | First Allied Pacific victory; stops Japan's drive toward Australia; carriers prove superior |
| Jun 1942 | Battle of Midway Island | U.S. destroys 4 Japanese carriers; Pacific turning point; Japan never recovers strategic initiative |
| Aug 1942–Feb 1943 | Battle of Stalingrad | Germany's Sixth Army destroyed; Eastern Front turning point; ~2 million casualties |
| Aug 1942–Feb 1943 | Guadalcanal campaign | First major Allied Pacific ground offensive; Allied victory opens path toward Japan |
| Oct–Nov 1942 | Battle of El Alamein | British defeat Rommel in Egypt; North African turning point |
| Jul 1943 | Battle of Kursk | Largest tank battle in history; Soviets defeat German Blitzkrieg; Germany permanently defensive in East |
| Jul–Aug 1943 | Allied conquest of Sicily; Mussolini falls | Italy switches sides September 1943 |
| Jun 4, 1944 | Allies recapture Rome | |
| Jun 6, 1944 | D-Day — Normandy invasion | ~150,000 Allied troops land; Western Front opens; liberation of France begins |
| Aug 1944 | Liberation of Paris | |
| Dec 1944–Jan 1945 | Battle of the Bulge | Germany's last offensive fails; reserves exhausted; defeat inevitable |
| Jan 1945 | Soviets take Warsaw | Eastern Front approaching Berlin |
| Apr 1945 | Allied forces approaching Berlin | |
| Apr 30, 1945 | Hitler commits suicide | |
| May 8, 1945 | **V-E Day** | Germany surrenders; WWII in Europe ends |
| Mar 1945 | U.S. fire-bombs Tokyo | ~100,000 killed |
| Early 1945 | U.S. captures Iwo Jima and Okinawa | Final stepping stones to Japanese mainland |
| Aug 6, 1945 | Atomic bomb dropped on Hiroshima | ~70,000–80,000 killed instantly; nuclear age begins |
| Aug 9, 1945 | Atomic bomb dropped on Nagasaki | Second bomb; combined ~110,000–210,000 deaths |
| Aug 15, 1945 | Japan announces surrender | |
| Sept 2, 1945 | **V-J Day** | Japan's formal surrender on USS Missouri; WWII ends |

## Turning Points at a Glance

| Theater | Turning Point | Date | Why It Mattered |
|---|---|---|---|
| European/N. Africa | Battle of El Alamein | Oct 1942 | Stopped Axis advance on Suez Canal |
| European/Eastern | Battle of Stalingrad | Feb 1943 | Destroyed German Sixth Army; Eastern Front turns |
| European/Eastern | Battle of Kursk | Jul 1943 | Shattered last German offensive capability in East |
| European/Western | D-Day | Jun 1944 | Opened Western Front; liberation of France |
| Pacific | Battle of Midway | Jun 1942 | Destroyed Japanese carrier fleet; U.S. takes initiative |
| Pacific | Guadalcanal | Feb 1943 | First Allied ground offensive; proved Japan could be beaten |

> [!tip]
> **Chronological pattern:** All major turning points cluster in 1942–1943. Before that, Axis powers were on offense. After, they were on permanent defense.
