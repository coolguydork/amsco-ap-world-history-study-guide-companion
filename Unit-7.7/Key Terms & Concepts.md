# Key Terms & Concepts

*27 terms — all resolved. Source: AMSCO pp. 511–520.*

---

## Government: War

**Vichy**
Collaborationist French government established after Germany's rapid conquest of France in 1940; based in Vichy under Marshal Pétain; controlled southern France while Germany occupied the north; cooperated with Nazi racial policies and deportation of Jews. Represents the spectrum of responses to Nazi occupation, from resistance to full collaboration.

**Lend-Lease Act**
1941 U.S. law that allowed the United States to lend or lease war materials to Allied nations (primarily Britain and later the USSR) without requiring immediate payment; ended U.S. pretense of neutrality before Pearl Harbor; provided critical supplies that sustained Allied resistance. A key example of mobilizing economic power before direct military involvement.

**Battle of Britain**
July–October 1940 sustained German air campaign (Luftwaffe) against British cities and airfields aimed at achieving air superiority before a land invasion. Britain's RAF held out, denying Germany control of the air and forcing Hitler to abandon invasion plans. First major German defeat; proved airpower alone could not win wars.

**Siege of Leningrad**
German siege of Leningrad (1941–1944) lasting nearly 900 days; over a million Soviet civilians and soldiers died from shelling, starvation, and cold. Ultimately broken by Soviet forces. Exemplifies the Eastern Front's enormous human cost and Soviet determination to resist at any price.

**Pearl Harbor**
December 7, 1941 surprise Japanese air attack on the U.S. naval base at Pearl Harbor, Hawaii; destroyed much of the U.S. Pacific Fleet; brought the United States into WWII the following day. Ended U.S. isolationism overnight and made WWII truly global — Hitler declared war on the United States within days.

**Battle of El Alamein**
1942 battle in Egypt where British forces under Montgomery defeated German General Rommel's Afrika Korps; halted Axis expansion in North Africa and the Middle East. Paired with Stalingrad as the moment when the Axis definitively lost the strategic initiative.

**Battle of Stalingrad**
August 1942–February 1943 — pivotal WWII battle where Soviet forces surrounded and destroyed Germany's Sixth Army; Germany's first catastrophic defeat on the Eastern Front. One of history's deadliest battles (~2 million total casualties); turned the tide of the war in Europe.

**Battle of the Coral Sea**
May 1942 naval battle in the Pacific fought entirely by carrier-based aircraft; stopped the Japanese attempt to invade New Guinea and threaten Australia. First naval battle in history where opposing ships never directly sighted each other; demonstrated the dominance of aircraft carriers over battleships.

**Battle of Midway Island**
June 1942 decisive U.S. naval victory that destroyed four Japanese aircraft carriers; halted Japanese Pacific expansion and transferred naval initiative to the United States. Considered the turning point of the Pacific War.

**Guadalcanal**
Pacific island where the first major U.S. ground offensive against Japan took place (1942–43); prolonged land, sea, and air campaign ending in Allied victory. Proved the U.S. military could defeat Japan in sustained ground combat and opened the island-hopping campaign.

**island-hopping**
U.S. Pacific strategy of selectively seizing lightly defended Japanese-held islands while bypassing heavily fortified ones, cutting off supply lines and moving progressively toward Japan. Allowed the Allies to advance efficiently without attacking every fortified position.

**D-Day**
June 6, 1944 — the Allied amphibious invasion of German-occupied Normandy, France, under Supreme Allied Commander Eisenhower; ~150,000 troops landed. Opened the long-awaited Western Front; massive logistical achievement that led to the liberation of France and collapse of Nazi Germany.

**Battle of the Bulge**
December 1944–January 1945 — Germany's last major offensive on the Western Front, fought in the Ardennes Forest; initially pushed Allied lines back but was ultimately repulsed. Germany exhausted its last reserves, confirming Nazi Germany's inevitable defeat.

**Battle of Kursk**
July 1943 — largest tank battle in history, fought ~300 miles south of Moscow; Soviets successfully held and counterattacked, decisively breaking German Blitzkrieg. After Kursk, Germany was permanently on the defensive on the Eastern Front.

**V-E Day**
Victory in Europe Day — May 8, 1945; Germany's official unconditional surrender after Hitler's suicide and the fall of Berlin. Ended WWII in Europe after nearly six years.

**Hiroshima**
Japanese city where the United States dropped the first atomic bomb (August 6, 1945), killing ~70,000–80,000 people instantly with tens of thousands more dying from radiation. Began the nuclear age and precipitated Japan's decision to surrender.

**Nagasaki**
Japanese city where the United States dropped the second atomic bomb (August 9, 1945). Combined with Hiroshima, the bombings caused Japan's unconditional surrender and ended WWII. Created profound moral and strategic debates about nuclear weapons that continue to the present.

**V-J Day**
Victory over Japan Day — August 15 (announcement) / September 2, 1945 (formal surrender ceremonies). Japan's unconditional surrender ended WWII globally.

## Government: Treaties & Agreements

**[[Concept Notes/Nonaggression Pact|Nonaggression Pact]]**
The German-Soviet (Molotov-Ribbentrop) Pact of August 1939; pledged mutual non-aggression and secretly divided Eastern Europe into spheres of influence. Freed Hitler to invade Poland without a two-front war; Stalin violated it when Germany invaded the USSR in June 1941. See also [[Unit-7.6/Causes of World War II Overview\|Causes of World War II Overview]].

**Destroyers-for-Bases Agreement**
September 1940 deal: the U.S. transferred 50 destroyers to Britain in exchange for 99-year leases on eight British air and naval bases in the Western Hemisphere. Broke U.S. neutrality before Pearl Harbor; demonstrated Roosevelt's commitment to aiding Britain.

**[[Concept Notes/Atlantic Charter|Atlantic Charter]]**
August 1941 joint declaration by Roosevelt and Churchill stating Allied war aims: no territorial aggrandizement, self-determination for peoples, free trade, freedom of the seas, and disarmament of aggressor nations. Foundational document for post-war international order; influenced the UN Charter.

## Government: Leaders

**Winston Churchill**
British Prime Minister (1940–1945); rallied British morale through the Blitz and Battle of Britain with stirring speeches; forged the Anglo-American alliance; his refusal to surrender was decisive in preventing total German domination of Europe.

**Erwin Rommel**
German field marshal known as the "Desert Fox"; commanded German forces in North Africa (1941–1943); his tactical brilliance threatened Egypt and the Suez Canal. Ultimately defeated at El Alamein; respected by both sides as a skilled commander.

**Douglas MacArthur**
U.S. Army general commanding Allied forces in the Pacific; devised the island-hopping strategy; liberated the Philippines; presided over Japan's formal surrender ceremony; later commanded UN forces in the Korean War.

## Technology: Warfare

**blitzkrieg**
German military tactic ("lightning war") using fast-moving tanks and aircraft in coordinated attacks; defeated France in 6 weeks (1940) and Poland in 4; challenged at Kursk (1943) where Soviet defensive lines absorbed and counterattacked the advance.

**Luftwaffe**
Germany's air force under the Nazi regime; gained combat experience in the Spanish Civil War; a powerful weapon in WWII Blitzkrieg campaigns; its failure in the Battle of Britain (1940) was Hitler's first major strategic setback.

**aircraft carriers**
Warships with flight decks allowing planes to take off and land at sea; revolutionized naval warfare in WWII by extending air power far beyond coastal ranges. Demonstrated superiority over battleships at Coral Sea and Midway; central to Allied strategy in the Pacific theater.

> [!tip]
> A useful AP framework: **WWII turning points by theater** — European: El Alamein + Stalingrad (1942), D-Day (1944); Pacific: Coral Sea + Midway (1942); Eastern Front: Kursk (1943). Each halted Axis momentum and transferred initiative to the Allies.
