## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

Martin Luther's 95 Theses (c. 1517) are best understood as a response to

A) the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople and the spread of Islam into Christian Europe  
B) the corruption within the Catholic Church, particularly the sale of indulgences and church offices  
C) the political ambitions of German princes seeking to weaken the Holy Roman Emperor  
D) the failure of the Catholic Church to stop the Black Death plague

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — the Ottoman conquest (1453) predated Luther and wasn't his focus.
- **B is correct** — Luther's core objection was the sale of indulgences (buying forgiveness) and simony.
- C is wrong — German princes *exploited* the Reformation; Luther didn't write the 95 Theses for their benefit.
- D is wrong — the Black Death (14th century) was a background pressure on the Church's credibility, but it's not what the 95 Theses addressed.

**Question 2**

The Peace of Westphalia (1648) was historically significant because it

A) ended all religious conflict in Europe and established complete toleration for all faiths  
B) united all German states under a single Catholic monarchy  
C) increased state autonomy within the Holy Roman Empire and allowed rulers to choose among Catholicism, Lutheranism, and Calvinism  
D) established the supremacy of papal authority over secular rulers in matters of religion

**Answer: C**
- A is wrong — it didn't end all conflict or establish full toleration; it allowed three specific choices.
- B is wrong — it *fragmented* the Holy Roman Empire rather than unifying it.
- **C is correct** — the Peace gave states autonomy in religious choice, weakening the Holy Roman Empire.
- D is wrong — it actually *reduced* papal influence by empowering secular rulers.

**Question 3**

Akbar's religious policies in the Mughal Empire are best described as

A) enforcing Sunni Islam as the sole legitimate religion, similar to the Safavid approach  
B) tolerating all faiths, giving land and money to Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, and even supporting a Catholic church  
C) requiring all subjects to convert to his syncretic Din-i Ilahi religion  
D) discriminating against Hindus by reinstating the jizya (poll tax) on non-Muslims

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — that describes the Safavids; Akbar was tolerant.
- **B is correct** — Akbar practiced broad religious tolerance, granting resources to multiple faiths.
- C is wrong — Akbar promoted Din-i Ilahi but didn't require conversion; it failed anyway.
- D is wrong — Akbar *abolished* the jizya; Aurangzeb *reinstated* it.

**Question 4**

The emergence of Sikhism in India during the Mughal period is best understood as

A) a politically motivated religion created by Akbar to unite his diverse empire  
B) a syncretic faith combining Hindu and Sufi Muslim elements that developed in the Punjab region  
C) a reformation movement within Islam rejecting Sufi practices  
D) a foreign religion imported to India via the Indian Ocean trade network

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — Sikhism was not created by Akbar; it developed independently.
- **B is correct** — Sikhism combined Hindu and Sufi Islamic influences; it's definitionally syncretic.
- C is wrong — it drew from Islam but was not a reform movement within Islam.
- D is wrong — Sikhism originated in India, not from external trade.

**Question 5**

The Gutenberg printing press (c. 1450) most directly contributed to the Protestant Reformation by

A) allowing European rulers to print money and fund religious wars  
B) enabling the rapid spread of Luther's ideas and translated Bibles, making religious debate accessible to literate Europeans  
C) helping the Catholic Church publish and distribute the Index of Prohibited Books  
D) creating a new merchant class that resented Church taxation

**Answer: B**
- A is wrong — the press printed text, not currency.
- **B is correct** — the press allowed Luther's 95 Theses to spread rapidly; it made vernacular Bibles accessible.
- C is wrong — the Index was a *response* to the Reformation, and the Church used the press too, but this isn't the primary contribution.
- D is wrong — the merchant class existed before the press; this conflates two different developments.

## Short Answer Questions

**SAQ 1 — Causation**

Explain ONE cause of the Protestant Reformation in the period c. 1450–1550.

*Sample Response:* One major cause of the Protestant Reformation was the **corruption of the Catholic Church**, particularly the sale of indulgences. Indulgences were payments made to the Church in exchange for remission of the punishments for sin — in effect, purchasing divine forgiveness. Church officials also sold ecclesiastical positions (simony), making religious leadership dependent on wealth rather than spiritual merit. These practices were seen as deeply contradictory to biblical teachings, which emphasized faith and humility. When Martin Luther published his 95 Theses c. 1517, challenging these practices in the name of "sola fide" (faith alone), he articulated what many Christians already believed — that the Church had strayed from its spiritual mission.

---

**SAQ 2 — Comparison**

Briefly explain ONE similarity and ONE difference between the religious policies of the Safavid Empire and the Mughal Empire in the period 1450–1750.

*Sample Similarity:* Both the Safavid and Mughal empires used religion as a tool of **political legitimization**. The Safavid shah used Shi'a Islam as an ideological foundation that distinguished his empire from Sunni rivals and unified his Persian subjects under a single religious identity. Similarly, the Mughal emperor Akbar used religious policy — whether through tolerance, patronage, or eventually the promotion of Din-i Ilahi — to legitimize his rule over a religiously diverse population.

*Sample Difference:* The Safavids imposed **mandatory Shi'a Islam** and denied legitimacy to Sunnis, while the early Mughal Empire under Akbar practiced broad **religious tolerance**, giving money and land to Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, and even Christians. The Safavid approach was exclusionary and theocratic; the Mughal approach (at least under Akbar) was pluralistic and pragmatic.

---

**SAQ 3 — Continuity and Change**

Explain ONE continuity and ONE change in the role of Christianity in Europe from the period c. 1200–1450 (Unit 1/2) to the period c. 1450–1750 (Unit 3).

*Sample Continuity:* Throughout both periods, **Christianity remained the dominant legitimizing ideology** for European rulers. In the earlier period, popes claimed authority over kings, and the Crusades mobilized European armies in the name of Christian faith. In the later period, rulers from Louis XIV to Henry VIII still grounded their authority in Christian claims — divine right of kings, God's appointment, the Church's blessing. The idea that political power required religious sanction was a deep continuity.

*Sample Change:* In the period 1450–1750, Christianity **fractured into competing denominations** — Catholic, Lutheran, Calvinist, and Anglican — in a way that had no precedent in the earlier unified medieval Church. The Protestant Reformation (c. 1517 onward) ended the Church's monopoly on Western Christianity and triggered decades of religious warfare (Thirty Years' War, French Wars of Religion). Rulers now had to navigate *which* Christianity to support rather than simply enforcing a single Church's authority.

## LEQ Guidance

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 3.3:**

- "Evaluate the extent to which religious conflict shaped the political development of empires in the period 1450–1750."
- "Evaluate the extent to which the Protestant Reformation caused significant change in European society in the period 1517–1648."

**Strong evidence to use:**
- Luther's 95 Theses (1517) and the theology of sola fide
- Gutenberg press enabling spread of Reformation ideas
- Peace of Augsburg (1555) — states choosing religion
- Thirty Years' War and Peace of Westphalia (1648) — massive destruction, state autonomy
- Akbar's religious tolerance vs. Aurangzeb's intolerance and their consequences
- Safavid Shi'a state religion and Ottoman-Safavid conflict
- Edict of Nantes (1598) and its revocation (1685)

**Common mistakes:**
- Confusing the **Council of Trent** (Catholic reform) with the **Peace of Augsburg** (political settlement)
- Saying Sikhism was created by Akbar — Sikhism developed independently, though Akbar gave it land grants
- Forgetting that the Protestant Reformation had **social consequences** (women's literacy, end of convent leadership for Protestant women) not just political ones
- Missing that the **Scientific Revolution** grew *out of* this same period of religious disruption
