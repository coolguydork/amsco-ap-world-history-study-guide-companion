# Topic 3.3 — Key Terms and People

---

## The Protestant Reformation

**Martin Luther** — German monk; nailed the 95 Theses to a church door in Wittenberg c. 1517; excommunicated 1521; argued for "sola fide" (faith alone); founded Lutheranism.

**95 Theses** — Luther's list of charges against the Catholic Church, particularly the sale of indulgences and simony; launched the Protestant Reformation.

**indulgences** — Payments made to the Catholic Church to receive absolution from the punishments for sin; the sale of indulgences was a major target of Luther's criticism.

**simony** — The selling of Church offices; another target of Reformation critics.

**sola fide** — "Faith alone" — Luther's theological argument that salvation comes through faith in God, not through Church intermediaries or purchased absolution.

**John Calvin** — French theologian who broke with Catholicism c. 1530; developed Calvinism; emphasized predestination; helped reform Geneva.

**predestination** — Calvin's doctrine that God had already decided who was among the "elect" (destined for salvation) before birth; believers showed their election through pious, hard-working lives.

**elect** — In Calvinist theology, those predestined by God to be saved.

**Puritans** — English Calvinists who wanted to "purify" the Church of England of Catholic remnants; later settled in Massachusetts Bay Colony.

**Huguenots** — French Calvinist Protestants; subject of decades of religious war with French Catholics; protected by the Edict of Nantes (1598); expelled after its revocation (1685).

**Protestant Reformation** — The collective movement of 16th-century religious reformers — Lutherans, Calvinists, Anglicans, and others — who broke with the Roman Catholic Church.

**Anglican Church** — Church of England, established by Henry VIII when he broke with Rome to seek an annulment; became a state church with the monarch as its head.

**Henry VIII** (ruled 1509–1547) — English king; broke with Rome over papal refusal to annul his marriage; established the Church of England.

**Anne Boleyn** — Henry VIII's second wife; her marriage prompted the break with Rome.

**Charles V** — Holy Roman Emperor; tried to suppress the Protestant Reformation; eventually abdicated in 1555; divided his territories between his son (Philip II of Spain) and his brother.

**Philip II** — King of Spain; led Catholic crusade to Netherlands; sent the Spanish Armada (1588) against England; failed to conquer England.

**Spanish Armada** — Spanish naval fleet defeated by English naval power in 1588; marked the limits of Spanish Catholic power in Europe.

## Wars of Religion

**Peace of Augsburg (1555)** — Treaty ending conflict between Lutherans and the Holy Roman Empire; each German state could choose Catholicism or Lutheranism; subjects had to follow the state's religion or relocate.

**Edict of Nantes (1598)** — Issued by Henry IV of France; granted Huguenots religious toleration for 87 years; revoked by Louis XIV in 1685.

**Thirty Years' War (1618–1648)** — Final great Catholic-Protestant war; began as religious conflict in the Holy Roman Empire; became a broader European power struggle; caused widespread famine and economic devastation.

**Peace of Westphalia (1648)** — Ended the Thirty Years' War; allowed each Holy Roman Empire area to choose Catholicism, Lutheranism, or Calvinism; gave states greater autonomy; weakened the Holy Roman Empire as a unified institution.

---

## Counter-Reformation

**Counter-Reformation** — The Catholic Church's vigorous response to the Protestant Reformation; used the Inquisition, Jesuits, and Council of Trent to fight Protestant expansion.

**Inquisition** — Church institution established in the late 12th century to identify and punish heresy; expanded during the Counter-Reformation; sometimes used torture.

**Jesuits** (Society of Jesus) — Religious order founded in 1540 by **Ignatius of Loyola**; opposed Protestantism; conducted missionary work in Spanish colonies, Japan, and India.

**Council of Trent (1545–1563)** — Catholic council that corrected abuses (simony, indulgences), reaffirmed Catholic sacraments and rituals, and published the *Index of Prohibited Books*.

---

## Russia and the Church

**Holy Synod** — Body created by Peter the Great to replace the Orthodox patriarch; composed of clergymen overseen by a secular official answering to the tsar; incorporated the Church into the Russian state.

---

## Islamic Belief Systems

**shariah** — Islamic legal system governing criminal justice, marital laws, and inheritance; replaced Byzantine Justinian Law in the Ottoman Empire after 1453.

**Sikhism** — Monotheistic religion founded in the Punjab region of India; combines Hindu and Sufi Muslim elements; recognized rights of other faiths; world's fifth most popular religion by the 21st century.

**Din-i Ilahi** ("divine faith") — Akbar's attempt to create a syncretic religion reconciling Hinduism and Islam; failed to gain widespread acceptance; died with him in 1605.

**sati** — Hindu ritual in which widows killed themselves by jumping on their husbands' funeral pyres; Akbar unsuccessfully tried to prohibit it.

---

## The Scientific Revolution

**empiricism** — Francis Bacon's scientific method (1620) requiring observable, testable evidence to support any hypothesis; challenged traditional religious-based knowledge claims.

**Francis Bacon** — English scientist and philosopher; developed empiricism; argued evidence must back up hypotheses.

**Isaac Newton** — Published *Principia* (1687); synthesized Galileo's terrestrial motion laws and Kepler's planetary laws into a unified theory of gravity; foundational to the Scientific Revolution and later Enlightenment.
