# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 3.3: Empires: Belief Systems

---

## Background / Pre-Period

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 632 CE | Death of Muhammad; Sunni-Shia split begins | Dispute over succession → Abu Bakr (Sunni) vs. Ali (Shia); lays groundwork for Ottoman-Safavid rivalry |
| 1054 | Great Schism | Christianity splits into Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox; divided Christianity weakens unified religious authority before Reformation |
| 1309–1377 | Babylonian Captivity of the papacy | Papacy moves to France under French royal influence; damages Church credibility; empowers critics |
| Late 1300s–early 1400s | Wycliffe (England), Hus (Bohemia) challenge Church | Pre-Reformation critics lay intellectual groundwork; Hus burned at the stake (1415) |

## Period Core (1450–1750)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1453 | Ottoman conquest of Constantinople | Byzantine Empire ends; Islam replaces Orthodox Christianity in the region; shariah replaces Justinian Law |
| c. 1499 | Guru Nanak founds Sikhism in the Punjab | New syncretic monotheism from Hindu-Muslim contact; 5th largest world religion today |
| 1501 | Shah Ismail I imposes Twelver Shia Islam on Persia | Safavid Empire becomes Shia state; intensifies Sunni-Shia rivalry with the Ottomans |
| 1517 | Martin Luther posts 95 Theses | Protestant Reformation begins; fractures Western Christianity |
| 1521 | Pope Leo X excommunicates Luther; German princes adopt Lutheranism | Reformation becomes politically entrenched; princes gain church lands and independence from Rome |
| 1530 | John Calvin breaks with Rome | Calvinism begins; Geneva becomes center of Reformed Christianity |
| 1534 | Henry VIII establishes Church of England | Anglicanism born from political/dynastic, not theological, motives |
| 1540 | Jesuits founded by Ignatius of Loyola | Counter-Reformation's missionary arm; spreads Catholicism globally |
| 1545–1563 | Council of Trent | Catholic Church corrects abuses and hardens doctrine; Counter-Reformation institutionalized |
| 1555 | Peace of Augsburg — *cuius regio, eius religio* | German princes choose religion for their territory; Lutheranism given legal standing |
| 1556–1605 | Akbar rules Mughal Empire | Removes jizya; promotes tolerance; sponsors Sikhism and Din-i-Ilahi; peak of Mughal religious syncretism |
| 1588 | Spanish Armada defeated by England | Philip II's Catholic reconquest of England fails; Protestantism secured in England |
| 1593 | Henry IV converts to Catholicism | "Paris is well worth a Mass" — pragmatic use of religion to unify France |
| 1598 | Edict of Nantes | French Huguenots granted limited religious freedom; temporary truce in French religious wars |
| 1605 | Akbar dies | Din-i-Ilahi dissolves; later Mughal rulers reverse tolerant policies |
| 1618–1648 | Thirty Years' War | Last great European religious war; devastating for Holy Roman Empire |
| 1648 | Peace of Westphalia | Adds Calvinism to legal options; increases German princely autonomy; proto-modern state system |
| 1682–1725 | Peter the Great rules Russia | Abolishes patriarch; creates Holy Synod; Church subordinated to state |
| 1685 | Louis XIV revokes Edict of Nantes | Huguenot exodus; skilled craftsmen leave France; economic and social disruption |

## AP Chronology Notes

> [!exam-tip]
> **Critical sequence to know:** 1453 (Ottoman conquest) → 1499 (Sikhism) → 1501 (Safavid Shia) → 1517 (Luther) → 1534 (Henry VIII) → 1555 (Augsburg) → 1556 (Akbar) → 1598 (Nantes) → 1618–1648 (Thirty Years' War) → 1648 (Westphalia). These events are all testable individually and as part of broader CCOT arguments.

> [!tip]
> **Overlap to notice:** Akbar's reign (1556–1605) overlaps almost exactly with the most intense period of European religious wars. While Europe was tearing itself apart over Christianity, the Mughal Empire was experimenting with religious synthesis. This contrast is a powerful comparative argument for the AP exam.

> [!warning]
> Don't confuse: **Peace of Augsburg** (1555, ends first German religious war, Lutheranism only) vs. **Peace of Westphalia** (1648, ends Thirty Years' War, adds Calvinism). Students routinely mix these up. Key difference: Augsburg = two options (Catholic/Lutheran); Westphalia = three options (adds Calvinist).
