# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 9 — Institutions Developing in a Globalized World | AP Period 4 | AMSCO pp. 696–706**

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1863 | International Red Cross founded | Earliest major international humanitarian NGO; predates UN by 80+ years |
| 1891 | International Peace Bureau founded | One of the world's oldest peace organizations; Nobel Peace Prize 1910 |
| 1920 | League of Nations established | First attempt at collective security; US never joined; failed to prevent WWII |
| 1944 | Bretton Woods Conference; World Bank & IMF created | Economic governance architecture created before UN itself; reflects Allied intent to prevent economic nationalism that contributed to WWII |
| Oct 24, 1945 | United Nations founded (51 member states) | Replaced League of Nations; UN Day still observed annually |
| 1945 | UNESCO created | Repair WWII cultural damage; later literacy, World Heritage Sites |
| 1948 | Universal Declaration of Human Rights adopted | Eleanor Roosevelt led drafting; first global human rights standard; 500+ language translations |
| 1948 | First UN peacekeeping mission (Arab-Israeli conflict, Palestine) | Established the peacekeeping model; 72+ missions have followed |
| 1961 | World Food Program established | UN food aid; first missions in Iran, Thailand, Algeria 1962; 1.5 billion+ fed since |
| 1963 | Organization of African Unity founded | African states' first collective governance institution; became African Union 2002 |
| 1988 | UN has 5 active peacekeeping operations | Baseline before Cold War's end triggered explosion of new missions |
| 1993 | UN peaks at 28 active peacekeeping missions | Post-Cold War expansion as regional conflicts erupted |
| 1994 | Rwanda genocide — 800,000 killed in 100 days | UN peacekeepers present but unable to intervene; defining failure of international institutions |
| 1994 | Palau becomes independent — last UN trust territory | Trusteeship Council suspends operations; decolonization mission complete |
| 1997 | Kofi Annan becomes UN Secretary-General | First from within UN staff; Nobel Peace Prize 2001; "responsibility to protect" doctrine |
| c. 1970s–present | NGO explosion (Amnesty, MSF, Greenpeace, HRW) | Non-state actors take on governance roles; parallel system to intergovernmental institutions |
| 2000 | UN Millennium Development Goals announced | 8 goals to reduce poverty, disease, inequality by 2015 |
| 2015 | UN Sustainable Development Goals (17 goals) | Extended MDG framework through 2030; climate, inequality, education, health |
| 2018 | IMF & World Bank publish "Pathways for Peace" | Coordinated framework for preventing violent conflicts |
| 2019 | 193 UN member states; 102,000 peacekeepers in 15 missions | Growth from 51 founding members; peacekeeping forces peaked 2014 at 95,000 |

## Cause → Effect Chain

**WWI (1914–1918)** → League of Nations created (1920) — too weak, US absent → **WWII (1939–1945)** → [[Concept Notes/United Nations\|United Nations]] created (1945), Bretton Woods institutions (IMF, World Bank) → **Cold War deadlocks** Security Council through veto mechanism → **Post-Cold War peacekeeping surge** (1988–1993: 5 to 28 missions) → **Rwanda failure (1994)** exposes limits → **UN reform debates (1990s–2000s)** → "Responsibility to Protect" doctrine → **21st-century multilateral challenges** (terrorism, climate, pandemic)

---

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam loves the League vs. UN comparison. Key contrasts: (1) US joined UN, not League; (2) UN has Security Council with enforcement power; (3) UN has specialized agencies; (4) League lacked major-power participation. Both failed to prevent some conflicts, but UN's specialized agencies (WHO, UNICEF, WFP) succeeded in humanitarian domains the League never attempted.

> [!tip]
> Peacekeeping numbers (5 in 1988 → 28 in 1993 → 15 larger missions in 2019) tell a story about changing post-Cold War priorities. The Cold War paralyzed Security Council action; once it ended, the US and Russia could agree enough to authorize missions. The shift from many small missions to fewer larger ones reflects learning from Rwanda and Bosnia.
