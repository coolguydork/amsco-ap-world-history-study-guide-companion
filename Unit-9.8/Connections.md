# Connections

**Unit 9 — Institutions Developing in a Globalized World | AP Period 4 | AMSCO pp. 696–706**

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-7.3/Conducting World War I Overview\|Conducting World War I Overview]]** (AMSCO-cited)
WWI's devastation and the Paris Peace Conference created the League of Nations — the UN's direct predecessor and cautionary model. The League's structural failures (US absence, no enforcement mechanism) directly shaped how the UN was designed in 1945.

**[[Unit-8.1/Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview\|Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview]]**
WWII's destruction and the Allied coalition formed the political context for the UN's creation. The same wartime alliance that defeated the Axis — US, UK, France, USSR, China — became the five permanent Security Council members. The post-war order the UN was meant to stabilize is built in 8.1.

**[[Unit-9.4/Economics in the Global Age Overview\|Economics in the Global Age Overview]]**
The IMF and World Bank were created at the 1944 Bretton Woods Conference alongside the structures that became the WTO and GATT [[Unit-9.4/Economics in the Global Age Overview|Economics in the Global Age Overview]]. UN economic institutions are the governance framework within which economic globalization operates — and the target of critique by Global South nations in 9.4.

**[[Unit-8.5/Decolonization after 1900 Overview\|Decolonization after 1900 Overview]]** (AMSCO-cited)
The Trusteeship Council supervised the final transitions from colonial rule to independence. The explosion of new UN member states from decolonization (51 in 1945 → 193 by 2019) fundamentally changed the General Assembly's composition and shifted debates toward Global South concerns.

## Leads To

The institutions built in 9.8 shape the environment in which 9.9 (20th/21st-century synthesis) takes place. The UN framework, IMF conditionality, and NGO networks are the operating context for every major global issue discussed in synthesis topics — climate change, migration, pandemic response, and nuclear proliferation all run through the institutions created here.

## Compare With

**[[Unit-9.5/Calls for Reform & Responses Overview\|Calls for Reform & Responses Overview]]**
9.5 examines civil society reform movements and international rights advocacy; 9.8 examines the intergovernmental institutions those movements pressure. Eleanor Roosevelt and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights appear in both topics — the UDHR is both a UN product ([[Unit-9.8/Institutions Developing in a Globalized World Overview|9.8]]) and a tool used by reform movements ([[Unit-9.5/Calls for Reform & Responses Overview|9.5]]). NGOs like Human Rights Watch bridge both topics directly.

**League of Nations [[Unit-7.3/Conducting World War I Overview|Conducting World War I Overview]] vs. United Nations [[Unit-9.8/Institutions Developing in a Globalized World Overview|Institutions Developing in a Globalized World Overview]]**
The most classic AP comparison in this topic. Same goal (prevent war through collective security), opposite outcomes (League failed; UN more durable). Key variables: US membership, Security Council veto, specialized agencies, breadth of membership. A comparison essay on this topic will likely appear on every AP exam cycle.
