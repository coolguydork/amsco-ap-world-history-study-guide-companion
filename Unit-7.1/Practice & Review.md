**Unit 7 — Global Conflict** | AP Period 4 | AMSCO pp. 458–459

## Multiple-Choice Practice

**Questions 1–3** refer to the following passage:

> "China is now suffering from poverty, not from unequal distribution of wealth. Where there are inequalities of wealth, the methods of Marx can, of course, be used — a class war can be advocated to destroy the inequalities. But in China, where industry is not yet developed, Marx's class war and dictatorship of the proletariat are impracticable."
>
> — Sun Yat-sen, *Capital and State*, 1924

---

**1.** Which leader would be most likely to argue that his country faced the same problems Sun identified in China?

(A) King Leopold II of Belgium
(B) Cecil Rhodes of Rhodesia
(C) Vladimir Lenin of Russia
(D) Emiliano Zapata of Mexico

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Zapata led a movement in a largely non-industrialized, agrarian Mexico where the primary problem was land inequality and poverty — not the class conflict between industrial owners and workers that Marx described. Like Sun, Zapata's focus was on land redistribution and peasant welfare rather than proletarian class war. (A) and (B) are colonial exploiters, not leaders of impoverished countries. (C) Lenin argued that the dictatorship of the proletariat *was* practical and pursued it in Russia.

---

**2.** How did Sun Yat-sen's ideas compare to those of Karl Marx?

(A) They agreed that the dictatorship of the proletariat was practical in all countries.
(B) They agreed that class war was the most effective path to economic progress.
(C) They disagreed on whether countries should industrialize.
(D) They disagreed on whether the unequal distribution of wealth was a problem.

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Sun explicitly argues China does *not* suffer from unequal wealth distribution — it suffers from general poverty. Marx believed unequal wealth distribution under capitalism was *the* central problem. (A) is wrong — Sun says the dictatorship of the proletariat is impracticable in China. (B) is wrong — Sun rejects class war as a solution. (C) is not the passage's focus and misreads both thinkers.

---

**3.** Sun's relationship with Confucianism is best summarized by which statement?

(A) Sun was less concerned about economic inequality than Confucius was.
(B) Sun was less supportive of democracy than Confucius was.
(C) Sun thought that the Chinese should continue to study the teachings of Confucius.
(D) Sun thought that the Chinese should reject the teachings of Confucius.

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> AMSCO states that Sun "believed that China should continue to follow such Confucian principles as loyalty, respect for ancestors, and efforts to promote social harmony," blending these with his Three People's Principles. He did not reject Confucianism but selectively integrated it with modern political thought. (A) and (B) are not supported by the passage or AMSCO's narrative.

## Short-Answer Practice

**SAQ 1** uses the following paired passages:

> **Source A:** "Take up the White Man's burden—Send forth the best ye breed— / Go bind your sons to exile, to serve your captives' need / To wait in heavy harness, on fluttered folk and wild— / Your new-caught, sullen peoples, Half-devil and half-child."
>
> — Rudyard Kipling, "The White Man's Burden," 1899

> **Source B:** "What the partial occupation of his soil by the white man has failed to do… what the lash, have failed to do… the power of modern capitalistic exploitation, assisted by modern engines of destruction, may yet succeed in accomplishing. For from the evils of the latter, scientifically applied and enforced, there is no escape for the African… It kills not the body merely, but the soul. It wrecks his polity, uproots him from the land, invades his family life, destroys his natural pursuits and occupations, claims his whole time, enslaves him in his own home."
>
> — Edward D. Morel, *The Black Man's Burden*, 1920

**(A)** Identify ONE way in which the viewpoints of Kipling and Morel differ.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Kipling portrays imperialism as a humanitarian duty — the "burden" that civilized Western nations bear to uplift supposedly primitive peoples. Morel, by contrast, argues that capitalist imperialism destroys African societies at every level — economically, socially, politically, and spiritually — calling it a form of enslavement rather than uplift. Where Kipling uses the language of service and civilization, Morel uses the language of exploitation and soul-destruction.

**(B)** Explain how ONE important thinker or leader from Latin America from the 18th through 20th century would have responded to Morel's view of capitalism.

> [!tip] Model Response — Latin America
> Emiliano Zapata of Mexico would have largely agreed with Morel's critique of capitalist exploitation. Zapata led a revolutionary movement against the economic system that had dispossessed Mexican peasants — a system in which foreign investors (primarily American) and domestic elites controlled 97% of the land while the majority worked as landless laborers. Zapata's demand for land redistribution was a direct rejection of the capitalist property system Morel criticizes, though Zapata focused on domestic exploitation rather than European colonialism specifically. His slogan "Land and Liberty" articulated the same argument Morel makes: that capitalist control "uproots him from the land" and "destroys his natural pursuits."

**(C)** Explain how ONE important thinker or leader from Eurasia from the 18th through 20th century would have responded to Morel's view of capitalism.

> [!tip] Model Response — Eurasia
> Vladimir Lenin would have enthusiastically agreed with Morel's critique and gone further. Lenin's theory of imperialism (developed in his 1917 pamphlet *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism*) argued that European capitalist nations exported capital and exploited colonial labor precisely because workers at home had organized for better conditions — driving investment abroad to find cheaper, unprotected labor. From Lenin's perspective, Morel correctly identifies the symptoms (exploitation, destruction of local society) but the cure is not reform — it is communist revolution to eliminate capitalism entirely.

**SAQ 2:** Answer all parts of the question.

**(A)** Identify ONE historical situation in the period after 1900 in which a new, less diverse state replaced an old, more diverse empire.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identification
> The Republic of Turkey (1923) replaced the multiethnic Ottoman Empire, which had encompassed Turks, Arabs, Armenians, Greeks, Kurds, and dozens of other ethnic and religious groups. The new Turkish state was defined by Turkish ethnic nationalism and promoted Turkification — the cultural and linguistic assimilation of all citizens into a Turkish-Islamic identity. This made it far less ethnically and religiously diverse than the empire it replaced.

**(B)** Explain ONE long-term effect of the Russian Empire's collapse after 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Long-Term Effect
> The Russian Empire's collapse in 1917 and its replacement by the Soviet Union under the Bolsheviks created the world's first communist state, which became the ideological and geopolitical rival to Western capitalism for the next 70 years. The Soviet model inspired communist movements in China, Vietnam, Cuba, and elsewhere, and the resulting Cold War between the U.S. and USSR shaped decolonization, proxy wars, and international institutions throughout the 20th century. The long-term effect was thus not merely Russia's domestic transformation but a fundamental restructuring of global power and ideology.

**(C)** Explain ONE example of states challenging existing social order in the period after 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Social Order Challenge
> The Bolshevik government in Russia after 1917 fundamentally challenged the existing social order by abolishing private property in industry and land, eliminating the aristocracy and bourgeoisie as legal classes, and nationalizing the economy under state control. Where the tsarist order had maintained rigid class hierarchy with the nobility at the top and peasants at the bottom, the Bolsheviks declared all citizens workers and seized the means of production on their behalf. This was among the most radical challenges to a social order in modern history, and it explicitly aimed to eliminate class distinction rather than merely redistribute wealth within the existing system.
