# Key Terms & Concepts

**Unit 7 — Global Conflict** | AP Period 4 | AMSCO pp. 453–460

---

**Bolshevik**
Revolutionary Marxist party led by Vladimir Lenin that seized power in Russia in October 1917 and established the world's first communist government. The name means "majority" in Russian. Their success inspired communist movements globally and intensified anti-communist fears in capitalist nations — planting the seed of the 20th century's defining ideological conflict.

**communists**
Those who believe workers should collectively own the means of production so that economic output benefits all rather than private owners. The [[Concept Notes/Bolsheviks\|Bolsheviks]] were the first communists to govern a major nation, transforming Russia into the Soviet Union and making communism vs. capitalism a defining tension of 20th-century geopolitics.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP distinguishes between **Marxist theory** (workers eventually own the means of production through historical stages) and **Bolshevik practice** (violent revolutionary seizure of power, immediate nationalization). Marx predicted revolution would begin in industrialized countries; Lenin applied it to agrarian Russia.

**Young Turks**
Reform movement within the declining Ottoman Empire that advocated for a Western-style constitution and promoted Turkification to unify the empire's many ethnic groups under a Turkish-Islamic identity. Their scapegoating of Christian minorities contributed to the Armenian [[Concept Notes/Genocide\|Genocide]]. By allying with Germany in WWI they accelerated the empire's collapse, inadvertently creating the conditions for Turkish national independence.

**Turkification**
Ottoman policy promoted by the Young Turks requiring all citizens of the multiethnic empire to identify with Turkish culture and Islam. Particularly affected Christian minorities such as Armenians, who were scapegoated for Ottoman economic decline and subjected to mass violence. Turkification was the ideological engine behind the Armenian Genocide (1915–1916) and represents a broader pattern of ethnic nationalism weaponized against minorities.

> [!warning]
> **Common misconception:** The Young Turks were *reformers* at first — they wanted a constitution and modernization. Their turn toward ethnic nationalism and scapegoating of Armenians came as the empire weakened under external pressure. Reform movements can become authoritarian when they feel threatened.

**Mexican Revolution**
Armed uprising (1910–1920) that overthrew dictator Porfirio Díaz. Triggered by his jailing of opposition candidate Madero, the revolution resulted in approximately 2 million deaths, a landmark 1917 constitution guaranteeing land redistribution, universal suffrage, and public education, and decades of PRI political dominance. It is a major AP case study in 20th-century political upheaval and the causes/effects of revolution.

**Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI)**
Mexican political party formed in 1929 that dominated the country's politics for over seven decades; all presidents were PRI members until 2000. Though widely criticized as corrupt, the PRI provided political stability after the chaos of the Mexican Revolution. Its monopoly represents a common post-revolutionary pattern of single-party rule — compare to China's Kuomintang or the Soviet Communist Party.

**Sun Yat-sen**
Chinese nationalist leader who led the 1911 revolution that overthrew the [[Concept Notes/Qing Dynasty\|Qing Dynasty]] and founded the Republic of China. His **Three People's Principles** — nationalism, democracy, and livelihood — guided Chinese reform thought for decades. He founded the Kuomintang (Nationalist Party) but held the presidency only briefly before yielding to a warlord, illustrating the difficulty of consolidating revolutionary power without military force.

**Kemal Atatürk**
Military leader who expelled Allied forces from Anatolia (1921) and founded the Republic of Turkey (1923) as its first president. Aggressively secularized and Westernized Turkey — expanding education for girls, abolishing polygyny, granting women's suffrage, adopting Western dress and the Latin alphabet — while ruling as an authoritarian dictator until his death in 1938. Given the surname Atatürk ("father of Turks") by the national assembly. A key example of top-down nationalist modernization.

**Porfirio Díaz**
Mexican dictator (1876–1911) who promoted economic modernization and foreign investment — especially from the United States — but allowed the wealthiest 1% to control 97% of the land, leaving most peasants landless. His jailing of Francisco Madero in 1910 sparked the Mexican Revolution and ultimately sent him into permanent exile. Díaz exemplifies the contradictions of Gilded Age–era authoritarian "development" that enriches elites while impoverishing majorities.

**Francisco Madero**
Mexican opposition politician whose arrest by Díaz in 1910 ignited the Mexican Revolution. Escaped to El Paso, Texas, organized revolutionary forces, and with Pancho Villa's military victories forced Díaz into exile. Madero represents the moderate, democratic wing of the revolution — he wanted constitutional reform, not radical social transformation. His later overthrow and murder by rival generals illustrates the revolution's violent instability.

**Francisco "Pancho" Villa**
Revolutionary military commander whose forces defeated Díaz's army in 1911, enabling Madero to take power. Became a legendary guerrilla leader and folk hero in northern Mexico. Represents the popular, peasant-based military dimension of the revolution — contrasted with Zapata's agrarian focus in the south and Madero's urban political liberalism.

**Emiliano Zapata**
Agrarian revolutionary leader in southern Mexico who carried out actual land redistribution to impoverished peasants during the Mexican Revolution. His slogan "Land and Liberty" embodied the revolution's core promise to the landless poor. One of the most iconic figures of the Mexican Revolution and a lasting symbol of peasant resistance and land rights — his image and ideology (Zapatismo) remain politically potent in Mexico today.
