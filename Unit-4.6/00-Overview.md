# Unit 4.6: Internal and External Challenges to State Power

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

## AP Exam Period
**Period 4: 1450–1750**

## Summary

The maritime empires established between 1450 and 1750 faced sustained challenges from both within and without. Internally, colonial subjects — Indigenous peoples, enslaved Africans, and even creole elites — resisted imperial authority through rebellion, flight, negotiation, and cultural preservation. Externally, European states competed intensely for colonial territory, with the Dutch, English, and French challenging Spanish and Portuguese dominance through privateering, war, and the establishment of rival colonies. Religious tensions (the Protestant Reformation's challenge to Catholic universalism) compounded political competition. Competing European powers used alliances with Indigenous peoples and enslaved Africans as tools of inter-imperial warfare. The result was that no maritime empire was ever fully secure — all faced constant internal resistance and external competition that shaped and reshaped their policies throughout the period.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Identify the major forms of resistance to colonial rule by Indigenous peoples and enslaved Africans.
- Explain how competition between European powers shaped the development of colonial empires.
- Analyze how religious and political conflicts within Europe affected overseas empires.
- Evaluate the successes and limits of resistance movements in this period.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.3.II** — Challenges to state power came from internal resistance and external competition.

> [!exam-tip]
> AP essays on resistance should address **multiple forms** — armed rebellion, flight (maroon communities), cultural preservation, negotiation — not just dramatic military revolts. Showing the range of resistance strategies demonstrates historical complexity.
