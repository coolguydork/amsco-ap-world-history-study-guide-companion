# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 4.6: (4.6): Internal and External Challenges to State Power

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Maritime Empires Established]]** ([[Unit-4.4]]) and **[[Maritime Empires Maintained]]** ([[Unit-4.5]]) — Resistance is a direct response to the empires built in 4.4 and 4.5; you cannot understand 4.6 without knowing what people were resisting.
- **[[Mongol Resistance and State Fragility]]** (Unit 2–3) — Empires have always faced internal and external challenges; the Mongol Empire's fragmentation is a useful precedent.
- **[[Protestant Reformation]]** (Unit 3) — Religious division within Europe fueled inter-imperial competition and gave Protestant powers (Dutch, English) ideological motivation to challenge Catholic Spain and Portugal.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Atlantic Revolutions]]** (Unit 5) — The Pueblo Revolt, Tupac Amaru II, and creole grievances are direct precursors to the American, French, Haitian, and Latin American revolutions.
- **[[Haitian Revolution]]** (Unit 5) — The tradition of enslaved African resistance culminates in the only successful slave revolt in history (1791–1804).
- **[[Abolition Movements]]** (Unit 5) — Maroon communities and slave resistance demonstrated that enslaved people rejected their condition, providing moral ammunition for abolitionists.
- **[[Decolonization]]** (Unit 9) — The long tradition of resistance established in this period feeds into 20th-century nationalist and decolonization movements.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Pueblo Revolt vs. Tupac Amaru II** — Both Indigenous, both in Spanish America; compare causes, scale, outcomes, and lasting consequences.
- **Maroon communities in Jamaica vs. Quilombo dos Palmares (Brazil)** — Compare scale, duration, and eventual fates.
- **Nzinga (African diplomatic resistance) vs. Pueblo peoples (armed revolt)** — Different regions, different methods; compare effectiveness and legacy.
- **Dutch commercial competition vs. English privateering** — Different forms of external challenge to Iberian dominance; compare methods and outcomes.

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Pueblo Revolt** is the most important single resistance event to know for AP World History. Know: date (1680), cause (forced labor + religious suppression), outcome (12 years of Spanish expulsion, then reconquest with concessions), and significance (demonstrates Indigenous agency and resistance capacity).
