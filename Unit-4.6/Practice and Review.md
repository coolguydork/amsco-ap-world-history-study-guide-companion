# Topic 4.6 – Practice and Review

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** Ana Nzinga's use of diplomacy, military resistance, and alliance with the Dutch against Portugal most directly illustrates which of the following?

- A) African rulers were unable to resist European colonization
- B) African leaders exercised significant political agency in navigating European rivalries
- C) The Dutch were generally more respectful of African sovereignty than the Portuguese
- D) Conversion to Christianity was the most effective strategy for African resistance

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Nzinga used every tool available — military force, diplomatic alliances, religious conversion as a bargaining chip, and offering freedom to runaway enslaved people — to protect her state. This shows sophisticated political agency, not passive victimhood.

---

**2.** The Pugachev Rebellion in Russia (1773–1775) most directly resulted from

- A) religious persecution of Orthodox Christians by Catherine the Great
- B) growing resentment among serfs, Cossacks, and ethnic minorities over serfdom and noble privilege
- C) the expansion of Russian territory into Siberia displacing indigenous peoples
- D) disputes over succession to the Russian throne

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Pugachev rallied serfs (oppressed by serfdom laws that bound them to the land), Cossacks (fiercely independent warriors resentful of central authority), and ethnic minorities (marginalized by Russian imperial expansion) under the false claim that he was the murdered Tsar Peter III, offering them freedom from noble oppression.

---

**3.** The Pueblo Revolt of 1680 most directly demonstrates that

- A) indigenous peoples universally cooperated with Spanish missionaries
- B) Spanish colonial authority was so strong that indigenous revolts were impossible
- C) indigenous peoples actively resisted colonial attempts to destroy their religious and cultural practices
- D) the Spanish were driven permanently from New Mexico

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The Pueblo and Apache revolted specifically because the Spanish were trying to force religious conversions, destroying indigenous spiritual practices. The revolt temporarily succeeded, though Spain reconquered the area in 1692.

**4.** The Glorious Revolution of 1688–1689 in England was most significant for future democratic development because it

- A) abolished the monarchy entirely
- B) established Parliament's power to limit royal authority and defined constitutional limits on kingship
- C) gave ordinary English citizens the right to vote
- D) created a written constitution for England similar to the later American Constitution

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Glorious Revolution established that Parliament could remove a monarch who violated its authority and set conditions (Protestant rule) on who could hold the throne. This precedent of limiting royal power through law influenced later democratic thinking.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Question**: Using specific evidence, explain how ONE rebellion or resistance movement in the period 1450–1750 challenged state power AND identify ONE limitation of that challenge.

**Model Response (using Maroon Wars)**:

**Challenge**: The Maroon communities in Jamaica successfully challenged British colonial authority by establishing independent settlements of escaped enslaved people who resisted colonial forces for decades. Led by Queen Nanny and other leaders, Jamaican Maroons used guerrilla warfare tactics in the mountainous interior of Jamaica to defeat British military expeditions. Their success was so significant that the British eventually negotiated peace treaties in the 1730s that acknowledged Maroon autonomy in certain territories — a remarkable concession from a colonial power to a community of formerly enslaved people.

**Limitation**: However, the Maroon challenge had significant limits. The peace treaties the Maroons signed required them to return future escaped enslaved people to the British, effectively making the Maroons complicit in maintaining slavery for those not already free. This compromise preserved Maroon autonomy but undermined broader resistance to the institution of slavery itself, fragmenting the potential unified resistance of enslaved people in Jamaica.

## Comparison: Resistance Movements

| Movement | Who Resisted | Against Whom | Method | Outcome |
|----------|-------------|-------------|--------|---------|
| Ana Nzinga's resistance | Ndongo/Matamba Africans | Portugal | Diplomacy + military + alliance-switching | Built strong independent state |
| Pugachev Rebellion | Russian serfs, Cossacks, ethnic minorities | Catherine the Great | Military uprising | Failed; increased oppression |
| Maratha wars | Hindu Marathas | Mughal Empire | Military campaigns (1680–1707) | Created Maratha Empire; ended Mughal dominance |
| Pueblo Revolt | Pueblo and Apache | Spanish colonizers | Military revolt, destroyed churches | Temporary success; reconquered 1692 |
| Maroon Wars | Escaped enslaved Africans | British colonizers | Guerrilla warfare | Partial autonomy; peace treaties |
| Metacom's War | Wampanoag (and others) | British colonizers | Military coalition | Failed; Wampanoag subjugated |
| Glorious Revolution | Parliament + Protestant nobles | James II | Political maneuvering + military threat | Success; constitutional monarchy |

> [!tip]
> Notice a pattern: rebellions against *colonial* authorities (Ana Nzinga, Pueblo Revolt, Maroon Wars) had more mixed or locally successful outcomes, while rebellions against *domestic* state power (Pugachev) generally failed and led to increased oppression. However, the Glorious Revolution — an elite-led internal challenge — succeeded. The key variable seems to be whether the challengers had enough social and military resources to force negotiation or structural change.

## LEQ Connections

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 4.6:**
- "Evaluate the extent to which resistance movements successfully challenged state power in the period 1450–1750."
- "Compare the methods used by different groups to resist European colonial expansion in the period 1500–1750."
- "Explain the causes of ONE major rebellion or resistance movement in the period 1450–1750."

**Key argument for "evaluate the extent" prompts**: Most resistance movements in this period had limited success. They rarely overthrew colonial systems. But they forced modifications (Spain modified its approach in New Mexico; Britain negotiated with Maroons), preserved cultural practices, and demonstrated that no empire had total control. They are evidence of human agency even in conditions of extreme oppression. The Glorious Revolution is an exception — an elite-driven internal challenge that fundamentally restructured political power.
