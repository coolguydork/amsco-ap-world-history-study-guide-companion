# Practice Questions — Unit 4.6: (4.6): Internal and External Challenges to State Power

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below for Questions 1–3.**

> *"[The Pueblo people] have been very much grieved by the labor they have had to perform for the Spaniards, because they did not pay them and the work was excessive… [They] have been deprived of their idols and dances and other customs… The cause of the uprising was because the Holy Faith was being introduced by force."*
> — Testimony of an Indigenous captive describing causes of the Pueblo Revolt, 1681

---

**1.** The passage identifies which of the following as primary causes of the Pueblo Revolt?

- A) Competition between Spanish settlers and Indigenous peoples over land
- B) Forced labor, unpaid work, and religious suppression
- C) Spanish failure to protect Pueblo peoples from Apache raids
- D) Pueblo peoples' rejection of Christianity for political rather than religious reasons

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The testimony specifically names excessive unpaid labor and forced religious conversion as the primary grievances driving the revolt.*

---

**2.** The Pueblo Revolt of 1680 is best understood as evidence of which of the following broader historical patterns?

- A) The inevitable failure of European colonial rule in the Americas
- B) The capacity of colonized peoples to successfully challenge imperial authority through organized resistance
- C) The weakness of Spanish military power relative to Indigenous forces
- D) The role of disease in undermining colonial governance

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Pueblo Revolt succeeded in expelling Spanish colonists for 12 years — demonstrating that organized Indigenous resistance could achieve real results, even if temporary.*

---

**3.** Which of the following best explains why Spain moderated some of its colonial policies in New Mexico after reconquering it in 1692?

- A) Spain was following the New Laws of 1542 which required more humane treatment of Indigenous peoples
- B) Resistance demonstrated that excessive exploitation was counterproductive to maintaining stable colonial rule
- C) The Catholic Church pressured Spain to reform its practices in the Americas
- D) Spain lacked the military resources to reimpose the same level of control

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The revolt showed Spain that its policies had produced an unstable, explosive situation; moderate concessions (allowing some traditional religious practices, reducing labor demands) were pragmatic responses to demonstrated resistance capacity.*

---

**4.** The establishment of maroon communities like Quilombo dos Palmares represents which of the following?

- A) Evidence that enslaved Africans accepted their condition and sought only modest improvements
- B) Collective resistance to slavery that created alternative social structures outside colonial control
- C) The failure of colonial governments to control enslaved populations
- D) African cultural practices that were incompatible with plantation labor

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Maroon communities were acts of collective resistance — escaped enslaved people built alternative societies with their own governance, economics, and culture, actively refusing the colonial order.*

---

**5.** England's use of privateers to attack Spanish treasure fleets in the 16th century is best understood as an example of:

- A) Illegal piracy condemned by the English government
- B) External competition between European powers over colonial wealth
- C) England's religious war against Catholic Spain
- D) English attempts to establish colonies in the Americas

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Privateering was state-sanctioned commercial warfare — English monarchs authorized Drake and others to attack Spanish ships in order to disrupt Spanish silver wealth and challenge Iberian dominance.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain how Indigenous peoples in the Americas challenged Spanish colonial authority in the period 1500–1750."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE form of resistance used by Indigenous peoples against Spanish colonial rule.
**Part B:** Explain ONE cause of Indigenous resistance in the period.
**Part C:** Evaluate the effectiveness of Indigenous resistance in limiting Spanish colonial power.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Choose a specific in-range example: Pueblo Revolt (1680), Mixtón War (1540s), Tupac Amaru I (1572), maroon communities (Quilombo dos Palmares, c.1600–1694), or cultural/religious resistance. Note: Tupac Amaru *II* (1780) is after 1750 and outside this topic's scope.
> - **Part B:** Forced labor (encomienda/mita), religious suppression, land theft, epidemic disease — be specific.
> - **Part C:** Balanced answer: resistance achieved some concessions (Spain modified New Mexico policies) but rarely reversed colonial rule entirely. The Pueblo Revolt is the strongest example of genuine short-term success.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the methods used by TWO different groups to resist colonial authority in the period 1500–1750."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE method used by the first group.
**Part B:** Describe ONE method used by the second group.
**Part C:** Explain ONE significant difference between the two groups' forms of resistance.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - Strong comparisons: Indigenous peoples (armed revolt) vs. enslaved Africans (maroon flight); Nzinga (African diplomatic resistance) vs. Pueblo peoples (armed revolt); creoles (legal/political) vs. enslaved peoples (escape/rebellion).
> - **Part C:** Explain why the forms differed — different social positions, resources, and goals produced different resistance strategies.
