## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below for Questions 1–3.**

> *"[The Pueblo people] have been very much grieved by the labor they have had to perform for the Spaniards, because they did not pay them and the work was excessive… [They] have been deprived of their idols and dances and other customs… The cause of the uprising was because the Holy Faith was being introduced by force."*
> — Testimony of an Indigenous captive describing causes of the Pueblo Revolt, 1681

---

**1.** The passage identifies which of the following as primary causes of the Pueblo Revolt?

- A) Competition between Spanish settlers and Indigenous peoples over land
- B) Forced labor, unpaid work, and religious suppression
- C) Spanish failure to protect Pueblo peoples from Apache raids
- D) Pueblo peoples' rejection of Christianity for political rather than religious reasons

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The testimony specifically names excessive unpaid labor and forced religious conversion as the primary grievances driving the revolt.*

---

**2.** The Pueblo Revolt of 1680 is best understood as evidence of which of the following broader historical patterns?

- A) The inevitable failure of European colonial rule in the Americas
- B) The capacity of colonized peoples to successfully challenge imperial authority through organized resistance
- C) The weakness of Spanish military power relative to Indigenous forces
- D) The role of disease in undermining colonial governance

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Pueblo Revolt succeeded in expelling Spanish colonists for 12 years — demonstrating that organized Indigenous resistance could achieve real results, even if temporary.*

---

**3.** Which of the following best explains why Spain moderated some of its colonial policies in New Mexico after reconquering it in 1692?

- A) Spain was following the New Laws of 1542 which required more humane treatment of Indigenous peoples
- B) Resistance demonstrated that excessive exploitation was counterproductive to maintaining stable colonial rule
- C) The Catholic Church pressured Spain to reform its practices in the Americas
- D) Spain lacked the military resources to reimpose the same level of control

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The revolt showed Spain that its policies had produced an unstable, explosive situation; moderate concessions (allowing some traditional religious practices, reducing labor demands) were pragmatic responses to demonstrated resistance capacity.*

---

**4.** The establishment of maroon communities like Quilombo dos Palmares represents which of the following?

- A) Evidence that enslaved Africans accepted their condition and sought only modest improvements
- B) Collective resistance to slavery that created alternative social structures outside colonial control
- C) The failure of colonial governments to control enslaved populations
- D) African cultural practices that were incompatible with plantation labor

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Maroon communities were acts of collective resistance — escaped enslaved people built alternative societies with their own governance, economics, and culture, actively refusing the colonial order.*

---

**5.** England's use of privateers to attack Spanish treasure fleets in the 16th century is best understood as an example of:

- A) Illegal piracy condemned by the English government
- B) External competition between European powers over colonial wealth
- C) England's religious war against Catholic Spain
- D) English attempts to establish colonies in the Americas

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Privateering was state-sanctioned commercial warfare — English monarchs authorized Drake and others to attack Spanish ships in order to disrupt Spanish silver wealth and challenge Iberian dominance.*

**6.** The Maratha Confederacy, which challenged Mughal authority in 17th–18th century India, is best understood as an example of which of the following?

- A) The spread of Hindu nationalism as a response to Mughal cultural policies
- B) Internal fragmentation of a land-based empire driven by religious and regional resistance
- C) European-backed rebellion against Mughal power to gain trade advantages
- D) The Mughal Empire's deliberate decentralization of authority to manage a large territory

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Maratha Confederacy arose from a combination of Aurangzeb's reversal of Akbar's religious tolerance (re-imposing the jizya tax on non-Muslims, destroying Hindu temples) and Mughal overextension. It represents internal challenge driven by both religious resentment and regional political ambition — a classic pattern in how empires unravel from within.*

**7.** Use the following excerpt to answer the question.

> *"I shall never be a vassal to the King of Portugal nor enter into alliance with him… I and my people are free."*
> — Queen Nzinga of Ndongo (Mbundu kingdom, West-Central Africa), c. 1626

The strategy described by Queen Nzinga most closely resembles which of the following?

- A) An appeal to European legal frameworks to protect African sovereignty
- B) Diplomatic resistance combined with military alliances to limit colonial penetration
- C) Total military rejection of European contact and trade
- D) Acceptance of nominal Portuguese authority in exchange for commercial partnerships

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Nzinga pursued a sophisticated multi-pronged strategy: she negotiated with the Portuguese, allied with their Dutch rivals, adopted Christianity when politically useful, and built military coalitions with neighboring kingdoms. Her resistance was diplomatic and adaptive — not a total rejection of contact, but a determined defense of sovereignty through any available means.*

**8.** The Dutch East India Company (VOC) successfully displaced the Portuguese in the Indian Ocean trade network in the 17th century primarily because:

- A) Portugal had abandoned Indian Ocean trade to focus on Atlantic commerce
- B) The VOC combined joint-stock financing, military force, and monopoly control of key spice-producing islands
- C) Dutch navigational technology was significantly superior to Portuguese technology
- D) The VOC formed alliances with the Mughal Empire that gave it exclusive access to South Asian ports

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The VOC's innovation was organizational and financial, not just military. Joint-stock financing allowed it to sustain a large permanent fleet; its seizure of the Banda Islands (nutmeg) and Malacca gave it monopoly control over the most valuable spices. Portugal had established the system; the Dutch militarized and financialized it.*

**9.** The English Civil War (1642–1651) and the Glorious Revolution (1688) together represent which of the following patterns in 17th-century European political history?

- A) The collapse of monarchical authority in England and its replacement by a republican system
- B) A long-term shift in England toward parliamentary sovereignty and limits on royal power
- C) Religious conflict between Protestantism and Catholicism that permanently divided English society
- D) English resistance to the same process of absolutism that was succeeding in France under Louis XIV

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Both events mark steps in the same long-term process: constraining the English monarch. The Civil War ended with Charles I's execution; the Glorious Revolution ended with William and Mary accepting the Bill of Rights (1689), formally establishing parliamentary supremacy. Answer D has some merit but B is more precise and complete — the core lesson is the development of constitutional limits on the crown.*

**10.** The treaties between colonial governments and maroon communities — such as the 1739 treaty between Jamaica and the Leeward Maroons — are significant in the history of slavery and resistance primarily because they:

- A) Ended slavery in British Caribbean colonies decades before abolition
- B) Demonstrated that enslaved people could successfully negotiate for freedom and de facto territorial autonomy
- C) Required maroon communities to integrate into colonial society and abandon African cultural practices
- D) Were universally honored by colonial governments, providing lasting protection for maroon settlements

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Jamaican Maroon treaty of 1739 granted the Leeward Maroons formal freedom and recognized their territory in exchange for an agreement not to harbor new runaways. It is significant because it proves that organized resistance could extract real concessions — legal recognition of a free Black community within a slave colony. However, Answer C is the key trap: the treaties often required maroons to return future runaways, a deeply controversial compromise. Answer D is false — many treaties were later violated by colonial governments.*

> [!tip] Avoid using Tupac Amaru II (1780–1781) or the Seven Years' War (1756–1763) as examples — both fall after 1750 and belong to Units 5–6.

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain how Indigenous peoples in the Americas challenged Spanish colonial authority in the period 1500–1750."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE form of resistance used by Indigenous peoples against Spanish colonial rule.
**Part B:** Explain ONE cause of Indigenous resistance in the period.
**Part C:** Evaluate the effectiveness of Indigenous resistance in limiting Spanish colonial power.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Choose a specific in-range example: Pueblo Revolt (1680), Mixtón War (1540s), Tupac Amaru I (1572), maroon communities (Quilombo dos Palmares, c.1600–1694), or cultural/religious resistance. Note: Tupac Amaru *II* (1780) is after 1750 and outside this topic's scope.
> - **Part B:** Forced labor (encomienda/mita), religious suppression, land theft, epidemic disease — be specific.
> - **Part C:** Balanced answer: resistance achieved some concessions (Spain modified New Mexico policies) but rarely reversed colonial rule entirely. The Pueblo Revolt is the strongest example of genuine short-term success.

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the methods used by TWO different groups to resist colonial authority in the period 1500–1750."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE method used by the first group.
**Part B:** Describe ONE method used by the second group.
**Part C:** Explain ONE significant difference between the two groups' forms of resistance.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - Strong comparisons: Indigenous peoples (armed revolt) vs. enslaved Africans (maroon flight); Nzinga (African diplomatic resistance) vs. Pueblo peoples (armed revolt); creoles (legal/political) vs. enslaved peoples (escape/rebellion).
> - **Part C:** Explain why the forms differed — different social positions, resources, and goals produced different resistance strategies.
