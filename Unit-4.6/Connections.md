# Connections

## How Topic 4.6 Fits the AP World Story

Topic 4.6 closes Unit 4 by showing the underside of empire-building. Units 4.1–4.5 explain how states formed, expanded, and consolidated power. Topic 4.6 answers: *what happened to the people on whom that power was exercised?* The answer is resistance — across every continent, every empire, every decade of the period. *(AMSCO pp. 243–247)*

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-1.6/Medieval Europe Overview|Medieval Europe (1.6)]]** — Catholic Church dominance in medieval Europe set the stage for the Protestant-Catholic conflicts that drove the Glorious Revolution and European religious wars. The serfdom that generated the Pugachev Rebellion also has roots in medieval European labor systems.

**[[Unit-3.3/Empires: Belief Systems Overview|Empires: Belief Systems (3.3)]]** — The Sunni–Shia split in Islamic empires prefigures the Protestant–Catholic split in European ones. Both represent internal religious divisions that states tried to control and that became flashpoints for political violence. Both involve rulers trying to impose religious conformity and populations resisting.
→ See also [[Unit-3.3/Themes & Historical Reasoning|Themes & Historical Reasoning (3.3)]] for the comparison.

## Leads To

**Enlightenment [[Unit-5.1/Enlightenment Overview|Enlightenment Overview]]** — The Glorious Revolution (1688) helped inspire Enlightenment thinkers. John Locke used it as evidence that governments exist to protect natural rights and can be legitimately overthrown when they fail. The revolts of 4.6 fed the political philosophy of 5.1.

**Atlantic Revolutions [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism & Revolutions Overview|Nationalism & Revolutions Overview]]** — The Haitian Revolution (1791), American Revolution (1776), and Latin American independence movements are the direct successors of the resistance patterns established in 4.6. Maroon communities, slave revolts, and indigenous resistance in the Americas all prefigure the Atlantic Revolutionary era.

**Nation-States [[Unit-7.1/Shifting Power Overview|Shifting Power Overview]]** — The Peace of Westphalia's principle of state sovereignty (1648) — a product of European religious wars — eventually became the foundation of the modern nation-state system.

## Compare With

**Sunni–Shia split [[Unit-3.3/Empires: Belief Systems Overview|Empires: Belief Systems Overview]] vs. Protestant–Catholic conflict (4.6)**
Both internal religious fractures used by political actors to wage war. Both ended in (partial) negotiated settlements. Key difference: the Islamic split was about political succession as much as theology; the Christian split involved new doctrinal claims about salvation that imperial authority couldn't simply suppress.

**Decolonization (Topic 8.5)** — The 20th-century independence movements are the long-run consequence of the same dynamic: colonized peoples resisting state power and demanding self-determination. Ana Nzinga, Metacom, and the Pueblo rebels are the ancestors — in spirit if not lineage — of 20th-century independence leaders.

## AP Threads Running Through 4.6

- **State-building generates resistance** — the central thread: every empire in this topic created the conditions for its own challenges.
- **Religion as both tool and trigger** — states used religious coercion to control populations; populations used religious identity to resist.
- **Labor systems and revolt** — serfdom (Russia), slavery (Americas), and coerced labor (Pueblo peoples) all generated organized resistance.
- **Cross-regional comparison** — the exam loves asking you to compare resistance across empires; 4.6 is purpose-built for this.
