# Chapter 8: Timeline — The Mongol Empire

> [!tip]
> **Big Picture:** The Mongol Empire's history within AP Period 1 (c. 1200–1450) spans from spectacular rise (1206) to fragmentation and collapse (mid-to-late 14th century). The entire arc — conquest, Pax Mongolica, plague, collapse — occurs within roughly 150 years, making this one of the most compressed and consequential periods in world history. The key CCOT insight: Mongol rule brought *change* (destruction, integration) but the successor states represented *continuity* (China returned to Ming Chinese governance; Persia returned to Islamic governance).

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| 1206 | Temujin unified as Genghis Khan at kurultai | Mongolia | Founding of the Mongol Empire; **CHANGE**: the steppe nomads become an empire-building force |
| 1215 | Mongols sack Zhongdu (Beijing); beginning of conquest of northern China | East Asia | Demonstrates Mongol siege capability; begins the end of the Jin Dynasty |
| 1219–1221 | Mongol conquest of the Khwarezmian Empire; sack of Samarkand, Bukhara | Central Asia | Destruction of the Silk Road's most important oasis cities; massive population loss |
| 1227 | Death of Genghis Khan | Mongolia | Empire passes to his sons; begins eventual fragmentation into khanates |
| 1234 | Fall of Jin Dynasty (northern China) | East Asia | Mongols control northern China; Song Dynasty in south still independent |
| 1237–1242 | Mongol invasion of Russia and Eastern Europe (Battle of Mohi, Battle of Legnica) | Russia, Eastern Europe | Russia conquered and tributary to the Golden Horde for 200+ years; Poland and Hungary devastated but not permanently occupied |
| 1241 | Death of Ögedei Khan; Mongol withdrawal from Europe | Eastern Europe | Kurultai recalled armies westward — Western Europe spared by political accident |
| 1258 | **Mongols sack Baghdad; Abbasid Caliph executed** | Middle East | End of the Abbasid Caliphate; destruction of the House of Wisdom; **CHANGE**: the symbolic and political center of the Islamic world eliminated |
| 1260 | Battle of Ain Jalut: Mamluk Sultanate defeats Mongols in Egypt | Middle East | First major Mongol military defeat; marks the western limit of Mongol expansion; **CONTINUITY**: the Islamic world survives in Egypt |
| 1260 | Kublai Khan becomes Great Khan | East Asia | Moves Mongol capital to China; begins transformation of Mongol rule toward settled administration |
| 1271 | Kublai Khan founds the Yuan Dynasty in China | East Asia | Mongols formally adopt Chinese dynastic identity; Marco Polo arrives at court c. 1275 |
| 1274, 1281 | Two Mongol invasions of Japan repelled | East Asia | The *kamikaze* ("divine winds") typhoons destroyed Mongol fleets; Japan the most notable failed conquest |
| 1279 | Song Dynasty conquered; all of China under Yuan | East Asia | **CHANGE**: first time all of China unified under non-Chinese rulers |
| c. 1250s–1340s | Pax Mongolica — peak of Silk Road commerce | Eurasia | Unprecedented safe travel; Marco Polo, Ibn Battuta, diplomatic missions; technology and disease both travel |
| c. 1330s | Bubonic plague originates in Central Asian rodent populations | Central Asia | The slow-motion catastrophe begins; plague will travel trade routes westward |
| 1335 | Ilkhanate collapses (last Ilkhan dies without heir) | Middle East | First major Mongol khanate to collapse; Persia fragments into competing successor states |
| 1346–1353 | Black Death sweeps Eurasia | Middle East, Europe, N. Africa | Kills 25–50% of populations reached; **CHANGE**: single most destructive demographic event in the period |
| 1368 | Yuan Dynasty collapses; Ming Dynasty founded by Zhu Yuanzhang | East Asia | Chinese resistance drives out Mongols; **CONTINUITY**: China returns to Chinese governance under the Ming |
| c. 1370–1405 | Timur (Tamerlane) briefly reunifies Central Asian Silk Roads | Central Asia | Last Mongol-style empire; Timurid Renaissance in Samarkand; temporary restoration of overland trade |
| 1402 | Timur defeats Ottoman Sultan Bayezid I at Battle of Ankara | Anatolia | Temporarily disrupts Ottoman expansion; demonstrates how Mongol-legacy rulers still shaped major geopolitics into the 15th century |
