# Chapter 8: The Mongol Empire and the Making of the Modern World (c. 1206–1368)

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 2 — Networks of Exchange
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (c. 1200–1450)
**Key Theme:** Empire-Building, Destruction, Integration, and Unintended Consequences

## Summary

The Mongol Empire, founded by Genghis Khan in 1206, became the largest contiguous land empire in world history, stretching from the Pacific Ocean to Eastern Europe at its peak. The Mongol conquests were among the most destructive events in world history — Baghdad fell in 1258, ending the Abbasid Caliphate; the Song Dynasty was eliminated by 1279; and populations from Central Asia to Poland were decimated. Yet the Mongol Empire paradoxically became the greatest facilitator of exchange in the pre-modern world. The *Pax Mongolica* enabled safe travel and commerce across Eurasia, spreading not only trade goods but also technologies, religions, diplomatic contacts, and — most fatally — the bubonic plague. The Mongols' legacy is a study in unintended consequences: they destroyed the old world and, without planning it, built the framework for the modern one.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain the causes and effects of the Mongol conquests across Eurasia
- Analyze how the Pax Mongolica facilitated trade, cultural exchange, and disease transmission
- Evaluate the short- and long-term consequences of Mongol rule on different regions (China, the Islamic world, Russia, Eastern Europe)
- Explain how the Mongol Empire contributed to state-building and collapse across Eurasia

## AP World History Key Concepts

- **KC-2.1.II** — The Mongol Empire created the Pax Mongolica, facilitating trade and exchange across Eurasia
- **KC-2.3.I** — The spread of epidemic disease (bubonic plague) via Silk Roads caused massive demographic disruption
- **KC-1.3.III** — The Mongols disrupted existing states and contributed to new patterns of state-building
- **KC-2.2.I** — Cultural and intellectual exchanges occurred under Mongol patronage (Timurid Renaissance, Yuan court culture)
