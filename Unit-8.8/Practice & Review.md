## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Source:** Ronald Grigor Suny, "Empire Falls: The Revolutions of 1989," *The Nation*, 2009

*"Egalitarian Marxism, a more human form of communism without terror or Russians, continued to have broad appeal. But the crushing of anti-Stalinist uprisings of 1956 in Hungary and 1968 in Czechoslovakia left people feeling bewildered, if not betrayed. What they got was 'vegetarian' communism . . . more goods, some travel abroad, less repression, but only the most muted voice in politics.*

*Early '80s communist regimes looked stable, relatively prosperous and likely to endure. But the command economy couldn't uphold the social contract: Soviet bloc countries borrowed heavily from the West to maintain an aging industrial base and a standard of living comfortable enough to keep populations relatively quiescent. Foreign debt and a cycle of falling productivity and growing discontent accelerated."*

---

**Question 1.** Based on the passage, Suny might also support which of the following assertions?

- A. Had the socialist economies performed better, communist governments might have stayed in power longer.
- B. The use of terror and abuse of human rights worsened as communist governments faced internal challenges.
- C. The public's distaste for socialism doomed the communist governments from their beginnings.
- D. The communist regimes were purposefully undermined by Western interference in their economies.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Suny argues that communist regimes survived by providing enough economic goods to keep populations "quiescent" — but when the command economy could not sustain even this minimal social contract, discontent accelerated. This directly implies that better economic performance could have extended their survival. Choice B contradicts the passage (Suny describes reduced repression). Choice C contradicts Suny's argument that the regimes "looked stable" into the early '80s. Choice D is not supported — Suny blames internal economic failures, not Western sabotage.

---

**Question 2.** Which of the following additional factors contributed most to the collapse of the Soviet Union?

- A. Pro-democracy protests during the Prague Spring
- B. An increase in the standard of living for most people
- C. The success of NATO-led military operations
- D. The establishment of the Non-Aligned Movement

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The Prague Spring (1968) is the correct answer in the context of this source set — it connects to Suny's point about uprisings that left populations "bewildered" and reduced regime legitimacy. Note that the Prague Spring was *crushed* (it was not itself a cause of collapse), but it illustrates the accumulated loss of communist moral authority that Suny describes. Choice B is the opposite of the passage's argument. Choice C is wrong — NATO did not conduct military operations against the USSR. Choice D (Non-Aligned Movement) is unrelated to Soviet collapse.

> [!warning]
> Be careful here: the Prague Spring **failed** — Soviet tanks crushed it. Its significance is that the repression further delegitimized communist governments in the eyes of Eastern Europeans. The AP may test whether students understand that a *failed* uprising can still be historically significant.

---

**Question 3.** The events described in the passage contributed most directly to which of the following late 20th-century developments?

- A. The spread of free market capitalism and economic liberalism
- B. The expansion of social welfare states and socialist economies
- C. A rejection of Western European culture and consumer products
- D. Increased Cold War tensions between the U.S. and the Soviet Union

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> When communist governments collapsed, they largely transitioned to market economies — often rapidly and painfully via "shock therapy." The spread of free market capitalism and economic liberalism after 1989 was the direct structural consequence of communist economic failure. Choice B is wrong — the collapse moved away from social welfare states, not toward them. Choice C is wrong — Eastern Europeans generally embraced Western consumer culture eagerly. Choice D is the opposite of what happened after 1989 (tensions decreased).

## Short-Answer Questions

**SAQ 1** — Source: Ronald Reagan, address at the Brandenburg Gate, June 12, 1987

*"Behind me stands a wall that encircles the free sectors of this city, part of a vast system of barriers that divides the entire continent of Europe . . . . General Secretary Gorbachev, if you seek peace, if you seek prosperity for the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe, if you seek liberalization, come here to this gate. Mr. Gorbachev, open this gate. Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall."*

**Part A:** Identify President Reagan's argument in the passage above.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> Reagan argues that the Berlin Wall symbolizes Soviet repression and the division of Europe, and that genuine peace, prosperity, and liberalization require its demolition. He frames the Wall as the central obstacle to the freedoms he claims both sides seek.

**Part B:** Explain ONE historical situation that supports Reagan's argument.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain (Support)
> The construction of the Berlin Wall in 1961 supports Reagan's argument. East Germany built the Wall specifically to prevent its citizens from fleeing to West Germany — by 1961, approximately 3.5 million East Germans had emigrated. The Wall was thus a physical admission that the communist system could not retain its population voluntarily; it required coercion to survive. This directly supports Reagan's framing of the Wall as a symbol of coercion rather than legitimate governance.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which the resolution of Cold War tensions between the United States and the Soviet Union impacted other states.

> [!tip] Model Response — Impact on Other States
> The end of the Cold War allowed Eastern European states to pursue independent governance free from Soviet military threat. Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, and others were able to hold free elections and transition to democratic governments in 1989 because Gorbachev signaled that Soviet troops would no longer enforce communist rule. Without this Cold War resolution — specifically, the abandonment of the Brezhnev Doctrine — these states remained subject to Soviet military intervention, as Hungary experienced in 1956 and Czechoslovakia in 1968.

**SAQ 2** — No source

**Part A:** Identify ONE military and technological development of the United States that contributed to the end of the Cold War.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> The Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), proposed by Reagan in 1983, was a military-technological development that contributed to the Cold War's end. SDI proposed a space-based system to intercept Soviet nuclear missiles, potentially neutralizing the USSR's nuclear arsenal. The Soviets lacked the economic capacity to counter it, increasing pressure for arms reduction negotiations and contributing to the conditions that led to the 1987 INF Treaty.

**Part B:** Explain ONE way in which the Soviet Union contributed to its own collapse in 1991.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> Gorbachev's introduction of glasnost (political openness) inadvertently accelerated the Soviet collapse. By allowing freedom of expression and reducing censorship, glasnost unleashed public criticism that had been suppressed for decades. Citizens could now openly discuss Stalinist atrocities, economic failures, Afghanistan casualties, and the Chernobyl cover-up. This criticism delegitimized the Soviet state faster than Gorbachev could implement economic improvements, creating a crisis of authority that his reforms could not resolve. Rather than saving the system, glasnost provided the political space for independence movements in the Baltic states and republics that ultimately dismembered the USSR.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which the economic structures in communist countries led to the end of the Cold War.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> The Soviet command economy's chronic inefficiency drained the resources needed to maintain superpower competition. Unlike market economies, the command economy prevented farmers and manufacturers from responding to supply and demand, resulting in persistent shortages, low productivity growth, and an inability to match Western technological innovation. This economic weakness was exposed by the Afghan War (1979–1989), which required military expenditure the Soviet economy could not sustain, and by the pressure of matching US military spending during the Reagan buildup. When the economy could no longer provide adequate living standards, the social contract between the state and citizens collapsed, contributing directly to the political crises of 1989–1991.
