# Connections

*Topic 8.8 — End of the Cold War*

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-8.2/Cold War Overview\|Cold War Origins]]**
The superpower rivalry, nuclear deterrence framework, and containment policy established in 8.2 are what 8.8 resolves. Détente, SDI, and arms control treaties are responses to the nuclear standoff created in the postwar period. The Cold War's end is meaningless without understanding what it ended.

**[[Unit-8.3/Effects of the Cold War Overview\|Cold War Effects]]**
Proxy wars (Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan) and their costs — especially Afghanistan's drain on Soviet resources — are direct precursors to the Soviet collapse. The Afghan War is the link between Cold War proxy conflict and the internal Soviet crisis that perestroika tried to address.

**[[Unit-8.4/Spread of Communism after 1900 Overview\|Spread of Communism]]**
The communist governments that spread across Eastern Europe, Asia, and elsewhere in the mid-20th century are the governments that collapsed in 1989–1991. Understanding their origins explains why their collapse was so comprehensive — they were Soviet-installed, not organically grown.

**[[Unit-8.7/Global Resistance to Established Power Structures Overview\|Global Resistance]]**
Poland's Solidarity movement, the Prague Spring, and other forms of resistance within the Soviet bloc are the civil society infrastructure that made 1989's revolutions possible. The Cold War's end was not only a top-down diplomatic process; it was also driven by mass movements from below.

## Leads To

**Unit 9 — Globalization (Topics 9.1–9.9)**
The post-Cold War world — economic globalization, the "Washington Consensus," financial integration, and the spread of liberal democracy — emerged directly from the Soviet collapse. The US promoted market capitalism as the victor's ideology. Topics 9.2 (economic globalization), 9.4 (international institutions), and 9.5 (human rights) all assume a post–Cold War context.

**Terrorism and New Conflicts**
The vacuum left by the Cold War's end created new instabilities: ethnic conflicts in Yugoslavia (genocide in Bosnia, Kosovo), Rwanda's genocide (1994), and Afghanistan's civil war eventually contributed to the rise of the Taliban. The mujahideen the US armed against the Soviets became the context for al-Qaeda's growth. The 9/11 attacks and "War on Terror" are downstream consequences of Cold War proxy strategies.

## Compare With

**Chinese Communist Party after 1989**
Where the Soviet Union collapsed after internal reform, China's Communist Party survived by *suppressing* reform (Tiananmen Square, June 4, 1989). This comparison is AP gold: it shows that Soviet collapse was not inevitable — it required Gorbachev's specific choice not to use force. The contrast also raises questions about the relationship between economic reform and political liberalization.

**[[Unit-8.5/Decolonization after 1900 Overview\|Decolonization]]**
The Soviet dissolution created 15 newly independent states, many of which had been incorporated into the Russian/Soviet empire long before the Cold War. This parallels decolonization in Asia and Africa — former imperial subjects reasserting independence. The comparison illuminates the Soviet empire as a form of territorial empire alongside Western overseas empires.

**Détente vs. Containment vs. Reagan Doctrine**
Three distinct US strategies toward the USSR: containment (Truman — resist Soviet expansion), détente (Nixon/Kissinger — manage coexistence), and confrontation (Reagan — pressure for rollback). Comparing these strategies as a CCOT arc across US foreign policy from 1947 to 1991 is an excellent LEQ framing.
