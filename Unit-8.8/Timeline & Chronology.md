# Timeline & Chronology

*AMSCO pp. 608–614*

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1968 | Soviet military crushes Prague Spring in Czechoslovakia | Brezhnev Doctrine in action; shows limits of reform within Soviet bloc |
| 1972 | Nixon visits USSR and China; SALT I signed | Peak of détente; first nuclear arms cap; triangular US-Soviet-Chinese diplomacy |
| 1975 | Helsinki Accords | Recognized European borders; included human rights provisions Soviets would later regret |
| 1979 | Soviet invasion of Afghanistan; Carter halts grain sales | End of détente; beginning of the USSR's "Vietnam" — military overextension |
| 1980 | Solidarity founded in Poland | First independent trade union in Soviet bloc; signal of popular legitimacy crisis |
| 1981 | Ronald Reagan becomes US President | Shift to confrontational strategy; "evil empire" framing; massive military buildup |
| 1983 | Reagan announces Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI) | Arms race extended to space; Soviet economy cannot keep pace |
| 1985 | Gorbachev comes to power; introduces glasnost and perestroika | Reform era begins; intended to save Soviet system, instead accelerates collapse |
| 1987 | INF Treaty signed by Reagan and Gorbachev | First treaty to eliminate class of nuclear weapons; signals Cold War thaw |
| 1989 | Soviet army withdraws from Afghanistan | Military failure acknowledged; Soviet legitimacy further undermined |
| Nov 9, 1989 | Berlin Wall falls | Symbolic end of Cold War division of Europe |
| 1989–1990 | Democratic revolutions across Eastern Europe | Satellite states shed communist governments; Warsaw Pact collapses |
| Oct 1990 | German reunification | East and West Germany unite under democratic government |
| Aug 1991 | Failed coup against Gorbachev | Hardliner coup collapses; Gorbachev fatally weakened; Yeltsin rises |
| Dec 25, 1991 | Soviet Union formally dissolved; 15 independent states | Cold War officially over; US becomes sole superpower |
| 1991 | Gulf War: US-led coalition expels Iraq from Kuwait | First major post–Cold War military action; demonstrates US unipolar dominance |

---

> [!tip]
> **The speed of 1989** is exam-relevant. Multiple communist governments fell within months — not years. This speed illustrates that these governments had been surviving on Soviet coercion alone, not popular legitimacy. Once that coercive guarantee was lifted, collapse was rapid.

> [!exam-tip]
> Know **three key dates cold**: 1972 (SALT I / détente peak), 1985 (Gorbachev / reforms begin), 1991 (Soviet dissolution). If asked to identify turning points in the Cold War, these are strong candidates with clear justification.
