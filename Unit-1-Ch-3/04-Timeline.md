# Chapter 3: Timeline — South and Southeast Asia (1200–1450)

> **Big Picture:** South and Southeast Asia in 1200–1450 experience a complex interplay between indigenous Hindu-Buddhist traditions and the spreading influence of Islam via trade and Sufi missionary activity. The Delhi Sultanate establishes Islam as a political force in India, while Southeast Asian states shift from inland agrarian empires to maritime Islamic trading powers. The Indian Ocean remains the connective tissue linking all these developments to the wider world.

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| c. 1190s | Prithviraj Chauhan defeated by Muhammad of Ghur | Northern India | Last major Hindu resistance before Delhi Sultanate founding |
| 1206 | Delhi Sultanate founded (Qutb-ud-din Aibak) | India | **Change:** First permanent Muslim political power in South Asia |
| c. 1200 | Khmer Empire at its height under Jayavarman VII | Cambodia | Construction of Angkor Thom (Buddhist); empire at maximum territorial extent |
| c. 1267 | Samudra Pasai (northern Sumatra) founded as first Islamic sultanate in SE Asia | SE Asia | **Change:** Earliest Islamic political authority in Southeast Asia, over a century before Malacca |
| 1277–1287 | Mongol invasions of Pagan (Burma) | Burma | **Change:** Pagan kingdom destabilized; declines rapidly after Mongol raids; new Shan and Mon states emerge |
| c. 1250s | Theravada Buddhism spreads from Sri Lanka to mainland SE Asia | SE Asia | **Continuity and change:** Displaces Hindu-Mahayana synthesis in Thailand, Burma |
| 1275–1292 | Mongol invasions of Southeast Asia (Burma, Java, Vietnam, Champa) | SE Asia | All ultimately repelled; demonstrates limits of Mongol maritime power |
| 1293 | Majapahit Empire founded in Java | Indonesia | Last great Hindu-Buddhist maritime empire of SE Asia |
| 1297–1306 | Delhi Sultanate (Alauddin Khalji) repels Mongol invasions | India | India protected from Mongol devastation; Delhi Sultanate at peak power |
| c. 1300 | Bhakti movement spreading across northern India | India | **Continuity and synthesis:** Hindu devotional tradition incorporates Sufi influences |
| 1320–1414 | Khmer Empire declining; Tai (Thai) states expanding | Mainland SE Asia | **Change:** Shift in political power from hydraulic agrarian empire to Tai kingdoms |
| 1329–1364 | Gajah Mada serves as Majapahit prime minister | Indonesia | Majapahit reaches greatest extent; "Palapa Oath" to unify archipelago |
| 1336 | Vijayanagara Empire founded in South India | India | Hindu state specifically resisting Delhi Sultanate expansion southward |
| c. 1350s | Ayutthaya (Thai) kingdom founded | Thailand | New Theravada Buddhist kingdom filling vacuum left by Khmer decline |
| c. 1400 | Malacca founded on Malay Peninsula | Malaysia | **Turning point:** New maritime trading state in strategic straits position |
| c. 1414 | Malacca ruler converts to Islam | Malaysia | **Change:** Beginning of Islamic era in maritime SE Asia; trade-driven Islamicization |
| 1398 | Timur sacks Delhi | India | Devastating blow to Delhi Sultanate; sets stage for eventual Mughal founding |
| c. 1400–1450 | Islam spreads to Sumatra, Java coastal cities | SE Asia | Muslim merchants and Sufi missionaries establish communities in port cities |
| 1431 | Khmer abandons Angkor; capital moves south | Cambodia | **Change:** End of the Angkor era; reflects shift toward maritime trade economy |
