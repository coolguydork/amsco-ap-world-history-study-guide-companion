# Chapter 3: Practice Questions — South and Southeast Asia (1200–1450)

## Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** The Delhi Sultanate's approach to governing India's Hindu majority population is best described as:

- A) Forced mass conversion to Islam through systematic religious persecution
- B) Complete assimilation of Islamic rulers into Hindu culture and the caste system
- C) Pragmatic toleration of Hinduism while imposing Islamic political authority and the jizya tax
- D) A democratic system allowing Hindu and Muslim communities to govern themselves separately

**Correct Answer: C**
*Delhi Sultanate rulers generally tolerated Hindu religious practice (as the alternative — forcible conversion of hundreds of millions — was impractical) while asserting Muslim political dominance and taxing non-Muslims with the jizya.*

---

**2.** The construction of Angkor Wat by the Khmer Empire best reflects which of the following?

- A) Buddhist monastic rejection of worldly power and material wealth
- B) The Indian cultural model of kingship in which the ruler embodies divine authority
- C) Chinese influence on Southeast Asian architecture via the tribute system
- D) Islamic architectural traditions adapted to a Southeast Asian context

**Correct Answer: B**
*Angkor Wat exemplifies the Hindu concept of the god-king (devaraja), where the temple mountain represents the cosmic Mount Meru and the king's power is legitimized through identification with the deity Vishnu.*

---

**3.** *Stimulus:* "In this city there are merchants from Arabia, Persia, India, China, and Java, who speak 84 different languages. They exchange silk, porcelain, cloves, nutmeg, pepper, cotton, and gold in an open market that operates under Islamic law."

This description of a trading city most likely refers to:

- A) The Khmer capital of Angkor at its height
- B) Delhi under the Khalji dynasty
- C) Malacca on the Malay Peninsula, c. 1450
- D) Vijayanagara in southern India

**Correct Answer: C**
*Malacca's description as a multilingual, multi-ethnic Islamic entrepôt trading in Indian Ocean goods matches historical accounts of the Malay port city that became dominant in maritime Southeast Asian trade after its ruler's conversion to Islam around 1414.*

---

**4.** The Bhakti movement in South Asia is historically significant primarily because it:

- A) Successfully converted the Delhi Sultanate's rulers to Hinduism
- B) Represented a synthesis of Hindu and Islamic spiritual traditions that challenged caste hierarchy
- C) Restored the dominance of Brahmin priests over Indian religious life
- D) Spread Hinduism to Southeast Asia via the Indian Ocean trade network

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Bhakti movement's poet-saints (including Muslim-influenced figures like Kabir) drew on both Hindu devotional traditions and Sufi Islamic mysticism, challenging caste divisions and creating a more inclusive religious synthesis.*

---

**5.** Which of the following best explains why the Khmer Empire declined in the 14th–15th centuries?

- A) Conquest by a Mongol army that destroyed Angkor's hydraulic infrastructure
- B) The conversion of Khmer rulers to Islam, alienating the Buddhist population
- C) A combination of Tai military pressure, possible environmental problems, and the rise of maritime trade that bypassed the inland kingdom
- D) Chinese Ming Dynasty naval expeditions that disrupted Khmer Indian Ocean trade

**Correct Answer: C**
*Khmer decline was multi-causal: Tai kingdoms pressed from the north, the hydraulic system may have been stressed by climate change or poor management, and the growing importance of maritime trade routes through the straits advantaged coastal states over inland Angkor.*

---

**6.** The term "Indianization" as applied to Southeast Asia refers to:

- A) Indian military conquest and direct colonial administration of Southeast Asian states
- B) The adoption of Indian cultural practices (Sanskrit, Hindu-Buddhist religion, Indian statecraft models) by Southeast Asian elites via trade and missionary activity
- C) The migration of millions of Indian farmers to Southeast Asia to practice wet-rice cultivation
- D) The forcible conversion of Southeast Asians to Hinduism by Delhi Sultanate military expeditions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Indianization occurred through trade contact and voluntary adoption of prestigious Indian cultural models by Southeast Asian elites — not through military conquest. This is a key distinction from how Islam would later spread in the region.*

---

**7.** Women's social status in Southeast Asia between 1200 and 1450 differed from their status in South Asia or East Asia primarily because:

- A) Islam granted Southeast Asian women full legal equality with men
- B) Southeast Asian women were excluded from economic activity and confined to domestic roles
- C) Pre-Indian indigenous cultural traditions gave Southeast Asian women more social and economic freedom than existed in other major Eurasian societies
- D) Chinese cultural influence through the tributary system elevated women's status in Southeast Asian courts

**Correct Answer: C**
*Southeast Asian women historically had significant property rights, engaged in trade, and participated in public life — a reflection of the region's pre-Indianization indigenous culture that persisted despite the influence of Hindu and later Islamic traditions that were more restrictive toward women.*

---

**8.** The conversion of Malacca's ruler to Islam around 1414 was most likely motivated by:

- A) A military threat from the Delhi Sultanate that required Muslim alliance for protection
- B) The persuasive theological arguments of a Sufi missionary from Arabia
- C) Commercial advantages of membership in the Muslim merchant community dominating Indian Ocean trade
- D) The Yongle Emperor's insistence on Islamic conversion as a condition of his tribute system

**Correct Answer: C**
*The most compelling explanation for Malacca's conversion is commercial: Muslim merchants from India and the Middle East controlled much of Indian Ocean trade, and converting to Islam facilitated integration into these networks, attracting more merchants and increasing Malacca's commercial role.*

---

**9.** The monsoon wind system was essential to Indian Ocean trade primarily because it:

- A) Prevented piracy by making the Indian Ocean too stormy for small vessels
- B) Provided predictable seasonal winds that allowed reliable long-distance voyages without advanced navigation technology
- C) Carried nutrient-rich waters that made Indian Ocean fishing the world's most productive
- D) Created trade winds that connected the Indian Ocean to the Atlantic trading system

**Correct Answer: B**
*The monsoon's reversal (blowing northeast in winter, southwest in summer) created a natural "highway" that allowed ancient and medieval sailors to make round trips across the Indian Ocean with confidence, enabling the world's most extensive pre-modern trading network.*

---

**10.** The Vijayanagara Empire of southern India (founded 1336) is best understood as a response to which historical development?

- A) Chinese Ming Dynasty's expansion of the tribute system into South Asia
- B) Mongol invasions that destroyed the Delhi Sultanate and left a power vacuum
- C) The Delhi Sultanate's attempts to extend Muslim political authority into southern India
- D) Portuguese arrival on the Indian coast threatening Hindu civilization

**Correct Answer: C**
*Vijayanagara was explicitly founded as a Hindu kingdom to resist the Delhi Sultanate's southward expansion — its founders were former servants of the sultanate who defected and established a rival Hindu state to protect southern India's Hindu culture.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1
**a)** Describe ONE way in which the Delhi Sultanate changed South Asian society or culture between 1206 and 1450.

**b)** Describe ONE continuity in South Asian society or culture that persisted despite the establishment of the Delhi Sultanate.

**c)** Explain how the Bhakti movement reflects both change and continuity in South Asian religious life during this period.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Introduction of Persian language and Islamic architecture; imposition of the jizya; growth of Hindu-Muslim artistic synthesis; reduction of Brahmin political influence in Delhi Sultanate territories
> - (b) The caste system; Hindu religious practice; Sanskrit scholarship; Hindu kingdoms in southern India (Vijayanagara); agricultural patterns
> - (c) Change: Bhakti drew on Islamic Sufi traditions; challenged Brahmin/caste hierarchy. Continuity: remained within a devotional Hindu framework; maintained reverence for Hindu deities

---

### SAQ 2
**a)** Explain ONE way in which Southeast Asian states used religion to legitimize political authority between 1200 and 1450.

**b)** Explain ONE way in which trade contributed to political change in Southeast Asia between 1200 and 1450.

**c)** Explain ONE difference between how political authority was organized in mainland Southeast Asia versus maritime Southeast Asia during this period.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Khmer devaraja concept — god-king embodying Hindu deity; Buddhist kingship in Theravada states; Islamic legitimacy in Malacca after 1414
> - (b) Malacca's conversion to Islam to attract Muslim merchants; maritime trade revenues funding state formation; spice trade driving Majapahit expansion
> - (c) Mainland: hydraulic agrarian empire model (Khmer) with large sedentary populations and rice revenues. Maritime: mandala states controlling straits and trade routes rather than territory; more dependent on commercial revenues

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Prompt

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which trade networks transformed political and religious structures in South and Southeast Asia between 1200 and 1450.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring DBQ outline would include:**
> - **Thesis:** "While trade networks were a primary driver of religious and political change in maritime Southeast Asia — most visibly in the Islamicization of Malacca and coastal cities — their impact on mainland Southeast Asia and South Asia's internal caste and religious structures was more limited, as indigenous political and cultural traditions demonstrated resilience."
> - **Body paragraphs should address:**
>   - Indian Ocean trade driving Islamicization of maritime Southeast Asia (Malacca example)
>   - Delhi Sultanate as a trade-connected Islamic polity that nevertheless could not fully Islamicize India
>   - Khmer Empire's inland agrarian model versus coastal maritime alternatives
>   - Bhakti movement as a cultural synthesis enabled by Hindu-Muslim commercial contact
>   - Theravada Buddhism spread through Sri Lankan missionary networks parallel to trade routes
> - **Complexity point:** The direction of influence was not just from outside in — Indian merchants and cultural models (Sanskrit, Hindu statecraft) had *already* transformed Southeast Asia before Islam arrived. Trade networks spread culture in multiple directions simultaneously.
