# Chapter 3: Key People and Groups — South and Southeast Asia (1200–1450)

## Individuals and Groups

### Delhi Sultanate
**Period:** 1206–1526 | **Region:** Northern India

- Founded by Turkish military commanders; ruled through a combination of military force, Islamic legitimacy, and accommodation of India's Hindu majority
- Introduced Persian language, Islamic architecture (including the Qutb Minar in Delhi), and Muslim administrative practices to South Asia
- Never fully conquered southern India (where Vijayanagara and other Hindu kingdoms persisted), and faced constant challenge from the Hindu majority population and regional Hindu rulers

> [!exam-tip]
> The Delhi Sultanate is the precursor to the Mughal Empire. AP exams often ask about the **continuity** of Islamic rule in India from the Delhi Sultanate through the Mughals and the resulting Hindu-Muslim cultural synthesis.

---

### Alauddin Khalji
**Period:** 1296–1316 | **Region:** Delhi Sultanate, India

- Sultan of the Delhi Sultanate who extended Muslim rule to its greatest extent in South Asia, temporarily reaching into the Deccan plateau of southern India
- Implemented economic reforms including price controls and a standing army paid directly from the treasury, reducing the power of nobles
- Successfully repelled Mongol invasions of India (1297–1306), protecting the subcontinent while the rest of the Islamic world suffered Mongol devastation

---

### Khmer Empire / Angkor
**Period:** 802–1431 | **Region:** Mainland Southeast Asia (Cambodia)

- Built the world's most extensive hydraulic system — networks of reservoirs (barays) and canals that controlled water for rice cultivation in a region with unpredictable rainfall
- Angkor at its height was one of the largest pre-industrial cities in the world, with a population estimated at nearly 1 million
- Declined in the 14th–15th centuries due to a combination of Tai (Thai) state pressure from the north, possible environmental degradation of the hydraulic system, and the rise of maritime trade routes that bypassed the inland Khmer heartland

---

### Suryavarman II
**Period:** r. 1113–c.1150 | **Region:** Khmer Empire, Cambodia

- Khmer king who commissioned the construction of Angkor Wat as his state temple and intended mausoleum, dedicated to the Hindu god Vishnu
- Expanded Khmer territory and diplomatic relations, sending ambassadors to the Song Dynasty in China
- Represents the apex of the "devaraja" (god-king) concept, in which the king was seen as an earthly manifestation of a Hindu deity

---

### Majapahit Empire
**Period:** 1293–c.1527 | **Region:** Java and Maritime Southeast Asia

- The last and greatest Hindu-Buddhist maritime empire of Southeast Asia, controlling trade routes through the Indonesian archipelago
- Gajah Mada, the powerful prime minister (1329–1364), extended Majapahit's control over much of maritime Southeast Asia — his "Palapa Oath" to unify the archipelago is still remembered as an Indonesian national symbol
- The gradual Islamicization of its vassal port cities (Malacca, etc.) during the 15th century undermined its commercial dominance and contributed to eventual collapse

---

### Malacca (Melaka) Sultanate
**Period:** c. 1400–1511 | **Region:** Malay Peninsula, Southeast Asia

- Founded around 1400 by a prince from the Hindu Majapahit Empire; the ruler converted to Islam around 1414, making Malacca the first major Islamic polity in maritime Southeast Asia
- Became the most important entrepôt in Asia, where Chinese, Indian, Arab, Javanese, and Malay merchants exchanged goods; traders used a *lingua franca* called Malay that had some 84 different languages spoken in the port
- The conversion of Malacca accelerated the Islamicization of Southeast Asia as local rulers along trade routes followed Malacca's example

---

### Vijayanagara Empire
**Period:** 1336–1646 | **Region:** Southern India

- Founded as a Hindu kingdom specifically to resist Delhi Sultanate expansion into southern India
- Became a major center of Hindu culture, Telugu literature, and devotional religion (especially worship of Vishnu)
- Maintained a cosmopolitan court that employed Muslim soldiers and traded extensively through Indian Ocean networks, demonstrating the pragmatic coexistence of Hindu and Islamic elements in South Asian political life

---

### Ibn Battuta in India
**Period:** 1334–1342 | **Region:** Delhi Sultanate

- The Moroccan traveler spent eight years in India, serving as a qadi (Islamic judge) under Sultan Muhammad bin Tughluq of the Delhi Sultanate
- His accounts describe the extraordinary wealth and brutal governance of the Delhi Sultanate, as well as the complex Hindu-Muslim social dynamics in Indian cities
- His experience demonstrates both the cosmopolitanism of the Islamic world (a Moroccan serving as a judge in India) and the limits of that world's reach (Hindu religious life largely invisible in his account)

---

### Bhakti Poet-Saints
**Period:** c. 1200–1500 | **Region:** India

- A movement of Hindu and mixed Hindu-Muslim poets who wrote devotional songs in vernacular Indian languages (Tamil, Hindi, Marathi) rather than Sanskrit
- Figures like Kabir (c.1440–1518), Mirabai, and Ramananda challenged caste distinctions and preached direct devotion to God regardless of religious identity
- Represent the most significant cultural synthesis between Hindu and Islamic spiritual traditions in India, creating a tradition that influenced later movements like Sikhism
