# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 9 — Globalization | AP Period 4 | AMSCO pp. 688–695**

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1947 | GATT established | Began postwar trade liberalization; foundation of later WTO |
| 1988 | ~20,000 protest IMF/World Bank meetings in West Berlin | Early organized resistance to international financial institutions |
| 1993 | Maastricht Treaty creates EU; UK joins | Sets stage for later Brexit; supranational integration accelerates |
| Jan 1, 1994 | NAFTA takes effect; Zapatista uprising begins in Chiapas | Indigenous resistance timed to protest free trade on its launch day |
| 1994–present | Religious fundamentalism grows globally | Islamic, Christian, and Hindu reactions to Western cultural globalization |
| 1995 | WTO replaces GATT | Concentrated global trade governance; fueled anti-globalization |
| 1999 | Battle of Seattle; WTO protests | Anti-globalization movement's defining moment; first major internet-coordinated protest |
| 2001 | China joins WTO | Expanded WTO reach; deepened globalization |
| 2001–2002 | Anti-IMF/World Bank protests in 23 countries | Peak of organized economic resistance to international institutions |
| 2005, 2008, 2010 | Chocolate industry misses child labor deadlines | Exposed failure of corporate self-regulation in global supply chains |
| 2006 | Muhammad Yunus wins Nobel Peace Prize | Microcredit recognized as reform model for globalization's inequality |
| 2009 | Uighur-Han riots in Urumqi; China bans Twitter/Facebook | State response to social unrest; Weibo introduced as censored substitute |
| 2011 | Manal al-Sharif arrested for driving in Saudi Arabia | Digital activism and limits of social media in authoritarian contexts |
| 2013 | Rana Plaza factory collapse, Dhaka | 1,100+ dead; became symbol of garment industry exploitation |
| 2014 | World Bank restructuring | Partial reform; critics said wealthy nations still dominated |
| 2015 | U.S. Dept. of Labor: 2 million children in cocoa-related labor | Quantified scope of supply-chain child labor crisis |
| 2016 | Brexit referendum: 52% vote to leave EU | Major democratic expression of nationalist resistance to globalization |
| 2019 | Theresa May resigns over Brexit deadlock | Political consequences of anti-globalization nationalism |
| 2019 | Amazon warehouse labor conditions reported | Rich-world labor exploitation as anti-globalization grievance |
| 2020 | UK formally exits EU | Brexit completed |

## Cause-and-Effect Chain

```
Post-WWII trade liberalization (GATT → WTO, 1947–1995)
    ↓
Global supply chains expand → cheaper goods in wealthy nations
    ↓
Labor exploitation visible (Rana Plaza 2013, child labor)
Environmental damage (Brazilian deforestation, shipping emissions)
Cultural homogenization (Americanization, See [[Unit-9.6/Globalized Culture Overview|Topic 9.6]])
Debtor-nation austerity (IMF structural adjustment)
    ↓
Resistance mobilizes:
  • Labor/NGO → fair trade, debt relief, anti-WTO protests (1988–2002)
  • Indigenous → Zapatista uprising (1994)
  • Environmentalist → ecotourism, sustainable development demands
  • Nationalist → Brexit (2016), anti-immigration politics
  • Authoritarian states → internet censorship (China 2009, Saudi Arabia)
    ↓
Limited reforms: World Bank restructuring (2014), Fair Trade certifications,
microcredit models (Yunus/Grameen Bank)
    ↓
Globalization continues to expand despite resistance
```

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP often asks for cause (why resistance emerged) and effect (what changed as a result). The key effect to note: resistance **slowed** but did not **reverse** globalization. The WTO survived Seattle; China joined anyway; Brexit had severe economic consequences. Know what reformed (World Bank 2014) vs. what didn't.
