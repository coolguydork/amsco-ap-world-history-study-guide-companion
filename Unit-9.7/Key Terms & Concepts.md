# Key Terms & Concepts

**Unit 9 — Globalization | AP Period 4 | AMSCO pp. 688–695**

---

## Economic Resistance Terms

**World Trade Organization (WTO)**
International body that took over GATT's operations in 1995 and governs more than 90% of international trade. Critics argued its closed governance served corporate interests at the expense of workers, the environment, and developing nations. China joined in 2001. The 1999 Seattle protests targeted WTO policy directly.

**Battle of Seattle (1999)**
Mass protests that shut down the WTO's millennium trade summit in Seattle, drawing 40,000+ protesters from labor unions, environmental groups, family farmers, and student organizations. Considered the launch of the modern anti-globalization movement and one of the first social movements coordinated via the internet.

**child labor**
The employment of children in exploitative or dangerous conditions, banned by international conventions but prevalent in global supply chains for cocoa, clothing, and electronics. On the AP exam it represents the human cost of economic globalization and a major target of NGO and consumer-advocacy reform.

**Rana Plaza factory**
An eight-story garment factory in Dhaka, Bangladesh, that collapsed in 2013, killing over 1,100 workers. It exposed dangerous conditions in fast-fashion supply chains and sparked international demands for corporate accountability. On the AP exam it is a key example of the human costs of globalized production.

**Amazon**
A U.S.-based multinational technology and e-commerce corporation whose global warehouse network and labor practices became flashpoints in debates about workers' rights and the human cost of globalization. On the AP exam it represents the dominance of multinational corporations and resistance from labor organizers.

**fair trade**
A trade movement and certification system ensuring producers in developing countries receive equitable prices for goods such as coffee, cocoa, and bananas, improving labor and environmental standards in global supply chains. On the AP exam it represents consumer-driven resistance to the exploitative aspects of economic globalization.

**debt relief**
The partial or total cancellation of money owed by developing nations to wealthy creditors, international banks, or institutions like the IMF and World Bank. Campaigns like Jubilee 2000 pushed for relief so poor countries could invest in health and education. On the AP exam it is a key demand of the anti-globalization reform movement.

**debt restructuring**
The renegotiation of a country's debt terms to extend repayment timelines or reduce interest rates, helping governments avoid default. Often accompanied by IMF-imposed austerity conditions that critics argue harm the poor. On the AP exam it connects to critiques of international financial institutions and inequality under globalization.

**sustainable development**
Economic development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet their needs. A key demand of the anti-globalization movement, formalized by the 1987 Brundtland Commission. The AP exam pairs it with environmental activism and critique of multinational resource extraction.

**ecotourism**
Tourism centered on natural environments that aims to conserve ecosystems and support local communities rather than exploit them. Nations like Costa Rica pioneered it as an alternative development model. On the AP exam it represents reform responses to environmental degradation linked to globalization.

## Political / Sovereignty Terms

**Brexit**
Britain's 2016 decision (confirmed by referendum) to leave the European Union, driven by nationalist sentiment, concerns about immigration, and hostility to EU regulations. The formal exit occurred in 2020. On the AP exam Brexit is a major example of nationalist resistance to supranational integration and the political backlash against globalization.

**Uighur**
A Turkic Muslim ethnic minority concentrated in China's Xinjiang region who experienced cultural suppression, mass surveillance, and detention in re-education camps under Chinese government counterterrorism policies. On the AP exam they represent ethnic and religious resistance to state-enforced cultural homogenization.

## Rights and Social Movement Terms

**human rights**
The fundamental rights and freedoms to which all humans are entitled regardless of nationality, ethnicity, or religion, codified in the 1948 Universal Declaration of [[Concept Notes/Human Rights\|Human Rights]]. On the AP exam, human rights movements represent global reform responses challenging authoritarian governance and exploitative labor practices, connecting decolonization, feminism, and international institutions.

---

## Digital Resistance Terms

**Weibo**
A Chinese microblogging platform introduced as a domestic substitute for Twitter (which was blocked in China). While it became a vehicle for citizens to express grievances, it is also heavily censored. On the AP exam Weibo represents the tension between digital communication as both a tool of resistance and an instrument of state control.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP frequently contrasts these terms: **fair trade** (bottom-up consumer reform) vs. **WTO** (top-down corporate globalization); **debt relief** (cancellation) vs. **debt restructuring** (renegotiation). Know the distinction. Also: **ecotourism** is a *pro-globalization* reform argument, not an anti-globalization demand — it argues globalization *can* help the environment.
