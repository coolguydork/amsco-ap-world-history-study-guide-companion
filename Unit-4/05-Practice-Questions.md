## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–3.**

> *"The Indians [of Hispaniola] were totally deprived of their freedom and were put in the harshest, fiercest, most horrible servitude and captivity which no one who has not seen it can understand. Even beasts enjoy more freedom when they are allowed to graze in the fields. But our Spaniards gave no such opportunities to Indians."*
> — Bartolomé de las Casas, *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies*, 1542

---

**1.** Las Casas's account is best understood in the context of which of the following developments?

- A) The Protestant Reformation's challenge to Catholic Church authority
- B) The development of the encomienda system and debate over Indigenous rights
- C) The growth of the Atlantic slave trade as a replacement for Indigenous labor
- D) The rise of mercantilist economic theory in 16th-century Spain

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Las Casas was directly responding to the encomienda system and advocating for Indigenous legal protections; his account was presented to the Spanish Crown to influence colonial policy.*

---

**2.** Which of the following most directly resulted from the conditions described by Las Casas?

- A) The complete abolition of forced labor in Spanish colonies
- B) The Treaty of Tordesillas dividing the Americas between Spain and Portugal
- C) An increased demand for enslaved African laborers to replace Indigenous workers
- D) The Spanish Crown granting full citizenship to Indigenous peoples

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans — caused by disease, violence, and coerced labor — led colonizers to turn to enslaved Africans as an alternative labor source.*

---

**3.** A historian arguing that Las Casas's account has limited value as evidence would most likely point out that:

- A) Las Casas was not present in the Americas and wrote from secondhand accounts
- B) Las Casas had a specific political agenda and may have exaggerated conditions to influence the Crown
- C) The Spanish colonial system was actually more humane than other European colonial systems
- D) Las Casas wrote too early to understand the full development of the encomienda system

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Las Casas was a polemicist with an explicit reform agenda; while his factual observations are largely corroborated, historians note his rhetorical exaggerations for political effect.*

**Use the image/map description below to answer Questions 4–5.**

> *[Stimulus: A map of the Atlantic Ocean, c. 1600, showing arrows indicating the movement of enslaved Africans from West Africa to the Caribbean and South America, European goods flowing to Africa, and sugar, tobacco, and silver flowing back to Europe.]*

---

**4.** The trade pattern illustrated in the map is best described as which of the following?

- A) The Columbian Exchange
- B) The Manila Galleon trade
- C) The triangular trade
- D) The Indian Ocean trade network

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The three-directional flow — European goods to Africa, enslaved Africans to Americas, American commodities to Europe — is the defining structure of the triangular trade.*

---

**5.** Which of the following best explains the long-term demographic consequence of the trade pattern shown in the map?

- A) Population growth in Europe due to immigration from the Americas
- B) The creation of an African diaspora and cultural transformation of the Americas
- C) A sharp decline in the West African population that ended African political power entirely
- D) The elimination of Indigenous American labor systems as Africans replaced them

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The forced migration of ~12.5 million Africans created a lasting African diaspora; African cultural, religious, and linguistic traditions deeply shaped the Americas, especially the Caribbean and Brazil.*

---

**6.** The discovery of silver at Potosí in 1545 most directly contributed to which of the following global developments?

- A) The collapse of the Ottoman Empire's trade networks
- B) A global price revolution and the expansion of Chinese commerce
- C) The decline of the Portuguese spice trade in Southeast Asia
- D) The spread of the Protestant Reformation in Northern Europe

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Potosí silver flowed through Spain to China (which demanded silver for taxation), stimulating Chinese commerce while causing inflation across Europe — a classic transoceanic cause-and-effect chain.*

---

**7.** Which of the following best describes a significant continuity between the Indian Ocean trade networks of Unit 3 and the new global trade networks of Unit 4?

- A) Both were dominated by European joint-stock companies
- B) Both relied primarily on enslaved African labor for production
- C) Both connected distant regions through the exchange of luxury goods and commodities
- D) Both were controlled by a single dominant empire

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*Long-distance exchange of luxury goods (spices, silk, silver, porcelain) characterized both periods; what changed in Unit 4 was the inclusion of the Americas and the scale of forced labor.*

---

**8.** The establishment of the VOC and the British East India Company in the early 17th century represents which of the following broader historical developments?

- A) The decline of private commerce in favor of state-controlled monopolies
- B) The emergence of new forms of capitalist enterprise that blurred state and commercial power
- C) European states abandoning the mercantilist model in favor of free trade
- D) The end of Portuguese dominance in global trade

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Joint-stock companies were a genuinely new institutional form that pooled private capital and operated with quasi-governmental powers; the VOC could declare war, mint money, and sign treaties.*

---

**9.** The spread of crops such as the potato and maize from the Americas to Eurasia and Africa most directly resulted in which of the following?

- A) An immediate decline in Eurasian agricultural productivity
- B) Population growth in Europe and Africa during the 17th and 18th centuries
- C) The abandonment of wheat and rice cultivation across the Eastern Hemisphere
- D) Famine in Asia due to over-reliance on New World crops

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*American crops — calorie-dense and adaptable — supplemented existing diets and contributed to sustained population growth in Europe, China, and Africa.*

---

**10.** Which of the following best explains why European powers developed racial chattel slavery in the Americas rather than other forms of coerced labor?

- A) African peoples were considered biologically suited to agricultural labor
- B) The demographic collapse of Indigenous peoples created a labor shortage that Europeans filled by expanding the slave trade
- C) European indentured servants refused to work in tropical climates
- D) African enslaved people were less expensive to transport than European workers

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*While racism justified slavery ideologically, the primary structural cause was the catastrophic decline of Indigenous populations that eliminated the original coerced labor force.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Analyze the causes and effects of the Columbian Exchange in the period 1492–1750."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE cause of the Columbian Exchange.
**Part B:** Explain ONE effect of the Columbian Exchange on the Americas.
**Part C:** Explain ONE effect of the Columbian Exchange on Eurasia or Africa.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Strong answers will cite European maritime technology and state-sponsored exploration — not just "Columbus sailed." Mention the *caravel*, astrolabe, or Portuguese exploration model.
> - **Part B:** Most effective = epidemic disease and [[Demographic collapse]] (50–90% Indigenous population loss). Could also discuss ecological disruption from European livestock or destruction of agricultural systems.
> - **Part C:** Focus on American crops (potato, maize) driving Eurasian population growth, or silver causing the [[Price Revolution]]. Avoid vague claims — give specific examples with regional detail.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the labor systems used in Spanish colonial Latin America with ONE other coerced labor system from a different region in the period 1450–1750."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE feature of Spanish colonial labor systems in the Americas.
**Part B:** Briefly describe ONE feature of the labor system you chose for comparison.
**Part C:** Explain ONE significant difference between the two labor systems.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - Good comparison options: Ottoman devshirme, Russian serfdom, or plantation slavery in the Caribbean.
> - **Part A:** Focus on [[Encomienda]] or [[Mita]] — emphasize they were *state-sanctioned* and tied to specific regions/tasks (e.g., Potosí mining).
> - **Part C:** A strong difference = racial vs. non-racial basis of coercion, or permanence/heritability of chattel slavery vs. fixed-term/debt-based systems.

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which the Atlantic slave trade transformed African societies in the period 1500–1750."**

> [!tip] DBQ Outline Guidance
>
> **Thesis:** Craft a historically defensible thesis that makes a *specific, defensible claim* evaluating the degree of transformation. Avoid "yes/no" — argue for *significant transformation with important continuity* or similar nuance.
>
> **Complexity Argument Options:**
> - Explain how the slave trade both *caused* African state transformation (some kingdoms grew more powerful by participating) AND *undermined* African political stability (depopulation, warfare, loss of social cohesion).
> - Compare the impact on different African regions (West Africa vs. Central Africa vs. East Africa).
>
> **Documents to Expect (hypothetically):**
> - Account from a European slave trader (perspective: economic rationalization of slavery)
> - Testimony of an enslaved African (perspective: human cost, family destruction)
> - Letter from the King of Kongo to Portuguese king (perspective: African ruler negotiating within the slave trade system)
> - European map showing slave trade routes (perspective: scale and geography)
> - Account of an African merchant who sold captives (perspective: African participation in the trade)
>
> **Outside Evidence to Include:**
> - Specific statistics: ~12.5 million transported; ~2 million died in the Middle Passage
> - Dahomey's rise as a slave-raiding state
> - Kongo's political destabilization
> - Demographic impact: West Africa's population growth slowed relative to other world regions
>
> **Sourcing:** For every document you use, explain the author's *historical situation* (Who? When? Why writing?) and how that shapes what they include or omit.
