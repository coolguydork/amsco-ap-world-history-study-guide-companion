# Timeline — Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections (1450–1750)

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

> [!tip] Big Picture
> Unit 4 (1450–1750) marks the first time all inhabited continents were connected in a single, sustained global network. It follows the Mongol-facilitated Afro-Eurasian exchange of Unit 3 and sets the stage for the Industrial Revolution and Atlantic revolutions of Units 5–6. The key shift: Europe — previously a peripheral civilization — becomes the organizing hub of global commerce, violence, and cultural exchange.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1415 | Portugal captures Ceuta | North Africa | First European territorial expansion in Africa; launches era of Portuguese exploration |
| 1440s | Portuguese begin systematic slave trading on West African coast | West Africa | Establishes the earliest leg of what will become the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]] |
| 1488 | Bartolomeu Dias rounds Cape of Good Hope | Southern Africa | Proves a sea route from Europe to Asia around Africa is possible |
| 1492 | Columbus's first voyage; reaches Caribbean | Americas | Inaugurates sustained contact between Eastern and Western Hemispheres; begins [[Columbian Exchange]] |
| 1494 | Treaty of Tordesillas | Atlantic | Spain and Portugal divide the non-European world; reflects early European imperial ambition |
| 1497–1499 | Vasco da Gama sails to India | Indian Ocean | Opens direct European sea route to Asia; disrupts overland Silk Road commerce |
| 1519–1521 | Cortés conquers Aztec Empire | Mesoamerica | Marks decisive Spanish colonial expansion; [[Demographic collapse]] accelerates with smallpox |
| 1532–1572 | Pizarro conquers Inca Empire | Andes | Spanish control of South America; silver mining at [[Potosí]] begins soon after |
| 1545 | Silver discovered at Potosí | Bolivia (Andes) | Largest silver deposit in the world; triggers global [[Price Revolution]] and funds Spanish Empire |
| 1549 | Establishment of Brazilian sugar plantations | Brazil | Portuguese begin large-scale plantation agriculture; demand for enslaved African labor accelerates |
| 1564 | Manila Galleon trade begins | Pacific (Manila–Acapulco) | First transpacific trade route; American silver reaches China in exchange for silk and porcelain |
| 1600 | British East India Company chartered | England / South Asia | Key joint-stock vehicle for British commercial empire; later becomes instrument of Indian colonization |
| 1602 | Dutch East India Company (VOC) founded | Netherlands / Southeast Asia | World's first publicly traded company; displaces Portugal in Southeast Asian spice trade |
| c. 1600s | Height of the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]] | Atlantic | Millions of Africans forcibly transported; plantation economies of Americas fully established |
| 1619 | First enslaved Africans arrive in English Virginia | North America | Beginning of racial chattel slavery in what becomes the United States |
| 1640s–1680s | Sugar revolution in the Caribbean | Caribbean | English and French colonies shift to sugar monoculture; demand for enslaved labor surges |
| 1680s | Pueblo Revolt in New Mexico | North America | Successful Indigenous uprising against Spanish colonialism; **CONTINUITY**: demonstrates ongoing Indigenous resistance |
| 1700 | About 10 million Africans already transported across Atlantic | Atlantic | Starkest illustration of the scale and brutality of the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]] |
| 1756 | Seven Years' War begins (global conflict over colonial empires) | Global | Signals intensifying European competition for colonial control; sets stage for American and French Revolutions |

---

> [!warning] Continuity Note
> Despite dramatic change in trade routes and labor systems, **long-distance commerce and coerced labor were not new** — what changed was the scale, the racial character of slavery, and the integration of the Americas into global networks for the first time.
