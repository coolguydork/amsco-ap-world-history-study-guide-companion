# Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections (1450–1750)

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

## AP Exam Period
**Period 2: 1450–1750**

## Summary

Between 1450 and 1750, European maritime powers launched voyages of exploration that permanently linked the Eastern and Western Hemispheres for the first time. The resulting Columbian Exchange transferred crops, animals, diseases, and peoples across the Atlantic, triggering demographic catastrophe for Indigenous Americans while fueling population growth in Eurasia and Africa. To extract wealth from the Americas, European empires developed coercive labor systems — including the encomienda, mita, and chattel slavery — that forcibly relocated millions of Africans across the Atlantic in the largest forced migration in human history. New global trade networks centered on American silver connected Asia, Africa, Europe, and the Americas, financing the rise of powerful joint-stock companies and mercantilist empires. These transformations accelerated cultural exchange and syncretism, but also entrenched hierarchies of race, class, and gender that would shape the modern world.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how European exploration and colonization transformed global networks of exchange.
- Analyze the causes and effects of the Columbian Exchange on populations, environments, and economies.
- Evaluate the development of the Atlantic slave trade and its demographic, economic, and cultural consequences.
- Explain how global trade in silver and other commodities linked distant societies and fueled imperial competition.
- Describe how coercive labor systems emerged in the Americas and their social consequences.
- Analyze how cross-cultural encounters produced new forms of cultural and religious syncretism.
