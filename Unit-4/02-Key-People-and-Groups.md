# Key People and Groups — Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

## Individuals

### Christopher Columbus (1451–1506)
- **Region:** Genoa (Italy) / Spanish Crown
- **Period:** Late 15th century
- Sailed west under Spanish sponsorship in 1492, making landfall in the Caribbean and inaugurating sustained European contact with the Americas.
- His voyages triggered the [[Columbian Exchange]] and set the template for Spanish colonial exploitation.
- Though not the first European in the Americas, his voyages had by far the greatest historical consequence.

---

### Vasco da Gama (c. 1460–1524)
- **Region:** Portugal / Indian Ocean
- **Period:** Late 15th–early 16th century
- Led the first European maritime voyage to India (1497–1499) around the Cape of Good Hope, opening a direct sea route to the [[Spice trade]].
- His success gave Portugal a dominant position in Indian Ocean commerce for much of the 16th century.
- Demonstrated that European powers could bypass Ottoman-controlled overland routes to Asia.

---

### Hernán Cortés (1485–1547)
- **Region:** Spanish Mesoamerica
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- [[Conquistador]] who toppled the Aztec (Mexica) Empire in 1519–1521 with a small Spanish force, Indigenous allies, and the devastating spread of smallpox.
- His conquest of Tenochtitlán gave Spain control of the most populous and wealthy region in the Americas.
- Established the [[Encomienda]] system and laid the foundations of New Spain.

---

### Francisco Pizarro (c. 1471–1541)
- **Region:** Spanish South America
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Conquered the Inca Empire (1532–1572), capturing emperor Atahualpa and extracting a ransom in gold and silver before executing him.
- Opened the Andes to Spanish exploitation, including the massive silver mines at [[Potosí]].
- His victory, like Cortés's, depended heavily on civil war among the Inca and epidemic disease.

---

### Prince Henry the Navigator (1394–1460)
- **Region:** Portugal
- **Period:** Early–mid 15th century
- Portuguese royal patron who sponsored systematic maritime exploration down the African coast, funding expeditions that eventually rounded Africa and reached Asia.
- Established the Portuguese model of state-sponsored exploration and trade-fort networks.
- His support for cartography and navigation technology was foundational to the Age of Exploration.

---

### Bartolomé de las Casas (1484–1566)
- **Region:** Spanish Caribbean / Mexico
- **Period:** 16th century
- Spanish priest and former encomendero who became the foremost critic of Spanish colonial violence and Indigenous enslavement.
- His *A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1542) documented atrocities and influenced the New Laws of 1542, which attempted to limit the [[Encomienda]].
- Ironically, his advocacy for Indigenous peoples contributed to increased demand for enslaved Africans as a labor alternative.

---

## Empires and States

### Spanish Empire
- **Region:** Americas, Philippines, parts of Europe
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- Built the largest colonial empire in the Americas through conquest, the encomienda/mita systems, and silver extraction.
- Relied on [[Potosí]] and Mexican silver to finance European wars, ultimately contributing to inflation ([[Price Revolution]]).
- Developed the [[Casta system]] to manage a racially stratified colonial society.

---

### Portuguese Empire
- **Region:** Brazil, West Africa, Indian Ocean rim
- **Period:** 15th–18th century
- Pioneer of European maritime expansion; established trading-post empires (feitorias) along Africa and Asia before other Europeans.
- Dominated the early [[Atlantic Slave Trade]] and controlled Brazil's sugar plantations.
- Created the first direct sea route between Europe and Asia.

---

### Dutch Republic / VOC
- **Region:** Southeast Asia, Atlantic, South Africa
- **Period:** 17th century
- The [[VOC]] displaced Portugal as the dominant power in the Southeast Asian spice trade in the early 17th century.
- Pioneered the joint-stock company model and Amsterdam's stock exchange, transforming global finance.
- Established a colonial outpost at the Cape of Good Hope and settlements in the Caribbean and North America.

---

### British East India Company (EIC)
- **Region:** South Asia, Atlantic
- **Period:** 17th–19th century
- A joint-stock company chartered in 1600 to trade in Asia; eventually became the vehicle for British imperial control of India.
- Competed with the VOC in the Indian Ocean and built a plantation economy in the Caribbean using enslaved African labor.
- Its model of private–imperial partnership shaped modern corporate structures.

---

### West African Kingdoms (Dahomey, Ashanti, Kongo)
- **Region:** West and Central Africa
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- African polities that participated in — and were profoundly shaped by — the [[Atlantic Slave Trade]].
- Some kingdoms (e.g., Dahomey) became powerful by selling war captives to European traders; others (e.g., Kongo) saw their populations and political stability devastated.
- Demonstrate African agency within an unjust system, but also the system's corrosive effects on African societies.

---

### Indigenous American Peoples (Aztec, Inca, Taino, etc.)
- **Region:** Americas
- **Period:** Pre-contact through colonial era
- Experienced [[Demographic collapse]] of 50–90% due to epidemic disease, warfare, and coerced labor.
- Adapted, resisted, and ultimately transformed under colonial rule; Indigenous traditions persisted in syncretic religious and cultural forms.
- Their labor through [[Encomienda]] and [[Mita]] systems was the engine of early Spanish colonial wealth.
