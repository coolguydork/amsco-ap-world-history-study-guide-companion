# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 9.2 — Technological Advancements & Limitations: Disease | Printed pp. 641–648**

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1918–1919 | Influenza pandemic kills ~20 million worldwide | Spreads via WWI troop movements; more deaths than the war; longevity in U.S. drops 10 years |
| 1928 | Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin | Foundational antibiotic; transforms bacterial disease treatment (also 9.1) |
| 1946 | First effective drug treatment for tuberculosis (antibiotics + rest) | Prior to this, TB was incurable; demonstrates power of antibiotics |
| 1955 | Jonas Salk announces effective polio vaccine (injectable) | Launches global immunization campaigns; model of international health cooperation |
| 1961 | Albert Sabin's oral polio vaccine becomes available | Easier to administer in low-income countries; accelerates global eradication |
| 1967 | Christiaan Barnard performs first successful heart transplant (Cape Town) | Opens era of organ transplant surgery; landmark of surgical technology |
| 1971 | Doctors Without Borders (MSF) founded | Major NGO providing frontline healthcare in crisis zones |
| 1976 | Ebola virus first identified (Congo) | Hemorrhagic fever; periodic outbreaks; contained through international cooperation |
| 1979 | WHO declares smallpox eradicated | Only human disease ever eradicated; model of global public health cooperation |
| 1980s | Robert Jarvik's artificial heart used clinically | Bridge device for heart transplant patients; biomedical engineering extends medicine |
| 1981 | AIDS epidemic identified (U.S.) | Caused by HIV; eventually kills >25 million; global pandemic disproportionately striking sub-Saharan Africa |
| 1988 | Global Polio Eradication Initiative launched (WHO, UNICEF, Rotary, governments) | Joint public-private campaign; reduces polio cases by 99.9% within 30 years |
| Mid-1990s | Antiretroviral drugs developed for HIV/AIDS | Transform HIV from death sentence to manageable chronic condition; access inequality persists |
| 1996 | Brazil adopts free antiretroviral drug policy | First country to guarantee universal free ARV access; global model for drug equity |
| 2014 | Major Ebola outbreak in West Africa | Global alarm; WHO-led coordinated response contains outbreak — demonstrates international health governance |
| 2018 | ~40 million people globally living with HIV | Majority in developing countries; 600+ young women per week still becoming infected |
| 2019 | WHO certifies Algeria and Argentina malaria-free | Progress toward malaria elimination; but mosquito resistance to insecticides a growing concern |

## Thematic Groupings

### Medical Technology Milestones
1928 → Penicillin (Fleming) → 1946 → TB antibiotics → 1955 → Salk polio vaccine → 1961 → Sabin oral vaccine → 1967 → Heart transplant (Barnard) → 1980s → Artificial heart (Jarvik) → mid-1990s → Antiretroviral drugs

### Disease Eradication Campaigns
1967–1979 → [[Concept Notes/Smallpox\|Smallpox]] eradication campaign (WHO) → 1988–present → Polio eradication initiative → 2014 → Ebola containment (West Africa)

### HIV/AIDS Timeline
1981 → Identified → Early 1980s–mid-1990s → No effective treatment; stigma slows funding → Mid-1990s → Antiretrovirals developed → 1996 → Brazil free ARV policy → 2018 → 40 million living with HIV globally

### Diseases Linked to Poverty (persistent across entire period)
Malaria / TB / Cholera — all present throughout 1900–present; partially controlled but never eliminated because poverty persists

> [!tip]
> For LEQ and SAQ date use: the AP exam rarely asks exact dates but often expects you to place events in the correct half-century. Know: smallpox eradicated by 1979, polio vaccine 1955, AIDS identified 1981, antiretrovirals mid-1990s, heart transplant 1967. These are the most commonly tested chronological anchors for this topic.
