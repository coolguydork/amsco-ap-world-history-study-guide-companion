# Topic 2.5 — Practice and Review

## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

Neo-Confucianism, which developed during the Song Dynasty and spread to Korea, Japan, and Vietnam, is best understood as an example of:

A) The deliberate effort of Chinese emperors to impose Chinese culture on neighboring states  
B) The syncretism that occurred when Confucian ethics were combined with Buddhist and Daoist ideas, creating a new philosophical tradition  
C) The replacement of Buddhism throughout East Asia with a rationalist philosophy  
D) A religious movement that rejected all spiritual belief in favor of scientific inquiry

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Neo-Confucianism is a classic AP example of syncretism — the blending of Confucianism with Buddhist metaphysics and Daoist abstract thought into something new. A is wrong — it spread through intellectual networks, not imperial imposition. C is wrong — Neo-Confucianism incorporated Buddhist ideas rather than replacing Buddhism. D is wrong — it retained spiritual elements.

**Question 2**

The travels of Ibn Battuta differ most significantly from those of Marco Polo in that Ibn Battuta:

A) Traveled farther and visited more diverse locations  
B) Was primarily interested in understanding the Islamic world and its communities rather than in commerce and wealth  
C) Provided the only firsthand accounts of cities that Polo described only from secondary sources  
D) Was sponsored by the Chinese emperor to establish diplomatic relationships with foreign rulers

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> This is a classic point-of-view comparison question. Polo was a merchant whose accounts focused on commerce, goods, and material wealth. Battuta was a devout Muslim scholar whose travels were about understanding Islam's reach — he sought out scholars, pious leaders, and Islamic institutions. A is partially true (Battuta did travel farther) but not the *most significant* difference. C and D are incorrect.

**Question 3**

The development of the printing press in Europe (c. 1440s) was made possible in part by which earlier cultural transfer along the trade networks?

A) The introduction of silk weaving technology from China  
B) The spread of paper-making technology from China through the Islamic world to Europe  
C) The transfer of the astrolabe from Islamic astronomers to European navigators  
D) The adoption of the Arabic numeral system by European mathematicians

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Paper-making reached Europe from China (via the Islamic world) in the 13th century. Without paper, the printing press (invented c. 1440) would have had no medium to print on — parchment (animal skin) was too expensive and slow to produce for mass publication. A, C, and D describe real technology transfers but are not directly prerequisite to the printing press.

## Document Analysis Practice

**Source A**: Detail from a painting of fishing boats at dusk by Chan Buddhist painter Mu-ch'i, c. 1250, *Eight Views of Xiaoxiang*. Credited with starting the "sketch style" using fewest possible lines to suggest a subject. Very influential in East Asian art.

**Source B**: *"They use paper money as currency. The men as well as the women are fair-skinned and handsome. Most of them always dress themselves in silk, as a result of the vast quantities of that material produced in Hangzhou."* — Marco Polo, c. 1298

**Question**: What do these two sources together suggest about Song China's position in the world's trade and cultural networks?

**Model response**: Both sources reflect China's material prosperity and cultural sophistication. Polo's account, written for a European audience, documents specific commercial features (paper currency, silk production) that demonstrate China's economic development was far ahead of contemporary Europe. The Mu-ch'i painting shows the influence of Chan Buddhism (a syncretic fusion of Buddhism and Daoism) on Chinese aesthetic traditions — suggesting that even within China, ideas were being blended from multiple sources. Together, the sources suggest that Song China was simultaneously a major exporter of material goods (silk, porcelain) and cultural innovations (artistic styles, paper money) — a society whose trade connections made it both globally significant and internally sophisticated.

## CCOT Prompt

**"Describe ONE continuity and ONE change in the spread of Buddhism between c. 500 CE and c. 1450 CE."**

**Continuity**: Buddhism continued to spread through trade routes (primarily the Silk Roads) rather than military conquest throughout this entire period. The spread of Buddhism was consistently linked to commercial and diplomatic contact rather than forced conversion.

**Change**: As Buddhism spread from its South Asian origins to East Asia and Southeast Asia, it underwent significant transformation through syncretism. By the Song Dynasty period (960–1279), Buddhism in China had fused with Daoism to create Chan Buddhism, and with Confucianism to create Neo-Confucianism — producing distinctly East Asian forms of the religion that their South Asian originators would barely recognize.

## Comparison: Three Great Travelers

| Dimension | Marco Polo | Ibn Battuta | Margery Kempe |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| Origin | Venice, Italy | Morocco | England |
| Religion | Christian | Muslim | Christian (mystic) |
| Motive | Commercial/curiosity | Religious learning | Pilgrimage |
| Point of view | Merchant lens | Islamic scholarly lens | Personal/spiritual |
| What they noticed | Wealth, goods, commerce | Islamic institutions, scholars | Spiritual experiences, daily life |
| Historical significance | Evidence of Chinese wealth; paper money; Europe-China contact | Breadth of Islamic world; conditions in Mali, India, China | Rare female medieval voice; middle-class perspective |
| Source limitation | May have exaggerated/embellished; no firsthand access to some areas | Focused mainly on Islamic lands; less coverage of non-Muslim regions | Limited to places she personally visited; spiritual bias |

> [!exam-tip]
> On DBQ prompts that include accounts from these travelers, *always* address their point of view as a source limitation. Polo's merchant POV, Battuta's Muslim POV, and Kempe's personal/spiritual POV all shape what they noticed and what they missed.
