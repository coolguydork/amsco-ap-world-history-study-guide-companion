# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 2.5

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam rarely asks for exact dates, but chronological reasoning matters for arguments about causation and CCOT. Know the *sequence* — which developments preceded or enabled others.

## Chronological Events

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 7th c. CE | Xuanzang travels to India and back; Chan Buddhism emerges from Buddhist-Daoist synthesis | Buddhist doctrinal fusion produces China's most popular syncretic faith |
| 960–1279 | Song Dynasty China | Neo-Confucianism matures; printing spreads texts; Hangzhou flourishes as cultural and commercial hub |
| c. 1000–1450 | Timbuktu grows as a center of Islamic learning | Trade routes spread Islamic scholarship deep into West Africa |
| c. 1200–1450 | Islam spreads to Southeast Asia via Indian Ocean merchants | Peaceful commercial diffusion; Java and the Malay Peninsula adopt Islam |
| 13th century | Papermaking reaches Europe from China via the Islamic world | Foundational step toward rising European literacy and the Renaissance |
| c. 1204 | Fourth Crusade; Crusaders sack Constantinople | Weakens Byzantium; opens Europe further to Eastern trade and cultural exchange |
| c. 1245–1295 | Marco Polo's life and travels to Yuan China | Firsthand account of Chinese urbanization and commerce; stimulates European geographic curiosity |
| c. 1250 | Chan Buddhist painter Mu-ch'i's "sketch style" spreads across East Asia | Artistic diffusion along trade routes alongside religious and commercial exchange |
| c. 1271–1295 | Marco Polo in the court of Kublai Khan | Documents Hangzhou, paper money, silk, Chinese urbanization |
| 1293–1527 | Majapahit Kingdom (Java) | Hindu-Buddhist-Islamic synthesis; emblem of Southeast Asia as crossroads |
| 1304–1353 | Ibn Battuta travels 30+ years across Dar al-Islam and beyond | Primary source for Islamic cultural unity and diversity; insider perspective |
| c. 1325 | Ibn Battuta departs Morocco aged 21 | Beginning of the most extensive medieval travel account |
| c. 1346–1349 | Black Death devastates Constantinople (kills ~half the population) | Demonstrates lethal unintended consequences of trade connectivity |
| 1347–1351 | Black Death reaches and sweeps through Europe | ~25 million dead; weakens feudalism; trade routes as disease vectors |
| c. 1373–c. 1440 | Margery Kempe; *The Book of Margery Kempe* | Rare female, middle-class medieval travel account; early English autobiography |
| 1389–1390 | Tamerlane ravages Kashgar | Decline of a major Silk Roads urban center |
| 1453 | Ottoman conquest of Constantinople | Fall of Byzantine capital; some historians mark this as the end of the Middle Ages |

## Sequence to Know for Arguments

**Technology diffusion chain:** Chinese invention → Islamic world adapts and transmits → reaches Europe
- Papermaking: China → Islamic world (c. 8th c.) → Europe (13th c.)
- Gunpowder: China → Islamic world → Europe (late medieval)
- Compass, stern rudder: China → Indian Ocean seafarers → Europe

**Religious diffusion sequence:**
1. Buddhism: India → China (Silk Roads, ~1st–7th c.) → Korea, Japan, Vietnam (via China) / Southeast Asia (via Indian Ocean)
2. Islam: Arabia → spread across Afro-Eurasia from 7th c. onward; by 1200–1450 reaching deep into West Africa and Southeast Asia

**Urban rise and fall:**
- Rise: Hangzhou, Timbuktu, Samarkand, Kashgar, Calicut (all peak c. 1200–1400)
- Fall: Kashgar (Tamerlane, 1389–90); Constantinople (plague 1347–49; Ottoman conquest 1453)
