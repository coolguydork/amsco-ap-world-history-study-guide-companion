# Unit 2.5: Cultural Consequences of Connectivity

#apwh #unit-2 #period-1

## Overview

Trade networks during c. 1200–1450 functioned as cultural transmission belts, carrying not just goods but religions, languages, technologies, and ideas across continents. Islam spread via Indian Ocean merchants to Southeast Asia and East Africa, Buddhism traveled via the Silk Roads into Central and East Asia, and Sufi missionaries adapted Islamic practice to local customs to win converts across diverse regions. Persian emerged as the cosmopolitan lingua franca of the Islamic world, while Chinese technologies — paper, printing, gunpowder, and the magnetic compass — diffused westward along Silk Road corridors. The period represents one of the most consequential eras of religious and intellectual exchange in world history, reshaping the cultural geography of virtually every region these routes touched.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how increased connectivity through trade networks caused cultural change across Afro-Eurasia.
- Analyze the role of merchants, missionaries, and pilgrims as agents of cultural diffusion.
- Evaluate how local cultures adopted, adapted, or resisted outside cultural influences.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-2.1.III** — Exchanges along Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, and Trans-Saharan routes spread new crops, technologies, and disease as well as cultural, religious, and artistic traditions.
