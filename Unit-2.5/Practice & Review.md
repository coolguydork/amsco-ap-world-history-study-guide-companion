## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source passage (AMSCO p. 117):*

> "Ibn Battuta traveled primarily in Muslim-ruled lands, the Dar al-Islam [House or Abode of Islam], while the Christian Polo, son of a European merchant, lived and worked in countries whose cultures and religions were foreign to him. This difference makes a comparison of their works most interesting. Marco Polo's knowledge of four Asian languages as well as Italian allowed him to communicate with foreigners and even work as an administrator for the Chinese emperor. Yet, in all his travels, he remained culturally an 'outsider' to the peoples he met, and this fact enhanced his power of observation and stimulated his natural curiosity. By contrast, Ibn Battuta usually traveled as an 'insider' and his hosts accepted him as a respected Muslim jurist [qadi] and student of Islamic mysticism [Sufism]. Traveling to more than sixty Muslim courts, where he met rulers and their officials, Ibn Battuta was able to judge the behavior of his hosts in light of the Muslim scripture, the Koran, and the precepts of Islamic law. For him, the difference between their native cultures and his own North Arabic culture was a secondary importance."
>
> — *Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta: The Merchant and the Pilgrim*

---

**Question 1.** Which of the following would have been most likely to "set the stage" for Marco Polo to have been able to travel to the lands that he did?

(A) The Pax Mongolica
(B) The voyages of Vasco da Gama
(C) The conquests of the Umayyad Dynasty
(D) The development of the printing press

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The [[Concept Notes/Pax Mongolica|Pax Mongolica]] — Mongol-imposed stability across Central Asia — made overland trade and travel from Europe to China feasible. (B) Vasco da Gama sailed in the late 15th century, after Polo. (C) The Umayyad Dynasty expanded Islam westward into North Africa and Iberia, not eastward toward China. (D) The printing press is a technology transfer result, not a precondition for Polo's travel.

---

**Question 2.** Which of the following were most responsible for influencing the point of view of the travelers mentioned in the passage?

(A) Religion and economics
(B) Military and technology
(C) Social hierarchy and governance
(D) Religion and philosophy

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Polo was a merchant (economics); Battuta was a Muslim scholar and jurist (religion). The passage explicitly frames the comparison around these two dimensions. (D) is tempting but the passage foregrounds economics (Polo's merchant identity) more specifically than philosophy.

---

**Question 3.** Which of the following statements most accurately describes the difference in the worlds in which Polo and Ibn Battuta traveled?

(A) Polo traveled to areas that shared his language and religion while Ibn Battuta traveled to areas that did not.
(B) Polo traveled primarily to areas that were less prosperous than where he grew up while Ibn Battuta traveled primarily to areas that were more prosperous than where he grew up.
(C) Polo traveled to regions very different from Europe while Ibn Battuta traveled mostly within the Islamic world.
(D) Polo traveled to regions that Europe was closely connected to while Ibn Battuta traveled to regions that were not closely connected to his home region.

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The passage establishes Polo as a cultural outsider traveling to foreign lands (East Asia), while Battuta moved as an insider through the Dar al-Islam. (A) reverses the situation — Polo was the outsider. (B) is unsupported by the passage. (D) contradicts the premise — Europe was not closely connected to China in this period.

## Short-Answer Questions

### SAQ 1 — Sourcing a Primary Source (AMSCO p. 119)

*Source passage (AMSCO p. 119):*

> "The borrowing and lending necessary for most of the crusaders stimulated credit formation and the development of credit institutions and instruments. Indeed, the money economy as a whole must have been stimulated by these great enterprises which took so much money. The transformation of gold and silver altar ornaments into coin for crusaders may have helped to heighten the inflation that occurred during the Crusades, especially in the later 12th century. The sale of land to finance most assuredly helped to make the market in real estate which was bringing about a new social order in the age of the Crusades. The principal beneficiaries of all these financial transactions were the bourgeoisie, who loaned the money, bought the land, sold the provisions, furnished the transportation, and generally benefited from the financial activity of the crusaders."
>
> — Fred Cazel, "Financing the Crusades," 1989

**(A)** Identify Cazel's point of view of the Crusades from the passage.

> [!tip] Model Response — Sourcing (POV)
> Cazel views the Crusades primarily as an *economic* phenomenon rather than a religious or military one. His focus on credit, currency, land markets, and the rise of the bourgeoisie suggests he sees the Crusades as a catalyst for the commercialization of medieval European society. Writing in 1989 as an academic historian, his perspective reflects a materialist interpretive tradition that foregrounds economic causation.

**(B)** Explain Cazel's argument about the economic situation that resulted from the Crusades.

> [!tip] Model Response — Argument Summary
> Cazel argues that financing the Crusades transformed the European economy: it created credit markets, converted religious wealth into circulating currency (driving inflation), generated a land market as nobles sold estates to raise funds, and ultimately enriched the emerging bourgeoisie — the merchants, bankers, and provisioners who profited from crusader needs. The result was a "new social order" in which commercial classes gained economic power at the expense of the landed nobility.

**(C)** Explain ONE way in which the 12th-century economy affected the Crusades.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The growth of a money economy in 12th-century Europe made large-scale military expeditions financially feasible. As trade expanded (partly driven by earlier contact with Byzantine and Islamic markets), European merchants and banking families could extend credit to crusading nobles. Without these credit mechanisms, individual lords could not have funded the equipment, provisions, and transportation required for campaigns to the Holy Land. Economic development thus *enabled* the Crusades rather than simply being an effect of them.

### SAQ 2 — Cultural Consequences (AMSCO p. 119)

**(A)** Identify ONE way in which the diffusion of Buddhism to Southeast Asia was evident during the period 1200–1450.

> [!tip] Model Response — Evidence
> The Khmer Empire (Angkor Kingdom) in present-day Cambodia provides clear evidence: the royal monuments at Angkor Thom combined Hindu artwork (depicting Hindu gods, from an earlier phase) with Buddhist sculptures and artwork added by later Buddhist rulers — a physical layering of successive religious diffusion visible in the architecture itself. The Majapahit Kingdom on Java, officially Buddhist during this period, similarly reflects the spread of Indian religious traditions via Indian Ocean trade networks.

**(B)** Explain ONE way in which the arrival of Islam influenced African culture during the period 1200–1450.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The arrival of Islam transformed the intellectual landscape of West Africa by making Timbuktu a major center of Islamic learning. As Muslim merchants crossed the Sahara, they brought not just goods but scholars, texts, and madrasas. By c. 1000–1450, Timbuktu had grown into a city renowned for Quranic scholarship and book culture, reflecting how trade-route Islam diffused educational and intellectual institutions into sub-Saharan Africa. Linguistically, Swahili — a blend of Bantu and Arabic — emerged along the East African coast as a direct product of sustained Arab-African commercial interaction.

**(C)** Explain the perspective of ONE travel writer in the period 1200–1450.

> [!tip] Model Response — Sourcing (Situation)
> Ibn Battuta's perspective was shaped by his identity as a devout Muslim scholar and jurist from Morocco. Traveling as an insider through the Dar al-Islam, he evaluated the societies he visited against the standards of Islamic law and piety. His accounts prioritized descriptions of Muslim rulers, courts, mosques, and scholars, and he measured a society's legitimacy by how faithfully it observed Islamic precepts. This perspective gives his *Rihla* unparalleled depth on Islamic institutional and legal culture across 14th-century Afro-Eurasia, but also means he paid relatively little attention to non-Muslim populations or practices in the lands he visited.

## Historical Reasoning Practice — Sourcing & Situation

> [!exam-tip]
> **AP Skill: Sourcing & Situation** — For every primary source, practice applying these four lenses: *Point of View*, *Purpose*, *Historical Situation*, *Audience*. This is tested on every DBQ and many SAQs.

**Practice exercise (AMSCO p. 120):**
Using the Polo vs. Battuta comparison passage above, identify for *each* traveler: (1) their point of view, (2) their purpose, (3) their historical situation, (4) their intended audience, and (5) one strength and one limitation of their account as historical evidence.

| | Marco Polo | Ibn Battuta |
|--|-----------|-------------|
| Point of view | Christian Venetian merchant; cultural outsider | Devout Moroccan Muslim jurist; cultural insider |
| Purpose | Record commercial and geographic wonders | Document Islamic world; fulfill obligation to Sultan of Morocco |
| Historical situation | Post-Mongol unification; Yuan Dynasty China accessible | Peak Dar al-Islam connectivity; Mongol-disrupted but Islamic networks intact |
| Audience | European readers (skeptical) | Sultan of Morocco; Muslim scholars |
| Strength | Detailed on Chinese commerce, urbanization, material culture | Deep access to Muslim courts; legal and devotional detail across 60+ courts |
| Limitation | Commercial bias; may exaggerate for credibility; outsider gaps | Underrepresents non-Muslim populations; judges by Islamic standards |

## Quick Review

> [!tip] Five things to know cold for the AP exam
> 1. **Pax Mongolica** enabled Marco Polo's journey — Mongol stability = safe Silk Roads travel.
> 2. **Ibn Battuta** = insider; **Marco Polo** = outsider. This difference explains everything about their POVs.
> 3. **Black Death** = unintended consequence of trade connectivity. Enters Europe via trade routes, 1347.
> 4. **Technology chain**: China → Islamic world → Europe. Papermaking, gunpowder, compass.
> 5. **Syncretism** wherever religions meet: Chan Buddhism (Buddhist + Daoist), Urdu (Hindi + Arabic + Persian), Swahili (Bantu + Arabic).
