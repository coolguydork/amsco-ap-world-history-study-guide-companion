# Topic 2.5 — Key Terms and People

## Key Travelers

**Marco Polo (c. 1254–1324)** — Venetian merchant from Venice who visited Kublai Khan's court and returned to Italy in 1295; wrote (or dictated) accounts of China's wealth, size, and paper-money economy. Europeans initially refused to believe him. His point of view was commercial — focused on material wealth, trade goods, and urban prosperity. Quote from Hangzhou: *"They use paper money as currency. The men as well as the women are fair-skinned and handsome. Most of them always dress themselves in silk."*

**Ibn Battuta (1304–1368/1377)** — Muslim scholar from Morocco who traveled ~75,000 miles over 30 years through Central Asia, Southeast Asia, South Asia, China, Spain, North Africa, and Mali. Dictated *A Gift to Those Who Contemplate the Wonders of Cities and the Marvels of Traveling*. His point of view was religious — focused on Islamic scholarship, piety, and institutions. Unlike Polo, Battuta traveled to understand Islam's reach, not to scout commercial opportunities.

**Margery Kempe (c. 1373–c. 1440)** — English Christian mystic; could not read or write; dictated *The Book of Margery Kempe*, one of the earliest autobiographies in English. Traveled to Jerusalem, Rome, Germany, and Spain on pilgrimage. Historically significant as a rare firsthand account of a middle-class medieval woman's spiritual life and experiences. Had 14 children.

## Key Concepts

**Cultural diffusion** — The spread of cultural elements (ideas, technologies, religions, art styles, crops) from one society to another through contact — trade, migration, conquest, or missionary work.

**Syncretism** — The blending or merging of different religious or cultural beliefs into a new, combined form. Key syncretic developments in this period: Chan (Zen) Buddhism (Buddhism + Daoism), Neo-Confucianism (Confucianism + Buddhism + Daoism), Swahili language (Bantu + Arabic), Urdu (Sanskrit-Hindi + Arabic + Farsi).

**Chan (Zen) Buddhism** — A syncretic form of Buddhism that fused with Daoism in China; emphasized direct experience, meditation, and simplicity over scholarly study of scriptures. Remained popular among ordinary Chinese even when the scholar-gentry preferred Neo-Confucianism.

**[[Concept Notes/Neo-Confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]]** — Philosophical tradition originating in Tang Dynasty China; developed in the Song Dynasty. Fused Confucian ethics with Daoist and Buddhist abstract ideas. Became the dominant ideology of governance in China, Korea, Japan, and Vietnam. Korea adopted it as official state ideology.

**Bhakti movement** — Devotional religious movement in South Asia that sought to find common ground between Hinduism and Islam; emphasized personal devotion to god over ritual or caste. Evidence of the cultural blending that happened as Islam spread to South Asia.

## Key Cities

**Hangzhou** — The cultural capital of Song Dynasty China; population of ~1 million; located at the southern end of the Grand Canal. Marco Polo's descriptions of its silk industry, paper money, and prosperity astonished European readers. A model for how trade created urbanization.

**Constantinople** — Once the wealthiest city in Europe and a crossroads of trade between Europe and Asia. Suffered: the Fourth Crusade (1204), the Black Death (1346/1349), and finally fell to the Ottoman Turks in **1453** — marking the end of the Byzantine Empire and (for many historians) the end of the Middle Ages.

**Timbuktu** — Center of Islamic scholarship in West Africa, under the Mali and later Songhai empires. By the 1500s, books were the most valuable commodity produced and sold there. See [[Unit-2.4/Trans-Saharan Trade]].

**Kashgar / Samarkand** — Silk Roads oasis cities; centers of Islamic learning and commerce. See [[Unit-2.1/Key Terms and People]].

## Key Technology Transfers

**Paper-making** — From China (origin) → Islamic world (c. 750 CE) → Europe (c. 13th century via Spain and Italy). Combined with the printing press (~1440), it transformed literacy in Europe.

**Gunpowder** — From China → transmitted via Mongol conquests and trade → Islamic world and Europe. The Mongols may have synthesized the cannon from Chinese gunpowder + Muslim flamethrowers + European bell-casting techniques.

**Champa rice** — Fast-ripening, drought-resistant rice from Vietnam (with possible Indian origins); introduced to China as tribute; enabled double-cropping and fed China's growing population. Also spread throughout Southeast Asia.

**Urdu** — Language that emerged in South Asia as a blend of Sanskrit-based Hindi, Arabic, and Farsi — a linguistic artifact of Islam's spread to South Asia through conquest and trade.

**Swahili** — Language that blended Bantu (African) and Arabic; spoken by ~200 million people today. Evidence of the depth of Indian Ocean cultural exchange along the East African coast.

## Factors in City Growth vs. Decline

| City Growth | City Decline |
|-------------|-------------|
| Political stability | Political instability/invasion |
| Safe, reliable transportation | Disease (Black Death) |
| Rise of commerce | Decline of agricultural productivity |
| Plentiful labor supply | Overgrazing/environmental degradation |
| Increased agricultural output | Loss of trade route access |
