## Chronological Patterns

**Wave 1 — Anticolonial and Civil Rights (1940s–1960s):** Nonviolent resistance achieves major gains. Gandhi wins Indian independence (1947); MLK wins Civil Rights Act (1964–65); Mandela's struggle begins (1964).

**Wave 2 — Cold War Resistance (1956–1968):** Eastern European reform movements clash with Soviet power. Poland (1956), Hungary (1956), Czechoslovakia (1968) all crushed by Soviet military force. Brezhnev Doctrine defines hard limits.

**Wave 3 — 1968 Global Revolt:** Student and worker protest movements erupt simultaneously in both Western democracies and communist states. Shared grievances: anti-imperialism, racism, authoritarianism, Vietnam War.

**Wave 4 — Post-Cold War Terrorism (1980s–2000s):** As superpower proxy conflicts wind down, non-state terrorism becomes the dominant form of political violence. Shining Path (1980), continued IRA/ETA activity, and ultimately al-Qaeda (2001) reshape global security priorities.
