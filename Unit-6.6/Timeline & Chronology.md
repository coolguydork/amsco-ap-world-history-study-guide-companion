## Chronological Overview

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1788 | Britain establishes penal colony in Australia | First use of Australia as destination for coerced convict migration; sets up eventual free-settler colony |
| 1801 | Britain abolishes Irish Parliament | Push factor for Irish emigration: political subjugation and loss of self-governance |
| 1806 | Britain ends slave trade; first Chinese contract workers sent to Trinidad | Marks beginning of transition from slave labor to contract/indentured labor in British colonies |
| 1807–1808 | Britain and US abolish the transatlantic slave trade | Creates labor vacuum in plantation colonies → direct cause of indenture and contract systems |
| 1816 | Andrew Geddes Bain emigrates to Cape Town | Example of British technical-expert migration to colonies; eventually maps South African geology |
| 1833–1834 | Britain abolishes slavery in its empire; indenture begins | British colonies begin sending Indian indentured workers to Mauritius as replacement labor |
| 1845–1852 | Irish Great Famine | Potato blight plus British colonial policies kill ~1 million; drive over 1 million to emigrate immediately; millions more follow |
| 1846 | Repeal of the Corn Laws (Britain) | Increased evictions of Irish tenant farmers; accelerated emigration from Ireland |
| 1848–1851 | Gold rushes in California (1848) and Australia (1851) | Pull factor drawing tens of thousands of Chinese migrants and European settlers to Americas and Oceania |
| c. 1850–1864 | Taiping Rebellion | Devastating civil war in China (~20 million deaths); key push factor for Chinese emigration to Southeast Asia, Americas, and Australia |
| 1855 | Britain stops Chinese contract labor trade | International pressure mounts against "coolie" system |
| 1861 | Italian unification | Triggers first wave of Italian mass emigration driven by poverty and land scarcity |
| 1862 | US Congress bans Asian contract labor trade | Further curtailment of the contract labor system in the United States |
| 1865 | US abolishes slavery (13th Amendment) | Last major New World slave economy (after Cuba 1886 and Brazil 1888) ends; transforms post-war labor markets |
| c. 1815–1914 | 55–65 million Europeans emigrate | Largest voluntary migration in world history; primarily to Americas and Australasia |
| 1877 | China-Spain treaty terminates Chinese contracts in Cuba | About 125,000 Chinese had been sent to Cuba; treaty ends the system |
| 1880 | By this year, Indians working on plantations across British colonies | Caribbean, South Africa, Fiji, Southeast Asia all receive Indian indentured workers |
| c. 1880–1914 | Mass Italian emigration to Argentina | Over 4 million Italians leave; pull factor: Argentine pro-immigration policies and higher wages |
| 1885 | Japan-Hawaii labor agreement | Approximately 29,000 Japanese workers sent to Hawaiian sugar plantations over next nine years |
| 1886 | Cuba abolishes slavery | |
| 1888 | Brazil abolishes slavery | Last country in the Western Hemisphere to abolish slavery |
| 1893 | Japan establishes Colonization Society | Government-sponsored organization to manage Japanese emigration and establish overseas presence |
| 1899 | 790 Japanese workers sent to Peru | First major Japanese labor migration to South America |
| 1907 | Gentlemen's Agreement (US-Japan) | Informal diplomatic arrangement limits Japanese emigration to US; reflects anti-immigrant backlash against Asian migrants |
| 1916 | Britain abolishes indenture system | Ends over 80 years of Indian indentured servitude in British colonies; 1.5 million Indians had been shipped abroad |
| 1924 | US Immigration Act | Formally ends Japanese immigration to the US; part of broader restriction of Asian immigration |

## Thematic Chronology

**Coerced Migration Arc:**
1500s–1807 → Transatlantic slave trade (African enslaved people)
1806–1877 → Chinese contract labor system
1834–1916 → Indian indenture system
1788–1868 → British convict transportation to Australia

**Voluntary Migration Arc:**
1815–1914 → Mass European emigration (Irish, Italian, British, Jewish, others)
1850s onward → Chinese diaspora (gold rushes, construction, commerce)
1868 onward → Japanese overseas emigration (government-managed)

**Abolition Sequence (Americas):**
United States 1865 → Cuba 1886 → Brazil 1888

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam expects you to know approximate periods, not exact years. But the key anchors matter: Great Famine 1845–52, British abolition of slavery 1833, US 1865, Chinese contract labor mid-19th century, Japanese Gentlemen's Agreement 1907. The chronological arc from enslaved labor → contract/indenture → free migration is a core CCOT argument.
