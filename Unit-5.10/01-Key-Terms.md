# Key Terms — Unit 5.10: Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

1. **CCOT (Continuity and Change Over Time)** — A core AP World History analytical skill: identifying what changed across a time period and what remained the same, and explaining the causes of both change and continuity. (CCOT essays are the most common long-essay format on the AP exam for Period 3; they require both a change argument and a continuity argument with specific evidence for each.)

2. **Industrial capitalism** — The form of capitalism that emerged from the Industrial Revolution; distinguished from commercial capitalism (profit from trade) and financial capitalism (profit from investment); based on profit from machine-powered production using wage labor. (Industrial capitalism *evolved from* commercial capitalism — a key continuity. What changed was the *source of profit* and the *scale of production*.)

3. **Demographic transition** — The shift from high birth rates and high death rates (pre-industrial) to low death rates followed eventually by low birth rates (industrial and post-industrial); driven by improvements in medicine, sanitation, and food supply. (A major change of the 19th century; population grew explosively as death rates fell before birth rates adjusted downward — producing the population pressure that drove emigration and urbanization.)

4. **Global inequality** — The widening economic gap between industrialized nations (high-wage manufacturing economies) and non-industrialized regions (low-wage commodity producers) that the Industrial Revolution produced and deepened. (A major *change*: the gap between rich and poor nations was smaller in 1750 than in 1900; industrialization created global inequality at a scale that had not previously existed.)

5. **Persistence of patriarchy** — Despite industrialization and feminist organizing, gender hierarchy — men's control of political, economic, and cultural power — persisted through the entire period and beyond. (A major *continuity*: "separate spheres" ideology reconfigured patriarchy for industrial society but did not abolish it; women's suffrage had not been achieved in most nations by 1900.)

6. **Environmental transformation** — The use of fossil fuels (coal, petroleum) at industrial scale began producing measurable environmental change — air pollution, deforestation, river contamination — that would escalate in the 20th century. (A new *change*: pre-industrial economies had local environmental impacts; industrial fossil fuel use began producing planetary-scale impact for the first time.)

7. **Abolition of slavery** — The gradual legal elimination of chattel slavery across the Atlantic World: Britain (1833), France (1848), USA (1865), Brazil (1888). (A major *change* in formal legal status; but significant *continuities* in racial hierarchy, sharecropping, indentured labor, and economic subordination of formerly enslaved peoples persisted after formal abolition.)

8. **Nation-state** — A political unit in which a sovereign state's population shares a common national identity (language, culture, history); the dominant form of political organization after the Atlantic Revolutions and European nationalist movements. (A major *change* from the dynastic empires of the pre-1750 world; but many states remained multiethnic empires (Ottoman, Habsburg, Russian, British) — the *ideal* of the nation-state was more widespread than its *reality*.)

9. **Persistence of agrarian majority** — Despite industrialization, most of the world's population remained agricultural through 1900; even in industrialized Britain, a significant rural population persisted. (A major *continuity*: global urbanization did not become the majority experience until the 21st century; industrialization was geographically concentrated.)

10. **Core-periphery model** — The economic pattern in which industrialized "core" nations controlled high-value manufacturing while non-industrialized "peripheral" regions supplied raw materials; this pattern was established in the 16th century with European colonialism and deepened during industrialization. (An important *continuity*: the 19th-century global economy reproduced the hierarchical structure of the 16th-century colonial economy, albeit at greater scale and with different mechanisms.)

> [!exam-tip]
> For CCOT essays, the strongest arguments acknowledge complexity: change that produced new forms of an old problem (slavery abolished but racial hierarchy persists), continuity that changed in character (capitalism persists but transforms from commercial to industrial), and unexpected consequences (Enlightenment natural rights intended for property-owning men became tools for feminist and anti-slavery arguments).
