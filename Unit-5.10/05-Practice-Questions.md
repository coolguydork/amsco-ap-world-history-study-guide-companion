# Practice Questions — Unit 5.10: Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–2.**

> *"The bourgeoisie, wherever it has got the upper hand, has put an end to all feudal, patriarchal, idyllic relations… It has resolved personal worth into exchange value… The bourgeoisie cannot exist without constantly revolutionizing the instruments of production, and thereby the relations of production, and with them the whole relations of society."*
> — Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, *Communist Manifesto*, 1848

---

**1.** Marx and Engels argue that the Industrial Revolution represents which of the following types of historical change?

- A) A return to the economic relations of ancient slave societies
- B) A radical rupture that dissolves all traditional social relations and replaces them with market relationships
- C) An extension of feudal agricultural relations into an industrial context
- D) A temporary disruption that will return to pre-industrial stability once overproduction crises are resolved

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Marx argues that capitalism "resolves personal worth into exchange value" — destroying traditional social bonds (feudal, patriarchal, community) and replacing them with cash transactions. This is the change argument: capitalism dissolves the old world.*

---

**2.** A historian arguing for *continuity* across the 1750–1900 period would most directly challenge the argument in the passage by pointing to which of the following?

- A) The persistence of capitalism as a system of private ownership and market exchange from its commercial origins through its industrial form
- B) The decline of manufacturing in traditional agricultural societies
- C) The success of socialist movements in abolishing capitalism in several European nations
- D) The preservation of feudal land relations in Japan after the Meiji Restoration

**✅ Correct Answer: A**
*The best continuity counterargument to Marx's "radical rupture" claim is that capitalism itself — private property, wage labor, market exchange — *continued* from commercial to industrial form. What changed was capitalism's *mechanism* and *scale*, not its fundamental structure.*

---

**3.** Which of the following best illustrates the historical pattern of "change in form, continuity in substance" for the period c. 1750–1900?

- A) The abolition of slavery in the British Empire (1833) and the subsequent use of Indian indentured labor on Caribbean plantations
- B) The replacement of absolute monarchy by democratic republics in France after the Revolution
- C) The shift from water-powered to steam-powered factories in British textile production
- D) The transition from guild-based craft production to factory-based wage labor in European cities

**✅ Correct Answer: A**
*Abolition of slavery (formal legal change) followed by indentured labor (new form of coerced work) illustrates perfectly how the *form* of unfree labor changed while the *substance* — the economic exploitation of non-European workers for plantation agriculture — persisted.*

---

**4.** The widening economic gap between industrialized nations and non-industrialized regions between 1750 and 1900 represents which of the following?

- A) A continuity from the pre-industrial world, where similar gaps had always existed
- B) A change produced by industrialization's concentration of manufacturing capability and wealth in a few regions
- C) A temporary phenomenon reversed by the spread of industrialization to all regions by 1900
- D) A pattern unique to Asia and Africa; Latin American nations industrialized at similar rates to Europe

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The income gap between the richest and poorest nations was much smaller in 1750 than in 1900; industrialization produced and deepened global inequality rather than inheriting it from a pre-existing pattern — a genuine change.*

---

**5.** The persistence of patriarchal gender hierarchy despite the Atlantic Revolutions' Enlightenment principles best illustrates which of the following?

- A) Enlightenment thinkers did not actually believe in natural rights for any group
- B) Formal ideological change (the proclamation of natural rights) does not automatically produce structural social change (equal rights in practice)
- C) Women's rights movements did not exist until the 20th century
- D) Industrial capitalism inherently benefited women by providing new economic opportunities

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Natural rights were proclaimed in 1776, 1789, and 1848 (Seneca Falls) — but women's suffrage was not achieved in most Western nations until 1918–1928. The gap between ideological proclamation and social reality is the continuity argument for gender hierarchy.*

---

**6.** Which of the following represents the most significant environmental CHANGE produced by industrialization in the period c. 1750–1900?

- A) The acceleration of soil depletion through intensive agriculture
- B) The beginning of fossil fuel combustion at industrial scale, initiating long-term atmospheric carbon accumulation
- C) The spread of European disease to previously isolated populations
- D) The deforestation of European landscapes for shipbuilding and agricultural expansion

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Pre-industrial environmental changes (soil depletion, deforestation) represent continuities. The genuinely *new* environmental transformation of the Industrial Revolution is fossil fuel combustion at industrial scale — creating for the first time a mechanism for planetary-scale atmospheric change.*

---

**7.** The Latin American independence movements of the 1810s–1820s are best characterized as representing which of the following in terms of continuity and change?

- A) Radical transformation of social, economic, and political structures, similar to the Haitian Revolution
- B) Political independence (change) combined with the preservation of existing social hierarchies and colonial economic structures (continuity)
- C) Continuity in political governance under Spanish authority combined with significant economic and social reform
- D) Complete adoption of Enlightenment ideals including abolition of slavery and women's suffrage

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Latin American independence brought political change (end of Spanish rule, new republican constitutions) combined with social and economic continuity (creole elites preserved land ownership, racial hierarchy, and in most cases slavery — the opposite of the Haitian pattern).*

---

**8.** Which of the following best represents a *continuity* in economic organization between the pre-industrial commercial capitalism of the 1600s–1700s and the industrial capitalism of the 1800s?

- A) The factory system and the organization of production in centralized workplaces
- B) Private ownership of productive property and the pursuit of profit through market exchange
- C) The use of coal and steam as the primary energy sources for production
- D) The joint-stock corporation as the primary vehicle for capital investment

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Private ownership + market exchange + profit-seeking are the defining features of capitalism in *both* its commercial and industrial forms — the continuity that connects the VOC (Unit 4) to Carnegie Steel (Unit 5).*

---

**9.** The abolition of the Atlantic slave trade (Britain 1807, USA 1808) followed by the persistence of the *domestic* slave trade (within the American South) best illustrates which of the following?

- A) That international pressure was more effective than domestic reform in eliminating slavery
- B) That formal legal prohibitions on one aspect of a system may leave the underlying system intact
- C) That the economics of slavery were no longer profitable by 1807
- D) That Enlightenment natural rights principles were being consistently applied to enslaved people

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Abolishing the transatlantic *trade* while leaving domestic slavery in place illustrates how partial reform leaves underlying systems of exploitation intact — a pattern that recurs with the abolition of slavery itself (formal freedom but continued economic subordination in sharecropping).*

---

**10.** Which of the following provides the strongest evidence that the Industrial Revolution represented a *genuine* rupture with the pre-industrial past, rather than merely an acceleration of existing trends?

- A) The use of wage labor, which had existed in artisanal and agricultural economies before industrialization
- B) The substitution of fossil fuel energy for biological energy (human/animal/plant) as the primary power source for production — a qualitatively unprecedented shift
- C) The increased scale of international trade, which had been growing since the 15th century
- D) The emergence of private property and market exchange as the dominant economic relations

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Wage labor, trade, and private property all had pre-industrial precursors — they represent accelerations, not ruptures. The fossil fuel energy shift is genuinely unprecedented: for the first time in human history, an economy was powered by geological resources rather than the solar energy captured by living organisms.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ) — CCOT Focus

### SAQ 1

**"Evaluate the extent to which the period c. 1750–1900 represents a period of continuity or change in global economic systems."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE major change in global economic organization in this period.
**Part B:** Identify ONE significant continuity in global economic organization.
**Part C:** Explain which you consider more significant — the change or the continuity — and why.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Industrial capitalism's creation of factory production, global commodity markets, or new financial institutions (corporations, investment banking) — all represent changes from commercial capitalism.
> - **Part B:** Private ownership, market exchange, profit-seeking (capitalism's core), or the core-periphery trade structure (industrial nations export manufactures, non-industrial supply raw materials).
> - **Part C:** Either position can earn full credit *if defended with specific evidence and reasoning*. The strongest answers acknowledge that the answer depends on *time scale* and *geography*: for industrialized nations, change was dramatic; for colonial and non-industrialized regions, continuity of exploitation under new mechanisms is the dominant pattern.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the degree of change produced by the Industrial Revolution and the Enlightenment/Atlantic Revolutions for ONE of the following groups: women, enslaved peoples, or the rural poor."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE change the Industrial Revolution and/or Enlightenment ideals produced for the group you chose.
**Part B:** Identify ONE significant continuity in the experience of that group despite these changes.
**Part C:** Explain what the persistence of this continuity reveals about the limits of the changes in this period.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - For *women*: Change — Seneca Falls, Wollstonecraft, factory labor. Continuity — no suffrage, separate spheres, property subordination. Reveals that ideological change does not automatically produce structural change without organized political power.
> - For *enslaved peoples*: Change — legal abolition across the Atlantic. Continuity — racial hierarchy, sharecropping, indentured labor replacing chattel slavery. Reveals that formal legal liberation without economic redistribution leaves exploitation intact.
> - For *rural poor*: Change — enclosure, urbanization, new proletariat identity. Continuity — poverty, limited political power, dependence on powerful landowners/employers. Reveals that economic system change (feudalism → capitalism) can preserve class exploitation under new institutional forms.

## DBQ Outline Prompt (CCOT Synthesis)

**"Evaluate the extent to which the period c. 1750–1900 represents a period of continuity or change in the social structures of industrializing societies."**

> [!tip] Top-Score DBQ Guidance
> - **Thesis:** Must acknowledge BOTH change AND continuity; strongest theses identify the *relationship* between them (change in form producing continuity in substance, or partial change that left underlying inequalities intact).
> - **Complexity argument:** "While industrialization dissolved feudal social structures, it reconstituted class inequality in new industrial forms — replacing the lord/serf relationship with the capitalist/proletarian relationship — suggesting that the *mechanism* of exploitation changed more fundamentally than its *extent*."
> - **Sourcing:** For each document, ask: What position in the social hierarchy does the author occupy? How does that position shape what they see, what they emphasize, and what they omit? A factory owner's account of workers' conditions vs. a worker's own account vs. a government inspector's report will give three very different perspectives on the *same* social reality.
> - **Corroboration strategy:** Group documents into (1) evidence of dramatic change, (2) evidence of persistent continuity, (3) evidence of change in form without change in substance.
> - **Outside evidence:** Add: Haiti paying reparations to France until 1947 (abolition without economic liberation); Indian indentured workers replacing enslaved Africans (form changes, substance persists); Bismarck's welfare state that maintained elite power (reform without structural transformation).
