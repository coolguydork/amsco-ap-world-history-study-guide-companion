# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 5.10 — Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age · Printed pp. 351–356**

> [!tip]
> Most 5.10 dates are broad ranges (c. 1750–1900) because this is a synthesis topic. Learn the *pattern* — what was changing and what was persisting *throughout* the period — rather than pinning single events to single years.

## Chronological Overview: Key Events and Trends

| Date | Event / Trend | Change or Continuity? |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| c. 1750 | Industrial Revolution begins in Britain | **Change** — shift from agrarian to industrial production |
| 1776 | Adam Smith, *Wealth of Nations* | **Change** — intellectual foundation for capitalism over mercantilism |
| c. 1780s | James Watt's improved steam engine accelerates factory urbanization | **Change** — agricultural workers migrate to industrial cities |
| c. 1750–1800 | Western Europe shifts from [[Concept Notes/Mercantilism\|Mercantilism]] to industrial capitalism | **Change** — state-directed trade gives way to free-market competition |
| c. 1800–1900 | Latin America, Africa, South Asia remain raw material suppliers | **Continuity** — colonial-era economic periphery roles persist |
| 1805–1848 | Muhammad Ali industrializes Egypt (state-sponsored) | **Change** — non-Western state-directed modernization |
| 1832, 1867, 1884 | British Reform Acts extend male suffrage | **Change** — gradual democratic expansion, but men only |
| 1848 | *The Communist Manifesto* (Marx & Engels) | **Change** — new ideological framework for class conflict |
| 1848 | Revolutions of 1848 across European cities | **Change** — mass political mobilization for democratic rights |
| c. 1850–1900 | Labor unions form; collective bargaining spreads | **Change** — organized working-class political power |
| c. 1750–1900 | Women excluded from suffrage in all industrializing nations | **Continuity** — patriarchal political order persists |
| c. 1750–1900 | Women paid less than men; denied high-wage positions | **Continuity** — gender wage gap and occupational exclusion |
| 1868–1912 | Meiji Japan — state-sponsored industrialization | **Change** — Japan becomes most successful non-Western industrial power |
| 1883–1889 | Bismarck's social reforms in Germany | **Change** — world's first welfare state legislation |
| c. 1750–1900 | Global GDP grows dramatically; benefits distributed unequally | **Both** — economic growth is real; inequality also grows |
| c. 1750–1900 | Europe's manufacturing share: 23% → 62%; Rest of World: 73% → 11% | **Change** — fundamental shift in global economic power |
| c. 1900 | Women begin gaining suffrage (post-period) | Context — suffrage movements build throughout period but succeed after 1900 |

## The CCOT Periodization Frame

The AP expects you to frame Unit 5 (1750–1900) within AP Period 3 (1750–1900). Here is how 5.10 fits that larger frame:

**From Period 2 (c. 1450–1750):**
- Mercantilism and colonial trade networks → *continues* into early industrialization, then gives way to capitalism
- Artisan guilds and cottage industry → *replaced* by factory system
- Patriarchal gender norms → *continues* through 1900 and beyond
- European dominance in global trade → *intensifies* via industrial capitalism

**Within Period 3 (1750–1900):**
- Early phase (1750–1800): Industrialization in Britain; Enlightenment ideology drives political challenge
- Middle phase (1800–1848): Spread of industry; labor movement emerges; 1848 revolutions
- Late phase (1848–1900): Bismarck's reforms; state-sponsored industrialization outside Europe; women's movement grows; global manufacturing gap widens

**Into Period 4 (1900–present):**
- Women's suffrage achieved in most Western nations
- Labor rights legislation expands
- Global inequality (core-periphery) continues — now called "development gap"
- Marxist ideas inspire 20th-century revolutions (Russia, China)

> [!exam-tip]
> Contextualization on an LEQ about the Industrial Age should reach *back* to the Agricultural Revolution (the last comparable transformation, per the AMSCO narrative) or to the Scientific Revolution and Enlightenment — which provided both the technological basis and ideological justification for industrialization. Don't just contextualize within Unit 5.
