# Timeline — Unit 5.10: Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** This CCOT timeline presents key events of c. 1750–1900 organized to highlight what changed, what persisted, and what changed *form* but not *substance*. Use it to build CCOT essay arguments by selecting examples from across the period.

---

## Major Changes (c. 1750–1900)

| Date | Change Event | Significance |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| 1776 | American Declaration of Independence | Popular sovereignty replaces divine right as political legitimacy standard |
| 1789 | French Revolution; Declaration of Rights of Man | Feudalism abolished overnight; equality before law declared |
| 1791–1804 | Haitian Revolution | Slavery abolished in first Black republic; most radical Atlantic Revolution |
| c. 1760–1850 | Industrial Revolution in Britain | Energy source shifts from biological to fossil fuel; scale of production unprecedented |
| 1830s | Railroad age begins | Transportation costs fall ~90%; national markets integrated; urbanization accelerates |
| 1833 | British Emancipation Act | Chattel slavery abolished in British Empire; 800,000 enslaved people freed |
| 1848 | *Communist Manifesto* published | Revolutionary socialism articulated as systematic alternative to capitalism |
| 1856 | Bessemer steel process | Cheap steel enables Second Industrial Revolution; scale of industry expands further |
| 1865 | US abolition of slavery (13th Amendment) | Largest enslaved population in the Americas legally freed |
| 1868 | Meiji Restoration | Non-European nation successfully industrializes; challenges European racial hierarchies |
| 1871 | German/Italian unification | Nationalism reshapes European map; new industrial great powers emerge |
| 1883–1889 | Bismarck's welfare legislation | State assumes responsibility for workers' economic security; welfare state model created |
| 1888 | Brazilian emancipation | Last major slave state in the Americas ends chattel slavery |

---

## Major Continuities (c. 1750–1900)

| Continuity | Evidence |
|-----------|---------|
| Capitalism (private ownership, market exchange, profit) | Commercial capitalism (Unit 4) → Industrial capitalism (Unit 5) → Finance capitalism; system continues, form changes |
| European global dominance | Industrial power extends colonial reach; by 1900, Europe and USA control ~84% of world's land surface |
| Patriarchal gender hierarchy | Women still lack suffrage in most nations; "separate spheres" reconfigures but does not abolish male dominance |
| Core-periphery global economy | Non-industrial regions supply raw materials; industrial nations export manufactures; gap widens |
| Agricultural majority globally | Most of the world's population remains rural and agricultural through 1900 |
| Social hierarchy and class inequality | New forms (bourgeoisie/proletariat) replace old (aristocracy/peasantry); extreme inequality persists |
| Forced and coerced labor | Atlantic slavery abolished but replaced by indentured servitude (India, China → Caribbean, Pacific) |

---

## Events That Changed Form but Not Substance

| Event | What Changed (Form) | What Persisted (Substance) |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| Abolition of slavery | Legal status of enslaved people changed | Racial hierarchy, economic subordination, violence persisted |
| Atlantic Revolutions | Monarchies replaced by republics | Elite control of political power often persisted; women and poor excluded |
| Industrial capitalism | Source of profit (production vs. trade) | Private ownership, wage labor, market exchange persisted |
| "Separate spheres" ideology | New justification for gender hierarchy | Male dominance of political and economic life persisted |
| Latin American independence | Colonial rulers replaced by creole elites | Social hierarchy, land ownership patterns, racial structure largely unchanged |

> [!tip] CCOT Essay Structure
> - **Paragraph 1 (Change):** The most significant change of c. 1750–1900 was [energy source/political legitimacy/class structure/global scale]. Before 1750, [describe the pre-existing condition]. By 1900, [describe what had changed]. This change was caused by [specific factor].
> - **Paragraph 2 (Continuity):** However, [specific element] persisted throughout the period. [Evidence]. This continuity existed because [explanation — structural power, economic interest, institutional inertia].
> - **Paragraph 3 (Complexity):** Most precisely, [X] changed in *form* but not in *substance* — [new form] replaced [old form] while [underlying power relation] persisted. This complexity reveals [insight about historical change].
