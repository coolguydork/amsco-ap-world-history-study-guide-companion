# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 7 — Global Conflict | AP Period 4 | Printed pp. 521–530**

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1915–1916 | Armenian Genocide: Ottoman "Young Turks" deport and kill 600,000–1.5 million Armenians | First genocide of the 20th century; Hitler explicitly cited it as precedent for the Holocaust |
| Nov 11, 1918 | Armistice Day — Germany surrenders, ending WWI | Ends four years of catastrophic warfare; 8–9 million soldiers dead, 20+ million wounded |
| 1918–1919 | Influenza pandemic kills ~20–50 million worldwide | Spread by returning WWI soldiers; killed more than the war itself; early lesson in globalization's risks |
| 1932–1933 | Ukrainian famine (Holodomor): Stalin's collectivization kills 7–10 million | State-caused famine targeting peasant resistance; debates over whether it constitutes genocide |
| 1935 | Nuremberg Laws strip Jews of German citizenship | Legal foundation of Nazi persecution; excludes Jews from public life; step toward genocide |
| 1937–1938 | Rape of Nanking: Japanese troops kill ~100,000+ Chinese civilians | Mass atrocity against civilians during Sino-Japanese War; WWII precursor |
| 1941 | Nazi mobile killing units (Einsatzgruppen) begin mass shootings | First phase of Final Solution; Jews shot and buried in mass graves across Eastern Europe |
| Jan 1942 | Wannsee Conference: Nazis coordinate the Final Solution | Bureaucratic coordination of the Holocaust; death camps assigned quotas for extermination |
| 1942–1945 | Holocaust death camps operate (Auschwitz, Treblinka, Dachau) | ~6 million Jews killed; industrial genocide using trains, gas chambers, crematoria |
| 1943 | Hamburg firebombed by Allies; ~50,000 civilians killed | Allied strategic bombing targeting civilian cities; total war against civilian populations |
| Feb 1945 | Dresden firebombed by Allies; ~25,000 dead, 15 sq. mi. destroyed | Controversial late-war bombing; exemplifies Allied use of firebombing against civilian populations |
| Mar 1945 | Tokyo firebombed by U.S. forces | One of the most destructive conventional air raids in history; precedes atomic bombings |
| Aug 6, 1945 | Atomic bomb dropped on Hiroshima (~70,000–80,000 immediate deaths) | First use of nuclear weapons; inaugurated nuclear age; compelled Japanese surrender |
| Aug 9, 1945 | Atomic bomb dropped on Nagasaki | Combined with Hiroshima, forced Japan's unconditional surrender (Aug 15, V-J Day) |
| 1945–1946 | Nuremberg Trials | Nazi leaders prosecuted for crimes against humanity; established individual accountability under international law |
| 1948 | UN Genocide Convention | First international treaty obligating states to prevent and punish genocide; coined the term legally |
| 1948 | Universal Declaration of Human Rights | UN articulates universal rights for all people; counterweight to unchecked state sovereignty |
| 1975–1979 | Cambodian Genocide under Pol Pot: 1.6–1.8 million killed | Post-WWII genocide driven by communist ideology; covered in Topic 8.6 |
| 1992–1995 | Bosnian Genocide and ethnic cleansing by Serbian forces | 300,000+ killed; Slobodan Milošević indicted; part of Yugoslavia's balkanization |
| Apr–Jul 1994 | Rwandan Genocide: 500,000–1 million Tutsis and moderate Hutus killed | 100 days; organized by Hutu-led state; UN refused to intervene; defining case of international failure |
| 2002 | International Criminal Court (ICC) established | Permanent tribunal for war crimes, crimes against humanity, genocide; first ICC indictments followed |
| 2003–ongoing | Darfur genocide, Sudan: 200,000+ killed, 1 million+ displaced | ICC indicts al-Bashir (2009); demonstrates limits of international enforcement |
| 2005 | UN adopts "Responsibility to Protect" (R2P) doctrine | International community asserts duty to intervene when states commit atrocities against own people |

> [!exam-tip]
> For AP chronology questions on this topic, know the **sequence of [[Concept Notes/Holocaust\|Holocaust]] escalation**: Nuremberg Laws (1935) → ghettos → Wannsee Conference / Final Solution (1942) → death camps → liberation. This shows genocide as a process, not a sudden event — a key analytical point.

> [!tip]
> Notice the pattern: every genocide in this topic (Armenian, Holocaust, Rwanda, Bosnia, Darfur) is followed by international declarations of "Never again" — and then another genocide. The AP expects you to explain WHY enforcement has repeatedly failed (sovereignty, geopolitical interests, slow recognition, lack of political will).
