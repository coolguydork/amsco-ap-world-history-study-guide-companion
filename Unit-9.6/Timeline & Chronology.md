## Cause → Effect Chain

**Technology drives cultural globalization:**
Radio/film (1920s) → mass national audiences → television (1950s) → mass transnational audiences → internet (1990s) → global cultural exchange → social media (2000s) → individual producers reach global audiences + state censorship capabilities expand

**Americanization → backlash → hybridization:**
US dominance post-WWII → Americanization → cultural imperialism critique (throwaway culture, homogenization) → non-Western producers respond (Bollywood, K-pop, anime, reggae) → hybridized global culture that is neither purely American nor purely local

> [!exam-tip]
> AP timeline questions often ask about **continuities and changes** across periods. Key continuity: cultural exchange has always occurred ([[Concept Notes/Silk Roads\|Silk Roads]], Indian Ocean networks). Key change: the speed, scale, and bidirectionality of cultural exchange after 1960, enabled by technology.
