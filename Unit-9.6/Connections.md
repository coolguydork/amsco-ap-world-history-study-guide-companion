# Connections

**Unit 9: Globalization** | AMSCO pp. 679–687

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-9.1/Advances in Technology & Exchange Overview|Advances in Technology & Exchange Overview]]**
The technological infrastructure that enables cultural globalization — internet, satellites, fiber optic cables, smartphones — was built in 9.1. Streaming video, social media, and online commerce are downstream applications of those technological advances. No 9.1 → no K-pop going global via YouTube.

**[[Unit-9.4/Economics in the Global Age Overview|Economics in the Global Age Overview]]**
Economic globalization (free trade agreements, multinational corporations, global supply chains) created the distribution networks through which cultural products travel. McDonald's global reach is inseparable from the free-trade economic environment 9.4 describes. Consumer culture and global brands are economic as much as cultural phenomena.

**[[Unit-8.3/Effects of the Cold War Overview|Effects of the Cold War Overview]]**
The Cold War directly weaponized American popular culture as soft power (Radio Free Europe, Hollywood). The US-Soviet rivalry is the political context for why Americanization was so deliberate and well-funded. Post-Cold War openness (1991+) then removed the last major barriers to cultural exchange.

**[[Unit-7.9/Causation in Global Conflict Overview|Causation in Global Conflict Overview]]**
The global conflicts of the 20th century — WWI, WWII, Cold War — disrupted and then remade cultural boundaries. Modernism was a direct response to WWI trauma. The Harlem Renaissance emerged from the Great Migration and post-WWI disillusionment. World War II accelerated American global cultural dominance.

## Leads To

**[[Unit-9.5/Calls for Reform & Responses Overview|Calls for Reform & Responses Overview]]**
Social media and global communication networks ([[Unit-9.6/Globalized Culture Overview|9.6]]) directly enable the human rights activism 9.5 describes. Malala Yousafzai and the Arab Spring demonstrate that globalized communication empowers reform movements. The same tools also enable authoritarian surveillance.

**Unit 9.7 — Resistance to Globalization** [[Unit-9.7/Resistance to Globalization Overview|Resistance to Globalization Overview]]
Cultural globalization generates the resistance movements 9.7 covers. Critics of Americanization, throwaway culture, and cultural imperialism are the direct pushback against what 9.6 describes. Religious fundamentalism partly responds to the secular consumer culture spread by globalization.

## Compare With

**[[Unit-6.7/Effects of Migration Overview|Effects of Migration Overview]]**
Migration is a key vector for cultural diffusion in both periods. In 6.7, mass migration carries cultural practices across oceans; in 9.6, technology does the same work with or without physical movement. Compare: Jamaican diaspora spreading reggae culture to UK in person (6.7 mechanism) vs. YouTube spreading K-pop globally (9.6 mechanism).

**[[Concept Notes/Silk Roads|Silk Roads]]** (pre-modern)
The Silk Roads were the original vehicle for long-distance cultural diffusion — [[Concept Notes/Buddhism\|Buddhism]], Islam, art styles, and technologies spread along trade routes. The globalization of 9.6 is the modern analogue: trade routes replaced by internet infrastructure, caravans by streaming platforms. Key contrast: speed and bidirectionality.
