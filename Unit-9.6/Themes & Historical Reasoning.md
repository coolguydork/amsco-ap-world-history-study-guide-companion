# Themes & Historical Reasoning

**Unit 9: Globalization** | AMSCO pp. 679–687
**AP Themes:** CDI (Cultural Developments & Interactions), SIO (Social Interactions & Organization), ECN (Economic Systems)
**AP Skills:** Causation, Comparison

---

## CDI — Cultural Developments & Interactions

This topic is almost entirely CDI. The central question is how technology, economic integration, and political change caused unprecedented cultural exchange — and what consequences followed.

**Causes of cultural globalization:**
- Technology: radio → film → television → internet → social media created mass audiences that transcended borders
- Economic integration: NAFTA, EU, WTO reduced barriers to trade in cultural goods as well as physical ones
- Cold War: the US weaponized popular culture as soft power (Radio Free Europe); Soviet bloc responded by restricting it
- Migration and diaspora communities: spread cultural practices to new regions (Rastafarianism to Europe via Jamaican diaspora)
- Post–Cold War openness: cultural barriers fell alongside economic ones after 1991

**Effects and tensions:**
- Cultural homogenization: global brands, English as a lingua franca, shared entertainment franchises → "McDonaldization"
- Cultural hybridization: Bollywood (Indian + Hollywood), K-pop (Korean + American R&B + EDM), reggae (African + Caribbean + American) → new forms, not just copies
- Cultural imperialism critique: Americanization seen as eroding local identity; throwaway culture criticized as wasteful
- Local resistance and preservation: governments banned foreign social media (China), invested in domestic cultural exports (South Korea), or maintained language-specific media ecosystems

> [!exam-tip]
> The CDI theme asks you to explain how cultures **interact** — not just spread. The AP rewards students who can describe both the spread of Americanization AND the non-Western pushback (Bollywood, K-pop) or adaptation (glocalization of McDonald's menus).

## SIO — Social Interactions & Organization

**Rights movements and cultural expression:** Civil rights and women's rights movements brought marginalized voices into the mainstream, changing what culture could say and who could say it. The Harlem Renaissance is the clearest example: a social movement that created lasting cultural products.

**Religion and social identity:** Globalization disrupted traditional religious communities while also spreading religious movements. Falun Gong's suppression shows how religious practice became a site of conflict between state control and individual identity. New Age religion and Hare Krishna reflect Western societies' search for meaning outside established institutions.

**Secularization:** Younger generations in developed nations increasingly identify as nonbelievers — a social change with cultural consequences (less religious art, fewer religious institutions shaping public life). Tension with ongoing global spread of Christianity and Islam.

**Women in global sports:** Muslim female athletes adapting athletic wear to compete while honoring religious practice — a microcosm of negotiation between tradition and globalization. Illustrates that globalization is not a single path toward Western norms.

## ECN — Economic Systems

**Consumer culture as economic system:** Post–WWII prosperity created a self-reinforcing cycle: mass production → advertising → consumer demand → more production. Global brands (Nike, Apple, Coca-Cola) turned culture into a profit center. Online commerce (Amazon, Alibaba) extended this globally.

**Knowledge economy:** The shift from manufacturing to information and cultural production as the basis of economic value. K-pop and Bollywood are not just cultural phenomena — they are major export industries. The South Korean government's investment in K-pop reflects recognition that cultural exports generate economic returns.

**Throwaway culture critique:** The environmental and economic critique of disposable consumer goods connects to Topic 9.3 (environmental challenges). Planned obsolescence as economic strategy; its critics link consumer culture to ecological degradation.

## Historical Reasoning: Causation

**AMSCO's "Think as a Historian" prompt:** Compare the two waves of globalization:

| Factor | First Wave (1800–1914) | Second Wave (1960–present) |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocity | Largely one-directional (European/Western → rest) | More bidirectional (US dominance BUT non-Western flows back) |
| Who led | European colonial powers, industrializing states | US, then multinational corporations, then internet platforms |
| Motives | Raw materials, markets, civilizing mission | Consumer markets, soft power, profit, ideological competition |
| Knowledge economies | Minimal — industrial goods dominated | Central — music, film, software, data are the key exports |

> [!tip]
> The causation skill here requires you to distinguish proximate causes (specific technologies like YouTube) from long-term causes (post-WWII economic growth, Cold War competition). The AP rewards multi-causal explanations.

## Historical Reasoning: Comparison

**Key comparisons for AP essays:**

**Homogenization vs. Hybridization:** Americanization argument (all cultures becoming alike) vs. hybridization argument (Bollywood, K-pop, reggae as genuinely new cultural forms). AP essays that acknowledge both tend to score higher.

**First and second waves of globalization (1800–1914 vs. 1960–present):** See table above. Key difference: the second wave includes significantly more non-Western cultural production.

**Cultural globalization vs. economic globalization:** These often reinforce each other (consumer brands, online commerce) but can diverge — China accepted economic globalization while strictly filtering cultural globalization.

**Social media: liberation vs. control:** Arab Spring (liberation) vs. Chinese censorship (control) vs. Malala Yousafzai's Nobel Prize platform — same technology, radically different outcomes depending on context.

> [!exam-tip]
> The TAH comparison prompt in AMSCO specifically asks about globalization waves. Practice: "How did the motives for globalization in the period c. 1800–1914 differ from those in the period c. 1960–present?" Use the table above as your scaffold.
