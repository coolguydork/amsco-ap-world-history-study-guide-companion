# Unit 3 Overview: Land-Based Empires (c. 1450–1750)

#apwh #unit-3 #period-2

## What This Unit Covers

Unit 3 examines how powerful land-based empires rose, expanded, and administered vast multiethnic populations across Afro-Eurasia between roughly 1450 and 1750. This era is defined by the **Gunpowder Revolution** — the use of firearms and cannons to conquer territory and consolidate state power — alongside sophisticated bureaucratic, legal, and religious strategies for holding empires together.

## AP Key Concepts (KC) Covered

- **KC-4.1** — Empires expanded and conquered new peoples around the world, but they often had difficulty administering their large territories and maintaining the loyalty of their subjects.
- **KC-4.3** — Empires and states around the world continued to use the existing social structures, hierarchies, and belief systems to maintain power, while adapting them to new circumstances.
- **Unit 3 Learning Focus** — Rulers used a variety of methods to legitimize and consolidate their power, including the promotion of particular religious traditions, monumental architectural projects, and dynastic art patronage — all visible in Ottoman, Mughal, Safavid, and Qing imperial culture.

## AP Themes Foregrounded in Unit 3

- **Governance** — How did rulers use military force, religion, and bureaucracy to hold multiethnic empires together?
- **Cultural Developments & Interactions** — How did art, religion, and intellectual life reflect and reinforce imperial power?
- **Economic Systems** — How did land-based empires extract revenue and control trade routes?
- **Social Structures** — How did empires manage diverse populations across class, religion, gender, and ethnicity?

## The Big Picture

Unit 3 bridges the medieval world of Unit 1–2 (the Mongol networks, the Islamic Golden Age, the Silk Roads) and the oceanic world of Unit 4 (European exploration, the Atlantic system). While European states were beginning to project power outward across oceans, the most powerful states on earth between 1450 and 1750 were land-based: the Ottomans controlled the eastern Mediterranean, the Safavids dominated Persia, the Mughals ruled the Indian subcontinent, the Qing unified China, and the Russians expanded across Eurasia. Understanding these empires is essential for comparing state-building strategies on the AP exam.

> [!exam-tip]
> Unit 3 is a **high-yield unit** on the AP exam. Expect SAQ and LEQ prompts asking you to compare how two different empires justified or maintained power. The devshirme system, the mansabdar system, and the examination system are frequently tested in stimulus-based MCQs.
