# Timeline & Chronology

*Topic 8.1 | Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization*

## Key Events

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| November 1943 | Tehran Conference | First Big Three summit; USSR to focus on Eastern Europe, West to open D-Day second front; first step toward post-war division |
| February 1945 | Yalta Conference | Big Three negotiate post-war order; Stalin promises free elections in Eastern Europe (later broken); UN framework agreed |
| April 12, 1945 | FDR dies; Truman becomes president | Shifts US posture — Truman far less inclined toward accommodation with Stalin |
| May 1945 | Germany surrenders (V-E Day) | War in Europe ends; Soviet troops occupy Eastern Europe; US-Soviet tensions become open |
| July–August 1945 | Potsdam Conference | Last Big Three summit; Truman and Stalin clash over Eastern Europe; cold war begins in earnest |
| August 1945 | US drops atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki | Japan surrenders; US establishes nuclear monopoly; signals new era of warfare |
| 1945 | United Nations founded | New international framework for collective security and human rights |
| c. 1944–1947 | Bretton Woods system established | US dollar as global reserve currency; IMF and World Bank created; US economic dominance institutionalized |
| 1947 | Indian independence and partition (India/Pakistan) | First major decolonization; model and precedent for other independence movements |
| 1947 | Truman Doctrine announced | US formally commits to containment of Soviet expansion; Cold War officially named and theorized |
| 1948 | Universal Declaration of Human Rights | International normative framework for individual rights; moral vocabulary for anti-colonial movements |
| 1949 | USSR tests atomic bomb | Ends US nuclear monopoly; accelerates arms race |
| 1952–1953 | H-bomb tests (US 1952, USSR 1953) | Arms race escalates to weapons capable of destroying civilization; MAD doctrine emerges |
| January 1961 | Eisenhower's farewell address | Warns against military-industrial complex; marks end of "first generation" Cold War leadership |

## Chronological Patterns to Know

**1943–1947: From Alliance to Confrontation.** The arc from Tehran to the Truman Doctrine is the story of Cold War's emergence — cooperation under military necessity giving way to ideological confrontation once the common enemy (Hitler) was eliminated.

**1945–1949: The Nuclear Window.** The US held an atomic monopoly for exactly four years. That window shaped US confidence and Soviet urgency — both the Marshall Plan strategy and Soviet pressure on Eastern Europe make more sense when you remember the US had a weapon the USSR didn't.

**1947: The Pivot Year.** Indian independence AND the Truman Doctrine in the same year — decolonization and Cold War containment launch simultaneously, setting up their entanglement for the next four decades.

> [!exam-tip]
> Dates the AP loves to test: **Yalta 1945** (post-war order negotiated), **Truman Doctrine 1947** (Cold War formally named), **1949 Soviet atomic test** (end of US monopoly). Know what changed at each moment.
