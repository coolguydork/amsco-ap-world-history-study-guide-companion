# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 4.5: (4.5): Maritime Empires Develop

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Maritime Empires Link Regions]]** ([[Unit-4.4]]) — 4.5 develops the systems established in 4.4: plantation complex, silver trade, and joint-stock companies expand what was begun earlier.
- **[[Columbian Exchange]]** ([[Unit-4.3]]) — Demographic collapse created the labor vacuum that the Atlantic slave trade filled.
- **[[Indian Ocean Trade Network]]** (Unit 2) — VOC and EIC competed for control of an existing system; understanding 4.5 requires knowing what the Portuguese were displacing.
- **[[African Kingdoms and Trans-Saharan Trade]]** (Unit 2) — West African states' existing commercial experience made them effective (if morally compromised) participants in the slave trade.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Atlantic Revolutions]]** (Unit 5) — The Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) is a direct response to the plantation slavery system developed in 4.5. Creole grievances against mercantilist restrictions also originate here.
- **[[Industrialization]]** (Unit 5–6) — Atlantic slave trade profits funded early British industrial investment; plantation demand for textiles drove mechanization.
- **[[Abolition Movements]]** (Unit 5) — The scale and brutality of the trade (Equiano, etc.) provides the moral impetus for 18th–19th century abolitionism.
- **[[Capitalism and Global Inequality]]** (Unit 6–9) — The plantation complex and global silver trade are the foundations of the capitalist world-system that structures modern global inequality.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Atlantic slave trade vs. Indian Ocean slave trade** — Both existed; Atlantic trade was racially exclusive and produced heritable chattel slavery; Indian Ocean trade was older, less racially defined, more varied in enslaved peoples' social positions.
- **VOC vs. British East India Company** — Both joint-stock companies; VOC peaked earlier and declined faster; EIC transitioned into territorial empire more completely.
- **Spanish silver economy vs. Dutch commercial economy** — Spain relied on extraction of a finite resource (silver); Dutch built sustainable commercial networks. Long-term: Dutch model proved more economically resilient.

> [!exam-tip]
> **Silver trade flow**: Potosí/Mexico → Seville → Europe → China. Manila Galleon: Acapulco → Manila → China. Both routes converge in China, which was the ultimate *destination* of most American silver. This is a classic AP "explain the connection" question.
