# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 4.5 — Maritime Empires Develop** | Printed Pages 232–242 (AMSCO)

---

## Key Dates

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1494 | Treaty of Tordesillas | Established the legal framework for Iberian colonial monopolies — divided the non-European world between Spain and Portugal |
| 1509 | Portuguese defeat Muslim–Venetian fleet (Arabian Sea) | Confirmed Portuguese dominance of Indian Ocean trade routes; Venetian spice-trade profits collapsed |
| 1519–1521 | Cortés conquers Aztec Empire | Spanish colonial administration established in New Spain; Aztec books burned, cultural records destroyed |
| 1531 | Virgin of Guadalupe apparition (tradition) | Syncretic Catholic cult emerges; dark-complexioned Virgin becomes patron saint of New Spain |
| 1542 | New Laws limit encomienda | Bartolomé de las Casas's advocacy reduces (but does not end) indigenous forced labor; colonial labor system partially reformed |
| 1545 | Potosí silver production begins | Massive silver extraction drives mercantilist economic policies and triggers the Price Revolution across Europe and Asia |
| 1555 | Sahagún begins Florentine Codex | One of the most reliable primary sources on pre-conquest Aztec life; Franciscan friar interviews indigenous informants |
| 1588 | Spanish Armada defeated by England | Signals Spanish imperial overstretch; England begins rise as maritime power |
| 1591 | Morocco conquers Songhai Empire | Firearms decisive; disrupts trans-Saharan trade network; Spanish and Portuguese expand into the vacuum |
| 1600 | English East India Company (EIC) founded | State-chartered joint-stock company to compete with Portuguese in Asian trade; later becomes governing authority in India |
| 1602 | Dutch East India Company (VOC) founded | First major joint-stock company; shareholder investment model for empire; Dutch stock exchange also opens this year |
| 1607 | English colony at Jamestown | First permanent English settlement in North America; motivated partly by commercial profit |
| c. 1609 | Bank of Amsterdam established | Dutch financial innovation; international currency trading raises Dutch standard of living to Europe's highest |
| c. 1619–1620 | Dutch establish Batavia (Jakarta) | Hub for VOC spice trade monopoly in Southeast Asia; displaces Portuguese dominance |
| 1620 | Pilgrims found Plymouth Colony | Protestant religious dissent drives North American settlement; Anglican–Protestant split creates colonial migration |
| c. 1700s | Caribbean sugar/rum fortunes peak | Sugar becomes most profitable Atlantic commodity; British fortunes built on triangular trade; rum trade expands |
| Early 18th c. | South Sea Bubble (England) & Mississippi Company collapse (France) | Financial bubbles burst; contrast with stable Dutch financial model |
| c. 1750 | Creoles dominate New Spain politics | Colonial-born Spanish demand independence from peninsulares; seeds of 19th-century independence movements planted |

## Chronological Threads

**Economic Innovation Arc**
1494 Treaty → 1545 Potosí → 1600 EIC → 1602 VOC → 1619 Batavia → 1700s sugar peak

**Indigenous Cultural Disruption Arc**
1519 Aztec conquest → book-burning → 1542 New Laws (partial protection) → 1555 Florentine Codex → cultural erasure + syncretic survival

**Atlantic Slave Trade Impact Arc**
1494 (Iberian monopolies establish framework) → Portuguese slave trade begins mid-1400s → triangular trade formalized 1600s → Dahomey/Oyo rise on firearms → gender imbalance → polygyny → Columbian Exchange crops offset population decline

**Religious Syncretism Arc**
c. 1531 Virgin of Guadalupe → 1550s–1600s Santería/Vodun/Candomblé develop → African American church emerges in North America

---

> [!tip]
> For AP multiple-choice and SAQ, the most testable dates are: **1602** (VOC founded), **1542** (New Laws/las Casas), **1545** (Potosí), and **1494** (Tordesillas). Know what each represents, not just when it happened.
