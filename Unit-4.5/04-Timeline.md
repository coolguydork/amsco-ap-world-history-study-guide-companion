# Timeline — Unit 4.5: (4.5): Maritime Empires Develop

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

> [!tip] Big Picture
> Topic 4.5 covers the *peak and maturation* of Atlantic and Asian maritime empires — roughly 1550–1750. The key developments are the rise of the plantation complex, the Atlantic slave trade, the global silver trade, and the emergence of Dutch and English commercial empires challenging Iberian dominance.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1545 | Silver discovered at Potosí | Bolivia | Foundation of global silver trade; funds Spanish Empire and drives Price Revolution |
| 1564 | Manila Galleon trade begins | Pacific | First transpacific route; American silver reaches China for silk and porcelain |
| 1570s | Sugar plantation economy expands in Brazil | Brazil | Portuguese begin large-scale enslaved-labor sugar production |
| 1600 | British East India Company chartered | England | England enters Asian trade; begins decades-long competition with VOC |
| 1602 | VOC founded (world's first publicly traded company) | Netherlands | Dutch challenge Portuguese in Southeast Asia; new model of commercial empire |
| 1619 | First enslaved Africans arrive in English Virginia | North America | Beginning of race-based chattel slavery in what becomes the United States |
| 1621 | Dutch West India Company founded | Netherlands | Dutch challenge Spanish and Portuguese in Atlantic; seize Brazil briefly |
| 1641 | Dutch capture Malacca from Portugal | Southeast Asia | VOC displaces Portugal as dominant Southeast Asian commercial power |
| 1640s–1680s | Sugar Revolution in English and French Caribbean | Caribbean | Barbados, Jamaica, Saint-Domingue shift to sugar monoculture; slave imports surge |
| 1670s | Royal African Company monopoly on English slave trade | England / Atlantic | England institutionalizes and expands its participation in the slave trade |
| 1700 | ~10 million Africans already transported to Americas | Atlantic | Starkest illustration of the scale of the trade by this date |
| 1713 | Asiento granted to England (Treaty of Utrecht) | Atlantic | England gains right to supply enslaved Africans to Spanish colonies |
| 1750 | Atlantic slave trade approaching its peak volume | Atlantic | Peak decades (1750–1850) still ahead; trade will continue until mid-19th century |

---

> [!warning] Continuity Note
> Long-distance trade, coerced labor, and commercial competition all predate 1450. What changed after 1550 was the **racial character** of Atlantic slavery (heritable, racially exclusive), the **geographic scale** of the silver trade (truly global), and the **institutional form** of the joint-stock company (unprecedented in scope and power).
