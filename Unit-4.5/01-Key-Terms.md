# Key Terms — Unit 4.5: (4.5): Maritime Empires Maintained and Developed

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

1. **VOC (Dutch East India Company)** — The *Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie*, founded in 1602; the world's first publicly traded joint-stock company, which dominated Southeast Asian spice trade. (Model for modern corporations; could declare war, mint coins, and sign treaties — quasi-governmental power.)

2. **British East India Company (EIC)** — English joint-stock company chartered in 1600 to trade in Asia; later became the primary vehicle of British colonial rule in India. (Began as a trading company; gradually acquired territorial and military power in South Asia.)

3. **Joint-stock company** — A business in which investors pool capital and share profits and risks. (Enabled large-scale, long-duration imperial ventures by distributing financial risk across many investors.)

4. **Atlantic Slave Trade** — The forced transportation of approximately 12.5 million Africans to the Americas between the 16th and 19th centuries. (Largest forced migration in history; built the plantation economies of the Atlantic world.)

5. **Middle Passage** — The transatlantic voyage from West Africa to the Americas endured by enslaved Africans in horrific conditions. (Mortality rates of 10–20%; a defining symbol of the trade's brutality.)

6. **Triangular trade** — The three-legged Atlantic commercial circuit: European goods to Africa → enslaved Africans to Americas → American commodities to Europe. (Integrated Atlantic economies and sustained the plantation complex.)

7. **Plantation complex** — An agricultural system based on large-scale monoculture (sugar, tobacco, cotton) worked by enslaved labor in the Americas. (Generated enormous profits for European investors; created the African diaspora.)

8. **Manila Galleon trade** — The annual Spanish trading fleet carrying silver from Acapulco (Mexico) to Manila (Philippines) in exchange for Chinese silk, porcelain, and spices. (First sustained transpacific trade route; linked American silver to Asian luxury goods.)

9. **Potosí** — The Spanish colonial silver mine in present-day Bolivia, the world's largest and most productive silver deposit. (Its output financed the Spanish Empire and drove the global [[Price Revolution]] by flooding world markets with silver.)

10. **Price Revolution** — The period of sustained inflation in Europe and parts of Asia during the 16th–17th centuries caused largely by the influx of American silver. (Disrupted wages and prices; enriched merchants, impoverished those on fixed incomes.)

11. **Asiento** — A contract granting a country or company the exclusive right to supply enslaved Africans to Spanish colonies. (Became a major source of diplomatic competition between European powers.)

12. **Mercantilism** — The economic theory that national wealth is maximized through colonial extraction, favorable trade balances, and accumulation of precious metals. (Drove European competition for colonies and shaped all commercial policies of the period.)

13. **African diaspora** — The global dispersal of African peoples and cultures resulting from the Atlantic slave trade. (Created lasting African-descended communities in the Americas, Caribbean, and Europe; shaped music, religion, language, and cuisine.)

14. **Maroon communities** — Independent settlements established by escaped enslaved Africans, especially in Jamaica, Suriname, and Brazil. (Evidence of sustained resistance to slavery; some lasted centuries.)

15. **Creole** — A person of European descent born in the Americas. (Over time, creoles developed distinct identities and grievances against peninsulares, eventually driving independence movements.)

> [!exam-tip]
> Know **three key facts about the Atlantic slave trade** for any essay: approximately 12.5 million transported, ~2 million died in the Middle Passage, and the trade peaked in the 18th century (not the 16th). Students often misplace the chronological peak.
