## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Questions 1–3 refer to a map: "The African Slave Trade, 1500–1900" showing slave trade routes from West, West Central, and East Africa to the Caribbean, North America, South America, and other destinations.*

**Question 1.** Of the movements shown on the map, the largest resulted from a high demand for labor to grow

- A. sugar
- B. cotton
- C. rubber
- D. tea

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Sugar was the most profitable and labor-intensive crop in the Atlantic world. Caribbean sugar production drove the largest share of enslaved African transportation. Cotton became significant later (19th century, US South). Rubber and tea are red herrings.

---

**Question 2.** This map best supports the claim that between 1500 and 1800 Africa's population was becoming

- A. too large in 1500 to be supported by the technology of that time
- B. too small in 1800 to enable the slave trade to continue
- C. more diverse as immigrants replaced people who had been enslaved
- D. smaller than it would have without the slave trade

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> The slave trade extracted millions of people — predominantly young men — from West and Central Africa. The map shows scale of outward movement, supporting the claim that Africa's population was smaller than it would have been without the trade. (B) is tempting but the trade *continued* into the 19th century despite demographic strain — so "too small to continue" is wrong. (C) inverts the actual dynamic — there was no significant immigrant replacement. (A) is not supported by the map at all.

---

**Question 3.** The movements shown on the map were part of a global trend between 1500 and 1800 of

- A. greater contact among people of different ethnic backgrounds
- B. greater separation among people living in different continents
- C. states becoming smaller and more homogenous
- D. states becoming larger but more homogenous

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The Atlantic slave trade, triangular trade, and colonial expansion all brought previously separated peoples — African, European, indigenous American — into sustained contact, producing new cultural syntheses (syncretic religions, mixed-heritage social categories). (B) is the opposite of what happened. (C) and (D) are wrong — states became larger and more diverse, not homogenous.

## Short-Answer Questions

### SAQ 1 — Religious Syncretism (Vodun passage)

*The following passage is from the Haitian Consulate, "Haitian Vodou":*

> "Vodou [Vodun] as we know it in Haiti and the Haitian diaspora today is the result of the pressures of many different cultures and ethnicities of people being uprooted from Africa and imported to Hispaniola [the island that includes Haiti] during the African slave trade. Under slavery, African culture and religion was suppressed, lineages were fragmented, and people pooled their religious knowledge and out of this fragmentation became culturally unified. In addition to combining the spirits of many different African and Indian nations, pieces of Roman Catholic liturgy have been incorporated to replace lost prayers or elements in addition images of Catholic saints are used to represent various spirits or 'mistè' ['mysteries,' actually the preferred term in Haiti], and many saints themselves are honored in Vodou in their own right. This syncretism allows Vodou to encompass the African, the Indian, and the European ancestors in a whole and complete way."

**A.** Identify ONE way that Vodun is an example of religious syncretism.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identification
> Vodun blends West African spiritual traditions (combining spirits from multiple African ethnic groups — Dahomey, Kongo, Yoruba) with Roman Catholic elements: Catholic saints are used to represent African spirits ("mistè"), and Catholic liturgical prayers were incorporated to replace lost African prayers. This mixing of African spiritual practice and Catholicism is the defining feature of Vodun syncretism.

**B.** Explain ONE way that Christianity in Latin America demonstrated religious syncretism.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation/Explanation
> In Mexico, a cult developed around the Virgin of Guadalupe — a dark-complexioned Virgin Mary said to have appeared to an indigenous man in 1531. Catholic saints' days that coincided with days indigenous peoples had traditionally celebrated were especially emphasized, blending indigenous ritual calendars with Catholic practice. This produced a distinctly Mexican Catholic tradition that incorporated indigenous spiritual sensibilities into the European Christian framework.

**C.** Explain ONE specific example of religious syncretism other than Vodun that resulted from the Atlantic slave trade.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison/Explanation
> Candomblé, which developed among enslaved Africans in Brazil, combined Yoruba, Fon, and Bantu religious traditions from different parts of Africa into a unified belief system. Because enslaved people from many different African ethnic groups were mixed together on Brazilian plantations, they pooled their distinct religious traditions, producing a new syncretic practice that also incorporated some Catholic elements. Like Vodun, Candomblé demonstrates how forced displacement and cultural suppression paradoxically produced new religious syntheses among enslaved peoples.

### SAQ 2 — Mercantilism and the Global Economy

**A.** Identify ONE way in which mercantilism affected economies in Africa and Asia in the period 1450–1750.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identification
> European mercantilist powers established monopoly trading companies (such as the VOC and EIC) that dominated Asian spice, textile, and luxury-goods trade, displacing existing regional merchants and forcing Asian producers into European-controlled trade networks. In Africa, European demand for enslaved labor — driven by the need for cheap workers on American plantations that fed mercantilist supply chains — drew African kingdoms into a trade relationship centered on exchanging enslaved captives for European manufactured goods (especially firearms), creating economic dependency.

**B.** Explain ONE way in which the development of a global economy affected societal structures in European states between 1450–1750.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation/Explanation
> The development of joint-stock companies and new Atlantic trade routes enabled a growing European middle class — urban merchants and entrepreneurs — to invest capital in overseas ventures and share profits from empire. This gave the middle class economic and eventually political power that challenged the traditional landed aristocracy. The accumulation of capital shifted from purely land-based wealth to commercial wealth, gradually restructuring European social hierarchies away from feudal models toward commercial capitalism.

**C.** Explain ONE way in which the Commercial Revolution transformed global economies in the period 1450–1750.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation/Explanation
> The Commercial Revolution introduced the joint-stock company model, in which private investors could buy shares of trading ventures and receive proportional profits while enjoying limited liability. This mobilized middle-class capital at a scale no individual merchant or even most governments could match, funding large-scale long-distance trade and colonization. The VOC (1602) and EIC (1600) used this model to build commercial empires in Asia, fundamentally transforming Asian trade from a system of regional merchants and local rulers to one dominated by European corporate entities with state backing.

## Think As a Historian: Claims in Non-Text Sources (p. 241)

**Context:** Most primary sources from the conquest of Central and South America were written by Spaniards. A few pictorial manuscripts — including the Florentine Codex and the Kingsborough Codex (also called the Codex Tepetlaoztoc, named for a region of Mexico) — record events from the Aztec point of view.

**Question:** Which claim does the image from the Codex Tepetlaoztoc best represent?

1. Spaniards worked the indigenous people too hard.
2. The indigenous people were not prepared to do the physical labor necessary.
3. Spaniards treated the indigenous people with cruelty.
4. The indigenous people were obedient and did what they were told to do.

> [!tip] Answer: **3**
> The Codex Tepetlaoztoc was created by Aztec artists to document Spanish abuses under the encomienda system. The image records a *claim* — a position asserted to be true — that Spanish overseers were cruel to indigenous workers, not merely demanding or that workers were incompetent. Option 1 is close but "worked too hard" is a milder framing than "treated with cruelty." Options 2 and 4 reflect a Spanish perspective (blaming indigenous workers), not the Aztec perspective of the source.
>
> **Sourcing note:** The Codex's purpose (documenting abuse for a legal appeal or historical record) and its creator (Aztec artists, not Spanish colonizers) make it a rare counter-narrative. Its claim has an advocacy purpose — like las Casas, but in visual form.

## Reflect on the Essential Question

*In one to three paragraphs, explain the economic strategies maritime empires used to increase their power and the effect of the developing empires on political, economic, religious, and cultural dynamics.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Essay Outline
> **Para 1 — Economic strategies:** Maritime empires used mercantilism (maximize silver/gold reserves), joint-stock companies (private capital for empire), monopoly charters (control over trade routes and commodities), and the triangular trade (enslaved labor for plantation agriculture). These strategies transformed commerce from regional barter to global exchange using precious metals.
>
> **Para 2 — Political and cultural effects:** Spanish colonial administration replaced indigenous governments with viceroy/audiencia systems. Cultural destruction was deliberate — burning of Aztec books, language imposition, forced conversion. Yet cultural synthesis also occurred: syncretic religions (Santería, Vodun, Candomblé, Virgin of Guadalupe) emerged from the forced mixing of African, indigenous, and European peoples.
>
> **Para 3 — Long-term dynamics:** The Atlantic slave trade destabilized West Africa (demographic extraction, firearms dependency, gender imbalance), while the Columbian Exchange eventually aided African population recovery. Creole political frustration planted seeds for 19th-century independence movements. Religious conflicts intensified globally (Ottoman–Safavid, Catholic–Protestant), but [[Concept Notes/Syncretism|syncretism]] also produced new spiritual traditions that persist today.
