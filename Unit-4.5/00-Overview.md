# Unit 4.5: Maritime Empires Maintained and Developed

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

## AP Exam Period
**Period 4: 1450–1750**

## Summary

After the initial establishment of maritime empires in the late 15th and early 16th centuries, European powers faced the challenge of sustaining and expanding their control over vast territories and trade networks. The Spanish empire deepened its reliance on silver extraction and the plantation complex; the Portuguese faced competition from the Dutch and English in the Indian Ocean and Atlantic. The 17th century saw the rise of new imperial powers — the Dutch Republic and England — deploying joint-stock companies ([[VOC]], British East India Company) as instruments of commercial empire. The Atlantic slave trade reached its height in this period, transporting millions of enslaved Africans to fuel sugar and tobacco plantations in the Caribbean and Brazil. Silver from the Americas flowed through Spain to China, linking global economies. The period also saw intensifying competition between European powers, with the Dutch displacing Portugal in Southeast Asia and England challenging Spain in the Caribbean.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how joint-stock companies enabled the maintenance and expansion of maritime empires.
- Analyze the development and consequences of the Atlantic slave trade.
- Describe the global silver trade and its economic consequences.
- Evaluate how competition among European powers shaped colonial development.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.1.VI** — The development of new commercial and financial practices and institutions promoted European trade.
- **KC-4.2.III** — The Atlantic slave trade developed and intensified as European demand for plantation labor grew.

> [!exam-tip]
> Topic 4.5 is heavily tested. Know the **silver trade** (Potosí → Spain → China), the **Atlantic slave trade** (scale, routes, African agency), and the **joint-stock company** model. These three are the most frequently tested economic concepts in Unit 4.
