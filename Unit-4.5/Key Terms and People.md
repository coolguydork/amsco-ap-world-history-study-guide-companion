# Topic 4.5 – Key Terms and People

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Economic Terms

**Commercial Revolution**
The transformation of European economies from local, barter-based trade to large-scale international trade using gold and silver. Caused by: European colonies, new ocean trade routes, population growth, and inflation from American silver. *Significance*: Laid the groundwork for modern capitalism and the Industrial Revolution.

**Capital**
Material wealth (money, goods, equipment) that can be invested to produce more wealth. The accumulation of capital in Western Europe — through trade profits, silver extraction, and plantation agriculture — funded further commercial expansion and eventually industrialization.

**Price Revolution**
The high rate of inflation in the 16th and early 17th centuries caused by the influx of American silver into European economies combined with population growth. *Significance*: Disrupted traditional economic relationships; hurt those on fixed incomes (nobility, church) and benefited those who could raise prices (merchants, landowners).

**Joint-Stock Company**
A corporation owned by investors who buy shares (stock) in it, sharing both profits and risks. Feature of **limited liability** protected investors from losing more than their investment. *Examples*: British East India Company (1600), Dutch East India Company (1602). *Significance*: Revolutionary financial innovation that made risky long-distance trade feasible by distributing risk across many investors.

**Limited Liability**
The principle that an investor is not responsible for a company's debts or losses beyond the amount they personally invested. *Significance*: Made investing in risky commercial ventures far more attractive; democratized access to international trade.

**East India Company (British EIC)**
British joint-stock company founded in 1600, chartered to trade with India and Asia. Initially a purely commercial enterprise; gradually evolved into a governing power that controlled much of India by the 18th century. *Significance*: Shows how commercial interests can morph into political/military power; one of the most important institutions of the colonial era.

**Dutch East India Company (VOC)**
Dutch joint-stock company founded in 1602. Enormously profitable in Southeast Asia and the Spice Islands. Had its own stock exchange. *Significance*: The world's first publicly traded company; symbol of Dutch commercial dominance in the 17th century.

**Triangular Trade**
The three-legged Atlantic trading system: (1) manufactured goods from Europe to Africa; (2) enslaved Africans from Africa to the Americas; (3) colonial products (sugar, tobacco, rum) from the Americas to Europe. *Significance*: Integrated three continents into a single economic system built on enslaved labor; enormously profitable for European merchants.

**Monopoly**
Exclusive control over a market, trade route, or commodity, allowing the controller to set prices. European rulers granted monopolies to joint-stock companies and sometimes governments themselves. *Examples*: Spain's monopoly on tobacco from American colonies; Portugal's attempted monopoly on Indian Ocean spice trade.

## Cultural and Religious Terms

**Syncretism**
The blending of different religious beliefs, practices, and cultural traditions into new hybrid forms. *Significance*: Appears wherever different cultures come into sustained contact. In the Americas, syncretism produced new religions blending African traditions with Catholicism; in Asia, Sikhism blended Islamic and Hindu elements.

**Santería**
Syncretic religion blending Yoruba African traditions with Catholic saints. Originated in Cuba among enslaved Africans; spread throughout Latin America and North America. "Santería" means "the way of the saints."

**Vodun**
Syncretic belief system developed among enslaved Africans from Dahomey, Kongo, and Yoruba in Saint-Domingue (now Haiti). Blends West African spiritual traditions with Catholicism. "Vodun" means "spirit" or "deity." Often misrepresented in Western culture as "Voodoo."

**Candomblé**
Syncretic religion combining Yoruba, Fon, and Bantu African beliefs. Developed in Brazil among enslaved Africans. "Candomblé" means "dance to honor the gods."

**Virgin of Guadalupe**
A Catholic devotional figure that appeared (according to tradition) to an indigenous Mexican convert in the 1500s, depicted with dark complexion. *Significance*: A prime example of religious syncretism in Latin America — blending Catholic Marian devotion with indigenous religious traditions; became the most important devotional figure in Mexican Catholicism.

**Polygyny**
The practice of having more than one wife. Became more common in West Africa as the slave trade created severe gender imbalances (more men than women were taken as slaves), forcing women to adapt to traditionally male roles and social structures.

**Creoles** (in the colonial Americas context)
Those born in the Americas of Spanish or European ancestry. By 1750, creoles had achieved political dominance in New Spain despite being subordinate to Spanish-born peninsulares. *Significance*: The growing political ambitions of creoles would eventually drive the independence movements of the early 19th century.

## Administrative Terms

**Viceroys**
Spanish royal representatives who governed colonial territories. See [[Unit-4.4/Key Terms and People]] for detail.

**Audiencias**
Royal courts in Spanish colonies; a check on viceroy power. See [[Unit-4.4/Key Terms and People]] for detail.

## Key People

**Bernardino de Sahagún**
Franciscan priest who, beginning in 1545, compiled the **Florentine Codex** — one of the most important sources about Aztec life before the conquest. Important because it was based on indigenous testimony (gathered by Sahagún) rather than purely Spanish observation.

**Yemelyan Pugachev**
Cossack leader who led the **Pugachev Rebellion** (1773–1775) against Catherine the Great in Russia, rallying discontented serfs, ethnic minorities, and Cossacks against noble privilege. Defeated by the Russian army; the rebellion led Catherine to increase oppression of serfs. See [[Unit-4.6/Challenges Overview]].
