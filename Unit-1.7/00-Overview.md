# Unit 1.7: Comparison in the Period from c. 1200 to c. 1450

#apwh #unit-1 #period-1

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Compare the political, economic, cultural, and social developments across multiple regions from c. 1200 to c. 1450.
- Explain similarities and differences in how societies were organized and governed during this period.
- Use historical reasoning skills — especially comparison and continuity and change over time (CCOT) — to craft evidence-based arguments about the period.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-1.1:** Big Geography and the Peopling of the Earth
- **KC-1.2:** The Neolithic Revolution and Early Agricultural Societies
- **KC-1.3:** The Development and Interactions of Early Agricultural, Pastoral, and Urban Societies
- **KC-2.1 / 2.2:** Organization of and Interactions Between States and Societies (foundational to what Unit 1 inherits)
- **KC-3.1:** Expansion and Intensification of Communication and Exchange Networks (sets the stage)

## Summary

Topic 1.7 is a **synthesis and comparison topic** — it does not introduce new content but instead asks you to look across the regions studied in Topics 1.1–1.6 (East Asia, Dar al-Islam, South and Southeast Asia, the Americas, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Europe) and identify meaningful **similarities and differences**. The period from c. 1200 to c. 1450 is defined by the expansion of trade networks, the spread of universal religions, Mongol disruption and connectivity, and the persistence of both centralized empires and decentralized political structures. Mastering this topic means being able to write a strong **comparative argument** supported by specific evidence from at least two regions. On the AP exam, this topic is most likely tested through **LEQ (Long Essay Question)** or **SAQ (Short Answer Question)** prompts that ask you to compare two or more civilizations.

## Comparison Matrix: Six Regions at a Glance (c. 1200–1450)

| Region | Governance Type | Economic Base | Religion / Culture | Trade Connections |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **East Asia** (Song/Yuan/Ming China, Japan, Korea) | Centralized bureaucratic empire (China); feudal/decentralized (Japan) | Agriculture + commercialization; paper money; porcelain exports | Confucianism, Buddhism, Daoism; Neo-Confucian revival | Silk Roads; Indian Ocean; tributary system |
| **Dar al-Islam** (Abbasid remnants, Mamluk Egypt, Mali, Delhi) | Caliphate (fragmented); regional sultanates and emirates | Long-distance trade; artisan crafts; agriculture | Islam (Sunni dominant); Sufism spreads faith across regions | Trans-Saharan; Indian Ocean; Silk Roads |
| **South & SE Asia** (Delhi Sultanate, Khmer, Majapahit, Vijayanagara) | Sultanate + Hindu kingdoms; varying centralization | Rice agriculture; Indian Ocean spice trade | Hinduism, Buddhism, Islam (spreading); syncretic blends | Indian Ocean network; SE Asian maritime routes |
| **Americas** (Aztec predecessors, Inca predecessors, Maya city-states) | City-states; regional chiefdoms; early empire building | Maize agriculture; tribute collection; local markets | Polytheism; ritual sacrifice; ancestor veneration | Regional trade networks; no transoceanic contact |
| **Africa** (Mali, Great Zimbabwe, Swahili Coast) | Empire (Mali); city-states (Swahili); decentralized chiefdoms | Trans-Saharan gold/salt trade; Indian Ocean commerce | Islam (Mali elite); indigenous traditions; syncretic blends | Trans-Saharan; Indian Ocean |
| **Europe** (feudal kingdoms, Italian city-states, Byzantine remnants) | Feudal decentralization; emerging monarchies; city-republics | Manorial agriculture; growing urban commerce | Christianity (Catholic/Orthodox split); scholasticism | Mediterranean; Baltic (Hanseatic League) |

## How to Use This Topic

- **For MCQs:** Look for questions asking which two societies share a feature (e.g., both relied on trade wealth, both used religion to legitimate rulers).
- **For SAQs:** Be ready to compare two specific regions on governance, economy, or culture with concrete evidence.
- **For LEQs:** Practice writing a thesis that makes a defensible claim comparing at least two regions, then support it with three or more specific pieces of evidence.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam will not ask you to compare all six regions at once. Focus on building **paired comparisons** you know well: Song China vs. Mali, feudal Europe vs. feudal Japan, Delhi Sultanate vs. Aztec Empire. Practice identifying **one similarity and one difference** for each pair.

> [!warning]
> Do not treat this topic as simply a review of prior topics. The AP exam rewards students who make **explicit comparative claims** — saying "both Song China and Mali relied on commercial trade wealth" is far stronger than describing each separately.
