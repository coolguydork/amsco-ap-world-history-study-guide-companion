## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1.** Between c. 1200 and c. 1450, which of the following best describes a similarity in state-building across most regions of the world?

A) Most states relied on maritime trade as the primary source of political power
B) Religion served as a key source of political legitimacy in states across Eurasia and Africa
C) Nomadic peoples were universally absorbed into settled agricultural empires
D) Democratic governance gradually replaced monarchical systems in most regions

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The AMSCO text explicitly states: "One similarity in much of the world was that religion was a vital part of state-building." Islam legitimized rulers from West Africa to Southeast Asia. Confucianism underpinned the Song Dynasty. Hinduism and Buddhism legitimized Southeast Asian rulers. Christianity and the Roman Catholic Church shaped European political structures. A is too narrow (most power was land-based). C is wrong — the Mongols conquered settled empires, not the reverse. D is false — no major state in this period was democratic.

---

**Question 2.** Japan's political development in the period c. 1200–c. 1450 was unique compared to most of the world primarily because:

A) Japan adopted Islam as its state religion, unlike neighboring East Asian states
B) Japan became more decentralized and feudal as most other states were growing more centralized
C) Japan's economy grew more dependent on maritime trade than any other state
D) Japan was the only state in the world to adopt a Confucian civil service examination system

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Most of the world was moving toward larger, more centralized states (Song China, Mali, Aztec, Inca). Japan moved in the opposite direction — real power shifted away from the emperor toward the shogun and regional daimyo, producing a feudal system similar to (but not identical to) Europe's. A is wrong — Japan remained Buddhist/Shintoist. D is wrong — Korea also adopted the civil service system.

**Question 3.** The role of the Roman Catholic Church in European state-building differed from the role of Islam in West African state-building primarily in that:

A) The Church actively hindered the development of centralized states in Europe, while Islam supported centralized power in West Africa
B) Islam spread only through conquest, while Christianity spread only through peaceful conversion
C) The Church never became involved in political affairs, while Islam controlled all government functions in West Africa
D) Islam made no contribution to state-building in West Africa until the 15th century

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> In West Africa, Islam provided a *unifying* framework — shared language (Arabic), shared legal structures (shariah), and shared trade networks that supported the growth of centralized empires like Mali. In Europe, the Church initially *substituted* for weak states (providing education, social services, and identity) and later *competed* with emerging monarchies rather than simply supporting them. B, C, and D are all too absolute and factually wrong.

---

**Question 4.** The Aztec tribute system and the Incan mit'a system are best compared as:

A) Identical systems of labor extraction used by both empires
B) Different methods of resource extraction that both enabled large-scale imperial administration
C) Systems that gave conquered peoples full political equality within the empire
D) Religious rituals disguised as economic systems

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Aztec system extracted tribute in goods (food, cloth, luxury items); the Inca extracted labor (mit'a). Different mechanisms — but both achieved the same goal: funding imperial administration and military expansion while allowing some local autonomy. A is wrong because the systems were structurally different. C and D are wrong.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Prompt:** Use your knowledge of world history to answer all parts of the following question.

**Part A:** Briefly explain ONE similarity in the process of state-building in TWO different regions of the world in the period c. 1200–c. 1450.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE difference in the process of state-building between those same TWO regions in the period c. 1200–c. 1450.

**Part C:** Briefly explain ONE external factor that contributed to changes in state power in ANY region during this period.

> [!tip] Model Responses
> **Part A (China and West Africa — similarity):** Both the Song Dynasty in China and the Mali Empire in West Africa used control of trade to fund powerful, centralized states. The Song developed sophisticated commercial networks supported by paper money and proto-industrial production; Mali's rulers taxed the trans-Saharan gold trade, accumulating wealth that funded a large, well-administered empire. In both cases, trade surplus translated directly into state capacity.
>
> **Part B (China and West Africa — difference):** The Song Dynasty relied on a meritocratic bureaucracy staffed by Confucian scholar-officials selected through civil service examinations, creating a highly institutionalized government. Mali, by contrast, relied more heavily on personal loyalty networks and Islamic merchant connections rather than a formalized examination-based bureaucracy. The Song's institutional depth made it more administratively stable; Mali's power was more dependent on the personal authority of individual rulers like Mansa Musa.
>
> **Part C (external factor):** The Mongol conquests (c. 1206–c. 1368) dramatically reshaped state power across Eurasia. The Mongols destroyed the Abbasid Caliphate (1258), ended the Song Dynasty in China (1279), and devastated much of Central Asia. However, Mongol political stability — the Pax Mongolica — subsequently enabled expanded Silk Roads trade, which contributed to the rise of new states in its aftermath. The Mongols were thus both a destructive and, paradoxically, a state-building force.

## LEQ/DBQ Thinking: Comparison Prompt

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which religion was a more important factor than trade in the process of state-building in the period c. 1200–c. 1450.

**Brainstorm outline:**

- **Thesis angle:** Both religion and trade were essential to state-building in this period, but trade was ultimately more foundational because it generated the material surplus that made large-scale states economically viable, while religion primarily served as a legitimizing tool for states that trade had already made powerful.
- **Religion as more important (counterargument):** In the Islamic world, shared religion provided the only unifying framework after the Abbasid Caliphate fragmented — Islam held together politically divided states from West Africa to Southeast Asia. In China, Confucianism provided the ideological basis for the entire administrative system. In Europe, the Church was the only pan-regional institution in a politically fragmented landscape.
- **Trade as more important (your argument):** Mali's power came from controlling the gold trade — Islam legitimized Mansa Musa but the gold paid for the army. Song China's wealth came from its commercial economy, not just Confucian philosophy. The Aztec tribute system and Inca mit'a were economic, not religious, systems at their core. Great Zimbabwe, the Swahili coast cities, and the Southeast Asian maritime kingdoms were all built on trade control.
- **Complexity:** Religion and trade reinforced each other — Muslim merchants spread Islam; Islamic scholars traveled along trade routes; Confucian examinations produced administrators for a trade-funded state. The two causes are inseparable in most cases.

> [!exam-tip]
> For a top-scoring LEQ on this topic, you need a **line of reasoning** — not just a list of examples. Argue that trade provided the *material basis* while religion provided the *ideological superstructure*. That shows historical thinking, not just knowledge recall.
