# Unit 1.7: Key Terms for Comparison

#apwh #unit-1 #period-1

## Vocabulary for Comparative Analysis

Use these terms to build precise comparative arguments. When you compare two regions, try to use the same term for both to show structural parallels or contrasts.

---

**Feudalism** — A decentralized political and economic system in which lords grant land (fiefs) to vassals in exchange for military service and loyalty. *(Significant because it characterized both medieval Europe and Kamakura/Muromachi Japan, making it a go-to AP comparison.)*

**Tributary System** — A political arrangement in which weaker states acknowledge the dominance of a more powerful state by sending regular payments (tribute) in exchange for protection and trade access. *(Significant because China used this system throughout the period to organize relations with Korea, Vietnam, and other neighbors — demonstrating a non-conquest form of imperial influence.)*

**Caliphate** — A form of Islamic government in which a single leader (caliph) claims both political and religious authority as the successor to the Prophet Muhammad. *(Significant because the Abbasid Caliphate fragmented after the Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258, forcing Islam to spread through regional sultans and trade rather than a unified state.)*

**Decentralization** — The distribution of political authority away from a central government to local or regional rulers. *(Significant because decentralization was a common response to weak central authority — seen in feudal Europe, post-Mongol Central Asia, and many African chiefdoms — and contrasts sharply with the centralized bureaucracy of Song China.)*

**Empire** — A large political unit in which one state dominates other territories and peoples through military conquest, often ruling diverse ethnic and cultural groups. *(Significant because empires like the Mongol Empire, Mali, and the Delhi Sultanate shaped the political landscape of the period, while other regions lacked comparable large-scale states.)*

**City-State** — An independent political unit consisting of a city and its surrounding territory, governed as a single sovereign entity. *(Significant because city-states characterized both the Italian peninsula — Venice, Florence — and the Swahili Coast of East Africa — Kilwa, Mombasa — despite having no contact with each other, illustrating parallel development.)*

**Trade Diaspora** — A network of merchants from a shared ethnic, religious, or regional background who settle in foreign cities to facilitate long-distance commerce. *(Significant because trade diasporas — Arab merchants in the Indian Ocean, Jewish merchants in the Mediterranean, Chinese merchants in Southeast Asia — were the human infrastructure of medieval trade networks.)*

**Universal Religion** — A belief system that claims truth and applicability for all people regardless of ethnicity, nationality, or region, and that actively seeks converts. *(Significant because Islam, Christianity, and Buddhism all functioned as universal religions during this period, spreading along trade routes and serving as cultural glue across diverse political units.)*

**Tribute State** — A polity that maintains its independence but pays regular tribute to a more powerful neighbor; related to but distinct from full incorporation into an empire. *(Significant because many smaller kingdoms in Southeast Asia and along China's borders operated as tribute states, navigating between autonomy and submission.)*

**Bureaucracy** — A system of government administration carried out by trained officials appointed based on merit or loyalty rather than hereditary status. *(Significant because Song China's meritocratic bureaucracy — selected through the civil service exam — stands as the most developed example of the period and contrasts with the hereditary aristocracies of Europe and Japan.)*

**Patriarchy** — A social system in which men hold primary power in political, economic, and family life, and in which inheritance and authority pass through the male line. *(Significant because patriarchy was a near-universal feature across all six regions in this period, though its specific expressions varied — footbinding in China, purdah in parts of the Islamic world, and the relative autonomy of some African and Amerindian women show that patriarchy had different intensities and forms.)*

**Syncretic Religion** — A belief system that blends elements from two or more distinct religious traditions into a new, hybrid form. *(Significant because syncretism was common where Islam or Buddhism spread into new regions — Sufism blended Islamic mysticism with local practices; Southeast Asian Hinduism-Buddhism blends are another example — and helps explain how religions adapted to win converts.)*

**Mandate of Heaven** — A Chinese political concept holding that the emperor rules with divine approval, which can be withdrawn if the ruler governs unjustly, leading to natural disasters, rebellion, or dynastic collapse. *(Significant because this concept legitimated imperial authority in East Asia and functioned analogously to European divine-right or Islamic claims of ruling as God's representative — a useful comparison point.)*

**Dar al-Islam** — Arabic for "House of Islam"; the collective community of Muslim-majority lands and peoples bound by shared religious law, commercial networks, and cultural practices. *(Significant because Dar al-Islam was not a single political unit but a civilizational zone that stretched from West Africa to Southeast Asia, unified by the Arabic language, the hajj pilgrimage, and Sharia law — making it the largest cultural sphere of the period.)*

**Scholasticism** — A medieval European intellectual method that sought to reconcile classical Greek philosophy (especially Aristotle) with Christian theology through systematic reasoning. *(Significant because scholasticism — practiced by thinkers like Thomas Aquinas — parallels the intellectual synthesis happening simultaneously in the Islamic world, where scholars like Ibn Rushd (Averroes) were doing similar work, showing that both civilizations were grappling with the same philosophical inheritance.)*

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP exam, knowing a term is not enough — you need to **apply** it to a specific example. Practice writing sentences like: "Like feudal Europe, Kamakura Japan used feudalism to organize political authority, as local lords (daimyo) exchanged loyalty for land grants from the shogun."

> [!warning]
> Do not confuse **tribute state** with **tributary system**. The tributary system is the overall Chinese framework; a tribute state is a specific polity that participates in it by sending tribute to China.
