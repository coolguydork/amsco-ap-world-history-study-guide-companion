# Unit 1.7: Practice Questions

## Multiple Choice Questions (Stimulus-Based)

---

**Questions 1–2 refer to the following passage:**

> "The king of Mali is greatly respected by his subjects and lives in a most remote and magnificent palace. He governs his people with the assistance of chosen provincial governors and relies on the considerable wealth generated by the gold trade that passes through his realm."
> — Adapted from Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*, c. 1353

**Question 1.** Which of the following best explains a **similarity** between the political systems of Mali and Song China in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Both empires relied on hereditary aristocracies to govern provincial territories.
B) Both states used appointed officials to extend central authority into distant regions.
C) Both rulers claimed religious authority as caliphs or sons of heaven.
D) Both empires maintained their power exclusively through military conquest.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Both Mali's use of provincial governors and Song China's use of civil service-appointed bureaucrats represent centralized states delegating authority through appointed (not hereditary) officials.*

---

**Question 2.** The passage best supports which of the following claims about Dar al-Islam in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Islamic political authority was unified under a single caliphate centered in Mali.
B) The Islamic world was connected by trade and religious practice despite political fragmentation.
C) West African rulers resisted the spread of Islam in order to protect indigenous traditions.
D) The trans-Saharan trade network was less important than the Indian Ocean trade to Mali's economy.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Ibn Battuta's ability to travel freely and the gold trade's connection to the broader Islamic world shows that Islam unified diverse regions commercially and culturally even without a single political authority after the fall of the Abbasid Caliphate.*

**Questions 3–4 refer to the following image description:**

*A 13th-century European illuminated manuscript depicting a feudal lord granting land to a kneeling vassal, surrounded by armed knights.*

**Question 3.** Which of the following best explains a **similarity** between the political systems depicted in the image and the political system of Kamakura Japan (c. 1185–1333)?

A) Both systems featured a powerful centralized monarchy that directed military campaigns.
B) Both systems used land grants to create bonds of loyalty between lords and warriors.
C) Both systems were characterized by commercially driven urban economies.
D) Both systems relied on religious authority to sanction the transfer of political power.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Both European feudalism and Japanese feudalism (the shogunate system) used land grants — fiefs in Europe, rice-producing estates in Japan — to bind warrior classes (knights and samurai) to lords in relationships of military loyalty.*

---

**Question 4.** Which of the following represents the most significant **difference** between European feudalism (as depicted) and political organization in Song China during the same period?

A) European feudalism was based on agricultural production, while Song China's economy was entirely commercial.
B) European feudalism distributed political authority to local lords, while Song China centralized authority through a meritocratic bureaucracy.
C) European feudalism lacked any role for religion, while Song China used Buddhism as the state religion.
D) European feudal lords had no military obligations, while Song Chinese officials commanded large armies.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The defining contrast is between decentralized feudal authority in Europe (where local lords held power independently of the king) and the centralized bureaucratic empire of Song China (where officials were appointed based on civil service exam scores).*

**Questions 5–6 refer to the following passage:**

> "Merchants from Arabia and Persia came to our shores carrying cloth, porcelain, and copper. In return, they took our gold, iron, and ivory back across the sea. Many have chosen to remain and build mosques here, and their faith has taken root among our leading families."
> — Fictional account based on Swahili Coast trade records, c. 1300

**Question 5.** The process described in the passage best illustrates which of the following patterns in the spread of Islam during the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Islam spread through military conquest imposed by the Abbasid Caliphate.
B) Islam spread peacefully through the activities of merchants along trade routes.
C) Islam spread through the forced conversion of indigenous populations.
D) Islam spread as a result of the Crusades bringing European Christians into contact with Muslims.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The Swahili Coast's conversion to Islam followed the pattern of peaceful diffusion through trade — Arab and Persian merchants settled, built mosques, and the local elite voluntarily adopted Islam as a mark of commercial and cultural connection.*

**Question 6.** Which of the following regions experienced a **similar** process of Islamic diffusion during the same period?

A) The Iberian Peninsula, where Islam spread through Crusader military campaigns
B) Southeast Asia, where Muslim merchants converted coastal trading elites
C) East Africa's interior, where Mongol armies imposed Islamic law
D) Northern Europe, where Islamic missionaries displaced Christianity

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Southeast Asia mirrors the Swahili Coast pattern — Islam spread to coastal trading cities (like Malacca) through merchant communities, converting ruling elites who then patronized Islamic scholars and practice.*

**Questions 7–8 refer to the following passage:**

> "The tribute system required neighboring kingdoms to send periodic missions to the imperial court, bearing gifts and performing the ritual of submission. In return, they received imperial recognition, trade privileges, and protection."
> — Adapted from a description of the Chinese tributary system

**Question 7.** Which of the following best explains how the Chinese tributary system **differed** from the feudal system in medieval Europe?

A) The tributary system was based on military conquest, while European feudalism was based on voluntary agreements.
B) The tributary system organized relations between distinct polities, while European feudalism organized relations within a single political system.
C) The tributary system had no economic component, while European feudalism was fundamentally an economic arrangement.
D) The tributary system was used by weak states, while European feudalism was used only by strong centralized kingdoms.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: China's tributary system managed interstate relations — between China and neighboring kingdoms like Korea or Vietnam — while European feudalism was a domestic political arrangement organizing the hierarchy within a kingdom between king, lords, and vassals.*

---

**Question 8.** Which of the following empires in the period c. 1200–1450 used a system **most similar** to the Chinese tributary system to organize relations with neighboring peoples?

A) The Mali Empire, which collected gold tribute from surrounding agricultural communities
B) The Song Dynasty, which paid tribute to the Jurchen Jin Dynasty to avoid invasion
C) The Byzantine Empire, which paid tribute to the Mongols after the Fourth Crusade
D) The Inca Empire, which required labor tribute (mit'a) from conquered Andean communities

**Correct Answer: A**
*Explanation: Mali's collection of tribute from surrounding peoples most closely resembles the tributary system — the emperor in Mali received recognition and goods from subordinate communities, similar to China's receipt of tribute from neighboring states.*

---

**Question 9.** Which of the following best explains a **similarity** between the role of the Catholic Church in medieval Europe and the role of Islam in the Mali Empire?

A) Both institutions controlled military forces that defended their respective empires from Mongol invasion.
B) Both institutions served to legitimate political authority and provided a shared cultural identity across politically fragmented regions.
C) Both institutions were imposed on populations through violent conquest.
D) Both institutions operated independently of political rulers and avoided involvement in governance.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: In Europe, the Catholic Church provided cultural unity across feudal kingdoms; in Mali, Islam connected the empire to the wider Islamic world and legitimated the ruler's authority. Both show religion functioning as a centralizing cultural force despite political fragmentation.*

**Question 10.** A historian argues that "the period c. 1200–1450 was defined more by commercial connectivity than by political unity." Which of the following pieces of evidence **best supports** this claim?

A) The Mongol Empire unified Eurasia under a single political authority by 1260.
B) Song China, Mali, and the Swahili Coast all flourished through long-distance trade even as political structures varied widely.
C) The Crusades successfully unified European kingdoms under a single military command.
D) The spread of Islam created a single legal and political system across Afro-Eurasia.

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Song China, Mali, and the Swahili Coast represent three different political systems (imperial bureaucracy, tributary empire, city-states) in three different regions, all achieving prosperity through trade — supporting the claim that commercial networks were more consistent than political unity.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ Prompt 1

**"Comparison in the Period c. 1200–1450"**

Using your knowledge of AP World History:

**(a)** Describe ONE similarity in the **political organization** of Song China and the Mali Empire.

**(b)** Describe ONE difference in the **economic foundations** of Song China and the Mali Empire.

**(c)** Explain ONE cause that accounts for the difference you described in part (b).

**What a top score includes:**
- **Part (a):** A specific, accurate similarity with a concrete detail — e.g., both used appointed officials to govern distant territories; both were powerful centralized states at the peak of their wealth in this period.
- **Part (b):** A specific, accurate difference — e.g., Song China had a commercialized internal market with paper money and a manufacturing sector, while Mali's wealth was almost entirely dependent on controlling external trade routes (trans-Saharan gold and salt).
- **Part (c):** A causal explanation, not just a restatement — e.g., Mali's dependence on external trade reflects its geographic position as a middleman between the gold-producing forests of the south and the salt-producing Sahara; China's internal commercialization reflects centuries of agricultural surplus that freed labor for manufacturing and urban growth.

### SAQ Prompt 2

**"Universal Religions and Political Power"**

Using your knowledge of AP World History:

**(a)** Describe ONE way in which Islam served as a **political tool** for rulers in the period c. 1200–1450.

**(b)** Describe ONE way in which Christianity served as a **political tool** for rulers in Europe during the same period.

**(c)** Explain ONE way in which the **spread** of Islam differed from the spread of Christianity during this period.

**What a top score includes:**
- **Part (a):** Specific example — Mansa Musa used the hajj to demonstrate his legitimacy as a Muslim ruler and to build diplomatic relationships across the Islamic world; Delhi Sultans used Islamic law to organize governance over a diverse population.
- **Part (b):** Specific example — European monarchs relied on papal coronation to legitimate their authority (e.g., Holy Roman Emperor); the Church's crusading ideology mobilized military resources for political goals; canon law extended Church authority into secular governance.
- **Part (c):** A specific contrasting mechanism — Islam spread primarily through merchant diasporas and Sufi missionaries along trade routes into new regions (West Africa, Southeast Asia), while Christianity's expansion in this period was primarily through institutional consolidation within already-Christian Europe, with crusading representing a failed attempt at expansion into Muslim territory.

## Long Essay Question (LEQ)

### LEQ Prompt

**"Compare the methods by which TWO of the following regions maintained and legitimated political authority in the period c. 1200–1450:"**

- East Asia (Song/Yuan/Ming China)
- Dar al-Islam (Mali Empire or Delhi Sultanate)
- Medieval Europe

**Bullet guidance for a top score:**

- **Thesis (1 point):** Make a defensible, historically specific claim that addresses the comparison directly. Example: "While Song China legitimated imperial authority through the Mandate of Heaven and meritocratic bureaucracy, the Mali Empire used Islamic religious credentials and control of trade wealth to legitimate the emperor's rule — reflecting how the specific cultural and economic context of each region shaped its strategies of political legitimation."
- **Contextualization (1 point):** Explain the broader context of this era — the Mongol disruptions, the expansion of trade networks, the spread of universal religions. Connect this context to why legitimation was an important challenge for rulers.
- **Evidence (2 points):** Use at least two specific pieces of evidence for each region: civil service exam, Neo-Confucianism, Mandate of Heaven (China); Mansa Musa's hajj, Timbuktu's Islamic scholars, gold wealth (Mali); papal coronation, crusading ideology, feudal oaths, divine right theory (Europe).
- **Historical Reasoning — Comparison (2 points):** Make at least one explicit similarity and one explicit difference. "Both China and Mali used religious frameworks (Confucianism and Islam respectively) to legitimate imperial authority; however, China's legitimation was primarily domestic (civil service exams demonstrated the ruler's access to educated officials), while Mali's legitimation was both domestic and international (the hajj connected the ruler to the global Muslim community)."
- **Complexity (1 point):** Add nuance by explaining how the Mongol disruption forced adaptation in legitimation strategies (Yuan Dynasty adopted Chinese legitimation symbols despite being foreign conquerors; the post-Abbasid Islamic world developed regional sultanates that legitimated authority without a caliph).

> [!exam-tip]
> LEQ thesis statements must make a **claim** — not just restate the prompt. "China and Mali both used political and religious methods" is NOT a thesis. "China relied on bureaucratic meritocracy while Mali relied on Islamic credentials, both reflecting the centrality of cultural legitimation in the absence of purely military dominance" IS a thesis.
