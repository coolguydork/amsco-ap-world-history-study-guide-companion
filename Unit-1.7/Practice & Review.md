## LEQ Writing Checklist (AMSCO p. 70)

Before attempting the prompts below, review this process for developing a long essay argument:

1. **Read and analyze the task** — identify key words, geographic scope, time period, and the type of reasoning required (comparison, causation, CCOT)
2. **Gather and organize evidence** — list everything relevant, then look for patterns and connections
3. **Develop a defensible thesis** — a claim that lays out a line of reasoning, not just a restatement of the question
4. **Write an introduction** — connect your thesis to a broader historical context
5. **Write supporting paragraphs** — use evidence to support, corroborate, qualify, or modify your argument
6. **Write the conclusion** — extend your thesis rather than just restating it
7. **Reread against the rubric** — check for thesis, context, evidence, reasoning, and complexity

## Long Essay Questions (AMSCO p. 71)

### LEQ 1 — Religious and Ethical Systems

**Prompt:** By the 13th century, Islam, Hinduism, and Confucianism each included movements within them that placed greater emphasis on either emotion or reason. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which two religious or ethical systems of thought were **similar or different** up through the 15th century.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> **Thesis:** While both [[Concept Notes/Sufism|Sufi Islam]] and Neo-Confucianism emerged as reform movements that incorporated emotional and spiritual dimensions into their parent traditions, they differed fundamentally in their relationship to political power — Sufism spread through personal devotion and missionary networks largely independent of states, while [[Concept Notes/Neo-Confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]] was systematically adopted by the Song and later Korean and Japanese governments to legitimize centralized authority.
>
> **Body paragraph structure:** (1) Similarity — both movements were responses to perceived over-intellectualism in their parent traditions and both incorporated contemplative/spiritual practice; (2) Difference — Sufism's state relationship (spread through trade and missionary work, sometimes in tension with Islamic rulers) vs. Neo-Confucianism's state relationship (institutionalized through civil-service exams, directly tied to bureaucratic power); (3) Complexity — the Delhi Sultanate as a case study where both traditions coexisted and competed, showing synthesis as a third outcome.

### LEQ 2 — Chinese Cultural Influence in East Asia

**Prompt:** Because of its size, wealth, and cultural innovations, China had a significant influence on its East Asian neighbors in the years 1200 to 1450 — whether those neighbors were friendly or hostile. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which Chinese cultural traditions led to **political or social change** in other East Asian societies in that time period.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation + Comparison
> **Thesis:** Chinese cultural traditions — particularly Neo-Confucianism and the civil-service model — significantly shaped political organization in Korea and Japan between 1200 and 1450, but while Korea adopted Chinese administrative models closely, Japan adapted them selectively and ultimately developed a decentralized feudal system that diverged sharply from the Chinese centralized model.
>
> **Body paragraph structure:** (1) Korea — close adoption of Neo-Confucianism and Chinese-style bureaucracy (similarity to China); (2) Japan — selective adoption of Chinese court culture and writing but rejection of centralized bureaucracy in favor of feudal shogunate (synthesis/divergence); (3) Context — both Korea and Japan adopted Neo-Confucianism partly because of Mongol pressure, which forced East Asian states to reorganize politically. Complexity: the *extent* of change differed — Korea changed more fundamentally, Japan less.

### LEQ 3 — African State Economics and Politics (CCOT)

**Prompt:** Abundant natural resources and the rise of powerful centralized governments affected African states between the 12th and the 15th centuries. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which **change or continuity** over time occurred in the economics and politics during that time period in one African state.

> [!tip] Model Response — CCOT
> **Thesis:** The Mali Empire (c. 1235–1450) demonstrated significant political change from its predecessor the Ghana Kingdom — shifting from a tribute-based system to a more centrally administered state — while maintaining a fundamental economic continuity in its dependence on trans-Saharan gold and salt trade as the basis of state wealth.
>
> **Body paragraph structure:** (1) Continuity — trans-Saharan trade in gold and salt as the ongoing foundation of West African state power, from Ghana through Mali; (2) Change — Mali's greater centralization, use of Islam as a unifying political ideology (contrast with Ghana's more syncretic religious approach), and expansion of territory and administrative reach under Mansa Musa; (3) Evidence — Mansa Musa's 1324 pilgrimage as a demonstration of both wealth (continuity) and Islamic political identity (change); Ibn Battuta's account of Mali justice and order.

### LEQ 4 — American State Economics and Politics (CCOT)

**Prompt:** Abundant natural resources and the rise of powerful centralized governments affected American states between the 12th and the 15th centuries. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which **change or continuity** over time occurred in the economics and politics during that time period in one American state.

> [!tip] Model Response — CCOT
> **Thesis:** The Inca Empire in South America (c. 1438–1450) represented a dramatic political change from earlier Andean chiefdoms — building the most centralized and administratively integrated state in pre-Columbian America — while maintaining a continuity of agricultural and ecological adaptation to high-altitude Andean environments that had characterized Andean societies for centuries.
>
> **Body paragraph structure:** (1) Change — the Inca's unprecedented political centralization via the mit'a system, road network, and quipu record-keeping; the shift from small competing chiefdoms to a single empire spanning thousands of miles; (2) Continuity — dependence on Andean agricultural techniques (terracing, potato and quinoa cultivation), llama pastoralism, and the quipu tradition of record-keeping inherited from earlier Andean cultures; (3) Complexity — the mit'a system itself combined change (imperial scale) with continuity (community labor obligations had existed in smaller societies for generations).

## Document-Based Question (AMSCO pp. 72–74)

**Prompt:** Develop an argument evaluating the extent to which the kingdoms and empires of the Sub-Saharan region **succeeded in making achievements** between c. 1200 and c. 1450.

### Documents

**Document 1** — Egyptian official on Mansa Musa's visit to Cairo, 14th century
> *"This man, Mansa Musa, spread upon Cairo the flood of his generosity: there was no person, officer of the court, or holder of any office of the Sultanate who did not receive a sum of gold from him."*

**Document 2** — Leo Africanus, traveler and writer born in Granada, 16th century description of Timbuktu
> *"Here are many doctors, judges, priests, and other learned men that are well maintained at the king's costs. Various manuscripts and written books are brought here . . . and sold for more money than other merchandise."*

**Document 3** — Ibn Battuta, *Travels to the Kingdom of Mali*, mid-14th century
> *"Among their good qualities is the small amount of injustice amongst them, for of all people they are the furthest from it. Their sultan does not forgive anyone in any matter to do with justice. Among these qualities, there is also the prevalence of peace in their country, the traveler is not afraid in it nor is he who lives there in fear of the thief or of the robber by violence."*

**Document 4** — Hausa artisan, description of bronze sculpture process in Benin, 15th century
> *"In the name of Allah the Compassionate, the Merciful. This account will show how the [Benin] figures are made. This work is one to cause wonder. Now this kind of work is done with clay, and wax, and red metal, and solder and lead, and fire. . . . Next it is set aside to cool, then [the outside cover of clay] is broken off. Then you see beautiful figure."*

**Document 5** — Ubaydallah al-Bakri, scholar from Córdoba, Spain, based on traveler interviews, 1068
> *"The city of Ghana consists of two towns situated on a plain. One of these towns, which is inhabited by Muslims, is large and possesses twelve mosques. . . . In the environs are wells with sweet water, from which they drink and with which they grow vegetables. The king's town is six miles distant from this one and bears the name of Al-Ghaba. Between these two towns there are continuous habitations. The houses of the inhabitants are of stone and acacia wood."*

**Document 6** — Bronze head for a staff in the shape of a coiled snake, Igbo-Ukwu people in Nigeria (9th century)

**Document 7** — Benin bronze head, West Africa, 16th century

---

### DBQ Analysis Guide

> [!tip] Model Thesis
> Sub-Saharan African kingdoms and empires achieved significant accomplishments in governance, economic prosperity, and artistic production between c. 1200 and c. 1450, as evidenced by the Mali Empire's Islamic intellectual culture, just legal system, and gold wealth — though the extent of achievement varied across regions and was more pronounced in Mali and Benin than across Sub-Saharan Africa as a whole.

> [!tip] Sourcing — Document 1 (Egyptian official on Mansa Musa)
> **Point of View:** As an Egyptian official observing Mansa Musa's visit, this source reflects the perspective of an outsider from a more established Islamic court. The author may be inclined to emphasize Mali's wealth because it affected Egyptian markets (Mali's gold distribution caused inflation in Cairo). This makes the account credible on the question of Mali's wealth but potentially shaped by the economic disruption the visit caused.

> [!tip] Sourcing — Document 3 (Ibn Battuta on Mali)
> **Point of View:** Ibn Battuta was a Muslim traveler who valued Islamic law and governance. His emphasis on Mali's justice and peace reflects his perspective as a Muslim intellectual who evaluated societies partly by their adherence to Islamic ideals. His positive account is credible but may overstate the degree to which Mali met Islamic governance standards because he was writing for an Islamic audience that valued such qualities.

> [!tip] Sourcing — Document 5 (Al-Bakri on Ghana, 1068)
> **Historical Situation:** Al-Bakri wrote in 1068 — before the Mali Empire existed — describing the earlier Ghana Kingdom. He never visited Africa, relying on secondhand accounts. This limits his account's precision but also means it represents an outside Muslim scholarly perspective on the region. It shows that West African urban and commercial life was sophisticated enough to attract and sustain detailed descriptions by Islamic geographers.

> [!tip] Outside Evidence to Add
> To strengthen the DBQ argument, add: (1) the trans-Saharan trade network in gold and salt that funded Mali's state; (2) the University of Sankore in Timbuktu as an Islamic intellectual center; (3) Sundiata's military founding of Mali as evidence of political achievement; (4) Mansa Musa's 1324 pilgrimage as combining religious identity with political demonstration of wealth.

> [!exam-tip]
> For full DBQ credit: use at least 6 of 7 documents, explain sourcing (POV, purpose, historical situation, or audience) for at least 3 documents, include outside evidence beyond the documents, and ensure your thesis establishes a clear line of reasoning — not just "Sub-Saharan Africa made achievements."
