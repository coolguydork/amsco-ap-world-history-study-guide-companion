# Topic 5.7 — Practice & Review

---

## Key Facts to Know Cold

- **Corporation**: chartered legal entity; owned by stockholders; **limited liability** (key innovation)
- **Monopoly**: one corporation controls an industry → Alfred Krupp (German steel), Rockefeller (U.S. oil)
- **Transnational companies**: De Beers (Cecil Rhodes, diamonds), HSBC (British banking in Hong Kong, 1865), Unilever (British-Dutch, soap)
- Cecil Rhodes proposed Cape Town–Cairo railroad — **never completed**
- Unilever sourced palm oil from **British West Africa then Belgian Congo**
- Lloyd's of London: **marine insurance**, started in a coffee house
- Safety bicycle replaced penny-farthing in the **1880s** (chain drive)
- **Adam Smith's** *Wealth of Nations* (1776): capitalism's foundational text; laissez-faire replaced mercantilism

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The development of the corporation as a business structure in the 19th century most directly contributed to industrialization by:

A) giving governments greater control over the means of production
B) allowing large amounts of capital to be pooled from many investors with limited personal risk
C) eliminating competition between businesses through the creation of monopolies
D) replacing the putting-out system with centralized factory production

*Answer: B — Limited liability meant investors could back large projects (railroads, steel mills) without risking personal bankruptcy, channeling vast capital into industry.*

---

**2.** The operations of companies like De Beers, HSBC, and Unilever in the late 19th century best illustrate which of the following?

A) The spread of socialist economic principles across industrial nations
B) The decline of European imperial power in Asia and Africa
C) The connection between industrial capitalism and colonial resource extraction
D) The success of laissez-faire policies in raising living standards globally

*Answer: C — These transnational companies extracted resources (diamonds, palm oil) from colonized regions, paid local workers minimal wages, and repatriated profits to Europe — a classic pattern of economic imperialism.*

## Short Answer Question (SAQ)

**Prompt:** Explain ONE way that the development of corporations changed economic systems in the period 1750–1900.

**Strong response example:** The corporation's introduction of **limited liability** fundamentally changed how capital was invested in the 19th century. Before limited liability, an investor in a failing business could lose not only their investment but their personal assets. Under the corporate model, losses were capped at the amount invested. This dramatically lowered the risk of investing, encouraging far more people to put money into industrial ventures. The result was an unprecedented flow of capital into railroads, steel mills, and transnational companies — enabling the industrial scale of production that defined the late 19th century.
