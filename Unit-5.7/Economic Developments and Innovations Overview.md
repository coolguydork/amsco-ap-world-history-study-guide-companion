# Topic 5.7 — Economic Developments and Innovations

## Essential Question

> How did the development of new economic systems, ideologies, and institutions contribute to change during the period 1750–1900?

The [[Unit-5.3/Industrial Revolution Begins Overview|Industrial Revolution]] didn't just change *how* goods were made — it transformed the entire global economy. Topic 5.7 examines the new **economic ideas, business structures, and consumer culture** that industrialization produced. (AMSCO pp. 325–328)

---

## The Big Picture

Before industrialization, most economic life was local or regional. Trade existed, but the dominant economic philosophy was **mercantilism** — nations competed to accumulate wealth, often through colonial extraction. The [[Unit-5.3/Industrial Revolution Begins Overview|Industrial Revolution]] blew this model apart.

Three major shifts define Topic 5.7:

### 1. New Economic Ideologies
- [[Adam Smith]]'s [[capitalism]] and [[laissez-faire]] philosophy replaced mercantilism as the dominant economic framework
- Thinkers like [[Unit-5.8/Karl Marx & Socialism|Karl Marx]] challenged capitalism with [[socialism]]
- These competing ideologies had profound effects on how governments regulated (or didn't regulate) the economy

### 2. New Business Structures
- The **[[corporation]]** emerged as the dominant form of business organization
- **[[monopoly|Monopolies]]** arose as corporations competed and consolidated
- **[[transnational companies]]** began operating across national borders, knitting the global economy together

### 3. New Consumer Culture
- A **culture of consumerism** developed among working and middle classes
- New leisure activities, sports, music halls, and advertising shaped daily life
- Mass culture emerged as a product of industrialization

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam often tests 5.7 through document-based questions featuring economic data, advertisements, or descriptions of business practices. Know the key terms ([[corporation]], [[monopoly]], [[transnational companies]]) and be ready to explain their effects on global trade and society.

---

## Why This Matters

The economic innovations of this period created the **modern global economy**. Corporations like De Beers, HSBC, and Unilever were among the first truly global businesses. The [[laissez-faire]] ideology shaped government policy for generations. The consumer culture that emerged in the late 1800s is still with us today.

Understanding 5.7 helps explain:
- How the world economy became globally integrated
- Why some nations benefited from industrialization and others were exploited
- How economic inequality within industrial societies generated political responses ([[Unit-5.8/Karl Marx & Socialism|Karl Marx]], [[socialism]], labor movements)
