# Connections

**Unit 5.7 — Economic Developments and Innovations | AMSCO pp. 325–331**

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-5.3/Industrial Revolution Begins Overview\|Industrial Revolution Begins Overview]]**
The factory system, wage labor, and early capital accumulation created by the first Industrial Revolution were the preconditions for corporate capitalism. Without large-scale production, there would be no need for joint-stock corporations, no scale to exploit, and no profits large enough to form monopolies.

**[[Unit-5.4/Industrialization Spreads Overview\|Industrialization Spreads Overview]]**
As industrialization spread beyond Britain to continental Europe and the United States, capital needs grew beyond what any single entrepreneur could supply. This global spread created demand for transnational finance (HSBC) and cross-border corporate operations (Unilever).

**[[Unit-5.5/Technology in the Industrial Age Overview\|Technology in the Industrial Age Overview]]**
Specific technologies enabled the economic structures in 5.7. The Bessemer process let Krupp and Carnegie build monopolies. Railroad networks let transnational companies like De Beers operate across vast colonial territories. Technology was the mechanism; corporate capitalism was the result.

## Leads To

**Reactions to the Industrial Economy (Topic 5.8)** ← not yet built
The monopolies, wage inequality, and concentration of power described in Topic 5.7 directly provoked the responses in 5.8: labor unions, socialism, Marxism, and the political demands of the working class. You cannot understand 5.8 without 5.7 — they are cause and effect.

**Global Economic Development (Topic 6.4)** ← not yet built
The global banking system, transnational corporations, and free-trade ideology of the 19th century laid the infrastructure for 20th-century economic globalization.

**Economic Imperialism (Topic 6.5)** ← not yet built
De Beers, Unilever, HSBC — all the transnational companies in Topic 5.7 were instruments of economic imperialism. Topic 6.5 examines this relationship systematically: how industrial capitalism drove the conquest and economic subordination of non-European regions.

> [!exam-tip]
> **Compare and contrast question:** Joint-stock companies ([[Unit-4.2/Exploration: Causes & Events Overview\|Topic 4.2]]) vs. corporations (this topic). Both spread risk across multiple investors, both enabled large-scale trade/production, and both were tools of colonial extraction. Key difference: corporations featured *limited liability* and were not typically state-chartered monopolies — they operated in a competitive (if often monopolistic) market, not under royal charter.

## Thematic Connections

**ECN → SIO:** Corporate capitalism created mass consumerism, which reshaped class identity. The middle class defined itself partly through *what it bought* — a new form of social organization tied to economic participation rather than birth or guild membership.

**ECN → GOV:** Monopolies gained such economic power that they could influence political decisions (where to build factories, which politicians to support). This blurring of corporate and political power is a recurring AP theme from this period forward.
