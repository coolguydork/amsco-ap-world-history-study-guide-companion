# Timeline — Unit 5.7: Economic Developments and Innovations

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** The 19th century witnessed capitalism's transformation from a commercial to an industrial and then corporate form. New financial institutions (investment banks, corporations), trade policies (free trade, gold standard), and global commodity markets integrated the world economy at an unprecedented scale — creating both unprecedented prosperity for industrial nations and unprecedented dependence for commodity producers.

> [!tip] Continuity vs. Change
> **Continuity:** Long-distance trade for profit — the basis of commercial capitalism — had existed for millennia. The mechanisms (credit, insurance, partnership) were elaborated, not invented, in the 19th century.
> **Change:** The *scale* and *integration* are genuinely unprecedented — commodity markets linking five continents, capital flowing across the globe in minutes via telegraph, corporations larger than most national economies.
