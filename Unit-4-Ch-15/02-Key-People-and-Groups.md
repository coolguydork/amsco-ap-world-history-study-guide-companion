# Key People and Groups — Chapter 15 (4.2): Exploration

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

### Christopher Columbus (1451–1506)
- **Region:** Genoa / Spanish Crown / Caribbean
- **Period:** Late 15th–early 16th century
- Sailed west under Spanish sponsorship in 1492, making landfall in the Caribbean (San Salvador) and initiating sustained contact between the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.
- His four voyages established Spain's claim to the Americas and triggered the [[Columbian Exchange]].
- Died believing he had reached Asia; his misunderstanding of Earth's size was a famous navigational error.

---

### Vasco da Gama (c. 1460–1524)
- **Region:** Portugal / Indian Ocean
- **Period:** Late 15th–early 16th century
- Led the first European maritime expedition to India (1497–1499), establishing the Cape Route from Lisbon to Calicut.
- His success gave Portugal direct access to the [[Spice trade]], disrupting Ottoman and Venetian commercial dominance.
- His methods were often brutal — he bombarded Calicut when its ruler resisted Portuguese trading terms, establishing a precedent of coercive commerce.

---

### Ferdinand Magellan (1480–1521)
- **Region:** Portugal / Spanish Crown / Pacific
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Led the first circumnavigation of the globe (1519–1522) under Spanish sponsorship; killed in the Philippines; his fleet's completion of the voyage proved Earth's spherical shape.
- His expedition revealed the enormous scale of the Pacific Ocean and opened a second route to Asia for Spain.

---

### Hernán Cortés (1485–1547)
- **Region:** Spanish Mesoamerica
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Conquered the Aztec Empire (1519–1521) with 600 soldiers, Indigenous allies (Tlaxcalans), and smallpox.
- His success established New Spain and the encomienda model of colonial exploitation.
- His letters (*Cartas de Relación*) to the Spanish Crown are key primary sources for AP DBQ exercises.

---

### Francisco Pizarro (c. 1471–1541)
- **Region:** Spanish South America
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Conquered the Inca Empire (1532) by capturing emperor Atahualpa during a civil war, then executing him after collecting a vast ransom.
- Opened the Andes to Spanish mining exploitation, particularly silver at [[Potosí]].

---

### Queen Isabella I of Castile (1451–1504)
- **Region:** Spain
- **Period:** Late 15th century
- Sponsored Columbus's voyages and — with Ferdinand II — completed the Reconquista in 1492.
- Her reign linked religious mission (forced conversion of Jews and Muslims, missionary activity in Americas) with overseas expansion.
- Issued early protections for Indigenous peoples (though rarely enforced).

---

### Francis Drake (c. 1540–1596)
- **Region:** England / Atlantic / Pacific
- **Period:** Late 16th century
- English privateer who circumnavigated the globe (1577–1580) while raiding Spanish ports and treasure ships.
- Embodied England's challenge to Spanish Atlantic dominance and the use of privateering as state policy.

---

### The Taino People
- **Region:** Caribbean
- **Period:** Pre-contact through early 16th century
- The first Indigenous people encountered by Columbus; suffered near-total destruction within 50 years of contact due to disease, enslavement, and violence.
- Their fate became the earliest and starkest example of [[Demographic collapse]] in the Americas.

> [!exam-tip]
> Cortés's *Cartas de Relación* is a high-frequency AP document. Be ready to analyze it as a **biased primary source** — Cortés was writing to justify his unauthorized expedition to the Spanish Crown, so he consistently emphasizes his own heroism and the wealth available for Spain.
