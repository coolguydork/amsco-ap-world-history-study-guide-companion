# Chapter 15 (Topic 4.2): Exploration — Causes and Events

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

## AP Exam Period
**Period 4: 1450–1750**

## Summary

European maritime exploration from 1450 to 1750 was driven by a combination of economic, religious, and political motives. The desire to find direct sea routes to Asian spice markets — bypassing Ottoman-controlled overland routes — pushed Portugal down the African coast and ultimately to India. Spain, seeking a competing route, funded Columbus's westward voyages, inadvertently encountering the Americas in 1492. Over the following century, Spain and Portugal were joined by the Dutch, English, and French in a fierce competition to explore, claim, and exploit territories across the Atlantic, Indian, and Pacific Oceans. The resulting voyages established the first sustained links between all inhabited continents, transforming global trade networks, demographic patterns, and political power. Exploration was never purely geographic: it was inseparable from conquest, commerce, and missionary Christianity from its earliest stages.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Analyze the economic, religious, and political motivations behind European maritime exploration.
- Identify the major explorers and their routes, sponsors, and outcomes.
- Explain how the Treaty of Tordesillas and other diplomatic agreements shaped early European competition.
- Evaluate the consequences of first contact between Europeans and the peoples of the Americas and Africa.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.1.II** — European technological developments in cartography and navigation built on previous knowledge developed in the classical, Islamic, and Asian worlds.
- **KC-4.2.I** — The Columbian Exchange and global circulation of goods reshaped societies.

> [!exam-tip]
> AP prompts on exploration almost always ask you to evaluate **motivations** (economic, religious, political) — not just to list who went where. Practice explaining *why* each state explored and *who benefited* from the results.
