# Timeline & Chronology

*Source: AMSCO pp. 332–342 | AP Period 3: c. 1750–1900*

---

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1808 | Mahmud II becomes Ottoman sultan | Begins Ottoman modernization — road-building, postal service, European-style ministries |
| 1826 | Mahmud II abolishes the Janissaries | Eliminates key obstacle to centralized Ottoman reform; replaced with European-trained artillery |
| c. 1811–1816 | Luddite movement in Britain | Early working-class resistance; artisans destroyed factory machines to protest displacement |
| 1833 | Sadler Report released (Britain) | Parliamentary documentation of factory conditions; catalyzed public support for labor reform |
| 1839 | Tanzimat reforms begin | Ottoman reorganization: secularized education, codified law, reduced corruption |
| 1844 | Engels, *Condition of the Working Class in England* | Empirical documentation of industrial poverty; foundation for Marxist theory |
| 1848 | Marx and Engels publish the *Communist Manifesto* | Intellectual framework for workers' revolution; influenced socialist movements globally |
| c. 1850s | Labor unions gain ground in Britain | Gradual legal recognition; won wages, hours, safety conditions |
| 1850 | Ottoman commercial code | Tanzimat reform making the empire more accessible to foreign business |
| 1856 | Hatt-i Hümayun (Ottoman Reform Edict) | Declared legal equality for all men regardless of religion; regulated millets |
| 1858 | Ottoman penal code | Part of Tanzimat legal modernization |
| c. 1861–1895 | China's Self-Strengthening Movement | Selective military/industrial modernization while preserving Confucian tradition; ultimately insufficient |
| 1867 | Marx, *Das Kapital* Vol. 1 | Full theoretical elaboration of scientific socialism and critique of capitalism |
| 1868 | Meiji Restoration, Japan | Rapid Westernization begins: dismantled feudal domains, abolished samurai class, built industry |
| 1871 | Japan dissolves samurai class | Final lump-sum payment; samurai no longer fighting men; bushido becomes cultural rather than legal |
| 1876 | Sultan Abdulhamid takes power | Initially supported reform; became authoritarian; drove Young Turks into exile |
| 1881 | Mandatory education, Britain (ages 5–10) | Redefined childhood away from labor; long-term social transformation |
| c. 1870s–1880s | British Parliament expands male suffrage | Reform bills reduced property qualifications; universal male suffrage early 1900s |
| c. 1880s | AFL founded (Samuel Gompers) | American labor union movement; "bread and butter" focus on wages and conditions |
| 1894–1895 | Sino-Japanese War | China's defeat exposed failure of Self-Strengthening Movement; intensified reform pressure |
| 1894–1896 | Hamidian massacres (Ottoman Empire) | Sultan Abdulhamid's persecution of Armenians; 100,000–250,000 killed |
| 1898 | Hundred Days of Reform (China) | Emperor Guangxu's sweeping reforms; reversed by Empress Cixi's coup after 103 days |
| 1900 | Boxer Rebellion (China) | Anti-foreign uprising; paradoxically accelerated later reform under Cixi |
| 1901 | Qing Reform Edict | Post-Boxer attempt at modernization; acknowledged need for civil service reform |

---

> [!tip]
> The AP exam often asks about **sequencing** — notice that the Hundred Days of Reform (1898) came *after* the Sino-Japanese War defeat (1895). China reformed in response to humiliation, not proactively. Compare: Japan reformed proactively in 1868 *before* suffering military defeat.

> [!exam-tip]
> You don't need exact dates for most events on the AP exam — but you do need to know the *order* and *causation*: what triggered what, and roughly when. Know that Tanzimat preceded the Hundred Days; that the *Communist Manifesto* (1848) came before major union legalization; and that Meiji Japan (1868) modernized much earlier and more thoroughly than China.
