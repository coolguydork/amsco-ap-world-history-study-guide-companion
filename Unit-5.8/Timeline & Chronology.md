# Topic 5.8 — Timeline & Chronology

> [!tip]
> Use this timeline to see **causation in sequence** — what triggered what? Notice that reform efforts in the Ottoman Empire, China, and Japan overlap in time but respond to different pressures.

---

## Chronological Table

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1776 | Adam Smith publishes *Wealth of Nations* | Foundational text for laissez-faire capitalism; the target of later critics |
| 1799–1815 | Napoleonic Wars | Created economic disruptions (high wheat prices then decline) that affected Ottoman economy |
| 1806 | John Stuart Mill born | Britain's leading utilitarian reformer |
| 1808 | Mahmud II begins rule (to 1839) | First wave of Ottoman modernization |
| 1818 | Karl Marx born | Future author of the Communist Manifesto |
| 1826 | Mahmud II abolishes the Janissaries | Massacred those who revolted; cleared the way for military and administrative reform |
| 1831 | Abolition of Ottoman feudal system | Final defeat of Janissary-based power; tax revenue centralized |
| 1832 | British Reform Bill | Expanded male suffrage; reduced property requirements for voting |
| 1833 | Sadler Report published | Documented child labor and dangerous factory conditions; led to legislative reform |
| 1839 | Tanzimat reforms begin | "Reorganization" — Ottoman secular education, law codes, anti-corruption |
| 1839 | First Reform Edict (Hatt-i Şerif) | Declared reforms; did not mention women |
| 1843 | British law bans child mine labor under age 10 | Direct result of labor reform movement |
| 1845 | Engels publishes *Condition of the Working Class in England* | Empirical foundation for Marxist critique |
| 1848 | *Communist Manifesto* published | Marx & Engels launch "scientific socialism" |
| 1850 | Ottoman commercial code | Made Ottoman Empire more open to foreign business |
| 1853 | Commodore Perry sails into Tokyo Bay | Forces Japan to open trade; triggers the reform crisis |
| 1854 | Japan opens to trade under U.S. pressure | Shogun concedes; reform faction gains momentum |
| 1856 | Hatt-i Humayun issued | Ottoman Reform Edict declaring legal equality regardless of religion/ethnicity |
| 1858 | Ottoman penal code | Continued legal modernization |
| 1867 | British Reform Bill | Further expanded male suffrage |
| 1868 | Meiji Restoration | Emperor restored; shogun overthrown; Japan begins systematic modernization |
| 1868 | Charter Oath abolishes Japanese feudalism | Formal end of the feudal system |
| 1871 | Samurai class dissolved | Final lump-sum payment; swords banned; bushido demoted |
| 1876 | Tanzimat period ends; Abdulhamid II takes power | Initially supported constitution; later became authoritarian |
| 1880s | Safety bicycle replaces penny-farthing | Symbol of rising consumer/leisure culture in industrial Britain |
| 1881 | Mandatory education begins in Britain (ages 5–10) | Redefines childhood; children as students, not workers |
| 1883 | Karl Marx dies | His ideas continue to spread and evolve |
| 1884 | British Reform Bill | Extended male suffrage further |
| 1894–1896 | Hamidian Massacres | Abdulhamid's government killed 100,000–250,000 Armenians; he nicknamed "Red Sultan" |
| 1894–1895 | Sino-Japanese War | China's humiliating defeat shocks reformers into action |
| 1898 | Hundred Days of Reform | Emperor Guangxu issues sweeping reforms; Cixi stages coup and repeals them |
| 1900 | Boxer Rebellion | Anti-foreign uprising in China; foreign powers intervene |
| 1905 | Russo-Japanese War | Japan defeats Russia — a non-Western power defeats a Western one for the first time in the modern era |
| 1906 | Toyoda Loom Works founded | Early example of Japanese private industrial innovation; becomes Toyota |
| 1909 | Young Turks depose Abdulhamid | Constitutional government restored in Ottoman Empire |
| 1911 | Chinese Revolution | Qing dynasty collapses; republic declared |
| 1918 | Full male suffrage in Britain | Culmination of labor movement's political demands |
| 1928 | Women gain equal suffrage in Britain | 90 years after the Sadler Report sparked reform momentum |

## Causation Map: How Events Connect

```
Industrial conditions (low wages, child labor, dangerous factories)
        ↓
Sadler Report (1833) → Public awareness
        ↓
Labor unions + Mill's utilitarianism → Reform bills (1832, 1867, 1884)
        ↓
Full male suffrage 1918 → Women's suffrage 1928

Industrial conditions
        ↓
Marx & Engels → Communist Manifesto (1848)
        ↓
Scientific socialism → 20th century revolutions (Russia 1917, China 1949)

Perry crisis (1853)
        ↓
Meiji Restoration (1868) → Rapid modernization
        ↓
Sino-Japanese War victory (1894–95) → Russo-Japanese War victory (1905)
        ↓
Imperial expansion → WWII

Opium Wars + spheres of influence
        ↓
Self-Strengthening Movement (1860s–1890s)
        ↓
Sino-Japanese War defeat (1895) → Hundred Days of Reform (1898)
        ↓
Cixi's coup → Reform fails → Boxer Rebellion (1900) → Revolution (1911)
```

## Key Dates to Memorize

The AP exam rewards knowing *relative* chronology more than exact years. Here are the anchor dates:

- **1833** — Sadler Report (British labor awareness)
- **1839** — Tanzimat reforms begin (Ottoman)
- **1848** — Communist Manifesto (Marxism)
- **1856** — Hatt-i Humayun (Ottoman equality edict)
- **1868** — Meiji Restoration (Japan)
- **1876** — Abdulhamid II takes power (Ottoman conservative turn)
- **1894–95** — Sino-Japanese War (China's crisis, Japan's triumph)
- **1898** — Hundred Days of Reform / Cixi's coup (China)
