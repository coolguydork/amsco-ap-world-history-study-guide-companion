## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Questions 1–3 refer to the following passage:*

> "We have now received Her Majesty's decree to devote ourselves fully to China's revitalization, to suppress vigorously the use of the terms new and old, and to blend together the best of what is Chinese and what is foreign. The root of China's weakness lies in harmful habits too firmly entrenched, in rules and regulations too minutely drawn, in the overabundance of inept and mediocre officials and in the paucity of truly outstanding ones, in petty bureaucrats who hide behind the written word and in clerks and yamen runners [administrative clerks] who use the written word as talismans [an object that brings good luck] to acquire personal fortunes, in the mountains of correspondence between government offices that have no relationship to reality, and in the seniority system and associated practices that block the way of men of real talent."
>
> — Qing Reform Edict, January 29, 1901

**Question 1.** Which segment of Chinese society seems to be the focus of this excerpt from the Reform Edict?

- A) The military
- B) The emperor
- C) Civil servants
- D) Peasants

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The edict explicitly targets "inept and mediocre officials," "petty bureaucrats," "yamen runners," and "the seniority system" — all parts of the civil service system. This reflects Empress Cixi's late-reign acknowledgment that the scholar-gentry bureaucracy had become corrupt and dysfunctional. The military (A), emperor (B), and peasants (D) are not mentioned.

---

**Question 2.** Which of the following was most similar to the ideas in the source?

- A) The concept of concessions
- B) The principle of extraterritoriality
- C) The Open Door Policy
- D) The Self-Strengthening Movement

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Both the Self-Strengthening Movement and this edict sought to modernize China by selectively adopting Western methods while preserving Chinese tradition — "blend together the best of what is Chinese and what is foreign." Concessions (A) and extraterritoriality (B) were Western impositions on China, not Chinese initiatives. The Open Door Policy (C) was a U.S. foreign policy toward China, not a Chinese reform effort.

---

**Question 3.** How did the Boxer Rebellion lead to the Reform Edict?

- A) The failure of the Boxer Rebellion persuaded many members of the Chinese government to support more extensive reforms.
- B) The Boxers were the "inept and mediocre officials" that the edict was trying to remove.
- C) The success of the Boxer Rebellion against the Qing opened the way for more significant reforms to be enacted.
- D) The Boxers shifted their efforts from trying to drive foreigners out of China to serving as efficient government officials.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The Boxer Rebellion (1900) was a failed anti-foreign uprising. Its failure — and the subsequent Western military intervention — demonstrated once more that China's government and military were inadequate. This humiliation forced even conservative members of the Qing government to accept that reform was necessary, producing the 1901 Reform Edict. The Boxers were not government officials (B, D), and the Rebellion did not succeed (C).

## Short-Answer Questions

*Question 1 refers to a poster from the American Federation of Labor (Library of Congress). The poster depicted organized labor and union membership.*

**Part A.** Identify ONE way in which the poster reflects the intellectual ideas of the period 1750–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Part A
> The AFL poster reflects the intellectual idea that workers have collective rights and power — a concept central to both utilitarianism (which argued reform should benefit the greatest number) and Marxism (which called on the proletariat to recognize their shared class interest). The poster's emphasis on worker solidarity mirrors the broader intellectual critique of unregulated laissez-faire capitalism that characterized this period.

**Part B.** Explain ONE way in which the development of industrialization contributed to the development of trade unions in the period 1750–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Part B
> Industrialization concentrated large numbers of workers in factories and mines under dangerous, low-wage conditions. This concentration created the conditions for collective action — workers could recognize their shared interests and organize together in ways that had been impossible when most labor was dispersed across farms and small workshops. The Sadler Report (1833) documented these conditions and helped galvanize public and parliamentary support for workers' right to organize. Without the factory system's mass concentration of workers, labor unions as an institution would have had no organizational base.

**Part C.** Explain ONE historical situation in the period 1750–1900, other than the one illustrated in the poster, in which groups of people influenced changes in industrial societies.

> [!tip] Model Response — Part C
> In the Ottoman Empire, a group of reform-minded officials and intellectuals known as the "Young Turks" pushed for constitutional government and modernization in the late 19th century. Their advocacy contributed to the Tanzimat reform period (1839–1876), which included legal equality for non-Muslims, secular education, and a new commercial code. Though Sultan Abdulhamid II eventually drove the Young Turks into exile, their ideas outlasted his reign — they returned to power in 1908 and deposed him. This illustrates how organized intellectual and political groups could influence change in industrial-era societies even when facing government repression.

## Key Comparisons for LEQ/DBQ

**Reform vs. Revolution:** Mill (utilitarianism, reform within capitalism) vs. Marx (scientific socialism, replacement of capitalism). Both responded to the same conditions; their prescriptions were opposite.

**Three Non-Western Reform Responses:**
- **Ottoman Empire:** Top-down legal/educational reforms (Tanzimat); partial success; conservative backlash under Abdulhamid
- **China:** Selective modernization (Self-Strengthening); reversed by conservatives (Cixi); inadequate
- **Japan:** Rapid, comprehensive Westernization (Meiji); most successful; still provoked samurai resistance

**Bottom-up vs. Top-down reform:** Labor unions (workers organizing) vs. Tanzimat/Meiji (governments directing change from above). Both were responses to industrial capitalism; their mechanisms and social bases were completely different.
