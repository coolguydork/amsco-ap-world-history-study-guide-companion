# Topic 5.8 — Practice & Review

> [!exam-tip]
> Topic 5.8 appears in all four question types on the AP exam: MCQ (usually with a stimulus), SAQ (comparison and causation prompts), and as evidence in DBQ/LEQ responses. The comparison between Ottoman, Chinese, and Japanese reform responses is especially high-yield for essays.

---

## Multiple Choice Practice

### Question 1

*"For a second's sunlight, men must fight like tigers. For the privilege of seeing the color of their children's eyes by the light of the sun, fathers must fight like beasts in the jungle."*

*— Mary Harris "Mother" Jones, labor organizer, c. 1900*

Which of the following best explains the conditions described in the passage?

A) The expansion of feudal agricultural labor systems in the 19th century
B) The dangerous and poorly compensated conditions of industrial factory and mine work
C) The military conscription practices of major European states
D) The use of enslaved labor in colonial plantation economies

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The passage describes workers who rarely see sunlight — underground miners. "Mother" Jones was a labor organizer who fought for coal miners. Options A and D misidentify the system; C is not supported. The passage is classic evidence of the industrial labor conditions that drove the union movement.

### Question 2

Karl Marx argued that capitalism inevitably exploited workers because:

A) Capitalist owners were morally corrupt individuals who chose to pay low wages
B) Workers lacked the education to demand better conditions
C) Market competition forced bourgeois owners to minimize wages to maximize profits
D) Religious institutions supported the capitalist system against workers

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> This is the key nuance in Marx. He did NOT argue capitalists were villains by character — he argued the *system* forced them to exploit workers. Market competition made minimizing labor costs a survival requirement. Option A misrepresents Marx's structural argument.

### Question 3

The Tanzimat reforms in the Ottoman Empire (1839–1876) were most similar to which of the following?

A) The Hundred Days of Reform in China, because both were quickly reversed by conservative forces
B) The Meiji Restoration in Japan, because both involved systematic Westernization of all government institutions
C) The British reform bills, because all three expanded voting rights for women
D) The Self-Strengthening Movement in China, because both modernized some institutions while avoiding fundamental political restructuring

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Both Tanzimat and the Self-Strengthening Movement modernized military, law, and education without replacing the fundamental political structure (sultan/emperor at top). Option A is a trap: the Tanzimat wasn't quickly reversed (it ran 1839–1876); the Hundred Days was. Option B is too strong — the Meiji Restoration abolished feudalism and created a constitution, deeper than the Tanzimat. Option C is wrong — none of these reforms gave women voting rights.

### Question 4

Which factor best explains why Japan successfully industrialized in the late 19th century while China struggled to do so?

A) Japan had more natural resources than China
B) Japan's central government unified around a reform coalition while China's court was internally divided
C) China faced greater military pressure from Western powers than Japan did
D) Japan's geographic isolation prevented Western powers from interfering with its reforms

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Meiji oligarchs achieved political consensus around reform; in China, Cixi blocked Guangxu's reform efforts. Option A is incorrect (China has more resources). Option C is the opposite of reality. Option D overstates isolation — Perry forced Japan open.

### Question 5

The formation of labor unions in 19th-century Britain is best understood as an example of which AP historical thinking skill?

A) Continuity — workers continued practices from the guild system of the medieval era
B) Change — new collective institutions emerged to challenge industrial capitalism's power structure
C) Causation — unions were the primary cause of the Industrial Revolution
D) Comparison — unions in Britain were identical to Ottoman Tanzimat reforms

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Labor unions were a genuinely new form of organization — distinct from medieval guilds (which regulated craft quality, not labor rights). They represent a major change in how working people organized politically and economically. Option A is tempting but overstated — unions were legally new, not continuous from guilds.

## Short-Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1 — Causation (Internal Reactions)

*Use the following quotation to answer parts a, b, and c.*

> "The proletariat will use its political supremacy to wrest, by degrees, all capital from the bourgeoisie..." — Karl Marx, *Communist Manifesto*, 1848

**a)** Briefly describe ONE specific historical development that contributed to the conditions Marx described in the passage.

**b)** Briefly explain ONE way Marx's critique of capitalism differed from John Stuart Mill's.

**c)** Briefly explain ONE limitation of Marxist theory as a response to 19th-century capitalism.

> [!tip] Model Responses
>
> **a)** The Sadler Report (1833) documented dangerous working conditions, long hours, and child labor in British mines and factories — the very conditions that led Marx to argue capitalism produced "needless misery" alongside wealth.
>
> **b)** Mill advocated reforming capitalism (legalizing unions, limiting child labor) while keeping the market system intact. Marx argued capitalism was structurally incapable of reform and must be replaced — first by socialism, then communism.
>
> **c)** Marx predicted capitalism would collapse under its own contradictions and the proletariat would revolt globally. In reality, reforms (unions, voting rights, labor laws) alleviated conditions enough to prevent revolution in most industrialized societies. The system proved more adaptable than Marx anticipated.

---

### SAQ 2 — Comparison (Non-Western Reform)

**a)** Briefly describe ONE similarity between the Ottoman Empire's Tanzimat reforms and Japan's Meiji reforms.

**b)** Briefly describe ONE difference between China's reform efforts and Japan's reform efforts in the second half of the 19th century.

**c)** Briefly explain ONE reason why Japan's reform efforts were more successful than China's in producing industrialization.

> [!tip] Model Responses
>
> **a)** Both the Tanzimat and the Meiji reforms included modernizing the military along European lines and creating secular educational systems intended to produce trained bureaucrats and specialists. Both were responses to Western imperial pressure.
>
> **b)** Japan's Meiji Restoration abolished feudalism and created a constitutional monarchy — fundamentally restructuring the political system. China's Self-Strengthening Movement and Hundred Days of Reform attempted to modernize military technology and administration but avoided fundamental political restructuring.
>
> **c)** Japan's central government unified around a reform coalition (Meiji oligarchs), allowing consistent, systematic reform across decades. In China, Empress Dowager Cixi's political power blocked Emperor Guangxu's reform efforts, preventing coherent implementation.

## Long Essay Question (LEQ) Guidance

### Sample Prompt

*Evaluate the extent to which industrialization produced significant changes in political movements and ideologies in the period c. 1750–c. 1900.*

**Thesis strategy:** Argue that industrialization caused *significant but uneven* political changes — producing new ideologies (Marxism, utilitarianism), new political actors (labor unions, reform movements), and new reform programs in Western and non-Western states, while the fundamental structure of capitalism and elite political power largely continued.

**Evidence to use:**
- Labor unions → voting reform bills in Britain
- Mill's utilitarianism → factory safety and child labor laws
- Marxism → 20th-century revolutionary movements (forward connection)
- Tanzimat reforms → Ottoman political modernization (limited)
- Meiji Restoration → Japan's constitutional monarchy (more substantial)
- Cixi blocking reform → continuity of conservative power in China

**Complexity point:** Argue that reforms benefited men more than women (gender as a limit to change), or that Japan's success was exceptional while Ottoman/Chinese reform shows the limits of industrialization-driven change outside the West.

---

### Sample Prompt 2

*Compare how TWO different societies responded to the challenges posed by industrialization in the period c. 1750–c. 1900.*

> [!tip]
> Best pairings: **Japan vs. China** (most different outcomes), **Ottoman vs. China** (similar starting points, similar limitations), or **Britain vs. Japan** (internal vs. external pressure). Avoid pairing societies you know less well.

## Common Misconceptions — Watch Out!

> [!warning]
>
> **Misconception 1:** "The Tanzimat gave women equal rights."
> Reality: The 1839 Tanzimat didn't mention women. Mahmud II's legal reforms actually reduced some women's property rights. Don't assume reform = women's equality.
>
> **Misconception 2:** "Marx was a communist leader who ran a country."
> Reality: Marx was a philosopher/writer who died in 1883, decades before any communist state existed. Don't confuse his ideas with 20th-century Soviet or Chinese communism.
>
> **Misconception 3:** "Japan's Meiji Restoration was completely smooth and accepted."
> Reality: The samurai class resisted and was crushed by the 1870s. By the 1920s, conservative military officers again dominated the government. Success came with significant internal conflict and long-term backlash.
>
> **Misconception 4:** "China's Cixi was simply ignorant of the need for reform."
> Reality: Cixi was politically sophisticated. She blocked reform not from ignorance but to protect dynastic power. Toward the end of her reign she did allow some reform (civil service exam abolition). Her conservatism was strategic, not merely reactionary.
>
> **Misconception 5:** "The Hundred Days of Reform succeeded in changing China."
> Reality: It lasted about 100 days before Cixi's coup ended it. The reforms were repealed almost immediately.
