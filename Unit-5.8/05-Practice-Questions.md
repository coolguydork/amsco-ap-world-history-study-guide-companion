## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–3.**

> *"The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles. Freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, guildmaster and journeyman, in a word, oppressor and oppressed, stood in constant opposition to one another… Our epoch, the epoch of the bourgeoisie, possesses, however, this distinctive feature: it has simplified class antagonisms. Society as a whole is more and more splitting up into two great hostile camps, into two great classes directly facing each other — Bourgeoisie and Proletariat."*
> — Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, *The Communist Manifesto*, 1848

---

**1.** The passage most directly reflects which of the following Marxist concepts?

- A) Surplus value — the idea that capitalists appropriate workers' unpaid labor as profit
- B) Historical materialism — the theory that economic conditions and class struggle drive historical change
- C) The inevitability of proletarian revolution — the prediction that capitalism will collapse
- D) Utopian socialism — the vision of an ideal cooperative community replacing capitalism

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The passage's argument that "the history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles" is a direct statement of historical materialism — the idea that economic class conflict, not ideas or great men, is the engine of historical change.*

---

**2.** Marx and Engels wrote that in the industrial age, class antagonisms had been "simplified" into a conflict between bourgeoisie and proletariat. This argument was most directly based on which contemporary development?

- A) The spread of democratic revolutions that had eliminated all but two social classes
- B) The factory system's creation of a clear division between industrial capitalist owners and wage-earning factory workers
- C) The abolition of feudal serfdom across Europe, which reduced social complexity
- D) The declining influence of religion, which had previously divided society along sectarian lines

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The factory system created the specific class structure Marx described — owners of machines and factories (bourgeoisie) facing workers who owned nothing but their labor (proletariat) — simplifying the complex hierarchies of feudal and commercial societies.*

---

**3.** The Communist Manifesto was published in 1848, the year of the Springtime of Nations revolutions across Europe. Which of the following best explains why the revolutions of 1848 failed to produce communist revolutions as Marx had predicted?

- A) Workers in European countries had not yet developed sufficient class consciousness to organize revolutionary action
- B) The proletariat was still a relatively small minority in most European countries, which remained predominantly agricultural
- C) Marx and Engels' Manifesto was not distributed widely enough to influence the revolutionary movements
- D) European governments had adopted Bismarck's social welfare policies before the revolutions, satisfying workers' demands

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*In 1848, most of Europe was still largely agricultural; the industrial proletariat Marx identified as the revolutionary class was concentrated in Britain, Belgium, and parts of France and Germany — a small minority in predominantly peasant societies.*

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 4–5.**

> *"I was struck with the contrast between the silent, attentive, industrious and contented appearance of the workers… and the ignorant, idle, dissolute, and wretched appearance of a large portion of those of the neighbourhood not in the employ of my partners and myself."*
> — Robert Owen, describing his New Lanark factory workers, c. 1820

---

**4.** Owen's observation in the passage most directly supports which of the following arguments?

- A) Industrial workers were naturally happier and more productive than agricultural workers
- B) The character and well-being of workers depended on the conditions of their employment, not on their inherent nature
- C) Factory owners had a moral obligation to recruit workers from the most destitute segments of society
- D) Workers who were employed in factories were inherently more virtuous than those who were not

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Owen's core argument — that good conditions produced good workers — challenged the prevailing view that workers' degradation was their own fault. This was the foundation of his reform philosophy: improve conditions, improve people.*

---

**5.** Owen's approach to addressing industrial conditions differed most significantly from Marx's approach in that Owen:

- A) Advocated for violent revolution to overthrow capitalism immediately
- B) Sought to improve conditions within capitalism through model enterprises and cooperative communities, rather than predicting capitalism's overthrow
- C) Argued that workers' conditions could only be improved through trade union organizing
- D) Believed that religious conversion was the most effective means of addressing industrial poverty

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Owen was a "utopian socialist" (Marx's term) — he wanted to demonstrate that capitalism could be reformed or replaced by cooperative alternatives; he did not analyze it as a system doomed to collapse under its own contradictions.*

**6.** The British Chartist movement (1838–1857) is significant in AP World History primarily because it:

- A) Achieved all six of its original demands through a violent revolution in 1848
- B) Demonstrated that workers understood that political rights were necessary prerequisites for economic reform
- C) Established the first permanent labor unions in British history
- D) Was directly inspired by the Communist Manifesto and Marxist revolutionary theory

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Chartism was a political movement, not an economic one — workers demanded the vote, secret ballot, and parliamentary representation because they understood that without political power, they could not change the economic rules governing their lives.*

**7.** Bismarck's social welfare legislation (health insurance 1883, accident insurance 1884, old-age pensions 1889) is best understood as:

- A) Evidence that Bismarck had been converted to socialist economic principles
- B) A strategic use of state welfare to reduce the appeal of the Social Democratic Party among German workers
- C) A response to pressure from German industrialists who wanted a healthier, more productive workforce
- D) The result of successful labor union bargaining with the German government

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Bismarck explicitly stated he wanted to "bribe" workers away from socialism; his welfare legislation was a conservative political strategy, not a conversion to socialist principles.*

---

**8.** The debate between Eduard Bernstein's "revisionism" and Marx's revolutionary theory in the late 19th century is most significant because it:

- A) Led directly to the collapse of the Second International in 1916
- B) Established the fundamental tension within socialist politics between evolutionary reform and revolutionary transformation
- C) Proved that Marx's economic predictions about capitalist collapse were accurate
- D) Caused most European socialist parties to abandon Marxism in favor of liberal capitalism

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Bernstein-Marx debate (reform vs. revolution) is the foundational internal conflict of socialist politics — it recurs throughout the 20th century in debates between social democrats and communists, reformists and revolutionaries.*

---

**9.** The Paris Commune (1871) was significant in 19th-century labor history primarily because it:

- A) Successfully established the first long-term workers' government in history
- B) Demonstrated both the revolutionary potential of the urban working class and the willingness of bourgeois governments to use extreme violence to suppress it
- C) Inspired Bismarck to introduce his social welfare legislation as a preventative measure
- D) Led to the founding of the First International under Marx's leadership

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Commune lasted only 72 days before the French government killed ~10,000–30,000 Communards; it showed workers that they could seize power — and showed governments that they would crush such attempts with maximum force.*

**10.** Which of the following best explains why trade union organizing was more immediately successful at improving workers' conditions than revolutionary socialism in Britain and Germany?

- A) British and German workers were not sufficiently class-conscious to support revolutionary movements
- B) Trade unions focused on specific, achievable goals (wages, hours, safety) through collective bargaining, while socialist revolution required transforming the entire social order
- C) British and German governments actively supported trade union organizing to prevent more radical movements
- D) British and German workers had higher wages than workers in other industrialized nations, reducing incentives for revolutionary action

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Unions could achieve concrete, immediate gains through collective bargaining without requiring the wholesale overthrow of the social order — making them more attractive to most workers who wanted better conditions now, not a future communist society.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Evaluate the extent to which reform (rather than revolution) was the most effective response to industrial capitalism in the period c. 1820–1900."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE example of a reform movement or policy that improved industrial workers' conditions.
**Part B:** Identify ONE example of a revolutionary or radical movement and explain why it failed to achieve its goals.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why reform was more successful than revolution in most industrial nations during this period.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Factory Acts (Britain), Bismarck's welfare legislation (Germany), AFL union organizing (USA) — all achieved concrete improvements within capitalism.
> - **Part B:** Paris Commune (suppressed violently), Chartist petition campaigns (largely ignored initially), First International (dissolved in factional conflict).
> - **Part C:** Reform worked within existing political and economic systems that still had the coercive power (police, army) to crush revolutionary threats; it also addressed workers' immediate needs without requiring them to risk their lives for an uncertain future.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Explain how Karl Marx's analysis of industrial capitalism reflected the specific historical conditions of the 19th century."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE specific industrial condition that Marx observed and incorporated into his economic theory.
**Part B:** Explain how that condition supported one of Marx's core theoretical claims.
**Part C:** Evaluate ONE way in which Marx's predictions proved inaccurate or incomplete for the period c. 1850–1900.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A/B:** Extreme poverty of factory workers → surplus value theory (workers produce more than they're paid); business cycle crises (1848, 1873) → prediction of capitalism's inherent instability; concentration of industry in corporations → prediction of increasing concentration of capital.
> - **Part C:** Marx predicted that workers' conditions would worsen as capitalism developed (the "immiseration thesis"), but in Britain, conditions actually improved in the second half of the 19th century as real wages rose. Marx underestimated capitalism's adaptability.
