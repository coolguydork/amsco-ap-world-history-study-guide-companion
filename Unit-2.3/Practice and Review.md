# Topic 2.3 — Practice and Review

## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

The monsoon wind system of the Indian Ocean was significant to trade primarily because it:

A) Made sailing dangerous and reduced the volume of trade  
B) Created predictable seasonal routes that merchants could follow and required extended stays in port cities, which led to the formation of diasporic communities  
C) Was only understood by Chinese sailors, giving China a monopoly over Indian Ocean trade  
D) Eliminated the need for navigational instruments like the astrolabe and compass

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The monsoon system's *predictability* is what made it useful — merchants knew when to sail and when to wait. The enforced waiting periods in port cities are precisely why diasporic communities formed: merchants who stayed for months might marry, settle, and raise families. A is the opposite. C is false — Arab sailors were the dominant force. D is wrong — navigational instruments were still essential for open-ocean positioning.

**Question 2**

The Swahili city-states of East Africa (such as Kilwa and Mombasa) are best understood as:

A) Agricultural empires that grew wealthy from surplus food production  
B) Military powers that conquered Indian Ocean trade routes by force  
C) City-states whose prosperity depended on participation in the Indian Ocean trade network as exporters of ivory, gold, and slaves and importers of luxury goods  
D) Religious centers that spread Christianity through East Africa via merchant networks

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The Swahili cities were entirely trade-dependent — they had little agricultural hinterland and no military dominance. Their wealth came from being nodes in the Indian Ocean network. Chinese porcelain found in their ruins is physical evidence of this. D is wrong — Islam (not Christianity) spread through this network.

**Question 3**

The decision to end Zheng He's voyages in the 1430s illustrates which of the following historical patterns?

A) China's inability to build ships large enough for extended ocean voyages  
B) The collapse of Indian Ocean trade following Mongol invasions  
C) The way political and ideological factors can redirect or halt technological and commercial capabilities  
D) The failure of tribute-based diplomacy to generate lasting political relationships

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> China *had* the technology and organizational capacity to dominate Indian Ocean trade. The decision to stop was political and ideological — Confucian scholars viewed outward exploration as wasteful and culturally threatening. This is a classic AP example of how non-economic factors shape economic history.

## SAQ Practice

**SAQ Prompt:**

> *"Seek ye knowledge, even to China."*
> — Hadith (9th century, attributed to Muhammad)

**Part A**: Briefly explain ONE way in which the expansion of Islam contributed to the growth of the Indian Ocean trade network between c. 1200 and c. 1450.

**Part B**: Briefly explain ONE effect that expanded Indian Ocean trade had on the societies of East Africa during this period.

**Part C**: Briefly explain ONE way in which Indian Ocean trade differed from trade on the overland Silk Roads during c. 1200–c. 1450.

> [!tip] Model Responses
> **Part A**: Muslim merchants — Arab and Persian sailors — were the dominant traders in the Indian Ocean, connecting East Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia in a network anchored by Dar al-Islam. Muslim merchants settled in port cities like Calicut, married local women, and spread Islam gradually through commerce and intermarriage rather than conquest. The faith's shared norms (contracts, treatment of trading partners) also created a commercial culture of trust across enormous distances.
>
> **Part B**: Indian Ocean trade created thriving Swahili city-states along East Africa's coast, including Kilwa, Mombasa, and Zanzibar. These cities accumulated enough wealth to build mosques and merchants' homes from stone and coral, replacing traditional mud construction. Trade also brought Chinese porcelain, Indian cotton, and Islam to East Africa, transforming local cultures and contributing to the emergence of the Swahili language — a blend of Bantu and Arabic that reflects centuries of cross-cultural contact.
>
> **Part C**: Indian Ocean trade relied on maritime technology (dhows, lateen sails, the astrolabe) and monsoon wind knowledge, and it moved goods that were too heavy or bulky for overland transport — porcelain, cotton, gold, and iron. The Silk Roads, by contrast, relied on camels and caravanserai, and specialized in lighter luxury goods like silk and spices. The Indian Ocean also generated larger diasporic communities because merchants stayed in port cities for months waiting for seasonal winds.

## Connections Practice

**Explain how the Indian Ocean trade network spread Islam differently than conquest did.**

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Merchant settlement | Arab and Persian merchants settled permanently in port cities like Calicut, Mombasa, and Malacca |
| Intermarriage | Merchants married local women; children were raised Muslim |
| Cultural prestige | Islamic literacy, architecture, and scholarship attracted converts |
| State adoption | Local rulers (like the Sultan of Malacca) converted and used Islam for trade networks and legitimacy |
| Gradual integration | Islamic words, practices, and beliefs blended with local traditions over generations |

**Contrast with conquest-driven spread**: In Central Asia, Persia, and North Africa, Islam spread through conquest — often rapid, politically imposed, backed by military force. In the Indian Ocean world, conversion was typically slower, more voluntary, and involved cultural blending. The Swahili language (Bantu + Arabic) is a living artifact of this gradual process.

## CCOT Prompt

**"Describe ONE continuity and ONE change in the Indian Ocean trade network between c. 200 BCE and c. 1500 CE."**

**Continuity**: Arab and Persian merchants dominated Indian Ocean trade throughout this entire period — from pre-Islamic times through the height of the medieval trade network. The basic routes (East Africa ↔ Arabian Peninsula ↔ India ↔ Southeast Asia ↔ China) remained consistent.

**Change**: After c. 1000 CE, Islam transformed the cultural character of the Indian Ocean network. Merchant communities that had once been religiously diverse became predominantly Muslim. Islamic commercial law, shared language (Arabic), and religious networks created a more integrated economic community than the pre-Islamic period had seen.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: China dominated Indian Ocean trade during this period. In reality, Arab and Persian Muslim merchants were the primary operators of Indian Ocean commerce. China was a major *producer* (silk, porcelain) and destination, but not the dominant *trader*. Zheng He's voyages were an exception, and they were deliberately ended.

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: The Indian Ocean slave trade was small and insignificant. It was, in fact, a centuries-long institution that moved millions of people from East Africa to the Middle East, North Africa, and India — though it differed in organization and conditions from the Atlantic slave trade.
