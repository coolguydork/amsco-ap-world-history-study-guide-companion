# Timeline & Chronology — Exchange in the Indian Ocean

## Chronological Overview

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| c. 200 BCE | Indian Ocean trade begins between India, Arabia, and East Africa | Ancient origins of the world's most durable maritime trade network — long predates Islam |
| 7th–8th c. CE | Muslim merchants become dominant in the Indian Ocean Basin | Islam's expansion supercharges existing networks; diasporic communities form across the region |
| c. 1100s | Astrolabe improved by Muslim navigators | Enables latitude determination; critical advance for open-ocean navigation beyond coastlines |
| c. 1200–1400s | Swahili city-states flourish (Kilwa, Mombasa, Zanzibar) | East African coast fully integrated into global trade; Swahili language and culture emerge |
| c. 1400s | Malacca becomes the dominant port at the Strait of Malacca | Controls the trade corridor between Indian Ocean and South China Sea; Islam spreads to Malay Peninsula and Sumatra |
| 1402–1424 | Reign of Ming Emperor Yongle | Outward-looking phase of Ming China; sponsors Zheng He's voyages |
| 1405 | Zheng He's first voyage departs | Launches China's brief but spectacular engagement with the Indian Ocean world |
| 1405–1433 | Zheng He's seven voyages | China projects power from East Africa to Southeast Asia; largest wooden fleet ever assembled (300+ ships, 28,000 people) |
| 1424 | Emperor Yongle dies; Zhu Gaozhi takes power | End of outward-looking Ming policy; voyages cancelled |
| 1424–1425 | Zhu Gaozhi ends Zheng He's voyages | China withdraws from Indian Ocean; ban on multi-masted ships; piracy resumes in China Sea |
| c. 1400–1511 | Peak of Malacca Sultanate | Southeast Asian trade hub; Islam spreads throughout the Malay world |
| 1498 | Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut | Portuguese enter Indian Ocean — beginning of European disruption of existing trade networks |
| 1511 | Portuguese conquer Malacca | Shatters the dominant trade node; merchants disperse to other ports; European era of Indian Ocean intervention begins |

## Periodization Notes

The AP exam covers this topic within **Period 1: c. 1200–c. 1450**. Key benchmarks:

- **c. 1200** — Indian Ocean trade already well-established; the question is what *expanded* it
- **c. 1400** — Peak of Malacca, Swahili city-states, and Chinese engagement (Zheng He)
- **c. 1450** — AP period ends just before the Portuguese arrived; their disruption belongs to Unit 4

> [!exam-tip]
> The Portuguese conquest of Malacca (1511) falls **outside** the c. 1200–c. 1450 AP period for this topic. It is relevant as a **transition marker** — you can mention it as "what came after" in a CCOT argument — but the exam's focus is on the network *before* European arrival.
