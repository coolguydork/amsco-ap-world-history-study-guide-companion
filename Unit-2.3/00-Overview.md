# Unit 2.3: Exchange in the Indian Ocean Region (c. 1200–1450)

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 2 — Networks of Exchange
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (c. 1200–1450)
**Key Theme:** Maritime Trade, Monsoon Networks, and Cultural Diffusion

## Summary

The Indian Ocean trading network was the most active and economically significant maritime system in the world during the period c. 1200–1450. Powered by predictable monsoon winds and sustained by merchant diasporas from Arabia, Persia, India, Southeast Asia, and East Africa, this network connected civilizations from the Chinese coast to the East African shores of Zanzibar and Kilwa. Unlike the Silk Roads, the Indian Ocean network was largely decentralized — it was not controlled by a single empire but sustained by commercial relationships, shared legal systems (Islamic commercial law), and trust networks among diasporic merchant communities. Goods traded included spices (nutmeg, cloves, pepper), silk, porcelain, cotton textiles, gold, ivory, and enslaved people. The Indian Ocean network also transmitted Islam with extraordinary effectiveness, making it the primary vehicle for the religion's expansion into Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia in this period.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how Indian Ocean trade networks facilitated the exchange of goods, people, and ideas across the period c. 1200–1450
- Analyze the role of monsoon winds and maritime technology in enabling Indian Ocean trade
- Explain how Islam spread through Indian Ocean merchant networks
- Evaluate the impact of Indian Ocean trade on the development of East African, South Asian, and Southeast Asian states

## AP World History Key Concepts

- **KC-2.1.I** — Indian Ocean trade networks expanded in volume and complexity in this period, enabled by improved maritime technology and commercial practices
- **KC-2.2.I** — Islam spread through Indian Ocean merchant diasporas into East Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia
- **KC-1.3.I** — Indian Ocean states developed in response to and in dependence on trade networks
- **KC-2.1.III** — East African city-states (Swahili coast) and Southeast Asian port cities emerged as nodes in the Indian Ocean network
