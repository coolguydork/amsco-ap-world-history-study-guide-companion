# Unit 2.4: Timeline — Trans-Saharan Trade Routes (c. 1200–1450)

> [!tip]
> **Big Picture:** The Trans-Saharan trade network was already ancient by 1200 — the gold-salt exchange had connected West Africa to the Mediterranean for nearly a thousand years before Mali's rise. Topic 2.4 covers this network's mature peak under the Mali Empire (c. 1235–1450), when Timbuktu became an Islamic scholarly center of global significance and Mansa Musa's hajj announced West Africa's wealth to the world. The key CCOT pattern: the network remained structurally continuous (gold south → north, salt north → south, Islam spreading south) while the dominant political powers controlling it changed — Ghana → Mali → Songhai.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| c. 300–700 CE | **Ghana Empire** rises as first major West African trade empire | Sahel / West Africa | **CONTINUITY baseline**: gold-salt exchange ancient by Period 1; Ghana established the model of empire-through-trade-taxation that Mali and Songhai would inherit |
| c. 8th–10th c. | Islam spreads into West African elite and merchant communities via trans-Saharan trade | West Africa / Sahel | **CHANGE**: Islamic conversion begins among rulers and merchants; sets the stage for Mali's explicitly Islamic imperial identity |
| c. 1050–1076 | Almoravid movement (Berber Islamic reformers) disrupts Ghana Empire | West Africa / Maghreb | **CHANGE**: Ghana weakened; power vacuum in the Sahel opens the way for Malian expansion; demonstrates the political consequences of religious movements traveling trade routes |
| c. 1200 | Sosso kingdom controls former Ghana territories briefly | West Africa | Interregnum between Ghana and Mali; Sumanguru Kante rules; sets up Sundiata's rise |
| **c. 1235** | **Sundiata Keita defeats the Sosso at the Battle of Kirina; Mali Empire founded** | West Africa | **CHANGE**: Mali replaces Ghana as the dominant Sahelian power; Sundiata's story preserved by griots as the *Epic of Sundiata*, West Africa's foundational oral epic |
| c. 1240–1280 | Mali consolidates control of gold-producing regions (Bambuk, Bure) and trans-Saharan routes | West Africa | **CONTINUITY**: gold-salt exchange continues; **CHANGE**: Mali now monopolizes taxation of these routes, generating the wealth that will fund Mansa Musa's later hajj |
| c. 1280s | Timbuktu grows into major trans-Saharan trade and Islamic scholarly center | West Africa / Sahel | Sankore Mosque-University established; Diula merchants facilitate interior trade; **CHANGE**: Timbuktu transitions from a seasonal camp to a permanent urban center |
| **c. 1312** | **Mansa Musa becomes ruler (mansa) of the Mali Empire** | West Africa | Beginning of Mali's most powerful reign; direct control over gold-trade revenues and Saharan routes |
| **1324–1325** | **Mansa Musa's hajj to Mecca** | West Africa → Egypt → Arabia | **Landmark event**: 60,000+ person entourage; gold distribution causes Egyptian inflation lasting a decade; Mali placed on European and Islamic world maps; architect brought back reshapes Timbuktu's urban landscape |
| 1331–1332 | **Ibn Battuta visits Mali** (recorded in his *Rihla*) | West Africa | Primary source for Mali's governance, social structure, and Islamic practice at its height; describes Mansa Musa's court and Timbuktu's wealth |
| 1337 | Mansa Musa dies; Mali's gradual decline begins | West Africa | **CHANGE onset**: succession disputes weaken central authority; Sahelian rivals begin to challenge Mali's dominance |
| 1352–1353 | Ibn Battuta travels through Mali | West Africa | Detailed written account of Mali from an outside observer; notes prosperity and Islamic observance as well as practices that surprised him (women's relative freedom) |
| **c. 1375** | Catalan Atlas (Abraham Cresques) depicts Mansa Musa holding a gold nugget | Mediterranean / Europe | **Significance**: demonstrates that Mali's wealth was known and mapped in Europe decades after Musa's death; evidence of how trans-Saharan gold trade shaped global geographic knowledge |
| c. 1400–1430 | Songhai Empire under Sunni Ali's predecessors begins to challenge Mali in the eastern Sahel | West Africa | **CHANGE**: Mali's grip on eastern trade routes weakens; Timbuktu increasingly contested |
| **c. 1430–1450** | Mali Empire in significant decline | West Africa | Loss of control over key Saharan routes; Tuareg sack Timbuktu (1433); Songhai ascendant — **sets up Unit 4 context** |
| c. 1440s onward | Portuguese begin maritime contact with West African coast | West Africa / Atlantic | **Forward connection (Unit 4)**: Portuguese maritime routes begin to offer an alternative to trans-Saharan gold trade, eventually redirecting West African gold toward the Atlantic and hastening Mali's commercial decline |
