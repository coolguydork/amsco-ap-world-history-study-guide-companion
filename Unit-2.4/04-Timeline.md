# Unit 2.4: Timeline — Exchange in the Americas, Africa, and Europe

> [!tip]
> **Big Picture:** Unit 2.4 covers three regional networks that were all developing in parallel during Period 1 (c. 1200–1450), yet entirely unconnected to each other or to the Silk Roads and Indian Ocean (with Europe as a partial exception — connected to Silk Roads through Italian city-states). The Americas' isolation is the overarching CCOT theme: continuity within the Americas, catastrophic change coming from outside after 1492. The Trans-Saharan network's decline mirrors the Silk Road's decline — both eventually disrupted by European maritime alternatives.

## Africa: Trans-Saharan Trade

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| c. 300–700 CE | Ghana Empire dominates trans-Saharan gold trade | West Africa | The first great West African trade empire; precursor to Mali; **CONTINUITY**: gold-salt exchange ancient by the 13th century |
| c. 1230–1255 | Sundiata Keita founds the Mali Empire, defeating the Sosso | West Africa | **CHANGE**: Mali replaces Ghana as dominant West African power; Sundiata's story preserved by griots as the *Epic of Sundiata* |
| c. 1280–1337 | Mali Empire at peak power | West Africa | Controls gold and salt trade routes; largest and wealthiest state in West Africa |
| 1324–1325 | **Mansa Musa's hajj to Mecca** | West Africa → North Africa → Arabia | Demonstrates Mali's Islamic identity and extraordinary wealth; gold distribution causes Egyptian inflation; places Mali on world maps |
| c. 1325–1354 | Ibn Battuta visits Mali (1352–1353) | West Africa | Primary source for Mali's governance, Islamic practices, and social life at its height |
| c. 1375 | Catalan Atlas depicts Mansa Musa | Europe | Evidence of how Mali's reputation spread to Europe via the Islamic world — unprecedented European knowledge of West Africa |
| c. 1400 | Songhai Empire begins to challenge Mali | West Africa | **CHANGE**: Mali begins to decline; Songhai will eventually replace it as the dominant Sahelian state |

## Europe: Commercial Recovery and Black Death

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| c. 1000–1200 | Revival of European trade; Italian city-states emerge | Europe | **CHANGE**: after centuries of post-Roman commercial decline, Mediterranean trade revives; Venice, Genoa begin building commercial empires |
| 1095–1291 | The Crusades | Europe, Middle East | Military failure but commercial catalyst — exposure to Islamic luxury goods increased European demand; Italian merchants profited from supplying crusader states |
| c. 1241 | Hanseatic League begins to take shape in northern Europe | Northern Europe | Baltic and North Sea commercial network develops independently of Mediterranean — **CONTINUITY**: northern European trade grows steadily through the period |
| 1271–1295 | Marco Polo travels to China and back | Eurasia | European awareness of Asian wealth intensifies demand for direct access to Asian trade goods |
| 1346 | Black Death reaches the Black Sea port of Caffa | Central Asia/Black Sea | Plague arrives via Mongol-controlled Silk Road routes — first European exposure |
| 1347 | **Black Death arrives in Sicily via Genoese ships from Caffa** | Europe | **CHANGE**: the most destructive demographic event in European history begins |
| 1347–1353 | Black Death kills 25–50% of European population | Europe | Labor shortages, Church authority eroded, serfdom weakened in West, intensified in East |
| 1358 | Jacquerie peasant uprising in France | France | Direct social consequence of Black Death — labor scarcity empowers peasants |
| 1381 | Peasants' Revolt in England | England | Further evidence of Black Death's unintended social consequences — social hierarchy challenged by plague-empowered laborers |

## The Americas

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| c. 900–1200 | Cahokia (Mississippian culture) at peak | North America (Illinois) | Largest pre-Columbian city north of Mexico; extensive river-based trade network; **CONTINUITY** from ancient Woodlands culture trading patterns |
| c. 1100 | Cahokia population peaks (20,000–30,000) | North America | Comparable in size to contemporary London; demonstrates North American urban capacity |
| c. 1200–1400 | Cahokia declines | North America | Possible causes: environmental degradation, political instability, trade disruption — still debated |
| c. 1300–1400 | Aztec (Mexica) settle Tenochtitlán; begin building empire | Mesoamerica | **CHANGE**: a new dominant Mesoamerican power emerges, eventually controlling a massive tribute and trade network |
| c. 1350–1450 | Inca begin expanding from Cuzco | Andean South America | Foundation of the largest pre-Columbian empire; road system and mit'a system develop |
| 1438–1471 | Pachacuti expands Inca Empire dramatically | South America | **CHANGE**: Inca consolidation of Andean South America; road network expands |
| 1492 | Columbus makes contact with Caribbean | Americas | **CHANGE**: Americas' isolation ends; Columbian Exchange and catastrophic disease transfer begin — Unit 4 |
