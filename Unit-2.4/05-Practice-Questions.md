# Unit 2.4: Practice Questions — Exchange in the Americas, Africa, and Europe

> [!exam-tip]
> Unit 2.4's AP exam questions often involve **comparative analysis** — comparing the Trans-Saharan network to the Silk Roads or Indian Ocean, or comparing the Americas to the Old World. Practice making specific, evidence-based comparisons, not vague generalizations.

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Question 1**

*"Mansa Musa sent 14,000 slave girls as gifts. He gave away so much gold that the price of gold in Cairo fell for twelve years."*
— Arab historian Al-Umari, describing Mansa Musa's pilgrimage, c. 1337

Which of the following conclusions is best supported by the passage above?

A) The Mali Empire's wealth derived primarily from enslaved labor rather than trade in gold
B) Mansa Musa's pilgrimage demonstrated the extraordinary wealth of West Africa generated by the trans-Saharan gold trade
C) The Islamic world's gold supply was primarily dependent on West African sources by the 14th century
D) Mansa Musa's gift-giving caused a permanent decline in Egyptian commercial prosperity

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The passage's detail about the gold price falling for twelve years is direct evidence of the massive scale of West African gold production — the amount Mansa Musa distributed was enough to affect Egyptian markets for over a decade. This is evidence of trans-Saharan trade wealth, not primarily of enslaved labor or Egyptian dependence.

**Question 2**

The gold-salt exchange of the Trans-Saharan trade is best understood as evidence of:

A) West African kingdoms' military conquest of Saharan salt mines
B) The role of complementary resource distribution in creating self-sustaining long-distance trade networks
C) The Islamic world's deliberate strategy to control African commodity production
D) The failure of West African states to develop their own salt production technology

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The gold-salt trade is the AP exam's go-to example of complementary resources driving trade. Neither gold nor salt could be produced in the same region in sufficient quantity — this mutual scarcity created a natural commercial relationship without requiring imperial control or external pressure.

**Question 3**

Which of the following best explains why Islam spread effectively along Trans-Saharan trade routes in the period c. 700–1450?

A) Islamic armies conquered West African kingdoms and forced rulers to convert
B) Muslim merchants established diasporic communities at trading hubs, and West African rulers converted for commercial and diplomatic advantages
C) Islamic missionaries used the educational system of the Abbasid Caliphate to train West African scholars
D) The Mongol Empire's patronage of Islam spread it along all trade routes it controlled, including trans-Saharan routes

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Islam's spread in West Africa followed the same merchant-diaspora model as in the Indian Ocean. Rulers converted for commercial advantages (Muslim trading partners, access to Islamic commercial law) and political legitimacy (membership in a transregional Islamic community), not under military compulsion.

**Question 4**

The Black Death's impact on European serfdom in the 14th century most directly illustrates which of the following?

A) That epidemic disease can cause unintended social transformations by altering labor market conditions
B) That the Catholic Church lost its authority over European peasants following the plague
C) That European feudal lords deliberately used the plague to justify increasing control over peasants
D) That the Black Death only affected urban populations, leaving rural serf communities intact

**Correct Answer: A**
*Explanation:* The Black Death killed so many laborers that survivors could demand better conditions — lords needed workers so badly they had to negotiate rather than coerce. This is an unintended consequence of demographic disruption, not a deliberate policy or a result of religious change.

**Question 5**

The Italian city-states of Venice and Genoa in the period c. 1200–1450 are best understood as:

A) The political centers of a revived Roman commercial empire in the Mediterranean
B) The western terminals of Silk Road luxury goods trade, which they distributed to European markets
C) Rivals whose competition for trade routes prevented Europe from participating in global commerce
D) Suppliers of manufactured goods that they exported to Asian markets in exchange for raw materials

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Venice and Genoa's commercial significance was as middlemen — they brought Asian luxury goods (spices, silk, dyes) into European markets. This position was immensely profitable and made Italian city-states the wealthiest entities in Europe, funding the Renaissance.

**Question 6**

Which of the following most directly distinguishes the Inca Empire's economic system from the Aztec Empire's economic system?

A) The Inca used a market-based commercial economy, while the Aztec relied entirely on tribute from conquered peoples
B) The Inca managed redistribution through state-controlled labor (mit'a) and storehouses rather than through market exchange, while the Aztec used both tribute and active commercial markets
C) The Aztec Empire had no professional merchant class, while the Inca pochteca controlled long-distance trade
D) The Inca economy was based on gold production for export, while the Aztec economy relied on cacao and obsidian

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This is a comparison question. The Inca's mit'a system and qollqa (storehouses) represent a non-market redistributive economy; the Aztec used tribute from conquered peoples AND active commercial exchange through the pochteca and the Tlatelolco market — a more hybrid approach.

**Question 7**

The existence of sophisticated trade networks within the pre-contact Americas most directly challenges which of the following historical misconceptions?

A) That the Americas had no connection to any global trade network before European contact
B) That the Americas lacked complex political organization and long-distance commercial exchange before European arrival
C) That the Aztec and Inca empires were the only centers of political power in the pre-contact Americas
D) That American civilizations had no interest in producing luxury goods for trade

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Tlatelolco market, the pochteca network, the Inca road system, and Mississippian river trade all demonstrate that the Americas had sophisticated commercial exchange and political organization well before European arrival. The absence of Old World contact did not mean the absence of complexity.

---

**Question 8**

The Americas' biological isolation from the Old World in the period c. 1200–1450 is most significant as a historical context for understanding:

A) Why Aztec and Inca armies were unable to resist Spanish conquest in the 16th century
B) The catastrophic demographic collapse of American populations following European contact due to exposure to Old World diseases
C) The failure of American civilizations to develop ocean-going ships capable of reaching Europe
D) Why American states did not develop the same commercial institutions as Silk Road merchants

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Americas' biological isolation — no exposure to smallpox, measles, influenza, or other Old World diseases — is the primary explanation for the demographic catastrophe that followed European contact. Populations with no prior exposure had no immunity; mortality rates of 50–90% in some regions made conquest dramatically easier than it would otherwise have been.

**Question 9**

Which of the following best represents a similarity between the Trans-Saharan trade network in Africa and the Indian Ocean trade network?

A) Both networks relied primarily on overland camel caravan transport
B) Both networks spread Islam through merchant diaspora communities, contributing to political conversions by local rulers
C) Both networks were controlled by a single dominant empire that taxed and regulated all commerce
D) Both networks were primarily active in the winter months due to favorable weather conditions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This is a high-value comparison. Both the Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean networks spread Islam via the same mechanism: Muslim merchants established communities, local rulers converted for commercial and political advantages. The mechanism was identical even though the geographic context and specific goods were different.

**Question 10**

Which of the following developments in the period c. 1400–1500 most directly caused the decline of both the Trans-Saharan trade network and the overland Silk Road?

A) The Black Death's demographic collapse eliminated the merchant communities that sustained both networks
B) Portuguese maritime exploration provided direct sea access to West African gold and Asian spices, bypassing overland intermediaries
C) The Mongol Empire's collapse caused political instability that made both networks unsafe for commerce
D) The conversion of West African rulers to Islam disrupted relationships with Christian European trading partners

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This question requires connecting Unit 2 to Unit 3. Portuguese maritime exploration (sailing down the African coast to trade directly with West African gold producers, and eventually reaching India via the Cape of Good Hope) bypassed both networks simultaneously — cutting out the trans-Saharan middlemen and the overland Silk Road intermediaries.

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"West African kingdoms in the period c. 1200–1450 were shaped primarily by their participation in the Trans-Saharan trade network."**

**Part A:** Briefly explain ONE piece of evidence that supports this claim about the Mali Empire.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE piece of evidence that complicates or challenges this claim.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which the Trans-Saharan trade network connected West African states to broader global networks in this period.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Mansa Musa's pilgrimage (the gold, the entourage, the inflation in Cairo all demonstrate trade-based wealth); Mali's control of gold-producing regions and trade routes as the basis of its power
> - Part B: Mali's political power also rested on agriculture (the Niger River floodplain), military strength, and the legitimacy of its rulers independent of trade; Great Zimbabwe's wealth came from Indian Ocean trade, not trans-Saharan routes
> - Part C: Trans-Saharan routes carried Islam southward; Mali's Islamic identity connected it to the broader Islamic commercial and diplomatic network (Mansa Musa's hajj is evidence); gold from West Africa reached the Mediterranean and funded European commerce

---

### SAQ 2

**"The Black Death transformed European society in ways that extended far beyond demographic decline."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe the origin and transmission path of the Black Death into Europe.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE political or social consequence of the Black Death in Europe beyond population loss.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which European responses to the Black Death differed across social groups.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Originated in Central Asian rodent populations; traveled Mongol Silk Roads to Caffa (Black Sea); carried by Genoese ships to Sicily 1347; spread rapidly through Europe 1347–1353
> - Part B: Erosion of serfdom in Western Europe (labor shortages gave peasants bargaining power); weakening of Church authority (inability to explain or stop the plague); persecution of Jews as scapegoats; peasant uprisings (Jacquerie, Peasants' Revolt)
> - Part C: Flagellant movement (religious self-punishment by penitents); Jewish pogroms (scapegoating by Christians); peasant revolts (political action by laboring poor); artistic expression of death anxiety (the *Danse Macabre* genre)

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline

### DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which trade networks in Africa and Europe in the period c. 1200–1450 promoted similar patterns of political and cultural development."**

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**

**Thesis:** "Trans-Saharan trade in West Africa and the Italian city-state commercial network in Europe both created states whose political power derived directly from control of commerce, and both facilitated the spread of dominant religious-commercial cultures (Islam in West Africa, Catholic Christianity in Europe) along trade routes — but the mechanisms, scale, and social consequences of these developments differed significantly, reflecting the distinct environments in which each network operated."

**Possible Groupings:**
- Group 1: Political power derived from trade control (Mali rulers taxing gold trade; Venetian merchant oligarchs governing commercial republic)
- Group 2: Cultural/religious diffusion via trade (Islam spreading to Mali via trans-Saharan merchants; Catholic Church's commercial connections via Italian city-states)
- Group 3: Disruption and resilience (Black Death disrupting European trade and demographics; internal succession conflicts weakening Mali's control over trans-Saharan routes). Note: the Mongols did not significantly disrupt trans-Saharan trade — they never reached West Africa, and the Mamluks in Egypt successfully halted Mongol westward expansion at Ain Jalut (1260), protecting North Africa's connections to the Saharan network

**Outside Evidence:**
- Mansa Musa's hajj as demonstration of trade-based wealth and Islamic identity
- Timbuktu as Islamic scholarly center produced by trans-Saharan trade wealth
- Venetian and Genoese commercial innovations (double-entry bookkeeping, marine insurance) as products of commercial republic governance
- Black Death as the most consequential disruption to European trade networks in this period

**Complexity Point Options:**
- Argue **contrast**: Africa's trade wealth produced Islamic-integrated states with royal conversion; Europe's trade wealth produced commercial republics that challenged feudal hierarchy — different political forms from similar commercial conditions
- Argue **long-term consequence**: trans-Saharan trade eventually disrupted by Portuguese maritime routes (Unit 3); Italian city-state commercial dominance eventually displaced by Atlantic trade networks — both networks eventually marginalized by the same European maritime expansion
