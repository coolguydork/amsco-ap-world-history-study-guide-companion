# Timeline & Chronology — Trans-Saharan Trade Routes

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| c. 3rd century BCE | Camels appear in North Africa | Imported from Arabia; adaptation to Saharan conditions begins |
| c. 300 CE | Camels replace horses as primary trans-Saharan pack animal | Critical infrastructure shift enabling high-volume desert commerce |
| c. 5th century CE | Kingdom of Ghana founded in West Africa | First major state to profit systematically from trans-Saharan gold trade |
| 7th–8th century CE | Muslim merchants dramatically expand trans-Saharan trade | Islam and commerce spread together into sub-Saharan Africa |
| c. 800 CE | Trans-Saharan gold trade famous throughout Europe and Asia | West African gold becomes foundational to Afro-Eurasian monetary systems |
| 9th century CE | Islam introduced to Mali region via trade routes | Sets stage for Mali's later Islamic state-building and legitimacy |
| c. 1100s | Ghana weakened; Mali rises as dominant West African power | New empire controls gold, ivory, salt, and enslaved-people trade routes |
| 1235 | Sundiata founds Mali Empire | Largest empire in West Africa; Timbuktu and Gao become Islamic centers |
| 1304–1353 | Life of Ibn Battuta | His travels documented trans-Saharan trade and Mali in vivid detail |
| 1324 | Mansa Musa's hajj to Mecca | Spectacular display of Mali's gold wealth; floods Middle Eastern gold markets |
| c. 1337 | Mansa Musa dies | Mali begins gradual decline within a century |
| Late 1400s | Mali declining; Songhai rising | Sunni Ali conquers Timbuktu (1468); Songhai becomes dominant |
| 1468 | Sunni Ali conquers Timbuktu | Songhai Empire replaces Mali as West Africa's leading power |
| 1500s | Books in Timbuktu command premium prices | Height of Timbuktu as intellectual center under Songhai |

---

## Phasing the Timeline

**Phase 1 — Preconditions (pre-7th century)**
Camel technology spreads to North Africa. Ghana establishes the first empire profiting from trans-Saharan gold. Trade volume is modest.

**Phase 2 — Expansion (7th–9th century)**
Muslim merchants and trade networks dramatically increase Saharan commerce. Islam enters the region. Trade routes become famous across Afro-Eurasia.

**Phase 3 — Mali's Dominance (13th–14th century)**
Sundiata founds Mali (1235). Empire expands through military conquest and trade taxation. Timbuktu flourishes. Mansa Musa's hajj (1324) puts Mali on the world stage. This is the AP exam's primary focus period.

**Phase 4 — Transition to Songhai (late 14th–15th century)**
Mali declines through internal succession struggles and external pressure. Songhai takes over the same commercial infrastructure. Timbuktu remains an intellectual capital.

                                      by Sundiata       Timbuktu

> [!tip]
> **Sequence memory aid:** Think "G-M-S" — Ghana, Mali, Songhai — in that order. Each empire controlled the same trade routes but expanded them further. The AP exam will not ask you to memorize all dates, but knowing **1235** (Mali founded by Sundiata) and **1324** (Mansa Musa's hajj) is essential.

## Ibn Battuta as a Primary Source

Ibn Battuta traveled through Mali in the 1350s and left detailed written accounts. His descriptions are among the most important primary sources for trans-Saharan trade — he documented merchant practices, Niger River commerce, cowrie shell currency, and Mali's cities. The AP exam uses his writings as a stimulus document in MCQ and SAQ prompts. Key point: his account is a *Muslim scholar's perspective* — he praised what fit Islamic norms and criticized what did not, which introduces a viewpoint bias worth noting for sourcing practice.
