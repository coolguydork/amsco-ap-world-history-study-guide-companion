# Timeline & Chronology

**AMSCO pp. 317–324**

---

## Chronological Overview

The events in Topic 5.6 span roughly 1800–1914, tracking non-Western government responses to the industrializing world. Two parallel stories unfold: the **Ottoman/Egyptian track** (1800s–1870s, largely failing) and the **Japanese track** (1853–1914, succeeding dramatically).

## Key Dates

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| c. 1805 | Muhammad Ali becomes governor of Egypt | Launches state-directed modernization in Egypt; breaks Mamluk power; quasi-independent from Ottoman sultan |

## Chronological Reasoning

> [!exam-tip]
> MCQ Question 3 in AMSCO asks about chronological order of Japanese events. The correct sequence is: **Perry's ships arrive (1853) → Shogunate collapses / Meiji Restoration (1868) → Japan industrializes (1870s–1914)**. Note: the shogunate collapsed *as part of* the Restoration, not before Perry arrived.

### Two Tracks, Two Outcomes

The Ottoman and Egyptian stories peak in the **1860s–1880s** and end in failure (British occupation, Ottoman territorial losses). Japan's story begins in **1868** and accelerates through 1914. Understanding this divergence temporally helps explain causation: Japan had more time under coherent state direction, while the Ottoman Empire's window for reform was being closed by debt, nationalist revolts, and European intervention simultaneously.
