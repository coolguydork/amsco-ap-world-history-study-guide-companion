# Topic 5.6 — Key Terms & Concepts

These are the terms you need to own before the AP exam. Each definition is written with the exam in mind. (AMSCO pp. 317–321)

---

## Core Vocabulary

### State-Sponsored Industrialization
When a **government** — rather than private entrepreneurs — drives industrial development by building factories, funding infrastructure, subsidizing industries, and directing economic policy. Contrasts with the [[laissez-faire]] model of Britain. Key examples: Egypt under [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]], Japan under the [[Concept Notes/Meiji Restoration|Meiji Restoration]].

### [[Concept Notes/Meiji Restoration|Meiji Restoration]] (1868)
The political revolution in Japan that overthrew the Tokugawa shogunate and "restored" the Meiji Emperor to power. In practice, it launched Japan's systematic modernization program: abolishing feudalism, adopting Western-style military and government, building railroads, and subsidizing industry. Named for Emperor Meiji ("Enlightened Rule").

> [!exam-tip]
> The Meiji Restoration is one of the most tested events in Unit 5. Know it as a **cause** of Japan's rapid industrialization AND as an **effect** of Western pressure (Commodore Perry, fear of China's fate after the [[Concept Notes/Opium War|Opium War]]).

### [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]] (r. 1805–1848)
Albanian-born Ottoman military officer who became governor of Egypt and pursued an ambitious program of state-led modernization — military reform, agricultural restructuring, textile and armaments factories, schools, and Egypt's first newspaper. Called "the first great modern ruler of Egypt." His reforms were largely state-directed rather than market-driven.

### Shogunate / Shogun
Japan's military government, which had ruled since the 1600s. The Tokugawa shogunate kept Japan largely isolated from the outside world (1600–1854). Its overthrow in 1868 enabled the [[Concept Notes/Meiji Restoration|Meiji Restoration]].

### [[Concept Notes/zaibatsu|zaibatsu]]
Powerful Japanese **family business conglomerates** that emerged during the Meiji period as the private counterpart to government-led industrialization. Similar in structure to U.S. monopolies/conglomerates. Example: Toyoda Loom Works (1906) → eventually became Toyota Motor Company. The zaibatsu worked alongside the Meiji government to build Japan's industrial economy.

### Self-Strengthening Movement (China, 1860s–1890s)
China's attempt to modernize its **military technology** — building shipyards and arsenals, training artisans, hiring French and British advisors — while preserving traditional Chinese culture and institutions. The strategy was to **graft** modern technology onto Chinese tradition rather than transform the underlying system. Ultimately insufficient; exposed by defeat in the Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895).

### Hundred Days of Reform (China, 1898)
A brief burst of ambitious reform under Emperor Guangxu and reformer Kang Youwei following the humiliating Sino-Japanese War. Proposed abolishing the outdated civil service exam, eliminating corruption, and establishing Western-style industrial, commercial, and medical systems. Ended abruptly by [[Concept Notes/Empress Cixi|Empress Cixi]]'s coup.

### [[Concept Notes/Empress Cixi|Empress Cixi]] (r. 1861–1908)
Conservative Qing empress dowager who became the dominant power in China. She ended the Hundred Days of Reform by staging a coup, imprisoning Emperor Guangxu, and repealing his reforms. She even blocked the extension of railroads and telegraphs into China's interior, fearing foreign influence. Represents the **conservative resistance** that hampered Chinese industrialization.

### "Sick Man of Europe"
Derogatory term for the [[Concept Notes/Ottoman Empire|Ottoman Empire]] in the 19th century, reflecting its economic weakness, military decline, and territorial losses. European powers — especially Russia — saw opportunity in Ottoman weakness to expand influence. The empire was eventually dismantled after WWI and replaced by the Republic of Turkey.

### Genro
Elder statesmen in Meiji Japan, often former samurai leaders from powerful domains (especially Satsuma and Choshu) who helped guide the Meiji government. Some former samurai transitioned into this elite advisory role rather than resisting modernization.

### Mamluks
Former Turkish slaves who formed Egypt's military ruling class for approximately 600 years before [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]] rose to power. Muhammad Ali displaced the Mamluks after being sent by the Ottoman sultan to retake Egypt in 1801.

### Charter Oath (1868)
The founding document of the [[Concept Notes/Meiji Restoration|Meiji Restoration]] that formally abolished feudalism and laid out the principles of the new Japanese state, including equality before the law, the abolition of cruel punishments, and openness to knowledge from around the world.

### Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895)
War between China and Japan over influence in Korea. Japan's decisive victory shocked the world (and China) and demonstrated how successful the [[Concept Notes/Meiji Restoration|Meiji Restoration]] had been. The defeat accelerated China's attempts at reform (Hundred Days of Reform) and showed the failure of the Self-Strengthening Movement.

### Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905)
War in which Japan defeated Russia — a European great power — for the first time in modern history, proving that an Asian nation had industrialized well enough to defeat Western rivals. Settled by the Treaty of Portsmouth (mediated by U.S. President Theodore Roosevelt).
