# Topic 5.6 — Practice & Review

Test yourself! These questions are written in AP World History exam style. (AMSCO pp. 317–321)

---

## Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** Which of the following best explains why Japan's [[Meiji Restoration]] was more successful at industrialization than China's Self-Strengthening Movement?

(A) Japan had access to more natural resources than China  
(B) Japan's central government implemented comprehensive institutional reform, while China's reform was blocked by conservative elites  
(C) China faced more significant foreign military pressure than Japan did  
(D) Japan received direct financial assistance from Western powers

> **Answer: B** — The Meiji government abolished feudalism, reformed education and the military, and built industrial infrastructure. China's Self-Strengthening Movement attempted only technological borrowing, and [[Empress Cixi]] crushed the Hundred Days of Reform. C is wrong — both faced serious Western pressure; in fact China's was arguably more severe. D is wrong — Japan financed its own industrialization primarily through agricultural taxes.

---

**2.** [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]]'s economic reforms most closely resemble which of the following?

(A) British laissez-faire capitalism, which relied on private entrepreneurs to drive growth  
(B) State-sponsored industrialization in Meiji Japan, in which the government directed economic development  
(C) The mercantile system of the 17th century, which relied on colonial extraction  
(D) The socialist programs of [[Karl Marx]], which sought collective ownership of production

> **Answer: B** — Both Muhammad Ali and the Meiji government used state power to build factories, fund infrastructure, and direct economic activity. A is wrong because Muhammad Ali's system was state-directed, not market-driven. C and D don't match the evidence.

---

**3.** The phrase "Sick Man of Europe" in the 19th century referred to the [[Ottoman Empire]]'s:

(A) High rate of epidemic disease among its civilian population  
(B) Military defeats against Napoleon Bonaparte  
(C) Political weakness, economic decline, and inability to resist Western pressure  
(D) Failure to adopt Christianity despite European missionary efforts

> **Answer: C** — The phrase reflected Ottoman political corruption, economic dependency, ethnic nationalist unrest, and territorial losses — not literal disease. B is wrong chronologically; Napoleon's Egyptian campaign (1798–1801) preceded the height of this phrase's usage.

---

**4.** Which of the following was a shared feature of Japan's [[Meiji Restoration]] and [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]]'s reforms?

(A) Both established constitutional republics modeled on the United States  
(B) Both used high taxes on the agricultural sector to fund industrialization  
(C) Both invited European colonial powers to administer their economies  
(D) Both abolished the monarchy and established representative governments

> **Answer: B** — Both Japan and Muhammad Ali's Egypt extracted revenue from agriculture (Japan's high agricultural tax; Muhammad Ali's control of cotton production) to fund industrial development. A and D are wrong — both maintained monarchical/authoritarian government. C is wrong — both explicitly aimed to *prevent* colonial domination.

---

**5.** The [[zaibatsu]] in Meiji Japan were most similar to which of the following in Western industrialization?

(A) Medieval guilds that regulated craft production  
(B) Labor unions that organized industrial workers  
(C) Large conglomerates or monopolies like Rockefeller's Standard Oil  
(D) Government-owned enterprises that monopolized state production

> **Answer: C** — [[zaibatsu]] were powerful family-owned business organizations that dominated multiple industries, similar to American conglomerates and monopolies. They were private, not government-owned (D), and were industrial enterprises, not guilds (A) or labor organizations (B).

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ Style)

**SAQ 1:**

Use your knowledge of world history to answer all parts of the question.

(a) Briefly describe ONE similarity between Japan's [[Meiji Restoration]] and [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]]'s modernization program.

(b) Briefly describe ONE difference between Japan's [[Meiji Restoration]] and [[Muhammad Ali of Egypt]]'s modernization program.

(c) Briefly explain ONE reason why Japan's industrialization was more successful in the long term than Egypt's.

> **Sample Answers:**
> (a) Both Japan and Egypt pursued **state-sponsored industrialization** — using government authority to build factories, reform the military on European models, and establish modern schools. Both also used heavy taxation of the agricultural sector to fund industrial development.
>
> (b) Japan's reforms were **more comprehensive and institutionally deep**. The Meiji government abolished feudalism, created a constitutional system, reformed the entire educational system, and built railroads nationwide. Muhammad Ali's reforms were more dependent on his personal authority and did not restructure Egypt's fundamental political institutions.
>
> (c) Japan was able to industrialize more fully because it **maintained political sovereignty** throughout the reform period, giving its government the freedom and resources to pursue long-term investment. Egypt, by contrast, faced ongoing Ottoman nominal control and ultimately fell under **British imperial domination** (1882), which redirected Egyptian resources to serve British interests rather than Egyptian development.

---

**SAQ 2:**

(a) Briefly describe ONE cause of China's failure to industrialize effectively in the 19th century.

(b) Briefly describe ONE reform China actually attempted during the 19th century.

(c) Briefly explain how the [[Opium War]] affected China's ability to industrialize.

> **Sample Answers:**
> (a) **Conservative resistance** within the Qing court — exemplified by [[Empress Cixi]]'s coup against the Hundred Days of Reform — blocked the comprehensive institutional changes necessary for effective industrialization. Cixi repealed reform edicts, imprisoned Emperor Guangxu, and blocked infrastructure development to protect traditional Qing power structures.
>
> (b) China's **Self-Strengthening Movement** (1860s–1890s) attempted to modernize China's military technology by building shipyards and arsenals, training artisans in modern techniques, and hiring French and British military advisors. China also established a modern diplomatic corps and customs service.
>
> (c) The [[Opium War]] severely weakened China's capacity for industrialization by **forcing it into unequal treaties** that opened Chinese markets to foreign goods, imposed financial indemnities, ceded territory (Hong Kong), and established foreign spheres of influence. These concessions drained Chinese resources and revenues, reduced central government authority, and created a semi-colonial dependency relationship with Western powers that made comprehensive industrial investment nearly impossible.

## Long Essay Question (LEQ) Prompts

**LEQ Option 1:** Evaluate the extent to which government policy was the most significant factor in determining the success or failure of industrialization efforts outside of Western Europe in the period 1800–1914.

**LEQ Option 2:** Compare the causes of Japan's successful industrialization with the causes of China's failed industrialization in the 19th century.

**LEQ Option 3:** To what extent did states outside of Western Europe adopt Western models of industrialization during the period 1800–1914?

> [!exam-tip]
> **Strong LEQ structure for these prompts:**
> - **Thesis**: Make a clear, defensible claim with a line of reasoning
> - **Evidence**: Use specific examples from at least 2 regions (Japan + China or Egypt; add Ottoman Empire for contrast)
> - **Reasoning**: Don't just list facts — explain *why* the evidence supports your claim
> - **Complexity**: Add a "however" — discuss a counterexample or complication (e.g., Japan succeeded but also replicated industrial exploitation of workers)
