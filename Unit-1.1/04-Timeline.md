# Unit 1.1: Timeline — East Asia (1200–1450)

> **Big Picture:** This chapter covers the final century of the Song Dynasty, the full arc of Mongol rule in China (Yuan), and the founding and early consolidation of the Ming Dynasty. East Asia in this period transitions from the world's most commercially dynamic civilization (Song) through foreign conquest (Yuan) to imperial restoration (Ming) — a cycle that both preserves and transforms Chinese institutions. These events unfold during the same period as the Mongol conquests that shaped the entire Eurasian world.

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 960 | Song Dynasty founded | China | Established the commercial, bureaucratic, and intellectual model that defined "classical" China |
| 1127 | Northern Song falls to Jurchen Jin Dynasty | North China | **Change:** Forces Song court south; Southern Song period begins; reflects vulnerability of agrarian states to steppe nomads |
| c. 1100s | Champa rice adopted widely | China | Causes population boom; fuels urbanization and the Song commercial revolution |
| c. 1100s | Foot binding spreads across classes | China | **Continuity:** Patriarchal control over women intensifies; persists until the 20th century |
| 1206 | Genghis Khan unifies Mongols | Central Asia | Sets in motion the conquests that will destroy the Jin and Song dynasties |
| 1234 | Mongols destroy the Jin Dynasty | North China | Opens the path to full Mongol conquest of China |
| 1271 | Kublai Khan establishes Yuan Dynasty | China | **Change:** First foreign dynasty to rule all of China; integrates China into Mongol empire |
| 1274 & 1281 | Failed Mongol invasions of Japan | Japan | Typhoons (*kamikaze*) destroy Mongol fleets; demonstrates limits of Mongol expansion |
| 1279 | Yuan conquers Southern Song | China | **Change:** End of Song Dynasty; Mongol control complete over all of China |
| c. 1275–1292 | Marco Polo travels through Yuan China | China / Eurasia | Transmits knowledge of Chinese wealth and technology to European audiences |
| c. 1330s–1350s | Black Death spreads from Central Asia | Eurasia | Carried along Mongol trade routes; devastates populations across Eurasia and weakens Yuan rule |
| 1368 | Zhu Yuanzhang founds Ming Dynasty | China | **Change:** End of Yuan; restoration of Han Chinese rule; reassertion of Confucian governance |
| 1381 | Ming completes census and land survey | China | **Continuity:** Ming uses traditional Confucian administrative tools to consolidate power |
| 1403 | Yongle Emperor moves capital to Beijing | China | Construction of the Forbidden City; reorientation of Ming toward northern defense |
| 1405–1433 | Zheng He's seven voyages | Indian Ocean | Largest naval expeditions in world history to that point; extend Ming tribute system |
| 1421 | Forbidden City completed in Beijing | China | Symbolizes Ming imperial grandeur and Confucian cosmic order |
| 1433 | Zheng He's voyages ended | China | **Change/Turning Point:** Confucian scholars convince court to end maritime expansion; China turns inward |
| c. 1440s | Ming reinforces Great Wall | North China | Response to continued Mongol threat; massive labor mobilization |
