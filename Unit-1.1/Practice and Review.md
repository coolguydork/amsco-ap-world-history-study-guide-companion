# Topic 1.1 — Practice & Review

## AP-Style Multiple Choice

**Question 1.** The Song Dynasty's civil service examination system is best described as an example of which historical development?

A) The elimination of social inequality in Chinese society  
B) A meritocratic system that increased upward mobility while still favoring the educated elite  
C) A purely hereditary aristocracy that excluded commoners from government  
D) A religious institution controlled by Buddhist monks

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> A is too strong — the system did not eliminate inequality. C is wrong — it was the *opposite* of hereditary. D is wrong — the exams tested Confucian, not Buddhist, knowledge. B captures the nuance: there was *more* mobility than elsewhere, but the system still favored those who could afford education.

---

**Question 2.** The spread of Champa rice to China during the Song Dynasty most directly resulted in:

A) The decline of trade along the Silk Roads  
B) Increased population growth and agricultural surplus  
C) The adoption of Buddhism by Chinese farmers  
D) The formation of the tributary system with Vietnam

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Champa rice allowed double-cropping and farming in new regions, producing food abundance → population growth. The others are unrelated or distorted.

---

**Question 3.** Japan's feudal system in the period c. 1200–c. 1450 differed from European feudalism primarily because:

A) Japan had no warrior class while Europe had knights  
B) Japanese daimyo held more practical power than European kings over their nobles  
C) Japan was unified under a strong central government while Europe was fragmented  
D) Japanese feudalism was based on religious authority rather than military service

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> In Japan, daimyo were often more powerful than the shogun or emperor in practice. In Europe, kings theoretically outranked nobles. A is wrong (samurai = warriors). C is wrong (Japan was *more* decentralized). D is wrong.

---

**Question 4.** Which of the following best explains why Vietnam's relationship with China differed from Korea's?

A) Vietnam had no contact with Chinese cultural or religious traditions  
B) Vietnam converted to Islam, which made it resistant to Chinese influence  
C) Vietnam had a history of violent resistance to Chinese occupation and distinct social traditions  
D) Vietnam was geographically isolated and could not receive Chinese influence

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Vietnam pushed out China's army as the Tang declined, practiced guerilla warfare, rejected foot binding and polygyny, and had women with more independence. A and D are factually wrong. B is false.

## Short-Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**SAQ Prompt:**
"Using specific evidence, explain how the Song Dynasty's policies contributed to China's economic growth in the period c. 1200–c. 1450."

**Strong Response Outline:**
- **Claim**: The Song Dynasty pursued deliberate agricultural, commercial, and infrastructural policies that made China the world's most commercialized economy.
- **Evidence 1**: Agricultural policy — promoted Champa rice, irrigation systems, fertilization → doubled crop yields and population growth (25% → 40% of world population)
- **Evidence 2**: Money economy — the government paid workers in cash instead of requiring labor on public projects, increasing the money supply and stimulating commerce
- **Evidence 3**: Infrastructure & technology — the Grand Canal enabled cheap bulk transport; the compass and printed navigation charts allowed maritime trade; proto-industrial production of porcelain, silk, and steel created export goods
- **Reasoning**: These policies worked together: agricultural surplus freed labor for manufacturing; cash payments stimulated markets; technology linked China to global trade networks

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Setup

The following types of sources commonly appear on AP exams for this topic:

- **Chinese government documents** about the civil service system (government perspective; may downplay inequality)
- **Travel accounts** by foreign visitors to Chinese cities (outsider perspective on wealth and urbanization)
- **Confucian texts** about social hierarchy and women's roles (elite male perspective)
- **Buddhist scripture** or monk accounts (religious lens)

> [!exam-tip]
> When analyzing documents about China, ask: **Who wrote this?** Chinese scholar-officials will emphasize Confucian order. Foreign visitors will emphasize wonder and wealth. Women's voices are almost entirely absent from the documentary record before 1450.

## Continuity and Change Over Time (CCOT) Frame

| Category | Continuity (1200–1450) | Change (1200–1450) |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Government | Imperial bureaucracy (since Qin, 221 BCE) | Bureaucracy grew → became expensive burden |
| Religion | Buddhism popular since Tang Dynasty | Neo-Confucianism emerged as synthesis |
| Economy | Agricultural base; silk/porcelain trade | Proto-industrialization; commercialization; money economy |
| Social structure | Confucian hierarchy (scholars above merchants) | Scholar gentry replaced hereditary aristocracy |
| Women | Patriarchal traditions | Foot binding intensified, restricting mobility further |
| Technology | China ahead of Europe | Gunpowder weapons developed; maritime tech improved |

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> **"China's civil service exam was open to everyone."** — False. It was open to more people than other systems, but the poor were *vastly underrepresented* because years of Confucian education were expensive. Women were excluded entirely.

> [!warning]
> **"Foot binding was always part of Chinese culture."** — False. It became common during the Song Dynasty among aristocratic families — it was a *change*, not a continuity, in women's status.

> [!warning]
> **"Japan fully adopted Chinese culture."** — False. Japan adopted selectively (Buddhism, writing, art, some Confucianism) while maintaining distinct institutions (Shinto, feudalism, the samurai class). The Tale of Genji is a perfect example of Japanese literary independence.
