# Unit 1.1: Developments in East Asia (1200–1450)

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 1 — The Global Tapestry
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (1200–1450)
**Key Region:** East Asia (China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam)

## Summary

Between 1200 and 1450, the Song Dynasty (960–1279) was the most commercially and technologically dynamic state in East Asia — and arguably the world. The Song refined the civil service examination system, deepened Neo-Confucian philosophy, and produced a commercial revolution: paper money, letters of credit, and urban market economies supported China's largest pre-modern population. Song technological output — woodblock printing, gunpowder weapons, the magnetic compass, and advanced ironworking — diffused westward along trade routes and would eventually enable European maritime expansion. East Asia functioned as a regional system rather than just "China": Korea (Goryeo), Japan (Kamakura shogunate), and Vietnam all adopted Confucian political culture and adapted Chinese institutions to local conditions. The Mongol Yuan Dynasty (1271–1368) disrupted Song governance but reconnected China to Eurasian trade networks, while the Ming Dynasty (1368–) restored Chinese administrative traditions. Zheng He's voyages (1405–1433) demonstrated extraordinary Chinese maritime capacity but are more centrally relevant to the Indian Ocean exchange network — see [[Unit-2.3/00-Overview|Unit 2.3: Exchange in the Indian Ocean]].

> [!exam-tip]
> AMSCO Topic 1.1 centers on **Song Dynasty** achievements and the **East Asian regional system** (China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam). Yuan and Ming are context and transition — do not let them crowd out Song-era content. Zheng He belongs primarily in Unit 2.3 (Indian Ocean Exchange).

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how and why states in East Asia developed and changed over time
- Explain the effects of innovation and exchange on the development of states in East Asia
- Explain how the Mongol conquests affected Eurasian trade and state building
- Analyze continuity and change in Chinese governance from the Song through the early Ming
