# Unit 1.1: Developments in East Asia (1200–1450)

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 1 — The Global Tapestry
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (1200–1450)
**Key Region:** East Asia (China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam)

## Summary

Between 1200 and 1450, East Asia was dominated by a succession of Chinese dynasties — the Song, Yuan (Mongol), and early Ming — each of which shaped governance, culture, and trade in distinct ways. The Song Dynasty refined Confucian bureaucracy and produced technological breakthroughs that spread across Eurasia, while the Mongol Yuan Dynasty temporarily disrupted and then reconnected China to wider trade networks. The Ming Dynasty, founded in 1368, reasserted Chinese imperial traditions, constructed the Great Wall, sponsored maritime expeditions under Zheng He, and developed a highly sophisticated tribute system that shaped relationships with neighboring states. Chinese innovations in papermaking, printing, gunpowder, and the compass had profound global consequences that echo throughout the entire AP World History course.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how and why states in East Asia developed and changed over time
- Explain the effects of innovation and exchange on the development of states in East Asia
- Explain how the Mongol conquests affected Eurasian trade and state building
- Analyze continuity and change in Chinese governance from the Song through the early Ming
