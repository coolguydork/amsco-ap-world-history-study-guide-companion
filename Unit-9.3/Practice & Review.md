## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Questions 1–3 refer to an image (not reproduced here — the image depicts global environmental cooperation themes)*

**1. Which of the following best describes the point of view of the creator of the image?**

(A) Scientists have the primary responsibility to analyze global environmental issues.
(B) People from around the world need to work together to protect the earth from environmental damage.
(C) The impacts of climate change cannot be reversed by human action.
(D) People competing for scarce resources are causing conflicts among countries.

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The image depicts global cooperation on environmental issues. Option B reflects the chapter's core argument (Ban Ki-moon's opening quote: "global challenges require global solidarity"). A is too narrow (scientists only); C contradicts the entire chapter's argument that human action can reduce emissions; D describes resource competition but that's not the environmental protection message.

**2. Which of the following best explains the relationship of developed and developing countries in regard to climate change?**

(A) Developing countries in Africa emit more greenhouse gases per person than developed countries because they are more agricultural.
(B) Developing countries are more vulnerable to climate change than developed countries because they have fewer resources to fight it.
(C) Developing countries have more influence in international affairs to combat climate change than developing countries have.
(D) Developing and developed countries have agreed to allow China and the United States to continue emissions at current levels.

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Developing countries emit less per capita but have fewer financial and institutional resources to adapt to climate disasters (flooding, drought, crop failure). A is false — developed industrial nations emit far more per capita. C is nonsensical (both clauses say "developing"). D contradicts the Paris Agreement, which required both China and the U.S. to commit to reductions.

**3. Which of the following developments accelerated global awareness of climate change in the late 20th century?**

(A) The increasing population of developing countries
(B) The use of pesticides during the Green Revolution
(C) The development of nuclear power plants to produce electricity
(D) The growth of the information sector of the global economy

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> The information revolution — internet, 24-hour news, global data sharing — allowed climate scientists (James Hansen, IPCC) to disseminate findings globally and enabled activists (Thunberg) to organize internationally. A (population growth) is a cause of environmental damage, not awareness. B (pesticides) links to Rachel Carson's era (1960s), not late 20th century awareness. C (nuclear power) relates to energy concerns, not climate awareness specifically.

## Short-Answer Questions

### SAQ 1 — Paris Agreement Primary Source

*Use the following excerpts from the Paris Agreement (December 2015):*

- **Art. 2**: "The Paris Agreement… reaffirms the goal of limiting global temperature increase to well below 2 degrees Celsius, while pursuing efforts to limit the increase to 1.5 degrees."
- **Art. 4**: "Parties aim to reach global peaking of greenhouse gas emissions as soon as possible… so as to achieve a balance between… emissions… and removals… in the second half of the century."
- **Art. 5**: "The Paris Agreement also encourages Parties to conserve and enhance… sinks and reservoirs of GHGs… including forests."
- **Art. 12**: "Climate change education, training, public awareness, public participation, and public access to information is also to be enhanced."

**(A) Identify ONE environmental change that influenced the Paris Agreement.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> Global warming — the rise in Earth's average temperature caused by the accumulation of greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels — was the primary environmental change that influenced the Paris Agreement. By 2015, scientific data from the IPCC showed that temperatures had already risen approximately 1°C above pre-industrial levels, with accelerating effects on sea levels, storm intensity, and drought frequency.

**(B) Explain the context in which the Paris Agreement was developed.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain Context
> The Paris Agreement (2015) was developed in the context of two decades of failed or limited climate diplomacy. The Kyoto Protocol (1997) had been undermined when the United States — the world's largest historical emitter — refused to ratify it, and developing nations including China and India were exempt from its binding limits. By 2015, China had surpassed the U.S. as the world's largest total emitter, making any agreement without developing nations meaningless. The Paris Agreement emerged as a compromise: near-universal participation (195 nations) in exchange for voluntary national targets rather than binding quotas. Youth activism and increasing evidence of climate damage (Superstorm Sandy 2012, record heat years, coral reef die-offs) created political pressure for a stronger commitment.

**(C) Explain the significance of the Paris Agreement in light of the environmental debates after 1900.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain Significance
> The Paris Agreement was significant because it represented the first near-universal international commitment to limit global warming, resolving — at least partially — the core tension that had undermined previous agreements: the divide between developed and developing nations over responsibility. Unlike Kyoto (1997), which excluded developing nations and was never ratified by the U.S., Paris included both the United States and China, the world's two largest emitters. However, its significance was limited by its voluntary nature (national targets were self-set) and its political fragility, demonstrated when the U.S. withdrew in 2017 under President Trump. In the broader context of environmental debates since 1900, Paris showed that international climate governance was possible but remained vulnerable to domestic political shifts.

### SAQ 2 — Climate Change Debate

**(A) Identify ONE position in the debate about the nature and causes of climate change.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> One position held by the scientific consensus, backed by the UN's Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, is that human-caused greenhouse gas emissions from burning fossil fuels are the primary driver of global warming and that without significant reductions, catastrophic consequences — rising sea levels, intensifying storms, and severe droughts — are inevitable.

**(B) Explain ONE way in which human competition over resources affected global interactions from 1900 to the present.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> Competition over fossil fuel resources — especially petroleum — profoundly shaped global interactions from 1900 to the present. Oil's importance as an industrial energy source made control of petroleum reserves a central objective of great-power competition. This drove Western intervention in the Middle East (including the 1953 Iran coup and support for oil-rich authoritarian governments), contributed to World War II in the Pacific (Japan's need for oil supplies in Southeast Asia), and shaped Cold War alliance structures. The 1973 OPEC oil embargo demonstrated that oil-exporting developing nations could leverage resource control against industrialized powers, reshaping the global economic order.

**(C) Explain ONE way in which population growth affected the environment after 1900.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> Population growth drove massive deforestation after 1900, particularly in tropical regions. As the world population grew from 1.6 billion in 1900 to over 6 billion by 2000, the demand for agricultural land expanded dramatically. Forests were cleared for cropland and cattle ranching, especially in Latin America, Southeast Asia, and Sub-Saharan Africa. This deforestation had compounding environmental effects: it reduced carbon sinks (trees that absorb CO₂), increased soil erosion, destroyed biodiversity, and contributed to the greenhouse gas accumulation driving global warming — creating a feedback loop between population pressure, land use change, and climate change.

## Key Comparisons for Exam Essays

| | Kyoto Protocol (1997) | Paris Agreement (2015) |
|--|--|--|
| Scope | Industrialized nations only | 195 nations (near-universal) |
| Developing nations | Exempt from binding limits | Included with voluntary targets |
| U.S. participation | Never ratified | Signed; withdrew 2017; re-entered 2021 |
| Binding? | Yes (for ratifying parties) | Voluntary national targets |
| Outcome | Limited impact | More comprehensive; politically fragile |

| | Developed Nations | Developing Nations |
|--|--|--|
| Total emissions | High (historically) | Growing rapidly (China now #1 total) |
| Per-capita emissions | Very high | Much lower |
| Climate vulnerability | Moderate (resources to adapt) | High (fewer resources) |
| Position on responsibility | "Everyone must cut now" | "You industrialized first; lead the cuts" |
