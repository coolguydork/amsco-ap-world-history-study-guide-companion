# Timeline & Chronology

**AP Period 4** | Topic 9.3: Technology and the Environment | Printed pp. 649–656

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1848 | Marx publishes *Communist Manifesto* | Predicted capitalist overproduction would drive global resource extraction — later cited as predictive of environmental harm from globalization |
| 1900 | World population reaches 1.6 billion | Baseline for understanding population-driven environmental pressure; quadruples to 6.12B by 2000 |
| 1962 | Rachel Carson publishes *Silent Spring* | Launched modern environmental movement; documented pesticide devastation of ecosystems; led to DDT ban (1972) |
| 1968 | Club of Rome founded (Europe) | First major transnational body warning that resource depletion would limit economic growth |
| c. 1960s–present | Deforestation, ozone depletion, pollution intensify globally | Industrialization + population growth drive unprecedented environmental damage |
| 1972 | UN Conference on the Human Environment (Stockholm) | First major international environmental conference; beginning of global environmental movement; same year DDT banned in U.S. |
| 1977 | Wangari Maathai founds Green Belt Movement (Kenya) | Grassroots environmental-women's empowerment model; eventually plants 51M+ trees |
| 1986 | Chernobyl nuclear disaster (USSR) | Demonstrated catastrophic risks of nuclear energy technology; accelerated anti-nuclear movement globally |
| 1987 | Brundtland Commission defines "sustainable development" | UN commission coins concept: "meeting present needs without compromising future generations" — foundational for all subsequent climate policy |
| 1988 | James Hansen testifies before U.S. Senate on global warming | Brought climate science into mainstream political debate; helped build momentum for international agreements |
| 1992 | Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro; UN Framework Convention on Climate Change | First global framework for addressing climate change; established principle of "common but differentiated responsibilities" (developed vs. developing) |
| 1997 | Kyoto Protocol signed | First binding international agreement to cut greenhouse gas emissions; U.S. never ratified; developing nations (China, India) exempt |
| 2004 | Wangari Maathai awarded Nobel Peace Prize | First African woman Nobel laureate; UN recognized climate activism as a peace/security issue |
| 2007 | China surpasses U.S. as world's largest CO₂ emitter | Signals shift of industrial production — and emissions — to developing world |
| 2015 | Paris Agreement signed (195 nations) | Near-universal climate accord; includes developing nations; voluntary national targets; U.S. and China both sign |
| 2015 | UN adopts 17 Sustainable Development Goals | Global framework addressing poverty, inequality, and climate by 2030 |
| 2017 | President Trump announces U.S. withdrawal from Paris Agreement | Signals political fragility of international climate cooperation; Biden re-enters 2021 |
| 2018 | Greta Thunberg begins climate strikes (Sweden) | Youth-led global movement; 1.6M+ participants in 125+ countries |
| 2018 | Extinction Rebellion founded (UK) | Radical civil disobedience; 1,000+ arrests; UK Parliament convenes citizens' assembly on climate |
| 2019 | Panel of scientists votes to approve "Anthropocene" as name for current epoch | Scientific recognition that human activity has fundamentally altered Earth's geological history |

## Cause-Effect Chain Summary

**Population growth** (1900–2000: 1.6B → 6.12B)
→ demand for more cropland → **deforestation** + soil erosion
→ demand for more freshwater → **water scarcity** (1B+ without clean water)
→ demand for more energy → increased burning of **fossil fuels**
→ emissions of **greenhouse gases** → atmospheric heat trapping
→ **global warming** → rising seas, severe storms, droughts
→ international response: **Kyoto (1997)** → limited; **Paris (2015)** → broader but fragile

**Industrialization spreading globally** (especially post-Cold War)
→ manufacturing + middle-class growth in developing nations
→ increased resource extraction + consumption
→ air quality decline + accelerated emissions
→ developing-vs.-developed tension over responsibility
