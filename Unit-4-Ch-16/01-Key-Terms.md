# Key Terms — Chapter 16 (4.3): The Columbian Exchange

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

1. **Columbian Exchange** — The transfer of crops, animals, diseases, and peoples between the Eastern and Western Hemispheres following Columbus's 1492 voyage. (Most transformative biological event in recorded history; reshaped global demography and ecology.)

2. **Epidemic disease** — Infectious illness that spreads rapidly through a population with no prior exposure or immunity. (Smallpox, measles, and typhus killed 50–90% of Indigenous Americans — the primary driver of demographic collapse.)

3. **Demographic collapse** — The catastrophic decline of a population, used specifically to describe the 50–90% loss of Indigenous American population after European contact. (Created the labor vacuum that accelerated the Atlantic slave trade.)

4. **Virgin soil epidemic** — An epidemic in which an entire population has no prior immunity to a pathogen. (The Americas experienced multiple simultaneous virgin soil epidemics; mortality rates far exceeded those of the Black Death in Europe.)

5. **Smallpox** — A highly contagious and often fatal viral disease; the most devastating pathogen introduced to the Americas from Europe. (Arrived before many Europeans did, spreading ahead of Spanish conquistadors and killing populations before military contact.)

6. **Potato** — An Andean crop transferred to Europe and Asia via the Columbian Exchange. (Became a staple food in Ireland, Germany, Poland, and China, contributing to significant population growth in the 17th–18th centuries.)

7. **Maize (corn)** — A North and Mesoamerican crop that spread to Africa, Europe, and Asia. (Became a staple food crop across Sub-Saharan Africa; contributed to population growth and, indirectly, to the demographic base from which the slave trade drew.)

8. **Sweet potato** — An American crop that spread rapidly to China and Southeast Asia after 1492. (Contributed to China's population doubling between 1650 and 1850; grown in marginal soils unsuitable for rice or wheat.)

9. **Tomato** — A Mesoamerican crop adopted widely in Mediterranean Europe and later globally. (Transformed Italian, Spanish, and Mediterranean cuisines; a major dietary staple by the 18th century.)

10. **Tobacco** — An American crop adopted widely in Europe, Africa, and Asia as a luxury/recreational substance. (Created the foundation for plantation agriculture in Virginia and the Caribbean; major driver of demand for enslaved labor.)

11. **Cacao** — A Mesoamerican crop producing chocolate, adopted by Europeans as a luxury beverage. (Became enormously popular among European elites; plantation production in the Caribbean and West Africa later used enslaved labor.)

12. **Horse** — A European animal reintroduced to the Americas (horses had gone extinct there ~10,000 BCE) via the Columbian Exchange. (Transformed Indigenous societies on the Great Plains, enabling the Comanche, Lakota, and others to adopt mobile bison-hunting cultures.)

13. **Cattle / livestock** — European domesticated animals (cattle, pigs, sheep, goats) introduced to the Americas. (Spread rapidly across the Americas; disrupted Indigenous agricultural systems and altered landscapes through overgrazing.)

14. **Price Revolution** — Sustained inflation in 16th–17th century Europe caused partly by the influx of American silver. (Destabilized wages and prices; benefited merchants and hurt those on fixed incomes — an indirect consequence of the Columbian Exchange.)

15. **Biological exchange** — The broader concept of organisms (diseases, plants, animals, microbes) moving between ecosystems as a result of human contact. (The Columbian Exchange is the largest and most consequential biological exchange in recorded history.)

> [!exam-tip]
> For AP essays, always specify the **direction** of exchange: diseases moved *from* Eurasia *to* the Americas (devastating effect); crops moved *from* the Americas *to* Eurasia and Africa (population growth). Students who confuse the direction lose points.
