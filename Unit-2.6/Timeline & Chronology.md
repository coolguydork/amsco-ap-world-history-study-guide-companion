# Timeline & Chronology

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam rarely asks you to memorize exact dates for this topic — but you must know the **sequence** and approximate **century**: crop diffusion is ongoing c. 1000–1300; environmental degradation accelerates c. 1200–1450; the [[Concept Notes/Black Death\|Black Death]] peaks 1347–1351. Knowing these anchors lets you contextualize any primary source.

## Chronological Overview

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| c. 1000 CE | Champa rice introduced to China from Vietnam | Drought- and flood-resistant; yields two crops/year; drives Chinese population boom and southward urban migration |
| c. 1100s | Bananas introduced to sub-Saharan Africa by Indonesian seafarers | Nutrition boost; enables Bantu migration into non-yam zones; Indonesians settle Madagascar |
| c. 1200s | Sugar, cotton, and citrus spread via Islamic trade networks | New crops transform agriculture and diet across Mediterranean and beyond |
| c. 1200–1450 | Ongoing deforestation and soil erosion in feudal Europe | Agricultural overuse degrades land; population pressure exceeds sustainable limits |
| c. 1300 | Little Ice Age begins (c. 1300–c. 1800) | Reduces agricultural output; compounds famine and population stress in Europe |
| c. 1300–1450 | Overgrazing causes abandonment of Great Zimbabwe (late 1400s) | Environmental degradation ends East Africa's most powerful kingdom; see [[Unit-1.5/Africa Overview\|Topic 1.5]] |
| 1332–1347 | Black Death kills ~25 million in Asia | Plague originates in southern China; spreads along Mongol trade corridors into Central Asia |
| 1347 | Black Death arrives in Europe via Silk Roads and sea routes | Enters through Caffa (Black Sea) and Mediterranean ports (Sicily, Genoa, Marseille) |
| 1347–1351 | Black Death kills ~1/3 of European population | 75–200 million dead across Eurasia; labor scarcity raises wages; feudalism weakens |
| c. 1400s | Great Zimbabwe abandoned due to overgrazing | Population forced to relocate; political collapse of a major East African state |

## The Black Death's Path (1346–1353)

The plague spread in roughly annual waves westward across Europe, following existing trade routes:

1. **1346** — Caffa (Crimea) via Mongol-controlled steppe corridors
2. **1347** — Constantinople, Sicily, Italy (Genoa, Venice, Florence)
3. **1348** — France (Avignon, Paris), Iberia (Lisbon, Madrid, Barcelona)
4. **1349** — England, Holy Roman Empire (Vienna, Frankfurt)
5. **1350–1351** — Scandinavia (Bergen), Eastern Europe (Prague, Warsaw)
6. **1352–1353** — Russia (Moscow, Pskov)

Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia were largely spared because they had fewer integrated trading ports — connectivity determined mortality as much as population density.

## Cause-and-Effect Chain (memorize this)

```
Trade connectivity (Silk Roads + Indian Ocean)
    ↓
Agricultural diffusion (Champa rice, bananas, citrus)
    ↓
Population growth + expanded land use
    ↓
Environmental degradation (overgrazing, deforestation, soil erosion)
    ↓
Population vulnerability (+ Little Ice Age reduces food supply)
    ↓
Disease transmission along trade corridors (Black Death)
    ↓
Demographic collapse → labor scarcity → higher wages → feudal decline
```

> [!tip]
> This chain is the "cycle of cause and effect" AMSCO's Think As a Historian section asks you to analyze. It recurs across AP World History at different scales — compare to the [[Concept Notes/Silk Roads|Columbian Exchange]] [[Unit-4.3/Columbian Exchange Overview|Columbian Exchange Overview]] and the Industrial Revolution's environmental consequences ([[Unit-9.8/Institutions Developing in a Globalized World Overview|Topic 9.8]]).
