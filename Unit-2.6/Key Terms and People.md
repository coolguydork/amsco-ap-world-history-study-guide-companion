# Topic 2.6 — Key Terms and People

## Disease

**Bubonic plague (Black Death)** — Bacterial disease (*Yersinia pestis*) spread by fleas on rodents; originated in southern China or Central Asia; transmitted along Mongol trade and conquest routes; arrived in Europe in 1347; killed an estimated 75–200 million people across Eurasia. Peak in Europe: 1347–1351. Killed approximately one-third of Europe's population. Long-term consequences: labor scarcity → higher wages → weakening of feudalism.

**Giovanni Boccaccio (1313–1375)** — Italian writer who survived the Black Death in Florence; his masterwork *The Decameron* was inspired by the plague. Provides one of the most vivid firsthand accounts of the epidemic's horror: *"Dead bodies filled every corner."* A valuable primary source for the AP exam's document analysis questions.

## Crop Diffusion

**Champa rice** — Quick-ripening, drought-resistant, flood-resistant rice introduced to China from Vietnam (originally possibly from India). Enabled double-cropping (two harvests per year), fed China's growing population, and reshaped land use — people farmed areas previously considered unsuitable for rice. Also drove southward migration and urban growth in China.

**Bananas** — Introduced to Sub-Saharan Africa by Indonesian seafarers via Indian Ocean routes. Nutrition-rich; contributed to population growth and enabled Bantu-speaking peoples to migrate beyond the range of traditional yam crops.

**Sugar** — Its cultivation spread as Islamic caliphates expanded; European demand for sugar grew steadily through the trade networks. By the 1500s, this demand would become a major driver of Atlantic slave labor in the Americas — a long-term consequence of crop diffusion via medieval trade.

**Citrus crops** — Spread through Islamic caliphate expansion; arrived in Europe via Samarkand's markets and trans-Mediterranean trade. Added to European diets and generated new agricultural practices.

## Environmental Degradation

**Overgrazing** — When too many animals graze land, they strip vegetation, compact soil, and cause erosion. Great Zimbabwe (southern Africa) was abandoned in the late 1400s partly due to overgrazing around the city.

**Deforestation** — The clearing of forests for farmland; led to soil erosion in feudal Europe, reducing agricultural productivity. Combined with the Little Ice Age to weaken Europe's food supply before the plague hit.

**Soil erosion** — The loss of topsoil from deforestation and overuse of farmland; a long-term consequence of agricultural intensification driven by population growth and trade demands.

**Little Ice Age (c. 1300–c. 1800)** — A period of sustained cooler global temperatures that reduced agricultural yields. Overlapped with the Black Death to create a prolonged crisis in European food production and population.

## The Plague's Path: Summary Timeline

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| **1330s** | Outbreaks in China; tens of millions die |
| **1332–1347** | ~25 million Chinese and other Asians die |
| **1347** | Plague arrives in Europe via Black Sea trading ships; reaches Sicily |
| **1347–1351** | Peak mortality in Europe; ~one-third of population dies |
| **1346, 1349** | Plague kills approximately half of Constantinople's population |
| **Ongoing** | Additional outbreaks throughout the rest of the 14th century |

> [!exam-tip]
> Know that South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa were largely **spared** from the worst of the Black Death — because those regions had fewer trading ports connecting them to the primary plague transmission routes. This is useful comparative evidence.

## Cause and Effect Chain

> [!tip] The AP Chain: Black Death → Feudalism's Decline
>
> **Trade routes** expanded under the Mongols
> → Plague traveled the same routes
> → Europe lost one-third of its population
> → Severe labor scarcity
> → Workers could demand higher wages
> → Feudal lords lost power to compel cheap serf labor
> → Foundation of new economic relationships (proto-capitalism)
> → Long-term decline of the feudal system

This chain is exactly the kind of multi-step causation that AP free-response questions reward.
