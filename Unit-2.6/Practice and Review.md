# Topic 2.6 — Practice and Review

## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

The introduction of Champa rice to China is historically significant primarily because it:

A) Replaced Chinese silk as the most important export commodity on the Silk Roads  
B) Enabled double-cropping, supported population growth, and changed land use patterns by allowing farming in previously unusable areas  
C) Demonstrated that all major agricultural innovations originated outside China  
D) Led to the abandonment of traditional Chinese farming methods entirely

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Champa rice's drought- and flood-resistance plus its ability to yield two harvests per year are what made it transformative. These features fed more people, drove population growth, and enabled farmers to cultivate land previously considered too poor for rice. A is wrong — silk remained China's key export. C is an overgeneralization. D is wrong — Chinese farmers added Champa rice to their repertoire; they didn't abandon all previous methods.

**Question 2**

The Black Death's most significant long-term economic consequence in Europe was:

A) The complete collapse of European trade networks for the next century  
B) The rapid growth of European cities as survivors moved to urban centers for safety  
C) The creation of labor scarcity that allowed surviving workers to demand higher wages, weakening the feudal system  
D) The strengthening of the Catholic Church's authority as people turned to religion in times of crisis

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> This is the key causal chain: plague → one-third of population dies → severe labor scarcity → workers have leverage → higher wages → feudal system undermined. A is wrong — trade eventually recovered. B is wrong — people fled cities during the plague. D is the opposite — the Church's authority was weakened when its prayers failed to stop the epidemic.

**Question 3**

Which of the following best explains why Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia were largely spared from the Black Death?

A) Their populations had developed immunity to the plague bacteria  
B) Islamic scholars in those regions had discovered treatments for the disease  
C) Both regions had relatively few trading ports connecting them to the primary routes along which the plague spread  
D) The Mongol Empire never extended into those regions, so the plague transmission mechanism was absent

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> AMSCO explicitly states that "South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa were spared because there were few trading ports in those regions." The plague followed trading routes — where those routes didn't penetrate deeply, the plague didn't either. A is not supported by evidence. B is historically inaccurate. D is partially true for Sub-Saharan Africa but South Asia had significant Mongol-adjacent contact.

## SAQ Practice

**SAQ Prompt:**

> *"Dead bodies filled every corner."*
> — Giovanni Boccaccio, *The Decameron*, written during the Black Death in Florence (c. 1350)

**Part A**: Briefly explain ONE way in which the expansion of trade networks between c. 1200 and c. 1450 contributed to the spread of the bubonic plague.

**Part B**: Briefly explain ONE long-term economic consequence of the Black Death in Europe.

**Part C**: Briefly explain ONE agricultural crop that spread through trade networks during c. 1200–c. 1450 and explain its significance.

> [!tip] Model Responses
> **Part A**: The Mongol Empire revitalized the Silk Roads and created a vast, connected trade network across Eurasia during the Pax Mongolica. Caravans traveled regularly from China to Persia and beyond, carrying infected rodents and the fleas that transmitted bubonic plague. The caravanserai — inns where merchants and their animals stayed together — may have been key transmission points, concentrating humans and infected animals in confined spaces. The same infrastructure designed to make trade safer and more efficient inadvertently accelerated the pandemic.
>
> **Part B**: The Black Death killed approximately one-third of Europe's population, creating severe labor scarcity. With fewer workers available, surviving peasants and laborers could demand higher wages and better conditions. This shift undermined the feudal lords' ability to compel cheap serf labor — a key foundation of the feudal economic system. Over time, this helped erode feudalism and laid groundwork for more market-based labor relationships.
>
> **Part C**: Champa rice, a quick-ripening and drought-resistant strain from present-day Vietnam, spread to China where it was distributed broadly to meet the needs of the growing population. Because it could yield two harvests per year and grow in areas previously unusable for rice cultivation, it dramatically increased China's agricultural output, supported rapid population growth, and altered land use patterns across the country.

## Connecting Environmental History to the Bigger Picture

**The unintended consequences of trade:**

| Trade Feature | Intended Purpose | Unintended Environmental Consequence |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Caravanserai | Rest stops for merchants and animals | Transmission points for plague |
| Mongol road network | Safe, fast trade routes | Highway for plague movement |
| Expanding maritime trade | Commercial exchange | Introduction of plague to new regions via ship rats |
| Crop diffusion (Champa rice, bananas) | Food security, population growth | Population growth → overgrazing → environmental degradation |
| Sugar crop spread | Commercial crop | European demand → Atlantic slave labor (after 1500) |

This pattern — technology/trade enabling unintended negative consequences — is a recurring AP theme. The printing press spreads religious controversy; the Green Revolution creates soil depletion; the internet enables misinformation. The Black Death following Silk Roads trade routes is an early and dramatic example.

## CCOT Prompt

**"Describe how the environmental consequences of trade networks in Afro-Eurasia changed between c. 500 CE and c. 1450 CE."**

**Before 1200**: Crop diffusion occurred (Champa rice was already spreading) but at smaller scale; no catastrophic pandemic.

**After 1200**: The scale of trade expansion under the Mongols dramatically amplified both positive (more extensive crop diffusion) and negative (plague transmission) environmental consequences. The Black Death represents a qualitative break — something unprecedented in scale and demographic consequence.

**Key change**: The creation of a unified, connected trade network across Eurasia for the first time created the conditions for a truly global pandemic. Before the Mongols, no single political entity controlled enough of Eurasia to create such a continuous transmission pathway. The Pax Mongolica that revitalized trade also created the conditions for catastrophe.
