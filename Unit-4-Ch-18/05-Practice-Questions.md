## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below for Questions 1–3.**

> *"I was soon put down under the decks, and there I received such a salutation in my nostrils as I had never experienced in my life: so that with the loathsomeness of the stench, and crying together, I became so sick and low that I was not able to eat… I was now persuaded that I had gotten into a world of bad spirits, and that they were going to kill me."*
> — Olaudah Equiano, *The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano*, 1789

---

**1.** The passage most directly illustrates which of the following aspects of the Atlantic slave trade?

- A) The economic rationale that made the slave trade profitable for European merchants
- B) The brutal physical conditions of the Middle Passage experienced by enslaved Africans
- C) The resistance strategies employed by enslaved Africans during the transatlantic voyage
- D) The legal framework that European states used to justify enslaving Africans

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Equiano describes the horrific conditions below deck — stench, sickness, despair — which directly illustrate the Middle Passage experience.*

---

**2.** A historian analyzing Equiano's account would most likely note which of the following limitations?

- A) Equiano was not personally present during the Middle Passage he describes
- B) Equiano had an abolitionist agenda that may have led him to emphasize the worst conditions
- C) Equiano's account was written too long after the events to be accurate
- D) Equiano was not of African descent and could not accurately represent enslaved peoples' experiences

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Equiano wrote his narrative as an abolitionist argument; while his experiences were real, AP historians note that his explicit goal of persuading readers to oppose slavery shapes his emphasis and language.*

---

**3.** Which of the following best explains why the Atlantic slave trade expanded dramatically in the 17th and 18th centuries?

- A) European demand for silver required more enslaved African miners in the Americas
- B) The expansion of sugar, tobacco, and other plantation crops created enormous demand for agricultural labor
- C) The decline of Indigenous American populations made enslaved Africans the only available workers
- D) African rulers forced European merchants to purchase enslaved captives against their will

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The plantation complex — especially sugar in the Caribbean and Brazil — was the primary driver of the slave trade's expansion. Sugar required enormous amounts of labor in brutal conditions that killed workers quickly, creating constant demand.*

---

**4.** The founding of the VOC in 1602 most directly represents which of the following historical developments?

- A) The decline of Portuguese commercial power in the Indian Ocean
- B) The emergence of a new form of capitalist enterprise blurring state and private commercial power
- C) Dutch territorial conquest of Southeast Asian islands and their populations
- D) European states pooling resources to resist the Ottoman Empire's commercial expansion

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The VOC was a genuinely new institutional form — a joint-stock company with quasi-governmental powers (army, navy, treaty-making authority), representing a new phase of commercial capitalism.*

---

**5.** The global silver trade of the 16th and 17th centuries is best described as an example of:

- A) A bilateral trade relationship between Spain and China
- B) A truly global economic circuit connecting the Americas, Europe, and Asia
- C) A mercantilist policy that benefited all participating regions equally
- D) The decline of the Indian Ocean trade network in favor of Atlantic commerce

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*American silver → Spain → Europe → China; simultaneously, Manila Galleon silver → China directly. This circuit connected the Americas, Europe, and Asia in a single economic system for the first time.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain how the Atlantic slave trade was both driven by and contributed to the development of maritime empires in the period 1500–1750."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE economic factor that drove the expansion of the Atlantic slave trade.
**Part B:** Explain ONE way the slave trade contributed to the development of European maritime empires.
**Part C:** Explain ONE consequence of the slave trade for African societies.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Plantation agriculture (sugar, tobacco) required massive, cheap labor; Indigenous population collapse created the initial vacuum.
> - **Part B:** Slave trade profits funded further imperial expansion; plantation wealth financed European industry and commerce.
> - **Part C:** Population loss, political destabilization, warfare between African polities raiding for captives.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Evaluate the extent to which African rulers were active agents in the Atlantic slave trade."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE way African rulers participated in the slave trade.
**Part B:** Describe ONE way African societies were harmed by the slave trade despite (or because of) ruler participation.
**Part C:** Explain whether African rulers' participation makes them equally responsible for the trade alongside European powers.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Dahomey, Ashanti sold war captives; Kongo initially cooperated with Portugal.
> - **Part B:** Depopulation, political instability, internal warfare driven by demand for captives.
> - **Part C:** Strong answers note *structural inequality*: European demand created the market; African rulers operated within a system they didn't design. Agency existed, but within severe constraints.

## DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which economic factors explain the development of the Atlantic slave trade in the period 1500–1750."**

> [!tip] DBQ Outline Guidance
> **Thesis:** Acknowledge economic factors as central while noting political (imperial competition, asiento) and ideological (racial justification) factors as well.
>
> **Grouping strategy:** Group documents by: (1) economic demand (plantation expansion, labor shortage); (2) political/legal frameworks; (3) African perspectives on participation; (4) humanitarian critiques.
>
> **Complexity argument:** The slave trade was economically *driven* but ideologically *justified* by emerging racial categories — race and economy reinforced each other. Neither fully explains the trade without the other.
