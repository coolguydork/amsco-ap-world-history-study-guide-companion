# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 7.3 — Conducting World War I | AMSCO pp. 469–479**

## Key Dates

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| Aug 1914 | WWI begins; European powers mobilize | Alliance systems activate within weeks of Franz Ferdinand's assassination |
| 1914–1918 | Trench warfare dominates Western Front | Stalemate produced by defensive technology (machine guns, barbed wire) outpacing offensive tactics |
| Apr–Dec 1915 | Gallipoli Campaign (ANZAC & Allied forces) | Failed attempt to knock Ottomans out of war; ~130,000 Allied deaths; defines ANZAC identity |
| May 7, 1915 | Germany sinks RMS *Lusitania* | 1,198 killed including ~128 Americans; turns U.S. public opinion against Germany |
| 1916 | Battles of Verdun and the Somme | Over 1 million casualties at Verdun alone; epitomizes industrialized mass death; British debut tanks at the Somme |
| 1917 | Germany resumes unrestricted submarine warfare | Key cause of U.S. entry into WWI |
| Jan 1917 | Zimmermann Telegram intercepted | Germany offers Mexico U.S. territory; final catalyst for U.S. declaration of war |
| Apr 6, 1917 | United States enters WWI | Fresh troops and resources tip the balance toward the Allies |
| Nov 11, 1918 | Armistice ends WWI | Germany surrenders; ~17 million dead; Weimar Republic established |
| Jan–Jun 1919 | Paris Peace Conference | Big Four negotiate postwar settlement; Wilson's idealism vs. Clemenceau's punishment |
| Jun 28, 1919 | Treaty of Versailles signed | Reparations, war guilt clause, German disarmament, loss of colonies; seeds of WWII planted |
| 1919 | New nations created in Europe | Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia emerge from collapsed empires |
| Nov 19, 1919 | U.S. Senate rejects Treaty of Versailles | U.S. never joins League of Nations; League fatally weakened from the start |
| 1929 | *All Quiet on the Western Front* published | Remarque's anti-war novel captures "Lost Generation" disillusionment |

## Chronological Context

**Before 7.3** (from Topic 7.2): The war was triggered by the assassination of Franz Ferdinand (June 1914) and the activation of European alliance systems. The mobilization of millions of conscript soldiers within weeks of the assassination demonstrates how the pre-1914 military planning had anticipated exactly this scenario.

**During WWI (1914–1918):** The war's character shifted dramatically from the expected quick campaign to four years of industrial stalemate. By 1915 all major powers were engaged in total war mobilization. The entry of colonial troops from 1914 onward made it a genuinely global conflict from the start, not just after 1917.

**After 7.3** (leads to Topics 7.4 and 7.5): The economic devastation of the war and the reparations burden destabilized European economies through the 1920s. Germany's hyperinflation peaked in 1923. The unresolved tensions of the Paris Peace Conference — broken promises to colonial peoples, Italian resentment, German humiliation — created the instability that Topics 7.4 and 7.5 examine directly.

> [!tip]
> Memorize the 1919 Treaty of Versailles date — it appears frequently as an anchor point for AP free-response context sentences. The gap between Armistice (November 1918) and the treaty (June 1919) is significant: eight months of negotiations, during which the terms hardened against Germany.

> [!exam-tip]
> AP timeline trap: students sometimes place U.S. entry (1917) *before* the Lusitania sinking (1915). The Lusitania angered Americans but did not immediately bring the U.S. in — it took two more years and the Zimmermann Telegram before Wilson asked Congress to declare war.
