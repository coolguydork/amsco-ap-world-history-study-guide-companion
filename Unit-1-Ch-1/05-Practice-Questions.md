# Chapter 1: Practice Questions — East Asia (1200–1450)

## Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** The Song Dynasty's "commercial revolution" was primarily driven by which of the following?

- A) Conquest of new territories providing raw materials
- B) Adoption of Champa rice, urban growth, and innovations in currency
- C) The establishment of the tribute system with neighboring states
- D) Buddhist missionary activity stimulating craft production

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Song commercial revolution resulted from agricultural surplus (Champa rice), urbanization, and financial innovations like paper money — not territorial conquest, which was actually a Song weakness.*

---

**2.** Which of the following best explains why the Mongol Yuan Dynasty eventually collapsed?

- A) A successful Japanese invasion of the Chinese mainland
- B) Chinese rejection of all foreign trade, causing economic collapse
- C) Combination of peasant rebellions, plague, flooding, and loss of Mandate of Heaven legitimacy
- D) Ming Dynasty's superior military technology overwhelming Yuan forces

**Correct Answer: C**
*The Yuan collapse was multi-causal: the Black Death reduced population, floods destroyed harvests, and peasant rebellions (including Zhu Yuanzhang's) exploited weakening imperial control. The Ming's success came from this crisis, not superior technology.*

---

**3.** *Stimulus:* "The admiral's fleet consisted of over 300 vessels, some exceeding 400 feet in length, carrying silk, porcelain, and envoys. Rulers from Malacca to Malindi sent tribute and ambassadors back to the imperial court."

The voyages described in this passage are best understood as an example of which of the following?

- A) Chinese merchants seeking private commercial profit
- B) The Ming state using maritime power to extend its tributary diplomatic network
- C) Song Dynasty naval dominance over Indian Ocean trade
- D) A failed attempt to establish Chinese colonies in Southeast Asia

**Correct Answer: B**
*Zheng He's voyages were state-sponsored diplomatic missions to extend the Ming tribute system — not private trade expeditions and not colonization efforts.*

---

**4.** The decision to end Zheng He's voyages in the 1430s most directly reflects which of the following tensions within Chinese society?

- A) Between Buddhist monks and Confucian scholars over state funding
- B) Between the Yongle Emperor and regional warlords over tax revenue
- C) Between Confucian scholar-gentry values and maritime commercial expansion
- D) Between northern and southern China over control of the imperial treasury

**Correct Answer: C**
*Confucian scholar-officials viewed maritime trade as beneath the dignity of the empire and fiscally irresponsible. Their political influence over the court led to the cancellation of further voyages after 1433.*

---

**5.** The civil service examination system in China during the Song and Ming dynasties primarily served to:

- A) Ensure that only members of the aristocracy could hold government office
- B) Recruit administrators based on demonstrated knowledge of Confucian classics
- C) Allow merchants to gain social prestige by purchasing examination degrees
- D) Train military officers for defense of the northern frontier

**Correct Answer: B**
*The examination system tested mastery of Confucian texts and was theoretically open to all men, though in practice the time and resources required to prepare favored elites.*

---

**6.** *Stimulus:* "The Mongol rulers of China placed Mongols and Central Asians in the highest government positions, restricted Chinese from owning horses, and required Chinese to quarter Mongol soldiers in their homes."

These Yuan policies most directly caused which of the following?

- A) Chinese adoption of Mongol steppe nomadic culture
- B) Widespread resentment that fueled the rebel movements leading to Ming restoration
- C) Increased Chinese participation in trans-Eurasian Silk Road trade
- D) The spread of Neo-Confucianism to the Mongol ruling class

**Correct Answer: B**
*Yuan ethnic discrimination and economic extraction created deep resentment among the Chinese population, providing the social fuel for the peasant rebellions that brought down the dynasty.*

---

**7.** Korea's Goryeo Dynasty (918–1392) is best understood as an example of which broader historical process?

- A) The spread of Islam to East Asia via Indian Ocean trade
- B) Sinification — the adoption of Chinese cultural and political models by neighboring states
- C) Mongol conquest creating uniformity across Eurasia
- D) The decline of Buddhism as a major force in East Asian governance

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Goryeo Dynasty adopted Chinese-style civil service exams, Neo-Confucian philosophy, and Buddhist practices influenced by Chinese models — a prime example of Sinification.*

---

**8.** Japanese success in repelling the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281 can be attributed primarily to:

- A) Japan's superior naval technology and gunpowder weapons
- B) A successful diplomatic agreement negotiated with Kublai Khan
- C) Typhoons that destroyed the Mongol fleets, combined with Japanese samurai resistance
- D) The intervention of Chinese Song Dynasty naval forces on Japan's behalf

**Correct Answer: C**
*The *kamikaze* typhoons were decisive in destroying the Mongol fleets on both occasions; Japanese samurai forces also mounted effective resistance during the initial landings.*

---

**9.** Which of the following Chinese innovations had the most significant long-term impact on global military history?

- A) Paper money
- B) Movable type printing
- C) Champa rice cultivation
- D) Gunpowder

**Correct Answer: D**
*Gunpowder transformed warfare globally after spreading westward along Silk Road networks, eventually enabling European cannon, muskets, and the gunpowder empires of the early modern period.*

---

**10.** The Ming Dynasty's construction and reinforcement of the Great Wall of China most directly reflects which of the following?

- A) Economic desire to control Silk Road toll revenues
- B) Cultural ambition to create a monument to Chinese civilization
- C) Strategic response to continued threats from Mongol and other northern nomadic groups
- D) Buddhist belief in the sacred nature of China's northern borders

**Correct Answer: C**
*The Ming expanded the Great Wall primarily as a defensive response to continued nomadic threats from the north, especially the Mongols whom they had just expelled from China.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1
*"Between 1200 and 1450, Chinese governance demonstrated both significant continuity and important change."*

**a)** Describe ONE continuity in Chinese governance from the Song to the Ming Dynasty.

**b)** Describe ONE change in Chinese governance caused by the Mongol Yuan Dynasty.

**c)** Explain how the founding of the Ming Dynasty represents a response to the changes of the Yuan period.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Civil service examination system, Confucian bureaucracy, or imperial centralization — with specific evidence
> - (b) Ethnic hierarchy favoring Mongols, suspension of civil service exams, or promotion of foreign merchants — with specific evidence
> - (c) Zhu Yuanzhang's deliberate restoration of civil service exams, expulsion of Mongol institutions, and strengthening of centralized imperial authority as a *reaction to* Yuan practices

---

### SAQ 2
*Use the following image description: A Ming Dynasty map showing Zheng He's routes across the Indian Ocean, with treasure ships dwarfing European caravels.*

**a)** Explain ONE purpose of Zheng He's voyages as reflected in the image.

**b)** Explain ONE reason why the Ming court ended the voyages after 1433.

**c)** Explain ONE long-term consequence of the decision to end the voyages.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Extension of the tributary system, diplomatic prestige, or collection of exotic goods for the emperor
> - (b) Confucian scholar-gentry opposition, cost, or the death of Zheng He and the Yongle Emperor's successors' different priorities
> - (c) Reduced Chinese influence in Indian Ocean trade, allowing Arab/Indian merchants to dominate; or the historical counterfactual of what might have happened had China continued exploration

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Prompt

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which the Mongol conquest and rule of China (Yuan Dynasty, 1271–1368) changed Chinese society, culture, and governance.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring DBQ outline would include:**
> - **Thesis:** A defensible claim evaluating *degree* of change — e.g., "While Mongol rule significantly changed China's governance structure and social hierarchy, core Confucian cultural institutions demonstrated remarkable continuity, and the Yuan period paradoxically deepened China's commercial integration with Eurasia."
> - **Body paragraphs should address:**
>   - Political/governance changes (ethnic hierarchy, bypassing Chinese officials)
>   - Cultural continuities (Neo-Confucianism, Chinese language, Buddhist traditions survived)
>   - Economic transformation (integration into Mongol trade networks, Silk Road activity)
>   - Social disruption (Black Death, population displacement)
> - **Complexity point:** Argue that the Mongol period's most lasting impact was *indirect* — the resentment it generated produced the distinctly anti-foreign, Confucian-conservative Ming Dynasty, shaping Chinese policy for centuries
> - **HAPP analysis:** Consider Mongol documents (purpose: justify rule), Chinese chronicles (purpose: document grievances), and foreign accounts like Marco Polo (audience: European readers unfamiliar with China)
