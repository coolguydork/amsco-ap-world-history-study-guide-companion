# Topic 5.2 – Practice and Review

**Unit 5: Revolutions | Period 3 (c. 1750–c. 1900)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The Haitian Revolution was unique among Atlantic revolutions primarily because it

- A) was the first to establish a constitutional republic
- B) was directly inspired by Enlightenment ideas
- C) was the only revolution led by enslaved people that produced permanent independence
- D) succeeded without military conflict

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> A is wrong — the American Revolution established the first constitutional republic; Haiti created a republic, but that was not its distinguishing feature.
> **C is correct** — The Haitian Revolution was the only successful slave revolution in history and the only country to achieve permanent independence through a slave uprising, making it fundamentally different from all other Atlantic revolutions.
> B is wrong — all Atlantic revolutions were inspired by Enlightenment ideas; this does not make Haiti unique.
> D is wrong — the Haitian Revolution involved prolonged brutal military conflict against French, Spanish, and British forces simultaneously.

**2.** Which of the following best explains why creoles led independence movements in Latin America rather than mestizos or indigenous people?

- A) Mestizos and indigenous people supported continued Spanish rule
- B) Creoles had access to Enlightenment ideas and economic resources that other groups lacked
- C) Only creoles were legally permitted to own land in Spanish colonies
- D) Mestizos and indigenous people lacked the numbers to form armies

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> A is wrong — mestizos and indigenous people did not necessarily support Spanish rule; many participated in uprisings. The question is who had the resources and ideology to organize independence movements.
> **B is correct** — Creoles were wealthy landowners who received Enlightenment educations and chafed under Spanish mercantilism and the political dominance of peninsulares. They had both the motivation and the means to lead revolutions.
> C is wrong — land ownership rules varied across Spanish America and were not the decisive factor in who led revolutions.
> D is wrong — mestizos and indigenous peoples far outnumbered creoles; lack of numbers was not the issue.

**3.** Otto von Bismarck's "realpolitik" strategy for German unification most clearly involved

- A) appealing to shared German culture and language to build democratic consensus
- B) engineering wars to strengthen Prussia and defeat rivals
- C) forming a democratic parliament to represent all German states
- D) negotiating peacefully with Austria and France to redraw borders

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> A is wrong — Bismarck used nationalism as a tool, not through democratic appeals. He was a conservative who distrusted mass politics.
> **B is correct** — Bismarck deliberately engineered three wars (against Denmark 1864, Austria 1866, and France 1870) to unite Germany under Prussian leadership, crushing rivals and cementing Prussian dominance in each.
> C is wrong — Bismarck worked around or manipulated parliaments rather than building democratic consensus.
> D is wrong — Bismarck's entire approach was the opposite of peaceful diplomacy; he manufactured conflict as his primary instrument.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Question**: Using specific evidence, briefly explain ONE similarity and ONE difference between the French Revolution and the Haitian Revolution.

**Model Response**: One similarity between the French and Haitian Revolutions is that both were rooted in Enlightenment ideas about natural rights and equality. French revolutionaries declared "liberty, equality, and fraternity" and issued the Declaration of the Rights of Man, while Haitian revolutionary leader Toussaint L'Ouverture drew directly on Enlightenment thought in his strategy to end slavery and establish Haitian independence.

One key difference is the radicalism of the social transformation each revolution achieved. The French Revolution primarily transferred power from the monarchy and nobility to the bourgeoisie, leaving basic class structures mostly intact. The Haitian Revolution, by contrast, completely overturned the plantation slavery system — enslaved people overthrew their masters and established the first Black-led independent nation in the Western Hemisphere, achieving far more radical social change than any other Atlantic revolution.

## Document-Based and Long Essay Connections

**For DBQs**: Topic 5.2 commonly appears in documents about:
- Declarations and speeches from revolutionary leaders (Bolívar's "Jamaica Letter," Toussaint's Constitution of 1801)
- Responses to colonial rule in Latin America and the Caribbean
- Nationalist manifestos and arguments for political unification (Mazzini's writings on the Risorgimento)
- Elite reactions to revolution and fears of radical social change (conservative critiques, caudillo justifications)

**For LEQs**: Common prompts connected to 5.2:
- "Evaluate the extent to which Enlightenment ideas caused the revolutions of the period 1750–1830."
- "Compare the causes and outcomes of TWO revolutions in the period 1750–1830."
- "Evaluate the extent to which the outcomes of 19th-century revolutions reflected the ideals that inspired them."

> [!exam-tip]
> When writing about revolutions and their outcomes, pair a specific ideal with a specific outcome — then explain what was *not* achieved. The best AP essays acknowledge both change and limitation. For example: "While the Haitian Revolution abolished slavery, the new nation was forced to pay France a massive indemnity for 122 years, severely constraining its development and demonstrating that the international order still punished those who challenged its racial hierarchy."

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> **Haiti was not just another independence movement.** The Haitian Revolution was NOT simply a "French colony that wanted independence." It was the only successful slave revolution in world history — enslaved people overthrew their masters. This is fundamentally different from the American or Latin American revolutions, where wealthy elites sought political independence while keeping social hierarchies intact.

> [!warning]
> **Bolívar was not a pure democrat.** Despite his Enlightenment rhetoric, Bolívar feared mestizos, indigenous people, and mulattoes taking power, and his governments were often authoritarian. Don't confuse his inspiring ideals with his actual practice — "liberty" for creoles did not mean equality for all.

> [!warning]
> **Unification was driven by realpolitik, not idealism.** German and Italian unification were achieved by conservatives — Bismarck and Cavour — who used nationalism as a tool to preserve elite power and strengthen their states. They were not idealistic nationalists; they were political operators who exploited nationalist sentiment for strategic ends.

## Quick Review: Key Revolutions and Outcomes

| Revolution | Date | Key Leaders | What Changed | What Stayed the Same |
|-----------|------|------------|-------------|---------------------|
| American | 1775–1783 | Jefferson, Washington | Constitutional republic; independence from Britain | Slavery; women's exclusion; Indigenous dispossession |
| French | 1789–1799 | Lafayette, Robespierre, Napoleon | Feudalism abolished; Declaration of Rights of Man | Class inequality; Napoleon restored authoritarian rule |
| Haitian | 1791–1804 | L'Ouverture, Dessalines | Slavery abolished; first Black-led independent nation | Crippling debt to France; economic isolation for decades |
| Latin American | 1810s–1820s | Bolívar, San Martín | Independence from Spain/Portugal | Caudillo rule replaced colonial rule; women gained nothing |
| Italian Unification | 1858–1861 | Cavour, Garibaldi, Mazzini | Unified Italian nation-state under monarchy | Southern poverty; republic replaced by monarchy |
| German Unification | 1864–1871 | Bismarck | Unified German Empire; new European great power | Conservative Prussian elite control; Alsace-Lorraine grievance seeds WWI |
