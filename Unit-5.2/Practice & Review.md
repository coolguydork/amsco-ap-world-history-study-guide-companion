# Topic 5.2 — Practice & Review

---

## Key Facts to Know Cold

- Haiti declared independence: **January 1, 1804** (Dessalines)
- Haiti = first country in Latin America independent + first Black-led country in Western Hemisphere
- Only country permanently independent as result of slave uprising: **Haiti**
- L'Ouverture: formerly enslaved, well-read in Enlightenment; died in French prison 1803
- Bolívar: Venezuelan creole; liberated Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru; "Jamaica Letter" 1815
- Caudillos: strong local leaders who undermined democracy after Latin American independence
- Italian unification: Cavour (realpolitik) + Garibaldi (Red Shirts) + Mazzini (ideology)
- German unification: Bismarck (realpolitik) + 3 wars → German Empire 1871
- German Empire gained **Alsace-Lorraine** from France → major grievance → contributed to WWI

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The Haitian Revolution was unique among Atlantic revolutions primarily because it:

A) was the first to establish a constitutional republic
B) was directly inspired by Enlightenment ideas
C) was the only revolution led by enslaved people that produced permanent independence
D) succeeded without military conflict

*Answer: C — The Haitian Revolution was the only successful slave revolution in history and the only country to achieve permanent independence through a slave uprising.*

---

**2.** Which of the following best explains why creoles led independence movements in Latin America rather than mestizos or indigenous people?

A) Mestizos and indigenous people supported continued Spanish rule
B) Creoles had access to Enlightenment ideas and economic resources that other groups lacked
C) Only creoles were legally permitted to own land in Spanish colonies
D) Mestizos and indigenous people lacked numbers sufficient to form armies

*Answer: B — Creoles were wealthy landowners who had access to Enlightenment education and chafed under Spanish mercantilism and the political favoritism shown to peninsulares.*

---

**3.** Otto von Bismarck's "realpolitik" strategy for German unification most clearly involved:

A) appealing to shared German culture and language to build consensus
B) engineering wars to strengthen Prussia and defeat rivals
C) forming a democratic parliament to represent all German states
D) negotiating peacefully with Austria and France to redraw borders

*Answer: B — Bismarck deliberately manipulated Prussia's rivals into three wars, winning territory and cementing Prussian dominance in each.*

## Short Answer Question (SAQ)

**Prompt:** Briefly explain ONE similarity AND ONE difference between the French Revolution and the Haitian Revolution.

**Similarity (example):** Both the French and Haitian revolutions were rooted in Enlightenment ideas about natural rights and equality. French revolutionaries declared "liberty, equality, and fraternity" and issued the Declaration of the Rights of Man, while Haitian revolutionary leader Toussaint L'Ouverture drew directly on Enlightenment thought in his strategy to end slavery and establish Haitian independence.

**Difference (example):** The two revolutions differed significantly in the radicalism of their social transformation. The French Revolution primarily transferred power from the monarchy and nobility to the bourgeoisie, leaving basic class structures mostly intact. By contrast, the Haitian Revolution completely overturned the plantation slavery system — enslaved people overthrew their masters and established the first Black-led independent nation in the Western Hemisphere, achieving far more radical social change.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> The Haitian Revolution was NOT just a "French colony that wanted independence." It was the only successful slave revolution in world history — enslaved people overthrew their masters. This is fundamentally different from the American or Latin American revolutions, where wealthy elites sought independence.

> [!warning]
> Bolívar was NOT a pure democrat. Despite his Enlightenment rhetoric, he feared mestizos, indigenous people, and mulattoes taking power, and his governments were often authoritarian. Don't confuse his ideals with his practice.

> [!warning]
> German and Italian unification were driven by **realpolitik**, not idealism. Bismarck and Cavour used nationalism as a tool — they were conservatives who wanted to preserve elite power while creating strong nation-states.
