# Connections

## Builds On

**Topic 5.1 — The Enlightenment** → [[Unit-5.1/Enlightenment Overview|Enlightenment Overview]]
Enlightenment thinkers provided the ideological arsenal for every Atlantic revolution. Locke's natural rights language appeared in the Declaration of Independence word-for-word. Rousseau's popular sovereignty shaped French revolutionary doctrine. Montesquieu's separation of powers influenced constitutional designs across the Americas. Without 5.1, there is no 5.2.

**Topic 4.5 — Maritime Empires Develop** → [[Unit-4.5/Maritime Empires Develop Overview|Maritime Empires Develop Overview]]
Colonial exploitation — mercantilism, plantation slavery, peninsulares privilege — created the economic and social grievances that motivated revolutionaries in America, Haiti, and Latin America. The Atlantic slave trade that built European colonial wealth is the direct backdrop to the Haitian Revolution.

**Topic 4.7 — Changing Social Hierarchies** → [[Unit-4.7/Changing Social Hierarchies Overview|Changing Social Hierarchies Overview]]
The casta system — peninsulares → creoles → mestizos → mulattoes → indigenous/enslaved — structured who was motivated to revolt and who led the revolts in Latin America. Creoles used independence to displace peninsulares, not to flatten the hierarchy. This is why the limits of the Latin American revolutions are so legible from 4.7.

## Leads To

**Topic 7.1 — Causes of World War I**
The German Empire (1871) and Italian Kingdom, both products of nationalist unification movements, entered a European state system of competing alliances. Extreme nationalism — the same force that drove unification — became the powder keg for WWI. Bismarck's alliance system (Connections: Triple Alliance) and the unresolved grievances over Alsace-Lorraine were direct products of the unification era.

**Topic 8.4 — Communist Revolutions**
The Atlantic revolutionary tradition established the idea that ordinary people could overthrow ruling elites through revolution. The Bolshevik Revolution (1917) drew on this tradition while rejecting its liberal democratic outcomes — substituting class solidarity for nationalist identity. Compare revolutionary goals and methods.

**Topic 8.5 — Decolonization**
Anti-colonial nationalism that drove the American, Latin American, and Philippine revolutions in this period became a model for 20th-century decolonization. The language of natural rights and self-determination traveled directly from the Haitian and Latin American revolutions to African and Asian independence movements.

## Compare With

**The four Atlantic Revolutions compared to each other**
American, French, Haitian, and Latin American revolutions all drew on Enlightenment ideals, but differed dramatically in who led them, who benefited, and who was excluded. See [[Unit-5.2/Themes & Historical Reasoning|Themes & Historical Reasoning]] for a full comparison table.

**Italian vs. German unification (realpolitik comparison)**
Cavour and Bismarck used similar methods (realpolitik, wars as diplomatic tools) but operated in different political contexts. Italy unified around a constitutional monarchy with populist revolutionary energy (Garibaldi); Germany unified around Prussian military dominance with the revolutionary tradition subordinated to state power.

**Nationalist movements then vs. 20th-century decolonization**
19th-century nationalism: primarily ethnic/cultural identity within Europe + Atlantic world; led by educated elites (creoles, philosophes, students). 20th-century decolonization: similar educated elite leadership, but explicitly anti-racial in ideology, and increasingly mass-based. Connect to Topic 8.5.

## AP Threads Running Through This Topic

**Nationalism** — from cultural identity to political program to great-power competition. Track how nationalism morphs from a liberating force (1776, 1789, 1804) to a destabilizing one (post-1871 European rivalry).

**Popular sovereignty** — the core political idea of the period. Who counts as "the people"? The answer varied by revolution and exposed the limits of each.

**Limits of revolutionary change** — women, indigenous peoples, and the enslaved were systematically excluded from the rights revolutions proclaimed. This tension drives the next wave of reform movements (feminism, abolitionism, indigenous rights).

**Anti-colonialism** — the revolutionary tradition traveled outward from the Atlantic to Asia (Philippines) and set the template for 20th-century independence movements.

**Realpolitik** — practical political power strategies, divorced from idealism. The Cavour/Bismarck model shows that nationalism could be harnessed by state power rather than popular revolution.
