# Themes and Causation — Unit 5.2: Nationalism and Revolutions

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## Governance

The Atlantic Revolutions collectively dismantled the intellectual and in some cases institutional foundation of absolute monarchy and hereditary aristocracy. The American Revolution produced a constitutional republic with separation of powers and (limited) popular sovereignty — but also an Electoral College, a Senate insulated from direct democracy, and the preservation of slavery. The French Revolution went further: it abolished feudalism overnight, executed the king, and declared universal rights — only to replace royal tyranny with the Terror, and then Napoleon's imperial authoritarianism. These experiences taught 19th-century political thinkers that revolutionary change could produce new forms of tyranny as easily as liberation. Nationalism in Europe introduced a new form of political legitimacy — the nation-state — that eventually replaced dynastic empires as the dominant unit of political organization, reshaping the map of Europe in the Revolutions of 1848 and producing Italian and German unification by 1871.

> [!exam-tip]
> A classic AP causation question: How did Napoleon's conquests inadvertently *spread* nationalism? Answer: by occupying European territories, Napoleon triggered nationalist resistance movements (Spain, Germany, Italy) — demonstrating that nationalism could be a tool against imperial domination.

## Economic Systems

Revolutionary ideology challenged economic privilege as well as political privilege — but with very different outcomes in different contexts. The French Revolution abolished feudal obligations (dues owed to lords, church tithes) in a single night (August 4, 1789), freeing peasants from serfdom but leaving them without institutional support. Latin American independence movements were partly driven by creole resentment of Spanish mercantilist restrictions on trade, but independence did not immediately produce free markets — the old landowning elite simply consolidated its position without the oversight of the Spanish crown. The Haitian Revolution was the most economically radical: it abolished not just slavery but the entire plantation economy, with enormous consequences for the global sugar market (driving production to Cuba and Brazil) and for French economic interests.

## Cultural Developments and Religion

Nationalism required cultural raw materials — shared language, literature, historical memory, religion, or ethnicity — around which political movements could organize. **Romantic nationalism** in the 19th century celebrated folk culture, vernacular languages, and historical epics as the authentic expression of national spirit. The German philosopher Johann Gottfried Herder argued that each nation (*Volk*) had a unique cultural essence that should express itself in self-government. The Greek independence movement drew on classical Greek cultural prestige to attract international sympathy. Religion played complex roles: the Haitian Revolution incorporated African spiritual practices (vodou) alongside Catholic imagery; the French Revolution initially tried to de-Christianize France but abandoned it as politically counterproductive; Latin American independence movements relied on the Church for legitimacy even while challenging its institutional power.

## Social Structures

The great question of every Atlantic Revolution was: *whose* rights would be recognized? The American Revolution left enslaved people in bondage and women without political rights. The French Revolution declared universal rights but executed Olympe de Gouges for demanding that they apply to women, and reimposed slavery in Haiti under Napoleon. The Haitian Revolution was alone in making good on the promise of universal rights — and it was the most violently resisted as a result. Latin American independence did not redistribute land or end racial hierarchy; Indigenous peoples, enslaved Africans, and mixed-race populations gained little from creole-led independence. In Europe, nationalism sometimes aligned with social reform (Italian liberalism) and sometimes with social conservatism (German nationalism under Bismarck, which included welfare measures to undercut socialist organizing).

## Technology and Environment

Technology played a supporting role in the spread of revolutionary ideas. The **printing press** and the expansion of newspaper culture made revolutionary manifestos, constitutions, and declarations of rights portable across oceans. The **Atlantic trade network** carried news and political pamphlets alongside cotton and sugar. The **telegraph** (developed in the 1830s–1840s) would later accelerate information flow between revolutionary movements. Physically, the Haitian Revolution's military success depended partly on yellow fever decimating Napoleon's French army — an environmental factor that no ideology caused. The slave trade that the Haitian revolutionaries destroyed was itself an environmental and demographic transformation of the Caribbean.

---

## Human Migration

Revolutions generated massive forced and voluntary migration. The Haitian Revolution drove roughly 10,000–30,000 white colonists and free people of color out of Saint-Domingue, many resettling in Louisiana, Cuba, and Jamaica — spreading both Haitian revolutionary ideas and French Caribbean culture. The French Revolution and Napoleonic Wars displaced millions across Europe. Latin American independence produced internal migration as colonial administrative centers (Mexico City, Lima) lost their monopoly position and regional elites consolidated local power. The revolutionary period also produced the first significant **political refugees** — emigres from France, then from failed revolutions across Europe — establishing a new category of displaced persons in the modern world.
