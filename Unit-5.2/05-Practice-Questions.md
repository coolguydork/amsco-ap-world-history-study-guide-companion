# Practice Questions — Unit 5.2: Nationalism and Revolutions

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–3.**

> *"We have been harassed by a conduct which has not only deprived us of our rights but has kept us in a kind of permanent infancy with regard to public affairs… The viceroy is regularly filled by a Castilian Spaniard… All transactions, even the most trivial, must be sent to [Spain] for deliberation… [W]e have been denied citizenship."*
> — Simón Bolívar, *Jamaica Letter*, 1815

---

**1.** The grievances expressed by Bolívar in the passage most closely resemble the grievances of which other group of revolutionaries?

- A) The enslaved people of Saint-Domingue who launched the Haitian Revolution in 1791
- B) American colonists who protested British taxation and exclusion from political representation
- C) French peasants who resented aristocratic privileges during the Old Regime
- D) Greek nationalists who sought independence from Ottoman rule

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Bolívar's complaint — exclusion from self-governance despite being native inhabitants — closely parallels American colonists' grievances about Parliamentary taxation without representation and exclusion from imperial offices.*

---

**2.** Which of the following best explains why Bolívar's vision of pan-Latin American unity ultimately failed?

- A) Most Latin Americans did not share Enlightenment ideas about self-governance
- B) Spain successfully suppressed all independence movements in the 1820s
- C) Regional economic and political interests among creole elites prevailed over ideological unity
- D) The United States intervened to prevent the formation of a powerful rival republic

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*Bolívar himself lamented that Latin America was ungovernable; regional caudillos and elite interests fragmented his dream of a United States of South America into separate republics.*

---

**3.** The Latin American independence movements described in the passage most directly resulted in which of the following changes?

- A) The immediate abolition of slavery and feudal labor systems across the continent
- B) The replacement of Spanish colonial rule by creole-dominated republican governments
- C) The redistribution of land from colonial elites to Indigenous and mestizo populations
- D) The integration of Latin American economies into free-trade arrangements with Britain

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Creole elites led independence movements that removed the Spanish crown's authority — but preserved existing social hierarchies, land ownership patterns, and (in most cases) slavery.*

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 4–5.**

> *"Liberty! Equality! Are these just words? Let us swear to the Supreme Being, who hears us, that we will fight until our last breath for the implementation of the Rights of Man."*
> — Toussaint Louverture, Proclamation, c. 1797

---

**4.** Louverture's proclamation is significant in AP World History primarily because it demonstrates which of the following?

- A) The Haitian Revolution was driven solely by African spiritual traditions, not Enlightenment ideas
- B) Enslaved people appropriated Enlightenment rhetoric to challenge the very system that Enlightenment revolutionaries had maintained
- C) Enlightenment ideas were universally applied to all peoples by the revolutionary movements of the 1790s
- D) The French government supported Haitian independence as an expression of its revolutionary principles

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Louverture's invocation of "Rights of Man" is historically powerful precisely because the French National Assembly had declared those rights while keeping Saint-Domingue in slavery; the Haitian Revolution exposed this as hypocrisy.*

---

**5.** The Haitian Revolution differed from the American and French Revolutions most significantly in that it:

- A) Was inspired by Enlightenment ideas about natural rights and popular sovereignty
- B) Resulted in the complete abolition of the existing social and economic order, including slavery
- C) Was led primarily by educated creole elites who held European-derived ideas
- D) Received financial and military support from a major European power

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Haitian Revolution abolished slavery entirely — something neither the American nor French revolutions accomplished. The French Revolution briefly abolished slavery (1794) but Napoleon reversed it; the American Revolution explicitly preserved it.*

---

**6.** Napoleon Bonaparte's invasion of Spain in 1808 most directly contributed to which of the following?

- A) The French Revolution's shift from a republic to a military dictatorship
- B) The spread of nationalist ideology across Europe and the triggering of Latin American independence movements
- C) The abolition of slavery in France's Caribbean colonies
- D) The Congress of Vienna's decision to restore conservative monarchies across Europe

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*By deposing the Spanish king and installing his brother, Napoleon created a power vacuum in Spanish America that creole elites used to launch independence movements; simultaneously, his occupation of Spain and other European territories sparked nationalist resistance movements.*

---

**7.** The Congress of Vienna (1815) represented a direct response to the revolutions of the late 18th century primarily by:

- A) Recognizing the new republics created by the American and French revolutions
- B) Abolishing the remaining feudal privileges of the European aristocracy
- C) Attempting to restore conservative monarchies and prevent further revolutionary change
- D) Creating an international organization to protect the rights of colonized peoples

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The Congress of Vienna restored Bourbon monarchies in France and Spain, redrew the European map to contain France, and established the Concert of Europe as a conservative mechanism for suppressing future revolutionary movements.*

---

**8.** The Greek independence movement (1821–1830) gained international support from European powers largely because:

- A) Greece was a major economic partner for France and Britain
- B) European cultural and intellectual tradition celebrated ancient Greek civilization as the source of Western democracy
- C) The Ottoman Empire had attacked Greek Orthodox monasteries, angering the Catholic Church
- D) Greek independence leaders were educated in European universities and had personal connections with European governments

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*European Romantic nationalism celebrated Greece as the birthplace of democracy and Western civilization; poets like Byron volunteered to fight for Greek independence partly because of this cultural romanticism.*

---

**9.** The "Springtime of Nations" (Revolutions of 1848) in Europe most directly demonstrated which of the following?

- A) Nationalism and liberalism had become powerful political forces that existing empires could not fully suppress
- B) The working classes had fully embraced socialist ideology by the mid-19th century
- C) European monarchies had lost all ability to maintain order against revolutionary movements
- D) The United States was actively supporting nationalist movements across Europe

**✅ Correct Answer: A**
*The 1848 revolutions swept across France, Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy, demonstrating the power of nationalist and liberal ideology — even though most were suppressed within a year, they permanently weakened the legitimacy of the old order.*

---

**10.** Which of the following best explains why the Haitian Revolution had a different outcome from other Atlantic revolutions in terms of slavery?

- A) Haiti had a smaller enslaved population than other Caribbean colonies, making abolition easier
- B) The enslaved people of Saint-Domingue rose up themselves and fought to destroy the institution, rather than relying on elite-led reform
- C) The French government willingly granted freedom to Haitian enslaved people as an expression of revolutionary principles
- D) Haiti's abolition of slavery was modeled directly on British emancipation policies

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*In other revolutions, enslaved people were acted upon by elite decisions; in Haiti, the enslaved acted for themselves — making the revolution's abolition of slavery self-determined and irreversible.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Compare the causes and outcomes of TWO of the following Atlantic revolutions: American, French, Haitian."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE similarity in the causes of the two revolutions you chose.
**Part B:** Describe ONE difference in the outcomes of the two revolutions you chose.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why the outcomes differed despite similar causes.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Best pairing for contrast:** American and Haitian — both invoked natural rights, but American preserved slavery while Haitian abolished it.
> - **Part C:** Focus on *who led* each revolution: creole/white elites in America vs. the enslaved themselves in Haiti. Leadership determines whose interests a revolution serves.
> - Avoid vague statements like "they were both inspired by Enlightenment ideas" — be specific about which ideas and how each revolution applied or limited them.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Explain how nationalism changed the political map of Europe between 1815 and 1871."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE specific example of a successful nationalist movement in Europe in this period.
**Part B:** Explain how the nationalist movement you identified challenged existing political authority.
**Part C:** Explain ONE way the rise of nationalism in Europe set the stage for future conflicts.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Greek independence (1821–1830), Italian unification (1848–1861), or German unification (1866–1871) are all strong choices.
> - **Part C:** The best answers note that nationalism within multiethnic empires (Ottoman, Habsburg, Russian) created irredentist tensions that would explode in World War I.

## DBQ Outline Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which the Atlantic Revolutions (c. 1776–1825) advanced Enlightenment ideals of liberty and equality."**

> [!tip] Top-Score DBQ Guidance
> - **Complexity point:** The Haitian Revolution is your strongest evidence for an argument that Enlightenment ideals were both powerfully realized and structurally limited — it fully abolished slavery (advance) but the newly independent Haiti was forced to pay reparations to France for 122 years (limit).
> - **Corroboration strategy:** Group documents by (1) documents showing advance of liberty/equality, (2) documents showing limits (slavery preserved, women excluded), (3) documents showing contradictions within the same revolution.
> - **Outside evidence:** Olympe de Gouges's *Declaration of Rights of Woman*; Toussaint Louverture's proclamations; Bolívar's *Jamaica Letter*; reactions of slaveholders to Haiti.
> - **Sourcing:** Consider the perspective of each author — a creole leader has different interests than an enslaved revolutionary; both invoke "liberty" but mean different things.
