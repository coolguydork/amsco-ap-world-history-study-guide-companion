# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 5.2: Nationalism and Revolutions

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[The Enlightenment]]** ([[Unit-5.1]]) — Direct intellectual cause; natural rights, social contract, and popular sovereignty are the ideological toolkit of every Atlantic Revolution.
- **[[Atlantic Slave Trade]]** ([[Unit-4.4]]) — The plantation economy and African diaspora that the Haitian Revolution destroyed were built by the Atlantic slave trade of Units 3–4.
- **[[Colonial Administration in the Americas]]** ([[Unit-4.5]]) — The encomienda, viceroyalty system, and mercantilist restrictions were the specific grievances that fueled Latin American independence movements.
- **[[Changing Social Hierarchies]]** ([[Unit-4.7]]) — The casta system and racial hierarchy of colonial Latin America persisted after independence; understanding this is essential to understanding who benefited from revolution.
- **[[Print Culture]]** ([[Unit-4.1]]) — Pamphlets, newspapers, and revolutionary constitutions spread through the same print infrastructure established by Gutenberg's press.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Industrial Revolution]]** ([[Unit-5.3]]) — The political stability created by the American Constitution attracted investment; French revolutionary turmoil disrupted continental industrialization; Haitian independence disrupted the Caribbean sugar economy and redirected investment to Cuba and Brazil.
- **[[New Imperialism]]** (Unit 6) — Nationalism justified both independence movements *and* imperial expansion; European nations used nationalist fervor to mobilize populations behind colonial wars.
- **[[World War I]]** (Unit 7) — Nationalist tensions within the Habsburg and Ottoman empires, exacerbated by the nationalist movements of the 19th century, directly caused WWI; the "powder keg" was nationalist irredentism.
- **[[Abolitionism and Emancipation]]** (Units 5–6) — The Haitian Revolution permanently energized the abolitionist movement; slaveholders' fear of "another Haiti" shaped both pro-slavery and anti-slavery politics throughout the 19th century.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **American vs. French vs. Haitian Revolution** — All invoked natural rights; American preserved slavery, French briefly abolished then reversed it, Haitian abolished it permanently. Useful comparison of how *who leads* determines *whose rights* are recognized.
- **Atlantic Revolutions vs. Asian Nationalist Movements** — Compare with the Meiji Restoration (1868) in Japan — a top-down nationalist modernization driven by fear of Western imperialism rather than Enlightenment ideals. Shows nationalism takes different forms in different contexts.
- **Bolívar vs. Washington as revolutionary leaders** — Both creole elites, both inspired by Enlightenment ideals; but Washington stepped down and Washington's republic endured, while Bolívar's pan-American vision fragmented. Why?

> [!exam-tip]
> When comparing revolutions on the AP exam, always organize your analysis around these four questions: (1) Who led it? (2) What ideology motivated it? (3) What changed? (4) What was preserved (especially regarding race, gender, and social hierarchy)?
