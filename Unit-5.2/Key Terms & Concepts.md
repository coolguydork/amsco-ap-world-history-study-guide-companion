# Topic 5.2 — Key Terms & Concepts

**AMSCO:** pp. 285–293

---

## Political Terms

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Nationalism** | Intense loyalty to those who share one's language and culture; desire for an independent nation-state | Drove both independence movements (Greece, Haiti) and unification movements (Italy, Germany) |
| **Realpolitik** | Political decisions based on practical realities, not ideology or moral principles | Cavour and Bismarck both used realpolitik to unify their nations |
| **Risorgimento** | Italian for "resurgence/rebirth" — the movement for Italian cultural and political unification | Championed by Mazzini; achieved under Cavour and Garibaldi |
| **Plebiscite** | A direct popular vote on a political question | Several Italian regions voted by plebiscite to join Piedmont-Sardinia |
| **Estates-General** | France's representative body divided into clergy (1st), nobility (2nd), and commoners (3rd) | The 3rd Estate (97% of France) broke away to form the National Assembly in 1789 |
| **National Assembly** | The new governing body formed by French commoners in 1789 | Passed the Declaration of the Rights of Man |
| **Reign of Terror** | Period (1793–1794) in which the Jacobin government executed thousands of opponents | Included execution of Louis XVI and Marie Antoinette; ended when Robespierre himself was executed |
| **Ottomanism** | Late Ottoman movement (1870s–80s) to create a unified multi-ethnic state through standardized education and law | Backfired by intensifying subject peoples' feelings of difference |

## Revolution-Specific Terms

| Term | Definition | Context |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Declaration of Independence** (1776) | American document declaring independence from Britain; articulated Lockean natural rights | Jefferson; July 4, 1776 |
| **Declaration of the Rights of Man** (1789) | French document declaring basic human rights; abolished feudalism | Adopted early in the French Revolution |
| **Bastille** | Former Paris prison stormed July 14, 1789 | Symbolized the abuses of the monarchy; French Independence Day |
| **Jacobins** | Radical political club during the French Revolution | Led the Reign of Terror |
| **Maroons** | Escaped enslaved people who formed free communities | Joined L'Ouverture's forces in the Haitian Revolution |
| **Creoles** | People of European ancestry born in the Americas | Led Latin American independence movements |
| **Peninsulares** | People born in Spain or Portugal | Held top colonial government jobs; resented by creoles |
| **Mulattoes** | People of African + European or indigenous ancestry | Low in Latin American social hierarchy |
| **Caudillos** | Strong local military leaders who stepped in after Latin American independence | Undermined democracy; ruled through personal loyalty and force |
| **Gran Colombia** | Bolívar's large federation comprising modern Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador | Broke apart after Bolívar left power; his vision of unity failed |
