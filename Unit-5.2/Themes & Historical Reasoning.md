# Topic 5.2 — Themes & Historical Reasoning

---

## AP Themes

### Governance
The revolutions of 1750–1900 fundamentally transformed how political authority was legitimized. Divine right → consent of the governed. The American and French revolutions produced written constitutions limiting government. The Haitian Revolution went further: not just limiting government, but abolishing slavery. However, in Latin America, caudillos showed that constitutional forms don't guarantee constitutional realities.

### Social Structures
Every revolution promised equality but delivered it unevenly. The French Revolution abolished feudalism but the bourgeoisie benefited most. The Haitian Revolution abolished slavery — the most radical social transformation. Latin American revolutions changed little for mestizos, indigenous people, mulattoes, and women. Nationalism created new identities (Greek, Italian, German) that cut across old feudal bonds.

### Cultural Developments
Nationalism was partly a **cultural phenomenon**: people rallied around shared language, folk traditions, religion, and historical memory. The Risorgimento celebrated Italian culture. Greek nationalism revived ancient Greek identity. The Philippine Propaganda Movement used print culture to build a sense of Filipino identity.

### Human Migration
Revolutions and their aftermaths drove significant migration. The failed Italian unification (southern poverty) drove emigration to the U.S. and Argentina. Latin American independence reshuffled populations. The Haitian Revolution's success caused many French planters to flee to Louisiana and other parts of the Caribbean.

## Historical Reasoning: Causation

**What caused the Atlantic Revolutions?**

1. **Intellectual cause:** Enlightenment ideas (natural rights, social contract, popular sovereignty) gave people a framework to demand change
2. **Political cause:** Colonial rule, absolute monarchy, the Estates system denied political power to large groups
3. **Economic cause:** Mercantilism, taxation without representation, plantation slavery generated economic grievances
4. **Military/contextual cause:** France's debts from the American Revolution → French Revolution; Napoleon's conquests → Latin American revolutions (Spain weakened); French Revolution's example → Haitian Revolution

> [!exam-tip]
> For causation essays, organize causes into categories (intellectual, political, economic, contextual) and argue which was MOST important. The Enlightenment was a necessary but not sufficient cause — material conditions had to align.

## Historical Reasoning: Comparison

**Comparing Revolutions — Key Questions:**
1. Who benefited from the revolution? Who was left out?
2. How radical was the transformation of social structures?
3. Did it achieve constitutional/representative government?

**Ranking by Social Radicalism:**
1. Haitian Revolution — most radical (enslaved people overthrew masters; complete social inversion)
2. French Revolution — abolished feudalism, executed monarchy, but bourgeoisie dominated outcome
3. American Revolution — political independence, but slavery and women's exclusion unchanged
4. Latin American Creole Revolutions — least radical (creole elites replaced peninsulares; basic hierarchy continued)

## Historical Reasoning: CCOT

**What changed in political systems 1750–1900?**
- Monarchies challenged; constitutional governments established in U.S., France, eventually across Latin America
- Two new nation-states: Italy (1861) and Germany (1871) — fundamentally altering Europe's balance of power
- Former colonies became independent nations across the Western Hemisphere
- Slavery abolished across the Americas (Haiti 1804 → Brazil 1888)

**What continued?**
- Inequality persisted under new forms (caudillos, industrial capitalism, racial hierarchies)
- Women's political rights largely unchanged until 20th century
- European powers continued to dominate global politics despite losing some colonies (Americas → Asia/Africa expansion in Unit 6)
- Nationalism proved a double-edged sword — united nations but also intensified conflicts
