# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 5.4: Industrialization Spreads

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Industrial Revolution Begins]]** ([[Unit-5.3]]) — All other industrialization stories begin with Britain; 5.4 is the diffusion of what 5.3 originated.
- **[[Atlantic Trade Networks]]** ([[Unit-4.4]]) — British capital invested in American railroads and Latin American mines; global trade networks provided the demand that made industrial scale viable.
- **[[Zheng He and China's Maritime Withdrawal]]** (Unit 3) — China's historical pattern of withdrawing from external engagement contributed to the institutional conservatism that prevented Qing industrialization.
- **[[Japanese Isolation (Tokugawa)]]**(Unit 3–4) — Japan's Tokugawa isolation (1603–1853) set the stage for the shock of Perry's 1853 arrival, which directly triggered the Meiji Restoration.

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Technology in the Industrial Age]]** ([[Unit-5.5]]) — The railroad and steel industries of 5.4 are the infrastructure on which the Second Industrial Revolution technologies of 5.5 build.
- **[[Industrialization and Government's Role]]** ([[Unit-5.6]]) — The national cases of 5.4 (Germany, Japan, USA) provide the empirical evidence for the comparative government role analysis of 5.6.
- **[[New Imperialism]]** (Unit 6) — Industrial nations used their manufacturing and military advantage to colonize Africa and Asia; Japan's industrialization eventually drove its own imperialist expansion.
- **[[World War I]]** (Unit 7) — The industrial balance of power between Britain, Germany, France, Russia, and Japan directly shaped the alliances and conflicts of WWI.
- **[[Russian Revolution]]** (Unit 7) — Witte's industrialization of Russia created the social conditions (urban proletariat + autocracy) for the 1917 revolution.

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Japan vs. China as responses to Western pressure** — The AP's most productive comparison: Japan reformed its institutions and industrialized; China resisted institutional change and was colonized economically. The key variable is *willingness to transform political and social structure*.
- **Germany vs. Britain as models of industrialization** — Germany: protective tariffs, state banking, social welfare, rapid military buildup. Britain: free trade, private enterprise, minimal welfare state, naval supremacy. Two successful industrial models with very different political implications.
- **American industrialization vs. European** — American advantages: vast natural resources, large unified domestic market, mass immigration, no feudal social structure. European advantages: established capital, skilled labor, technological lead. American disadvantage: later start, but overcome by scale.

> [!exam-tip]
> When comparing industrialization across countries, always address: (1) timing, (2) government role, (3) primary industries, (4) labor source, and (5) social consequences. These five dimensions separate a strong comparative essay from a superficial one.
