# Topic 5.4 — Practice & Review

> [!exam-tip] Topic 5.4 is especially well-suited to comparison questions (Japan vs. India; Germany vs. France; top-down vs. bottom-up industrialization). Make sure you can compare confidently.

---

## Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)

**Question 1**
Japan's industrialization in the late 19th century most closely resembled Russia's in that both countries:

A) Were driven primarily by private entrepreneurs and market forces
B) Relied on large-scale immigration to supply factory labor
C) Industrialized through deliberate state direction rather than market-driven processes
D) Focused primarily on textile production as the basis of industrial growth

> **Answer: C** — Both Japan and Russia used top-down, state-directed industrialization (the Meiji government in Japan; the Russian state under Witte). Neither relied on private enterprise as the primary driver. A is wrong; B describes the U.S.; D describes Britain's early phase.

---

**Question 2**
The decline of Indian textile manufacturing in the 19th century was most directly caused by:

A) A series of droughts that devastated Indian cotton production
B) The Indian population's preference for European-made goods
C) British colonial policies, including tariffs and institutional control, that disadvantaged Indian producers
D) The Indian government's decision to focus on railroad construction instead of textiles

> **Answer: C** — British colonial policy — particularly the 5% equalizing tax on Bombay mills and East India Company regulations — directly undercut Indian textile competitiveness. A is false; B misrepresents the situation; D is wrong (India had no independent government to make such decisions).

---

**Question 3**
Germany's rapid industrialization after 1871 was most directly enabled by:

A) Massive immigration from Eastern Europe that supplied industrial labor
B) Political unification, which created a single national market and allowed coordinated resource use
C) The discovery of new coal deposits in the Rhine Valley
D) British investment in German factories and railroads

> **Answer: B** — German unification under Bismarck (1871) eliminated internal trade barriers and allowed the new nation to harness its existing coal and iron resources (especially the Ruhr) on a national scale. A describes the U.S.; C is inaccurate (the Ruhr deposits were known before 1871); D overstates British involvement.

---

**Question 4**
Which of the following best illustrates the concept of "defensive modernization"?

A) The United States building the transcontinental railroad with immigrant labor
B) Russia constructing the Trans-Siberian Railroad to boost agricultural exports
C) The Meiji government adopting Western technology and institutions to protect Japanese sovereignty
D) France delaying industrialization to preserve traditional craft industries

> **Answer: C** — Defensive modernization means deliberately adopting Western methods to *resist* Western domination — exactly what the [[Meiji Restoration]] accomplished. A and B are development projects but not defensive responses. D is wrong (France's delay was not a deliberate protective strategy).

---

**Question 5**
Egypt's loss of its textile export markets by the mid-19th century most directly resulted from:

A) A British colonial embargo on Egyptian goods
B) Egyptian government decisions to redirect labor toward cotton farming
C) The inability of Egyptian hand production to compete in price with European machine-made goods
D) Tariffs imposed by European governments to protect their own textile industries

> **Answer: C** — Egyptian textiles were priced out of markets by cheaper European industrial production. Unlike India, Egypt's decline was primarily market competition rather than explicit colonial policy. A is false; B is not supported; D gets the tariff direction backwards.

## Short Answer Questions (SAQs)

### SAQ 1 — Comparison

**Prompt:** Briefly explain ONE similarity and ONE difference between Japan's and India's responses to European industrial and economic pressure in the 19th century.

**Model Response Framework:**
- **Similarity:** Both Japan and India faced significant economic pressure from European industrial powers whose machine-made goods threatened existing production — both experienced disruption to traditional economic activities as a result of contact with industrializing Europe.
- **Difference:** Japan, as an independent state, was able to respond with the [[Meiji Restoration]]'s program of defensive modernization — adopting Western technology while maintaining sovereignty. India, as a British colony, had no such option; colonial policies (tariffs on Indian textiles, closure of iron mines) actively suppressed rather than promoted Indian industrialization.

---

### SAQ 2 — Causation

**Prompt:** Explain ONE cause of Germany's rapid industrialization after 1871.

**Model Response Framework:**
- **Cause:** German political unification (1871) under Otto von Bismarck.
- **Explanation:** Before 1871, Germany was fragmented into dozens of small states with separate currencies, laws, and tariff systems. This made large-scale industrial investment risky and limited market size. After unification, a single national market emerged, enabling German industries to operate at scale. The Ruhr Valley's abundant coal and iron could now fuel a truly national industrial economy, and the unified German state could invest in infrastructure (railroads, technical schools) on a national level.

---

### SAQ 3 — CCOT

**Prompt:** Explain ONE continuity and ONE change in the global distribution of manufacturing between 1700 and 1900.

**Model Response Framework:**
- **Continuity:** Throughout this period, textile production remained the most important manufacturing sector globally — from Indian hand-loom cloth to British machine-made cotton to early American mills, textiles drove trade and employment.
- **Change:** The *geographic center* of manufacturing shifted dramatically. In 1700, India and China were among the world's largest manufacturers, producing goods exported across Eurasia. By 1900, the industrial West — especially Britain, Germany, and the United States — dominated global manufacturing, while India and Egypt had experienced severe deindustrialization due to colonial competition.

## Essay Prompts (LEQ-style)

### Prompt 1 — Comparison
"Compare the industrialization of TWO of the following countries in the 19th century: Germany, Russia, Japan."

**Thinking tips:**
- For each country: What drove industrialization? Who directed it? What resources? What obstacles? What was the outcome?
- Strong essays compare *categories* (role of government, labor supply, focus of industry, speed) not just narrative
- Acknowledge similarities AND differences
- Germany and Japan both industrialized rapidly after major political transformations (unification; Meiji Restoration)
- Russia industrialized more slowly and remained agriculturally dominant

---

### Prompt 2 — Causation
"Evaluate the extent to which British colonial policy caused the deindustrialization of India in the 19th century."

**Thinking tips:**
- Strong argument: tariffs on Indian textiles, East India Company takeover of industries, Arms Act 1878, closure of mines after 1857
- Counterargument: price competition from industrial goods was also a factor (harder to compete even without colonial restrictions)
- Best answer acknowledges both — colonial policy *accelerated and entrenched* what market forces *began*
- Use specific evidence: 5% equalizing tax on Bombay mills, Indian Navy absorbed 1830, iron mines in Rajasthan abandoned

---

### Prompt 3 — CCOT
"Analyze continuities and changes in the role of the state in economic development in ONE of the following regions between 1750 and 1900: Japan, Russia, or Western Europe."

**Japan thinking tips:**
- Before Meiji: Tokugawa shogunate maintained isolation; state *blocked* engagement with global economy
- After 1868: Meiji government actively directed industrialization — sent students abroad, hired experts, built railroads and factories
- Continuity: the Japanese state remained authoritarian and directive throughout; it did not shift to laissez-faire
- Change: from blocking modernization → driving modernization; from feudal agricultural governance → industrial statecraft
