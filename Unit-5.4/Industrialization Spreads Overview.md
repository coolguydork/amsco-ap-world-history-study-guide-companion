# Topic 5.4 — Industrialization Spreads: Overview

**AP Unit:** 5 — Revolutions | **Period:** 3 (c. 1750–c. 1900) | **AMSCO:** pp. 305–306

**Essential Question:** How did different types and locations of production develop and change over time?

---

## The Big Picture

After Britain industrialized, the process spread — but unevenly. Some countries (Belgium, France, Germany, United States, Russia, Japan) industrialized and became new global powers. Others (India, Egypt, parts of the Middle East) saw their existing manufacturing **undermined** by European colonial policies even as they were drawn into supplying raw materials for industrial economies. This uneven spread of industrialization is a major cause of modern global inequality.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently compares industrialization across countries. Key comparison points: What factors enabled industrialization? What factors delayed it? And critically — how did **colonialism de-industrialize** places like India?

## Who Industrialized and When

| Country | When | Key Factors | Notable Features |
|---------|------|------------|-----------------|
| Britain | c. 1750 | Coal, capital, enclosure movement, geography | First; set the template |
| Belgium | Early 19th c. | Coal deposits, capital | Second to industrialize |
| France | Early-mid 19th c. | Capital, resources — but delayed by Revolution + wars | Sparse urban population limited labor |
| Germany | Post-1871 | Unified state + Ruhr Valley coal/iron | Quickly became steel/coal leader |
| United States | 19th c. → leading by 1900 | Immigrant labor, vast natural resources, railroad network | Human capital (immigrants) key |
| Russia | 1890s | State-directed railroad building, Trans-Siberian Railroad | 4th largest steel producer by 1900; agriculture dominant until 1917 |
| Japan | Post-1868 | Meiji Restoration; defensive modernization; state subsidies | First Asian country to industrialize; see [[Unit-5.6/Japan & the Meiji Restoration]] |

## Country Profiles

### France
France had some advantages — capital, resources, water transport — but its industrialization was delayed:
- **Sparse urban population** limited the available factory labor force
- The **French Revolution (1789–1799)** and subsequent Napoleonic wars consumed the attention and capital of France's elites
- Eventually industrialized but never quite matched British or German levels in this period

### Germany
Germany was politically fragmented into numerous small states — this **structural obstacle** prevented the scale of investment and infrastructure that industry required. Once Germany unified under Bismarck in **1871**, it rapidly became a leading producer of steel and coal, especially in the Ruhr Valley.

### United States
The U.S. industrial revolution accelerated through the 19th century and by **1900 was the world's leading industrial power**.
- **Human capital** was the key: immigrants from Europe and East Asia, plus rural Americans migrating to cities, provided the factory labor force
- Vast natural resources: timber, coal (West Virginia, Pennsylvania, Kentucky), iron, and oil
- Railroad network (including the Transcontinental Railroad, completed 1869) connected resources to markets

### Russia
Russia industrialized later and differently:
- Focus on **railroads and exports** rather than consumer goods
- **Trans-Siberian Railroad** (Moscow to the Pacific) facilitated trade with China and Japan
- Coal, iron, and steel industries developed mostly in the **1890s**
- By 1900: **4th largest steel producer** in the world
- But: the economy remained **overwhelmingly agricultural** until the Communist seizure of power in 1917

## The Other Side: De-industrialization

While some countries industrialized, others saw their existing manufacturing **collapse under European colonial pressure**. This is just as important as the spread of industry.

### India: Shipbuilding
- Indian shipbuilding had resurged in the late 17th century through political alliances with Western countries
- British **mismanagement** of resources → decline
- In 1830, Britain designated East India Company ships as the "Indian Navy"
- The Indian Navy was **disbanded by 1863** when Britain's Royal Navy took complete control of the Indian Ocean

### India: Iron and Mining
- **Company rule** (British East India Company, 1757–1858): steep British tariffs → India's ability to mine and work metals declined
- After the **Rebellion of 1857**, Britain closed mines — fearing metals would be used for ammunition against them
- **Arms Act of 1878**: restricted access to minerals AND firearms production
- Mines in mineral-rich Rajasthan were **abandoned; mining became extinct**
- Legacy: even after British rule ended (1948), mining remained primitive for decades — the false impression that India's resources were inaccessible

### India: Textiles
- India's textile industry (Bombay mills) was growing and competing with British mills
- Lancaster mill owners pressured the British government → imposed an **"equalizing" 5% tax** on Bombay mills → undermined their profitability

### Egypt: Textiles
- In the 18th century, Egypt exported carpets, silks, and textiles to Europe
- By the mid-19th century: **European industrial production crushed Egypt's textile industry**
- Egypt lost both its export market AND much of its domestic market to cheaper European goods

> [!exam-tip]
> The de-industrialization of India and Egypt is a crucial AP point. European industrialization didn't just grow — it actively destroyed rival manufacturing in colonized regions. This is economic imperialism in action, and it directly connects to [[Unit-6.1/Overview]] (New Imperialism).

## The Agricultural Trade Connection

As some regions de-industrialized, they were absorbed into the global economy as **raw material and agricultural suppliers**:

| Product | Source Region | Destination |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Palm oil | West Africa, Indonesia | Britain (cooking oil, soap) |
| Sugar | Caribbean, Brazil | Britain (refined sugar) |
| Cattle/Hogs | U.S., Ireland, Argentina | Britain, U.S. (meat) |
| Cotton | U.S. (slave-produced), India, Egypt | British and European textile mills |
