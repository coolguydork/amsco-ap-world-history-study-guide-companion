# Unit 5.4: Industrialization Spreads

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

British industrialization did not remain a British monopoly for long. From the 1820s onward, industrial techniques, machinery, and capital diffused to continental Europe, North America, and eventually Asia — though along very different paths and with different government roles in each case. **Belgium** (1820s–1830s) was the first continental nation to industrialize, benefiting from coal deposits, proximity to Britain, and deliberate state investment. **France** industrialized more gradually, held back by political instability and a larger peasant population but ultimately competitive by mid-century. **Germany** — unified in 1871 — industrialized rapidly under Bismarck's protective tariffs and state-supported banking, leaping to become Europe's largest industrial economy by 1900. The **United States** industrialized explosively after the Civil War, driven by vast natural resources, mass immigration providing cheap labor, land grants enabling railroad construction, and a large unified domestic market. Most dramatically, **Japan** launched the **Meiji Restoration** (1868) — a top-down, state-led industrialization program designed to prevent Western colonization — and within 30 years had built modern factories, railways, banks, and a navy capable of defeating imperial Russia (1905). The railroad was the critical technology diffusing industrialization across all these cases, integrating national markets, transporting coal and raw materials, and carrying finished goods to consumers.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how industrialization spread from Britain to other regions in the 19th century.
- Compare the different paths to industrialization taken by Germany, the United States, and Japan.
- Analyze the role of railroads in diffusing and accelerating industrialization.
- Explain the significance of the Meiji Restoration as a model of state-directed industrialization.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.1.II** — Industrialization spread from Britain to other parts of Europe, North America, and eventually Asia, with governments playing varying roles.
- **KC-5.1.III** — As industrialization spread, different regions took different approaches, reflecting available resources, government policies, and connections to global trade.

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Meiji Restoration** is among the most tested topics in this unit. Know: why Japan industrialized (fear of Western imperialism), how (state-led, top-down, selective borrowing), and what distinguished it from China's failure to industrialize similarly. The AP exam frequently asks you to compare Japan and China's responses to Western pressure.
