# Practice Questions — Unit 5.4: Industrialization Spreads

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–2.**

> *"The nation [Japan] shall be strengthened by the military; the army shall be strengthened by the people; the people shall be strengthened by wealth; and wealth shall be attained through promotion of industry."*
> — Ōkubo Toshimichi, Meiji government leader, c. 1874

---

**1.** The argument expressed in the passage most directly reflects which of the following aspects of Japanese industrialization under the Meiji government?

- A) Japan adopted a laissez-faire economic model similar to Britain's early industrialization
- B) The Meiji government viewed industrialization primarily as a means of building military power to resist Western imperialism
- C) Japanese leaders believed that foreign investment was more important than domestic manufacturing
- D) The Meiji government prioritized social welfare reforms before industrial development

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Ōkubo's statement explicitly connects industrialization to military strength, revealing that Japan's primary motivation was to resist Western imperialism — not to generate consumer goods or profit for its own sake.*

---

**2.** The approach to industrialization described in the passage most directly contrasts with which of the following?

- A) German industrialization under Bismarck, which also used state power to build military capacity
- B) British industrialization, which was driven primarily by private entrepreneurs with minimal government direction
- C) American industrialization, which relied heavily on government land grants and tariff protection
- D) Russian industrialization, which used state-directed railroad construction and foreign investment

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Japan's explicitly state-directed industrialization contrasts most sharply with Britain's laissez-faire model — in Britain, private entrepreneurs (Watt, Arkwright, Carnegie) drove industrialization with the government mainly providing legal infrastructure.*

---

**3.** The German Customs Union (Zollverein, 1834) contributed to German industrialization primarily by:

- A) Providing funds for the Prussian government to build state-owned factories
- B) Eliminating internal tariffs and creating a unified German market that supported industrial-scale production
- C) Attracting foreign investment from Britain and France for railroad construction
- D) Protecting German agriculture from American competition through import tariffs

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Zollverein's primary economic impact was market integration — by eliminating tariff barriers between German states, it created a market large enough to support industrial-scale production.*

---

**4.** China's failure to industrialize in the 19th century, despite the example of Japan's Meiji Restoration, is best explained by which of the following?

- A) China lacked the natural resources needed for industrial development
- B) China's population was too large to be effectively mobilized for factory production
- C) The Qing government was unwilling to transform political and social institutions, limiting industrialization to military technology alone
- D) Western powers deliberately prevented Chinese industrialization to maintain their economic dominance

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The Self-Strengthening Movement tried to adopt Western military technology while preserving Confucian institutions and Qing rule — but industrial success required institutional transformation that the Qing refused to undertake, as Japan's success demonstrated.*

---

**5.** The construction of the American Transcontinental Railroad (completed 1869) most directly resulted from which of the following?

- A) Private investment by robber barons who saw opportunity in cross-continental trade
- B) Federal government land grants and subsidies that made the project financially viable
- C) Immigration of Chinese and Irish workers who organized themselves to build the railroad
- D) British capital investment seeking returns from American westward expansion

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Congress granted the railroad companies ~170 million acres of land and tens of millions in federal bonds — without which private capital could not have financed a project of this risk and scale.*

---

**6.** The spread of industrialization from Britain to Germany and the United States by 1900 most directly resulted in which of the following?

- A) Britain's economic and industrial dominance increased as it supplied machinery to new industrial nations
- B) British industrial supremacy was challenged as Germany and the United States developed competitive manufacturing sectors
- C) International free trade replaced national mercantilism as the dominant economic system worldwide
- D) The industrial nations cooperated to prevent the spread of industrial techniques to Asia and Africa

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*By 1900, the USA had surpassed Britain as the world's largest industrial economy; Germany was close behind. Britain's relative decline had begun — the very success of industrial diffusion undercut British dominance.*

---

**7.** Russian industrialization in the 1890s under Finance Minister Sergei Witte is most significant because it:

- A) Demonstrated that laissez-faire economics could produce rapid industrialization in a short time
- B) Created a concentrated industrial proletariat that contributed to revolutionary pressure on the tsarist autocracy
- C) Enabled Russia to compete economically with the United States by 1900
- D) Proved that foreign capital investment was unnecessary for rapid industrial development

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Russia's concentrated industrial workers in St. Petersburg and Moscow, subjected to harsh conditions under an autocratic government that refused reform, became the social base for the 1905 and 1917 revolutions.*

---

**8.** Japan's victory over Russia in the Russo-Japanese War (1905) was significant in world history primarily because it:

- A) Demonstrated that Western military technology was superior to traditional Asian weapons
- B) Was the first time an Asian nation had defeated a European great power, challenging assumptions of European racial and industrial superiority
- C) Led directly to Japan's acquisition of European colonies in Africa and Southeast Asia
- D) Caused Russia to industrialize more rapidly to rebuild its military capacity

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Japan's victory shocked the world — it was the first time in the modern era that a non-European nation had defeated a major European power militarily, demonstrating that industrialization was not exclusively a European capacity.*

---

**9.** The concept of "selective borrowing" as practiced by Japan during the Meiji period is best illustrated by which of the following?

- A) Japan adopted Western democratic institutions including parliamentary government and universal suffrage
- B) Japan adopted Western military technology and industrial organization while maintaining the emperor system and Confucian social hierarchies
- C) Japan adopted Western religious traditions including Christianity to facilitate economic partnerships
- D) Japan adopted Western free-market economics, eliminating state control of industry by 1890

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Selective borrowing meant Japan took what was useful (Krupp artillery, Prussian constitutional model, British navy) while rejecting what threatened the imperial system and social hierarchy (democracy, individualism, religious pluralism).*

---

**10.** The spread of railroads across industrializing nations in the 19th century most directly contributed to industrialization by:

- A) Eliminating the need for oceanic shipping in long-distance trade
- B) Dramatically reducing transportation costs and integrating national markets, making industrial-scale production economically viable
- C) Providing a new industry that employed most of the working class in each industrializing nation
- D) Demonstrating the superiority of steam power over water power in all industrial applications

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Railroads' primary industrial contribution was market integration and transportation cost reduction — without cheap freight, industrial goods could not be sold profitably across the distances separating factories from consumers.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Compare the roles of government in the industrialization of Japan and Britain."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE similarity in how the governments of Japan and Britain contributed to industrialization.
**Part B:** Describe ONE significant difference in the governments' roles.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why Japan's government took a more active role in industrialization than Britain's.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Both governments protected property rights and created legal infrastructure (patent laws, contract enforcement, stable currency).
> - **Part B:** Japan's Meiji government built state-owned factories, hired Western advisors, and directed industrial development; Britain's government mainly enforced laws and let private entrepreneurs lead.
> - **Part C:** Japan was industrializing *later*, under threat of Western imperialism, with insufficient private capital — making state direction necessary rather than optional. Britain had decades of commercial capital accumulation before industrialization.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Explain why Japan succeeded in industrializing in the late 19th century while China did not."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE specific action taken by Japan's Meiji government that promoted industrialization.
**Part B:** Identify ONE specific reason why China's Self-Strengthening Movement failed to produce comparable industrial development.
**Part C:** Explain ONE consequence of Japan's industrialization for the broader Asia-Pacific region.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Abolition of feudalism, state-built railroads and factories, mandatory public education, Iwakura Mission, zaibatsu system, hiring Western advisors.
> - **Part B:** Self-Strengthening Movement tried to adopt military technology without reforming political institutions; Qing government preserved the examination system and Confucian bureaucracy; foreign wars and domestic rebellions (Taiping) drained resources.
> - **Part C:** Japan's industrialization led to aggressive expansion (Korea, Manchuria), the 1894–1895 Sino-Japanese War, and the 1905 Russo-Japanese War — reshaping Asian geopolitics and eventually contributing to WWII.
