# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 5.1 — The Enlightenment | AMSCO pp. 275–284**

---

## Key Dates

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| 1561–1626 | Francis Bacon (life) | Empiricism — knowledge from observed experience; method applied to all fields |
| 1651 | Hobbes publishes *Leviathan* | Social contract theory: people cede rights to strong government for order; justifies authoritarian rule |
| c. 1690 | Locke publishes *Two Treatises of Government* | Natural rights (life, liberty, property); citizens may revolt against tyranny; foundation of Atlantic revolutions |
| c. 1690 | Locke publishes *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* | Tabula rasa — mind shaped by experience, not birth; radical egalitarianism |
| 1748 | Montesquieu publishes *The Spirit of the Laws* | Separation of powers into executive, legislative, judicial branches; directly shaped U.S. Constitution |
| 1762 | Voltaire publishes *Candide* | Satire against religious intolerance and tyranny; model of Enlightenment wit and advocacy |
| 1762 | Rousseau publishes *The Social Contract* | Popular sovereignty; General Will; governments must serve the people; fuel for French Revolution |
| 1776 | Adam Smith publishes *The Wealth of Nations* | Laissez-faire capitalism; "invisible hand" of the market; free trade over mercantilism |
| 1776 | Thomas Paine publishes *Common Sense* | Plain-language case for American independence; mass political persuasion |
| 1789 | France adopts Declaration of the Rights of Man | Enlightenment ideals applied to law; limited monarchy; fueled further revolution |
| 1791 | De Gouges publishes *Declaration of Rights of Woman* | Extended Enlightenment logic to women; executed during Reign of Terror |
| 1792 | Wollstonecraft publishes *A Vindication of the Rights of Women* | Women deserve equal education and political participation |
| 1794 | Thomas Paine publishes *The Age of Reason* | Militant defense of deism; anti-church; damaged his popularity |
| 1807 | Denmark and Britain ban the slave trade | First nations to abolish slave trade; cascading effect on slavery itself |
| 1808 | United States bans the slave trade | Importation of enslaved people ended; domestic slavery persists and expands |
| 1848 | Seneca Falls Convention (New York) | Declaration of Sentiments; women demand voting rights, property rights, equal legal status |
| 1861 | Tsar Alexander II abolishes serfdom in Russia | Largest single emancipation in history — 23 million serfs freed |
| 1894–1906 | The Dreyfus Affair (France) | Anti-Semitism exposed in "enlightened" France; galvanized Zionism |
| 1888 | Brazil abolishes slavery | Last country in the Americas to do so |
| 1948 | State of Israel founded | Culmination of Herzl's Zionist movement |

## Chronological Patterns

**17th century (1600s):** The groundwork — empiricism (Bacon), social contract theory (Hobbes, Locke). Ideas still confined largely to educated elites and academic networks.

**18th century (1700s):** The Enlightenment peaks — philosophes (Montesquieu, Voltaire, Rousseau), economics (Smith). Print culture carries ideas to mass audiences. Atlantic revolutions begin (American 1776, French 1789, Haitian 1791).

**Early 19th century (1800s–1830s):** Age of Isms — socialism, classical liberalism, conservatism, nationalism compete for dominance. Reform movements in Britain (Reform Bills); utopian communities (Owen).

**Mid–late 19th century (1840s–1900):** Enlightenment ideas reach reform movements worldwide — feminism (Seneca Falls 1848), abolitionism (slavery ended across the Americas), Russian emancipation (1861), Zionism (Herzl, 1890s).

> [!tip]
> On the AP exam, you may be asked to place Enlightenment events in chronological context. Remember: the ideas predate the revolutions they caused. Locke (1690) → American Revolution (1776) → French Revolution (1789) → Haitian Revolution (1791) → Latin American independence (1810s–1820s). The 86-year gap between Locke and the American Revolution shows that Enlightenment ideas needed time, print culture, and political crisis to become revolutionary action.
