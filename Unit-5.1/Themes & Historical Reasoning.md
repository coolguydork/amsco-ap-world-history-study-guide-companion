# Topic 5.1 — Themes & Historical Reasoning

---

## AP Themes in the Enlightenment

### Governance
The Enlightenment fundamentally challenged the idea that monarchs ruled by divine right. Thinkers like Locke and Montesquieu argued that governments derive their legitimacy from the people and must protect natural rights. This sparked demands for **constitutional government** — placing legal limits on rulers — and fueled every major revolution of Period 3.

### Cultural Developments & Interactions
The Enlightenment was a **transnational intellectual movement**: ideas flowed across borders through books, pamphlets, encyclopedias, salons, and correspondence. Voltaire corresponded with Catherine the Great and Frederick the Great. Thomas Paine wrote for both the American and French revolutions. The *philosophes* saw themselves as citizens of reason, not just of France.

### Economic Systems
Adam Smith's *Wealth of Nations* marked a turning point from **mercantilism** to **capitalism**. His laissez-faire argument that free markets serve society better than government-managed trade became the economic philosophy of the Industrial Revolution and the 19th-century British Empire.

### Social Structures
The Enlightenment challenged inherited hierarchies. The *tabula rasa* undermined aristocracy (if environment shapes people, not birth, then rank is not natural). Feminist thinkers extended natural rights to women. Abolitionists applied them to enslaved people. These ideas reshaped social structures across the Atlantic world.

## Historical Reasoning: Causation

**Causation question:** What caused the revolutions of the late 18th and early 19th centuries?

The Enlightenment is the intellectual cause — but note that ideas alone do not cause revolutions. They interact with material conditions:

| Cause | Revolution |
|-------|-----------|
| Enlightenment ideas + British mercantilism + colonial legislatures | American Revolution |
| Enlightenment ideas + French debt/economic crisis + social inequality (Estates) | French Revolution |
| Enlightenment ideas + French colonial slavery + opportunity from French Revolution | Haitian Revolution |
| Enlightenment ideas + creole resentment of peninsulares + Napoleon weakening Spain | Latin American revolutions |

> [!exam-tip]
> A strong AP argument doesn't just say "the Enlightenment caused revolutions." It identifies **specific thinkers** (Locke, Rousseau) and **specific ideas** (natural rights, General Will) and shows how they combined with **local conditions** (taxation, slavery, colonial rule) to produce a specific revolution.

## Historical Reasoning: Continuity and Change Over Time (CCOT)

**What changed?**
- Political legitimacy shifted from divine right → consent of the governed
- Economics shifted from mercantilism → laissez-faire capitalism
- Social hierarchies were challenged by natural rights arguments
- Religion lost its monopoly on explaining the world → empiricism and reason gained prestige

**What continued?**
- Enlightenment thinkers were mostly elite men; women and enslaved people had to fight to apply these ideas to themselves
- Monarchies persisted (even Napoleon crowned himself emperor)
- Social inequality continued; the "isms" of the 19th century were still fighting battles the Enlightenment started

## Historical Reasoning: Comparison

**Compare Hobbes and Locke:**

| | Hobbes | Locke |
|--|--------|-------|
| View of human nature | Pessimistic — selfish, chaotic | More optimistic — rational, educable |
| Social contract leads to... | Strong sovereign (near-absolute) | Limited government with right to revolt |
| Influenced... | Absolute monarchies; strong-state arguments | American Revolution; liberal constitutions |

**Compare Enlightenment ideologies:**

| | Liberalism | Conservatism | Utopian Socialism |
|--|-----------|-------------|------------------|
| Change through... | Constitutional reform | Gradual tradition | Ideal communities |
| Attitude to capitalism | Supportive (laissez-faire) | Cautious | Critical — want alternatives |
| View of natural rights | Central | Skeptical | Extend to workers/poor |
