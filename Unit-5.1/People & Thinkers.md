## The Philosophes: Who Were They?

The **philosophes** were 18th-century writers and thinkers (named from the French word for "philosopher") who popularized Enlightenment ideas through essays, novels, encyclopedias, and correspondence. They included thinkers from Scotland (Adam Smith), America (Jefferson, Franklin), and France (Montesquieu, Voltaire, Rousseau).

## Thinker Profiles

### Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679)
- **Major work:** *Leviathan* (1651)
- **Core idea:** Social contract — people gave up some rights to a **strong central government** in exchange for law and order
- **View of human nature:** Pessimistic — without government, life is "nasty, brutish, and short"
- **AP significance:** Used to justify strong/absolute government; contrast with Locke

> [!warning]
> Hobbes supported the social contract but NOT democracy. He thought people needed a powerful sovereign, not a representative government. Don't confuse Hobbes with Locke on this point.

### John Locke (1632–1704)
- **Major works:** *Two Treatises of Government* (1690); *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1690)
- **Core ideas:**
  - Social contract implies the **right (even responsibility) to revolt** against unjust government
  - Natural rights: **life, liberty, and property**
  - *Tabula rasa* ("blank slate"): children are born empty; environment and education shape them
- **AP significance:** Directly inspired the American Declaration of Independence (Jefferson borrowed "unalienable rights" from Locke); foundational to classical liberalism

> [!exam-tip]
> Jefferson changed Locke's "life, liberty, and property" to "life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness." This is a classic AP comparison point.

### Baron Montesquieu (1689–1755)
- **Major work:** *The Spirit of Laws* (1748)
- **Core ideas:**
  - Praised Britain's constitutional monarchy and Parliament
  - Advocated **separation of powers** and **checks and balances**
- **AP significance:** Most directly influenced the U.S. Constitution — executive (president), legislative (Congress), and judicial (federal courts) branches reflect his design

### Voltaire (François-Marie Arouet, 1694–1778)
- **Major work:** *Candide* (1762)
- **Core ideas:**
  - Championed **civil liberties** and **religious liberty**
  - Satirized church corruption and aristocratic privilege
  - Lived in England → admired constitutional monarchy and civil rights → brought these ideas to France
  - Corresponded with Catherine the Great (Russia) and Frederick the Great (Prussia)
- **AP significance:** His advocacy for religious liberty influenced the U.S. Constitution's First Amendment

### Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712–1778)
- **Major works:** *Emile, or On Education* (1762); *The Social Contract* (1762)
- **Core ideas:**
  - **General Will** — a sovereign is obligated to carry out the collective will of the people
  - Optimistic — believed society could improve
  - Ideas on education (child-rearing, natural development)
- **AP significance:** Inspired French revolutionaries; more radical than Locke in demanding that government serve ALL people

### Adam Smith (1723–1790)
- **Major work:** *The Wealth of Nations* (1776)
- **Core ideas:**
  - Responded to **mercantilism** by calling for freer trade
  - **Laissez-faire** ("leave alone") — minimal government intervention in economics
  - **"Invisible hand"** — if individuals pursue self-interest, the market guides them to choices beneficial for society
  - Foundation of **capitalism**: private ownership of means of production for profit
- **AP significance:** Foundational text of capitalist economic theory; used by classical liberals to oppose government regulation

> [!tip]
> Remember: Adam Smith is an **economist**, not a political revolutionary. His revolution was economic — replacing mercantilism with free-market capitalism. This matters for understanding how the Industrial Revolution's economic ideology develops.

## Utopian Socialists

| Thinker | Origin | Key Idea |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Henri de Saint-Simon | France | Scientists + engineers + businesses = efficient, clean society; proposed the Suez Canal |
| Charles Fourier | France | Identified 810 human "passions" that, if encouraged, would make work enjoyable; harmonious communities over class struggle |
| Robert Owen | Britain | Established intentional communities (New Lanark, Scotland; New Harmony, Indiana); communal property; education for child workers |

The **Fabian Society** (England): gradual socialists who favored reforming society through Parliament rather than revolution. Members included H. G. Wells, Virginia Woolf, and George Bernard Shaw.

## Zionism's Founder

**Theodor Herzl** (Austro-Hungarian Jew): concluded that Jewish people would only be safe if they controlled their own land. Inspired by the **Dreyfus Affair** (1894) — a French Jewish military officer falsely convicted of treason, exposing rampant anti-Semitism even in "enlightened" France. Herzl's movement culminated in the founding of **modern Israel in 1948**.
