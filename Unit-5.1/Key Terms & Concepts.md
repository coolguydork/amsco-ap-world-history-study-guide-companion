# Topic 5.1 — Key Terms & Concepts

**AMSCO:** pp. 275–281

---

## Government & Political Theory

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Social contract** | Agreement between people and government — people give up some freedoms in exchange for protection and order | Foundation of Enlightenment political thought; basis of American and French constitutions |
| **Natural rights** | Rights people are born with that cannot be taken away (Locke: life, liberty, property) | Inspired the Declaration of Independence and Declaration of the Rights of Man |
| **Tabula rasa** | "Blank slate" (Locke) — humans are born empty; environment and education shape them | Radical idea that undermined hereditary aristocracy and justified universal education |
| **General Will** | Rousseau's concept — the collective will of the people that a sovereign must carry out | Inspired French Revolution's idea that government serves the people |
| **Separation of powers** | Montesquieu's idea — executive, legislative, and judicial branches should be separate | Directly built into the U.S. Constitution |
| **Checks and balances** | Each branch of government limits the others' power | Prevents tyranny; core of U.S. constitutional design |
| **Classical liberalism** | Belief in natural rights, constitutional government, laissez-faire economics, reduced military/church spending | Drove 19th-century reform movements in Britain (Reform Bills of 1832, 1867, 1884) |
| **Conservatism** | Belief in traditional institutions and practical experience over ideological theory | Opposed revolutions; advocated gradual change; represented by Burke and de Maistre |
| **Realpolitik** | Political decisions based on practical realities rather than ideology | Cavour and Bismarck used this; connects to [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism and Revolutions Overview]] |

## Economics

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Laissez-faire** | "Leave alone" (French) — government should not intervene in the economy | Adam Smith's core argument against mercantilism; foundation of capitalism |
| **Capitalism** | Economic system in which the means of production are privately owned and operated for profit | Dominant economic system of the Industrial Revolution and beyond |
| **Invisible hand** | Adam Smith's metaphor — individuals pursuing self-interest guide the market to benefit society | Justification for minimal government economic intervention |
| **Mercantilism** | Economic system in which a country regulates trade to maximize wealth (colonies sell only to mother country) | What Enlightenment economists like Smith argued against |

## Social Movements & Ideologies

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Empiricism** | Knowledge comes from sensed experience and observation | Francis Bacon's method; foundation of scientific and Enlightenment thinking |
| **Deism** | Belief that a divinity set natural laws in motion but does not interfere with the world | Enlightenment alternative to organized religion; Thomas Paine advocated this |
| **Socialism** | System in which the means of production are publicly or worker-owned | Emerged as a critique of capitalism's inequalities |
| **Utopian socialism** | Belief that society could be improved by creating ideal communities based on cooperative principles | Saint-Simon, Fourier, Owen; contrast with Marx's "scientific socialism" |
| **Feminism** | Movement for women's equal rights, education, and political participation | Direct application of Enlightenment natural rights thinking to women |
| **Abolitionism** | Movement to end the Atlantic slave trade and free all enslaved people | Extended natural rights logic to enslaved people |
| **Nationalism** | Intense loyalty to others who share one's language and culture; desire for an independent nation-state | Grew from Enlightenment ideas; led to revolutions and unifications in [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism and Revolutions Overview]] |
| **Zionism** | Movement for Jews to re-establish an independent homeland in the Middle East | Late 19th-century "ism"; response to centuries of anti-Semitism and pogroms |
| **Anti-Semitism** | Hostility toward Jewish people | Widespread in Europe; Dreyfus Affair exposed it in France; spurred Zionism |
| **Pogroms** | Violent attacks against Jewish communities | Persecution that drove Zionist movement |
