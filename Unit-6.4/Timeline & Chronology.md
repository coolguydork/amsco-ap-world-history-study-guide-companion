## Chronological Overview

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1721 | Britain bans Indian cotton textiles | Protects domestic wool industry; structurally prevents Indian manufacturing; colonies locked into raw material role |
| 1831 | Cyrus McCormick invents mechanical reaper | Mechanizes American agriculture; increases grain output; raises demand for fertilizers like guano |
| 1832 | Electric telegraph invented | Enables near-instantaneous communication; transforms business, military coordination, and journalism |
| 1839 | Charles Goodyear develops vulcanization | Stabilizes rubber; creates modern rubber industry; drives Amazon/Congo extraction and later SE Asian plantations |
| c. 1840–1880 | Peak guano trade, Peru and Chile | Millions of tons exported as fertilizer; mined by indentured Chinese/Polynesian labor; classic extraction economy |
| 1850 | President Fillmore calls for guano imports | First U.S. government acknowledgment of global resource dependency; illustrates how far industrial economies relied on distant colonies |
| 1850 | Telegraph service introduced in India | Five years after Britain; shows rapid colonial rollout of communications technology to serve imperial coordination |
| 1861–1865 | American Civil War; Union blockade | Cuts off 80% of Britain's cotton supply; triggers global cotton scramble; Egypt and India dramatically expand production |
| 1866 | First permanent transatlantic telegraph cable | U.S.–Britain connection; instantaneous communication across the Atlantic for first time; global markets become truly coordinated |
| 1869 | Suez Canal opens | Reduces Europe-to-India voyage by thousands of miles; built by de Lesseps using Egyptian corvée labor; Britain seizes Egypt in 1882 to control it |
| c. 1870 | More efficient steam engines developed | Makes long-distance ocean steamship travel economically viable; dramatically accelerates global commodity trade |
| 1870 | Cecil Rhodes arrives in South Africa | Begins ascent from cotton farmer to diamond magnate to Prime Minister; epitomizes economic imperialism |
| c. 1870s | Global telegraph network connects continents | Instantaneous global communication enables coordinated global markets and imperial administration |
| c. 1870s | Compression refrigeration on steamships | Perishable meat and dairy can be shipped across oceans; Southern Hemisphere ranching integrated into global food economy |
| 1871 | Rhodes joins Kimberley diamond rush | Begins diamond empire; De Beers Mining Company follows in 1880 |
| c. 1870s | British transplant rubber seedlings to SE Asia | Rubber cultivation moves from Amazon to Malaya, Ceylon, Singapore; Southeast Asian plantation economies emerge |
| 1880 | De Beers Mining Company founded | Rhodes's diamond monopoly; by 1891 controls 90% of world production; model of corporate imperialism |
| c. 1880 | Railroads transform colonial interiors | Lowered cost of moving raw materials from interior to coast; built primarily by low-wage indigenous labor; India's rail network largest in colonial world |
| 1886 | Witwatersrand gold fields discovered | South Africa's gold rush; Rhodes gains stake; accelerates British political control of southern Africa |
| c. 1890s | Egypt: 93% of export revenue from cotton | Textbook monoculture; entire national economy organized around one crop for one market (British textiles) |
| 1890 | Rhodes becomes PM of Cape Colony | Diamond/gold wealth converted to political power; racist policies lay groundwork for apartheid |
| 1891 | De Beers controls 90% of world diamonds | Peak of Rhodes's corporate monopoly |

## Thematic Timeline: Three Technologies

**Railroads**
- Pre-1800s: Colonial transportation by river or poorly maintained roads
- Early 1800s: Railroad technology introduced; Britain begins building in India
- Mid-1800s: India's interior connected to coastal ports; Africa's railroad building begins
- 1890s: Rhodes's Cape-to-Cairo railroad project (never completed)

**Steamships**
- Early 1800s: Steamboats operate on navigable rivers (Ganges, Congo)
- 1870: More efficient engines enable long-distance ocean voyages
- 1870s: Refrigeration equipment makes perishable goods shippable; global food trade integrates

**Telegraph**
- 1832: Electric telegraph invented
- 1850: Telegraph introduced in India
- 1866: First permanent transatlantic cable (U.S.–Britain)
- 1870s: Cables connect Europe to Australia and South America; global network complete

## Key Causation Chain

**Industrial Revolution** → demand for raw materials and new markets → **economic imperialism** → railroads/steamships/telegraph lower extraction costs → colonies forced into cash crops and export economies → **monocultures and dependency** → long-term structural poverty in former colonies

> [!exam-tip]
> AP questions often ask you to sequence causes and effects. Practice moving through this chain in both directions: from the Industrial Revolution forward to monocultures, and from modern colonial legacies backward to 19th-century extraction policies.
