# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 9.4 — Economics in the Global Age | AMSCO pp. 657–666**

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1944 | Bretton Woods Conference establishes IMF, World Bank, and U.S. dollar as reserve currency | Foundation of postwar global economic order; U.S. economic hegemony institutionalized |
| 1947 | General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) signed | Begins systematic reduction of global tariffs (from ~40% toward under 5% by 1990s) |
| 1960s–1990s | Japan, then Asian Tigers achieve rapid export-led industrialization | Model for state-guided integration into global markets; hundreds of millions lifted from poverty |
| 1973 | Augusto Pinochet seizes power in Chile; "Chicago Boys" implement free-market reforms | First major test of neoliberal economics in Latin America; economic growth without poverty reduction |
| 1978–1981 | Deng Xiaoping launches Four Modernizations in China | Opens China to FDI and market mechanisms; sets stage for China becoming the world's manufacturing hub |
| 1979 | Margaret Thatcher becomes British PM; begins Thatcherism | Privatization, deregulation, union-busting; European face of neoliberal revolution |
| 1981 | Ronald Reagan becomes U.S. President; begins Reaganomics | Tax cuts, deregulation, anti-union policies; American face of neoliberal revolution |
| 1989 | Tiananmen Square crackdown, Beijing | Chinese government violently suppresses pro-democracy protests; signals limits of Deng's liberalism |
| 1990s | India opens markets; IT sector booms; middle class grows | Demonstrates how developing nations can capture knowledge-economy benefits of globalization |
| 1990s | Finland pivots from manufacturing to knowledge economy after Soviet collapse | Model for small-nation tech-sector development |
| 1991 | Mercosur founded (Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay) | Regional South American trade bloc; AP example of regional economic integration |
| 1994 | NAFTA takes effect (U.S., Canada, Mexico) | Accelerates maquiladora growth; exports jobs from U.S.; creates low-wage manufacturing zones in Mexico |
| 1995 | World Trade Organization (WTO) replaces GATT | Governs 90%+ of world trade; becomes target of anti-globalization protests |
| Late 1990s | Anti-globalization movement organizes; 1999 Seattle WTO protests | Popular backlash against neoliberalism and WTO rules; connects to Topic 9.7 |
| 2000s | China becomes world's largest manufacturing nation | Economic globalization's most dramatic outcome; supply chains restructure global economy |
| 2014 | India's middle class estimated at 350 million — world's largest | Result of 20+ years of economic liberalization and IT-sector growth |

## Cause → Effect Chain

**Cold War ends (1989–1991)**
→ Eastern Bloc nations and nonaligned states open to global markets
→ Supply of cheap labor and new consumers expands massively
→ MNCs accelerate outsourcing and global supply chain construction
→ Manufacturing concentrates in Asia and Latin America
→ Knowledge economies develop in wealthy nations
→ Inequality between workers in different nations persists/widens
→ Anti-globalization resistance emerges [[Unit-9.7/Resistance to Globalization Overview|Resistance to Globalization Overview]]

**Neoliberal ideology spreads (1970s–1990s)**
→ GATT/WTO reduce tariffs; IMF imposes structural adjustment programs
→ Governments privatize state industries and deregulate markets
→ Corporations gain power relative to states and labor unions
→ Short-term economic growth in many nations; long-term inequality debates

## AP Period 4 Context

This topic sits at the heart of AP Period 4 (1900–present): the acceleration of globalization after the Cold War represents both the culmination of trends running through all of Unit 9 and a direct consequence of events in Unit 8 (Cold War, decolonization). The economic integration of this era also sets up Unit 9's tensions: the same forces that created the knowledge economy and lifted millions from poverty also generated the resistance movements in Topics 9.5–9.7.
