## Ideology & Racial Policy

**Nazis**
Short for the National Socialist German Workers' Party (NSDAP). Hitler's political movement and ruling party of Germany (1933–1945). Nazi ideology combined extreme nationalism, anti-Semitism, Aryan racial supremacy, and totalitarian state control. The Nazis were responsible for WWII in Europe and the Holocaust.

**Third Reich**
Hitler's name for Nazi Germany (1933–1945), meaning "Third Empire" — framing it as the successor to the medieval Holy Roman Empire and the 19th-century German Empire. It encompassed the totalitarian Nazi state, its territorial expansion, and the Holocaust.

**Mein Kampf**
Hitler's autobiography and political manifesto ("My Struggle"), written in prison (1924–25). It outlined his anti-Semitic ideology, belief in Aryan racial superiority, and plan for Lebensraum through conquest of Eastern Europe. It is a primary source revealing Nazi ideology years before WWII.

**scientific racism**
A pseudoscientific theory claiming that racial hierarchies are biologically determined and that some races are genetically superior to others. Nazis used it to justify persecution of Jews, Slavs, Roma, and others. The AP exam connects this to Social Darwinism and the broader ideology of European imperialism.

**anti-Semitism**
Hostility, prejudice, and discrimination against Jewish people. Long-rooted in European history, in Nazi Germany Hitler weaponized anti-Semitism into state policy through the Nuremberg Laws, Kristallnacht, and ultimately the Holocaust.

**Aryans**
In Nazi ideology, the supposed "master race" of Germanic/Nordic peoples. Hitler used this pseudoscientific concept to justify the persecution of Jews, Slavs, Roma, and others deemed "impure." The term is historically corrupted from its actual linguistic meaning (Indo-Iranian peoples).

**Lebensraum**
German for "living space." Hitler's ideology that Germany needed to conquer Eastern Europe and Russia to provide territory and resources for a racially "pure" German population. It was the central justification for Nazi expansionism into Austria, Czechoslovakia, and Poland.

> [!exam-tip]
> Lebensraum, Mein Kampf, and scientific racism form a triangle: ideology → justification → territorial aim. On the AP exam, cite all three when explaining how Nazi ideology caused WWII.

## Persecution of Jews

**Nuremberg Laws**
1935 German racial laws that stripped Jews of citizenship, forbade marriage between Jews and non-Jews, and unleashed a cascade of discriminatory decrees. They were the legal foundation of Nazi persecution and an early step toward the Holocaust — a key AP example of state-sponsored racism institutionalized through legislation.

**Kristallnacht**
November 9–10, 1938 — the "Night of Broken Glass." Nazi-orchestrated pogroms across Germany and Austria destroyed nearly every synagogue, killed over 90 Jews, and sent 30,000 to concentration camps. Although presented as a spontaneous response to a Jewish teenager's assassination of a German diplomat, it was entirely state-engineered. It marked a decisive escalation in Nazi anti-Jewish violence.

## Key Institutions and Events

**[[Concept Notes/Weimar Republic\|Weimar Republic]]**
The democratic German government (1919–1933) established after WWI. It accepted the Treaty of Versailles, making it deeply unpopular. Its instability during the [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]] — hyperinflation, mass unemployment, political extremism — enabled the Nazi Party's legal rise to power by 1933.

**Reichstag**
Germany's parliament building. The Nazis blamed a 1933 fire there on communist radicals as a pretext to suspend civil liberties and consolidate Hitler's dictatorship. It represents how the Nazis used manufactured crises to eliminate democratic institutions.

**Anschluss**
The March 1938 political union of Austria with Germany. Hitler used the threat of invasion and pressure through Austrian Nazis to absorb his birthplace into the Third Reich without military resistance. It violated the Treaty of Versailles and demonstrated Britain and France's unwillingness to act.

**Munich Agreement**
September 1938 agreement among Germany, Britain, France, and Italy allowing Hitler to annex Czechoslovakia's Sudetenland in exchange for a pledge of no further territorial demands. It is the defining symbol of appeasement's failure. Hitler violated it within months by seizing all of Czechoslovakia.

**German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact**
August 23, 1939 agreement between Hitler and Stalin pledging mutual non-aggression. It secretly divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of influence. The pact freed Hitler to invade Poland without fear of a two-front war and shocked Western democracies. Germany violated it by invading the USSR in June 1941.

## Geography

**Sudetenland**
The German-speaking borderland of Czechoslovakia that Hitler demanded in 1938, claiming its ethnic German population justified annexation. It was granted to Germany by the Munich Agreement. Hitler's subsequent seizure of all of Czechoslovakia proved the failure of appeasement.

**Danzig**
A Polish port city (modern Gdańsk) with a majority German population that Hitler used as a pretext to pressure Poland. Britain and France had pledged to defend Poland, making the German invasion (September 1, 1939) the immediate trigger for WWII in Europe.

## Alliance System

**Rome-Berlin Axis**
October 1936 military and political alliance between Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy, formalizing their shared ideology and economic interests. The term "Axis" later expanded to describe the three-power bloc.

**Anti-Comintern Pact**
1936 agreement between Germany and Japan (Italy joined 1937) pledging mutual opposition to the Soviet-backed Communist International (Comintern). It formed the ideological framework for the Axis alliance and demonstrated the convergence of fascist and militarist powers against communism and liberal democracy.

**Axis Powers**
The military alliance of Germany, Italy, and Japan in WWII, formalized through the Rome-Berlin Axis (1936), Anti-Comintern Pact (1936), and Tripartite Pact (1940). United by fascism, militarism, and imperial ambition, they opposed the Allied Powers.

## Key People

**Adolf Hitler**
Austrian-born German politician who became chancellor (1933) and then dictator of Germany. He founded Nazi ideology based on Aryan racial supremacy, anti-Semitism, and Lebensraum. His aggressive territorial expansion — Austria, Sudetenland, Czechoslovakia, Poland — directly caused WWII. He also orchestrated the Holocaust, murdering approximately 6 million Jews and millions of others.

**Neville Chamberlain**
British Prime Minister (1937–1940) who pursued a policy of appeasement toward Hitler, most famously at the Munich Agreement (1938). His policy failed when Hitler seized all of Czechoslovakia in 1939, and Chamberlain's name became synonymous with the dangers of conceding to aggressors.

---

## Key Policy

**appeasement**
The British and French strategy of making concessions to Hitler's territorial demands to avoid another European war. The Munich Agreement (1938) was its peak moment. When Hitler invaded Poland in 1939, appeasement was discredited as a policy. The lesson drawn by later leaders: dictators interpret concession as weakness and demand more.

> [!warning]
> A common misconception: appeasement was not cowardice but a calculated strategy by leaders who remembered WWI's devastation and believed Hitler had limited, rational aims. It failed because those assumptions were wrong. Explain the logic, then explain why it failed.
