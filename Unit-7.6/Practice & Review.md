# Practice & Review

**Topic 7.6 — Causes of World War II | Printed pp. 503–510**

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Source:** Franklin D. Roosevelt, address delivered at Charlottesville, Virginia, June 10, 1940

> "Perception of danger, danger to our institutions, may come slowly or it may come with a rush and a shock as it has to the people of the United States in the past few months. This perception of danger, danger in a world-wide area—it has come to us clearly and overwhelmingly—we perceive the peril in a world-wide arena, an arena that may become so narrowed that only the Americas will retain the ancient faiths. Some indeed still hold to the now somewhat obvious delusion that we of the United States can safely permit the United States to become a lone island, a lone island in a world dominated by the philosophy of force."

**Question 1.** What position is best supported by Roosevelt's words?

A. The United States should follow a strategy of appeasement toward Hitler.
B. The United States should avoid war with Hitler at all costs.
C. The United States should join the fight against Hitler.
D. The United States alone could stop Hitler.

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Roosevelt calls isolation a "delusion" and warns that a world "dominated by the philosophy of force" threatens U.S. institutions — he is arguing against isolationism and implying the U.S. must engage. He does not claim the U.S. alone can stop Hitler (D), and he explicitly rejects appeasement/avoidance framing (A, B).

---

**Question 2.** Based on the passage, Roosevelt would have most strongly supported:

A. The Monroe Doctrine
B. The League of Nations
C. The mandate system
D. The Munich Agreement

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Roosevelt's argument for collective international action against fascist aggression aligns best with the League of Nations' collective security ideal. He explicitly rejects isolation (Monroe Doctrine, A). The Munich Agreement (D) was appeasement — the opposite of what Roosevelt advocates. The mandate system (C) is unrelated.

---

**Question 3.** Which statement accurately provides context for the passage?

A. Hitler's forces were overrunning Europe, and Britain asked the United States for help in spite of the strength of U.S. isolationism.
B. Hitler's forces were overrunning the Soviet Union, which asked the U.S. for help in spite of U.S. anticommunist sentiments.
C. Japan's forces were taking over communist China, which asked the U.S. for help in spite of U.S. anticommunist sentiments.
D. Japan had attacked the U.S. naval base at Pearl Harbor, yet many in the United States still supported isolationism.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The speech is dated June 1940 — France had just fallen to Germany and Britain was under threat. The U.S. was strongly isolationist, and Roosevelt was building the case for intervention. The Soviet Union was not yet involved (Germany invaded the USSR in June 1941, B is wrong). Japan had not yet attacked Pearl Harbor (December 1941, D is wrong and anachronistic). China (C) is not the context here.

## Short-Answer Questions

**Source A:** Reich Citizenship Law of September 15, 1935
*(Article 1: Subjects of the German state. Article 2: Reich citizens are those of "German or related blood" who serve the German people — "the sole bearer of full political rights.")*

**Source B:** Law for the Protection of German Blood and German Honor, September 1935
*(Forbids marriage between Jews and non-Jews; forbids Jews from flying the German flag.)*

---

**SAQ 1A.** Identify ONE way that German society changed after 1933.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> After Hitler took power in 1933, Jews were systematically stripped of their legal rights and social standing. The Nuremberg Laws (1935) removed Jews' German citizenship and banned marriages between Jews and non-Jews, transforming Jewish Germans from full citizens into legal outsiders. This represented a fundamental change in who was considered a member of the German national community.

---

**SAQ 1B.** Explain ONE way these documents demonstrate Hitler's view of Jews.

> [!tip] Model Response — Point of View / Sourcing
> The documents reflect Hitler's belief that Jews were a biologically distinct and inferior "race" that threatened the purity of the German Aryan nation. The Law for the Protection of German Blood categorizes Jews as not of "German or related blood," embedding the pseudoscientific assumption of racial hierarchy into law. From Hitler's perspective, racial separation was not persecution but necessary protection of the German nation — an assumption rooted in scientific racism and anti-Semitism that he had articulated in *Mein Kampf*.

---

**SAQ 1C.** Explain ONE way the German state's actions reflected Germany's aggressive militarism after 1933.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Hitler systematically violated the Treaty of Versailles' military restrictions. In March 1935, he announced the creation of a German air force and reinstated conscription to rebuild the army. In March 1936, German troops occupied the Rhineland buffer zone — a direct military violation of Versailles. When Britain and France only protested without acting, Hitler interpreted their inaction as a green light for further aggression. Each unchallenged military step reinforced his belief that the Western powers would not fight, emboldening him toward the annexations and invasions that started WWII.

## Quick Review — High-Priority Connections

**Causation chain to memorize:**
WWI → Versailles humiliation → Great Depression → Nazi electoral rise → Hitler appointed chancellor (1933) → rearmament → Rhineland → Anschluss → Munich (appeasement) → Czechoslovakia → Poland invasion → WWII

**Three categories of WWII causes (from AMSCO diagram p. 507):**
- *Diplomatic:* Treaty of Versailles imbalance; failure of appeasement; failure of League of Nations
- *Economic:* Global Depression; Versailles economic burdens
- *Political:* Nazi ideology / Hitler's ambitions; Japanese militarism

**Key term pairs to distinguish:**
- Nuremberg Laws (1935, legal discrimination) vs. Kristallnacht (1938, physical violence)
- Rome-Berlin Axis (1936, fascist alliance) vs. German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact (1939, tactical deal between enemies)
- Anschluss (Austria absorbed, 1938) vs. Munich Agreement (Sudetenland, 1938) vs. Invasion of Czechoslovakia (1939, appeasement ends)
