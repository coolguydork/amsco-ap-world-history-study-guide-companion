# Causes of World War II

**AP Period 4 | Unit 7: Global Conflict | Printed pp. 503–510**

**Essential Question:** What combination of factors caused World War II, and how did they build on the legacy of WWI?

**AP Themes:** GOV (state power, ideology, aggression), ECN (Depression's political effects), CDI (racism, fascism, nationalism)

**AP Skill:** Causation — identifying multiple, interlocking causes of a major historical event

---

## The Core Argument

World War II did not emerge from a single cause but from the convergence of several mutually reinforcing factors: the unresolved grievances of WWI (especially the punishing Treaty of Versailles), the [[Concept Notes/Great Depression\|Great Depression]]'s political destabilization of Europe, the rise of fascist and militarist regimes willing to use force, and the failure of Western democracies to respond effectively through collective security or military deterrence.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently asks you to explain the *causes* of WWII using evidence. Know at least three categories: (1) unresolved WWI tensions / Treaty of Versailles; (2) Great Depression enabling extremist politics; (3) failure of appeasement / League of Nations. Each maps to a different AP theme (GOV, ECN, CDI).

---

## Background: Unresolved WWI Grievances

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed harsh economic reparations, territorial losses, and military limits on Germany. The [[Concept Notes/Weimar Republic\|Weimar Republic]] that accepted these terms was politically weak from the start. During the Great Depression, unemployment soared and extremist parties — especially the [[Concept Notes/Nazism\|Nazis]] — gained mass support by promising to restore German greatness and repudiate Versailles. In Italy, similar economic distress had already brought Mussolini's fascists to power in 1922.

## The Rise of Hitler and Nazi Germany (1933–1935)

After the 1932 elections gave the Nazis a parliamentary plurality, President Paul von Hindenburg appointed Hitler chancellor in January 1933. Hindenburg's death the following year allowed Hitler to merge the offices of chancellor and president. The Nazis then used manufactured crises — most famously the Reichstag fire (1933) — to suspend civil liberties and ban opposition parties.

Hitler's ideology combined several elements into a totalitarian program:
- **Racial supremacy** (Aryan master race, pseudoscientific racism)
- **Anti-Semitism** — scapegoating Jews for Germany's problems
- **Lebensraum** — the drive to conquer Eastern Europe for "living space"
- **Ultranationalism** — repudiating Versailles, rearming Germany

The **Nuremberg Laws** (1935) stripped Jews of citizenship and forbade marriage with non-Jews, institutionalizing persecution as state policy. **Kristallnacht** (November 1938) — the "Night of Broken Glass" — saw state-orchestrated pogroms destroy synagogues and send 30,000 Jews to concentration camps, marking a decisive escalation toward the Holocaust.

## The Axis Alliance System

Hitler built alliances with ideologically compatible regimes:

- **Rome-Berlin Axis** (1936) — military pact with Mussolini's Italy
- **Anti-Comintern Pact** (1936) — Germany and Japan, later Italy, pledged mutual opposition to communism
- **Axis Powers** — the three-nation bloc formalized by the Tripartite Pact (1940), opposing the Allied Powers

> [!tip]
> The Axis was united more by shared ideology (fascism/militarism) and opposition to communism than by deep strategic coordination. This internal tension ultimately weakened Axis war planning.

## German Territorial Expansion and Appeasement

Hitler systematically dismantled Versailles restrictions, each time testing whether Britain and France would respond:

| Year | Action | Western Response |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| 1935 | Rearmament — conscription and air force | Protest only |
| 1936 | Troops enter Rhineland buffer zone | Protest only |
| 1936–38 | Support for Franco in Spanish Civil War | Non-intervention |
| Mar 1938 | **Anschluss** — Austria absorbed into Third Reich | Protest only |
| Sep 1938 | **Munich Agreement** — Sudetenland given to Germany | Appeasement |
| Mar 1939 | Invasion of all Czechoslovakia | Policy collapses |
| Sep 1939 | Invasion of Poland | **War declared** |

Britain's policy of **appeasement** — exemplified by Neville Chamberlain at Munich — rested on the assumption that concessions would satisfy Hitler's ambitions. Instead, each concession emboldened him. When Hitler demanded the Polish port of **Danzig**, Britain and France finally pledged to defend Poland.

---

## The German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact and the Outbreak of War

Hitler secured his eastern flank through the **German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact** (August 23, 1939), secretly promising Stalin control of eastern Poland and the Baltic States in exchange for Soviet neutrality. This freed Germany to invade Poland without fear of a two-front war. On September 1, 1939, Germany invaded Poland. Britain and France declared war on September 3 — the official start of WWII in Europe.

---

## Japan's Parallel Expansion in Asia

In Asia, Japan had been aggressively expanding since 1931 (invasion of Manchuria, creation of **Manchukuo**). By 1937, Japan launched a full-scale invasion of China. The Nanjing Massacre demonstrated the brutality of Japanese imperial warfare against civilians. Japan's expansion was driven by resource scarcity and military nationalism, parallel to European fascism but with distinct regional roots.

> [!exam-tip]
> WWII began in two theaters driven by related but distinct causes: in Europe, Nazi ideology and failed appeasement; in Asia, Japanese militarism and imperial expansion. The AP exam may ask you to compare these two paths to war.

---

## Why the League of Nations Failed

The League lacked enforcement mechanisms and U.S. participation. When Japan invaded Manchuria (1931) and Italy invaded Ethiopia (1935), the League condemned but could not stop them. This demonstrated that collective security had failed, emboldening further aggression.
