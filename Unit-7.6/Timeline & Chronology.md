# Timeline & Chronology

**Topic 7.6 — Causes of World War II | Printed pp. 503–510**

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1922 | Mussolini's Fascists take power in Italy | Fascism's first major success; becomes model for Nazi ideology |
| 1924–25 | Hitler writes *Mein Kampf* in prison | Primary source: lays out Nazi ideology (anti-Semitism, Lebensraum, Aryan supremacy) years before WWII |
| 1929 | Great Depression begins | Destabilizes Weimar Republic; enables Nazi electoral rise |
| Jan 1933 | Hitler appointed chancellor of Germany | Legal takeover; Weimar Republic begins to collapse |
| Feb 1933 | Reichstag fire; civil liberties suspended | Manufactured crisis used to justify dictatorship |
| 1933 | Nazis ban all opposition parties | Nazi totalitarian state consolidated |
| Sep 1935 | Nuremberg Laws enacted | Legal institutionalization of anti-Semitism; citizenship stripped from Jews |
| Mar 1935 | Germany announces rearmament | Violation of Treaty of Versailles; Britain and France protest only |
| Oct 1936 | Rome-Berlin Axis formed | Germany-Italy military-ideological pact |
| Nov 1936 | Anti-Comintern Pact (Germany-Japan) | Ideological basis for Axis; later joined by Italy |
| Mar 1936 | German troops enter Rhineland | First territorial violation of Versailles; West does nothing |
| 1936–1939 | Spanish Civil War | Germany and Italy support Franco; test weapons and tactics; preview of WWII |
| 1931 | Japan invades Manchuria; creates Manchukuo | First major challenge to League of Nations; begins Japanese imperial expansion in Asia |
| 1937 | Japan invades China; Nanjing Massacre | Total war vs. civilians; WWII begins in Asia |
| Nov 1938 | Kristallnacht | State-orchestrated anti-Jewish pogroms; decisive escalation toward Holocaust |
| Mar 1938 | Anschluss — Austria annexed | Third Reich expands; West does not act |
| Sep 1938 | Munich Agreement; Sudetenland ceded | Peak of appeasement; Britain and France sacrifice Czechoslovakia for "peace" |
| Mar 1939 | Germany seizes all of Czechoslovakia | Appeasement definitively fails; Britain and France pledge to defend Poland |
| Aug 23, 1939 | German-Soviet Nonaggression Pact | Hitler secures eastern flank; Stalin gains territory; the stage is set |
| Sep 1, 1939 | Germany invades Poland | WWII officially begins in Europe |
| Sep 3, 1939 | Britain and France declare war on Germany | WWI's "mutual defense" dynamic repeats at a larger scale |

## Chronological Patterns — AP Exam Notes

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam sometimes provides a map, cartoon, or passage and asks you to place it in context. Key chronological anchors: Nuremberg Laws (1935) → Kristallnacht (1938) → Munich (1938) → invasion of Poland (1939). These form the escalation sequence from discrimination to war.

> [!tip]
> **Asia vs. Europe timelines:** WWII began in Asia in 1937 (Japan's invasion of China) — two years before Europe (1939). The AP sometimes asks about this distinction. The two theaters shared causes (militarism, failed collective security) but developed independently until Pearl Harbor (1941) linked them.

> [!warning]
> Do not confuse the **Anti-Comintern Pact** (1936 — Germany and Japan, ideological) with the **German-Soviet [[Concept Notes/Nonaggression Pact\|Nonaggression Pact]]** (1939 — Germany and the USSR, strategic). They are essentially opposites in purpose: the first expressed anti-communism; the second temporarily suspended it for Hitler's tactical benefit.
