# Timeline & Chronology

*Topic 8.9 synthesizes Unit 8. This timeline covers the major Cold War and decolonization events relevant to AP causation arguments.*

---

## Master Timeline: Cold War & Decolonization

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1945 | WWII ends; U.S. drops atomic bombs on Hiroshima & Nagasaki | Nuclear age begins; U.S. emerges as sole nuclear power — briefly |
| 1945–1947 | Yalta and Potsdam conferences; Soviet occupation of Eastern Europe | Immediate cause of Cold War: disagreements over Eastern Europe's future |
| 1947 | Truman Doctrine; Marshall Plan announced | Containment policy formalized; Western Europe begins U.S.-funded recovery |
| 1949 | USSR tests first nuclear weapon; NATO founded; Chinese Communist Revolution | Arms race accelerates; Cold War goes global; First World gains military alliance |
| 1950–1953 | Korean War | First major Cold War proxy war; U.S. containment tested; Korea permanently divided |
| 1955 | Bandung Conference (Indonesia) | 29 Asian/African nations launch Non-Aligned Movement; Third World articulates sovereignty |
| 1955 | Warsaw Pact founded | Soviet military alliance formalizes Eastern European satellite state system |
| c. 1945–1975 | Decolonization wave: 100+ new nations created | Most rapid redrawing of the global map in history; Cold War intersects with independence |
| 1956 | Suez Crisis | Egypt (Nasser) nationalizes canal; Britain/France/Israel attack; U.S. and USSR both oppose; demonstrates limits of old colonial powers |
| 1957 | USSR launches Sputnik | Space race begins; technological competition as Cold War proxy |
| 1959 | Cuban Revolution (Castro) | Cold War enters Western Hemisphere; Soviet-aligned communist government 90 miles from U.S. |
| 1960 | UN meeting of non-aligned leaders (Nehru, Nasser, Nkrumah, Sukarno, Tito) | Peak of Non-Aligned Movement symbolism |
| 1961 | Berlin Wall constructed | Iron Curtain physically visible; East Germany prevents emigration |
| 1962 | Cuban Missile Crisis | Brinkmanship at its peak; closest the Cold War came to nuclear war |
| 1965–1975 | Vietnam War peak years | Deadliest Cold War proxy war; ~4 million total deaths; U.S. withdraws 1973; South Vietnam falls 1975 |
| 1975 | Angola independence from Portugal; civil war begins | Cold War proxy war in Africa: Soviet/Cuban vs. U.S.-backed factions |
| 1979 | Soviet invasion of Afghanistan | Last major Soviet military expansion; became USSR's "Vietnam" |
| 1980s | Welfare state under pressure in the West; Eastern bloc economic stagnation | Economic divergence between capitalist and communist blocs widens |
| 1989 | Berlin Wall falls | Symbolic end of the Cold War; Eastern European satellite states begin breaking free |
| 1991 | USSR dissolves; Cold War ends | U.S. emerges as sole superpower; Eastern European economies begin painful market transitions |
| 1991–1999 | Yugoslav Wars | Power vacuum after Tito + Soviet collapse → ethnic nationalism explodes; genocide in Bosnia |
| Post-1991 | WTO founded (1995); global institutions expand | Cooperative multilateral framework replaces bipolar balance-of-power system |

## Decolonization Chronology by Region

| Period | Region | Key Events |
|--------|--------|------------|
| 1945–1955 | South/Southeast Asia | India & Pakistan (1947), Indonesia (1949), Vietnam war vs. France (1945–1954) |
| 1950s–1960s | Middle East & North Africa | Egypt (1952 revolution), Algerian War of Independence (1954–1962) |
| 1957–1965 | Sub-Saharan Africa | Ghana (1957), most francophone Africa (1960), Kenya (1963) |
| 1960s–1975 | Southern Africa / Portuguese colonies | Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau — all violent independence struggles |
| 1970s–1980s | Caribbean / Pacific | Continued decolonization of smaller islands and territories |

> [!exam-tip]
> For AP essays, the key decolonization dates are: 1947 (India/Pakistan — peaceful model), 1954 (Vietnam defeats France — armed resistance model), 1960 ("Year of Africa" — 17 nations gain independence), 1975 (Angola — Cold War proxy war model). These show the **range of processes** within decolonization.

## Cause → Effect Chain for AP Essays

```
WWII ends (1945)
    ↓
Power vacuum + ideological rivalry → Cold War begins
    ↓
Containment + Soviet expansion → world divided into three blocs
    ↓
Proxy wars in Asia, Africa, Latin America
    ↓
Political: governments destabilized; authoritarian regimes backed by superpowers
Economic: Marshall Plan boosts West; Third World locked in neo-colonial dependency
Social: millions killed in proxy wars; McCarthyism; Soviet repression
Cultural: East-West cultural divergence; colonial language legacy persists
    ↓
1991: USSR collapses
    ↓
Global institutions expand (UN, WTO); but ethnic conflicts and inequality persist
```
