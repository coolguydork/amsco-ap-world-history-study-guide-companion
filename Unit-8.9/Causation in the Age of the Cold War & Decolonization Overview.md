# Causation in the Age of the Cold War & Decolonization

**AP Period 4 | Unit 8 | Printed pp. 615–629**
**AP Themes:** GOV, ECN, SIO, CDI | **AP Skills:** Causation, Argumentation

**Essential Question:** Why and to what extent were the effects of the Cold War similar in the Eastern and Western Hemispheres?

---

## The Big Picture

Topic 8.9 is a synthesis and causation chapter. AMSCO uses it to pull Unit 8 together by asking one central question: in what ways did the Cold War produce similar or different effects across the globe? The answer is the foundation of every LEQ and DBQ you will write about this era.

The post-WWII world reorganized around two superpowers — the United States and the Soviet Union — whose ideological rivalry extended far beyond Europe. The Cold War was at once a political contest, an economic competition, and a cultural war. Its effects were global, touching every region through proxy wars, economic aid, decolonization pressures, and social repression.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam loves "extent to which" prompts about the Cold War. Your default structure is a **similarity** argument (both hemispheres experienced superpower interference, proxy wars, and economic development challenges) modified by a **difference** (Eastern Hemisphere bore a disproportionate military cost; Western Hemisphere had earlier independence). Always distinguish Eastern vs. Western Hemisphere and economic vs. political vs. social effects.

---

## Three Cold War Alignments

The Cold War divided the world into three blocs:

- **First World:** The United States and its democratic, free-market allies ([[Concept Notes/NATO\|NATO]] members, Western Europe, Japan)
- **Second World:** The Soviet Union, Eastern Europe (Soviet bloc), China, and other communist states
- **Third World (Non-Aligned):** Former colonial states in Asia, Africa, and Latin America that tried to avoid alignment — the Non-Aligned Movement

Most Third World countries were in the Eastern Hemisphere (Asia, Africa, Oceania) or Latin America. Their colonial pasts made them suspicious of both superpowers, yet both superpowers competed intensely to draw them into their orbit.

> [!tip]
> "Third World" is a Cold War term meaning non-aligned, not a synonym for "developing." On the AP exam, use it in context but know it refers to the non-aligned bloc of mostly postcolonial states.

## Political Effects of the Cold War

**Europe:** The continent split along the Iron Curtain. West Germany and East Germany became separate states. Western Europe retained democratic governments; Eastern Europe became Soviet satellite states under authoritarian communist rule. NATO and the Warsaw Pact formalized the military divide.

**Asia (Eastern Hemisphere):** The Cold War produced some of its bloodiest proxy wars here. The Korean War (1950–1953) divided the peninsula. The Vietnam War (1955–1975) killed approximately 4 million people. Communist revolutions took Cambodia and Laos. The Soviets invaded Afghanistan in 1979. Anti-communist crackdowns struck Indonesia and the Philippines.

**Africa (Eastern Hemisphere):** Decolonization and Cold War competition intersected fatally. Angola's independence war from Portugal became a superpower proxy conflict — Soviet-backed Cuban troops vs. U.S.-backed UNITA rebels. Communist governments took power in Ethiopia and Angola. These conflicts extended the decolonization era and deepened poverty.

**Latin America (Western Hemisphere):** Cuba's 1959 revolution brought the Cold War directly to the Western Hemisphere. Communist insurrections occurred in Nicaragua, El Salvador, Colombia, Peru, and Guatemala. The U.S. consistently backed anti-communist governments — often military dictatorships — against Soviet- or Cuban-backed insurgencies.

> [!warning]
> Do not conflate all Third World revolutions as Soviet-driven. Many independence movements were primarily nationalist rather than ideologically communist. The U.S. often misread nationalism as communism, a key cause of Cold War overreach.

## Economic Effects of the Cold War

**Western Europe:** The Marshall Plan (U.S. economic aid) rebuilt war-devastated economies. Western governments created **welfare states** — public healthcare, housing, unemployment insurance, and pensions — partly to blunt the appeal of communism. Result: economic boom, the "economic miracle" of the 1950s–1960s.

**Eastern Bloc:** Command economies struggled to recover. The military-industrial complex consumed ~20% of the Soviet labor force. After the Cold War, Eastern bloc nations faced painful transitions to free-market economies. Russia's economy declined sharply in the 1990s. China, taking a more gradual path, became a global economic powerhouse.

**Developing/Third World Countries:** Many former colonies remained economically dependent on their former colonizers — exporting raw materials, importing manufactured goods. This neo-colonial relationship was a key grievance. States like Angola used resource revenues (oil) to rebuild infrastructure after civil wars ended.

> [!exam-tip]
> For LEQ prompts about economic effects: the key argument is that **geography and colonial legacy determined economic outcomes more than ideology alone**. Western Europe had Marshall Plan aid and existing infrastructure. The Third World lacked both and remained dependent on raw material exports.

## Social & Cultural Effects of the Cold War

**Social costs:** Proxy wars displaced millions. Vietnam alone saw ~4 million deaths and mass urbanization as refugees fled the countryside to Saigon. McCarthyism in the U.S. and political prison camps in the Soviet Union showed that social repression accompanied ideological competition on both sides.

**Cultural divergence:** Western Europe, aided by U.S. investment and political freedom, experienced a cultural renaissance — science, music, art, architecture flourished. Eastern Europe, under state censorship, had limited cultural output beyond government-approved work.

**Cultural legacy of colonialism:** Decolonization did not erase imperial cultural imprints. European languages (French, Portuguese, English, Dutch/Afrikaans, Spanish) remained dominant across Sub-Saharan Africa. Colonial-era borders stayed in place, setting the stage for ethnic conflict. When superpowers withdrew (Yugoslavia, Afghanistan), power vacuums triggered violent ethnic clashes.

> [!tip]
> Yugoslavia is a perfect DBQ/LEQ example of a **continuity**: Cold War-era suppression of ethnic nationalism did not resolve it — those tensions erupted once Soviet power retreated (1991 Yugoslav Wars, Slobodan Milošević).

## Development of Global Institutions

As the Cold War ended and globalization accelerated, the world moved from a balance-of-power framework toward international cooperation:

- **[[Concept Notes/United Nations\|United Nations]]** — conflict resolution, human rights, peacekeeping
- **World Trade Organization (WTO)** — global trade rules
- Environmental organizations — addressing climate change and environmental degradation
- Human rights organizations — enforcing international norms
- International health organizations — responding to epidemic diseases

> [!exam-tip]
> Global institutions are a key **consequence** of the Cold War's end. AP prompts may ask you to argue whether post-Cold War globalization represented change or continuity from Cold War-era international relations. Argument: **change** (cooperative multilateral institutions replaced bipolar rivalry), but qualified by **continuity** (U.S. remained dominant; Global South economic dependency persisted).

## AP Argument Framework for 8.9

The most common essay structure for this topic:

**Thesis (similarity modified by difference):**
"While the Cold War produced similar political disruptions in both hemispheres through superpower-backed proxy wars and interference in newly independent states, the Eastern Hemisphere bore a disproportionately greater military and economic cost due to the concentration of decolonization conflicts in Asia and Africa."

**Three body paragraphs:**
1. **Political similarity** — both hemispheres experienced superpower interference and proxy wars (Korea/Vietnam in Asia; Cuba/Nicaragua in Latin America)
2. **Economic similarity with difference** — welfare state development in the West; neo-colonial dependency in the Third World; collapse in the Eastern bloc
3. **Social/Cultural divergence** — Cold War suppressed culture in the East; unleashed it in the West; colonial legacy persisted in postcolonial states
