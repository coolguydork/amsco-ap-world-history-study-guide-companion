# Themes & Historical Reasoning

**AP Skill Focus: Causation | Argumentation**

## Causation Framework for the Cold War

The AP skill of **Causation** requires identifying causes, explaining mechanisms, and analyzing effects. Topic 8.9 provides the synthesis framework for applying causation to the entire Cold War era.

### Causes of the Cold War

**Long-term causes:**
- The ideological incompatibility of liberal capitalism and Marxist-Leninist communism, dating to 1917
- Soviet suspicion of Western intentions based on the Allied intervention in the Russian Civil War (1918–1921)
- Western fear of communist revolution spreading to industrial democracies

**Short-term / immediate causes:**
- The power vacuum left by the destruction of WWII — only the U.S. and USSR had sufficient military and economic strength to fill it
- Disagreements at Yalta and Potsdam over the future of Eastern Europe
- Soviet refusal to withdraw from Eastern European territories occupied during the war
- China's communist revolution (1949) and the fall of the Berlin Blockade

> [!exam-tip]
> AP causation essays reward **distinguishing long-term structural causes from immediate triggers**. Long-term = ideological incompatibility, historical mistrust. Immediate = WWII power vacuum, Yalta/Potsdam disagreements.

## GOV — Governance: Competing Systems and Their Effects

The Cold War was fundamentally a contest between **liberal democratic capitalism** and **Soviet communism** as organizing principles for post-WWII society. Its effects on governance were profound:

- **Western Europe:** Consolidated liberal democracy; governments created welfare states to reduce inequality without abandoning capitalism; [[Concept Notes/NATO\|NATO]] guaranteed security
- **Eastern Europe:** Authoritarian communist governments under Soviet oversight; satellite state system; limited political freedom
- **Third World:** Newly independent states faced intense pressure from both superpowers to adopt their preferred model; many experienced coups, civil wars, or authoritarian governments backed by one superpower or the other

Continuity: Colonial-era borders, often drawn without regard to ethnic or linguistic communities, remained in place after decolonization — a structural source of governance instability that outlasted the Cold War.

> [!warning]
> Don't argue that the Cold War "caused" authoritarianism in the Third World — colonialism had already embedded authoritarian structures. The Cold War **amplified and extended** those structures by backing authoritarian anti-communist regimes.

## ECN — Economic Systems: Three Models Under Pressure

The Cold War accelerated the differentiation of three economic models:

**1. Mixed-market welfare capitalism (Western Europe, Japan, U.S.)**
Free markets combined with robust state intervention: public healthcare, pensions, unemployment insurance. Driven partly by ideological competition with communism and partly by the Keynesian consensus that state spending could prevent depression-era instability.

**2. Soviet command economy (Eastern bloc)**
State ownership of the means of production, central planning, and prioritization of military-industrial output. Initially rapid industrialization, but increasingly inefficient. Collapsed under the weight of military spending, technological stagnation, and the inability to compete with Western consumer economies.

**3. Neo-colonial dependency (Third World)**
Former colonies remained locked in extractive economies — exporting raw materials, importing manufactured goods. The terms of trade consistently favored industrial nations. Superpower competition occasionally provided aid, but usually with political strings attached. The Non-Aligned Movement and the G-77 tried to renegotiate these terms; results were limited.

## SIO — Social Structures: Upheaval and Repression

**Social costs of Cold War proxy wars** were concentrated in the Global South — Southeast Asia paid an especially high price. Vietnam alone saw approximately 4 million deaths (soldiers and civilians combined), mass displacement, and long-term environmental damage from chemical defoliants.

**Social repression** existed on both sides: McCarthyism in the U.S. destroyed careers based on suspected communist sympathies; Soviet political dissidents faced gulags and forced internal exile. The logic of ideological conformity subordinated individual rights to state security.

**Social mobility and urbanization:** Proxy wars accelerated urbanization as rural populations fled conflict zones. Saigon tripled in size during the Vietnam War. This pattern repeated across Cold War conflict zones — forced urbanization without the economic infrastructure to support it.

**Post-colonial cultural continuity:** Despite formal independence, colonialism's cultural imprints persisted. European languages remained the languages of government, education, and commerce across much of Sub-Saharan Africa. This continuity facilitated neo-colonial economic relationships and reflected the incomplete nature of decolonization.

## CDI — Cultural & Demographic Interactions

**Cultural divergence within Europe:** Western Europe experienced a cultural renaissance — science, arts, music, architecture flourished with American support and political freedom. Eastern Europe had limited cultural output beyond state-approved art and science, though it produced notable achievements in mathematics and space technology.

**Migration and cultural blending:** The decolonization era triggered large-scale migration from former colonies to metropoles (see [[Unit-8.6/Newly Independent States Overview\|Newly Independent States Overview]]). Algerians moved to France; West Indians to Britain; Indonesians to the Netherlands. These migrations transformed European demographics and culture.

**Colonial language legacy:** French, Portuguese, English, Afrikaans, and Spanish remained widely spoken across Sub-Saharan Africa. This linguistic legacy tied former colonies to metropoles economically and culturally — a dimension of neo-colonialism.

**Power vacuums and ethnic violence:** When Cold War-era states collapsed, suppressed ethnic and religious identities re-emerged violently. Yugoslavia's dissolution (1991–1999) killed tens of thousands and created hundreds of thousands of refugees. Afghanistan's Soviet withdrawal (1989) triggered a civil war that eventually brought the Taliban to power.

## Comparing Eastern and Western Hemisphere Effects

The core AP causation question for this topic is **why effects were similar and different** across hemispheres:

| Dimension | Similarity | Eastern Hemisphere Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Political | Superpower interference in domestic politics | More proxy wars; more direct military conflict |
| Economic | State-backed economic development; Cold War aid | Decolonization + Cold War = compounded disruption |
| Social | Displacement, repression, urbanization | Greater military casualties; colonial legacy of poverty |
| Cultural | Superpower cultural competition | Post-independence cultural nationalism vs. colonial legacy |

> [!exam-tip]
> **The strongest AP argument** is that similarity was real but difference was one of **degree and duration**: both hemispheres experienced superpower interference, but the Eastern Hemisphere (particularly Asia and Africa) bore a disproportionately greater human cost because decolonization and Cold War proxy conflicts overlapped simultaneously.
