## Long Essay Questions (LEQ)

*From AMSCO pp. 625–626. Suggested time: 40 minutes each.*

**LEQ 1:**
After World War II, Europe weakened and the Soviet Union and the United States emerged as superpowers, creating policies that affected postcolonial states. Develop an argument evaluating the extent to which the effects of the Cold War were similar or different in the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

**LEQ 2:**
During the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union used technology, military might, and economic power to promote their competing ideologies. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which the ways the United States and the Soviet Union sought to maintain influence over the course of the Cold War were similar or different.

**LEQ 3:**
After 1900, countries in Eurasia, Africa, and Latin America experienced communist revolutions or insurrections, many of which involved land reform. Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which various causes and effects of movements to redistribute economic resources were significant.

**LEQ 4:**
From 1900 to the present, colonies in Africa, Latin America, and Asia developed nationalist movements and became independent states. Develop an argument evaluating the extent to which the process of decolonization resulted in economic change or continuity over time.

---

## Model LEQ Thesis Statements

> [!tip] Model Thesis — LEQ 1 (Similar vs. Different Effects)
> While the Cold War produced broadly similar political disruptions in both hemispheres — superpower-backed proxy wars, intervention in domestic politics, and economic development pressures — the Eastern Hemisphere bore a disproportionately greater military and humanitarian cost because decolonization and Cold War proxy conflicts overlapped simultaneously in Asia and Africa, producing a scale of violence and instability absent from most of the Western Hemisphere.

> [!tip] Model Thesis — LEQ 2 (U.S. vs. USSR Methods)
> Although the United States and Soviet Union used similar instruments of power — military alliances, economic aid, and ideological promotion — to maintain Cold War influence, they differed significantly in their methods: the U.S. relied primarily on economic leverage (Marshall Plan, aid packages) and covert intervention, while the Soviet Union depended more heavily on direct military presence in satellite states and overt military support for communist insurgencies.

> [!tip] Model Thesis — LEQ 4 (Decolonization: Economic Change or Continuity)
> While decolonization produced significant political change by creating 100+ new independent states between 1945 and 2000, it resulted in substantial economic continuity for most former colonies, which remained locked in neo-colonial relationships of raw material dependency, unequal trade, and financial subordination to former colonial powers and new superpower patrons.

## Document-Based Question (DBQ)

*From AMSCO pp. 626–629. Suggested time: 15 min planning + 45 min writing.*

**Prompt:** Develop an argument that evaluates the extent to which decolonization had an impact on the political actions of former colonial powers and newly independent nations during the Cold War.

---

### Document Summaries & Analysis

**Document 1 — Sukarno, Bandung Conference Opening Speech (1955)**
*Point of View:* President of Indonesia and host of Bandung; leader of a newly independent nation whose own independence required armed struggle against the Dutch. His framing of colonialism as a source of global fear reflects the perspective of states that suffered colonial rule.
*Key argument:* "We are united by a common detestation of colonialism in whatever form it appears." The Non-Aligned Movement was fundamentally anti-colonial, not merely anti-superpower.

**Document 2 — Jawaharlal Nehru, Bandung Conference Speech (1955)**
*Point of View:* Prime Minister of India; led India's independence struggle through nonviolent means; wary of being drawn into superpower conflicts.
*Key argument:* Superpower military thinking produces "false values." If the world divides into two blocs, "the inevitable result would be war." Nehru articulates the Third World's interest in avoiding entanglement in Cold War competition.

**Document 3 — Eisenhower Doctrine on the Middle East (1957)**
*Point of View:* U.S. President; framing the Middle East in Cold War terms to justify U.S. involvement in a region historically dominated by Britain and France.
*Key argument:* The U.S. "supports without reservation the full sovereignty and independence of each and every nation of the Middle East" — while also positioning itself to intervene against "International Communism." Reveals the U.S. use of anti-colonial rhetoric to extend Cold War influence.

> [!tip] Document 3 HAPP Analysis
> **Historical Situation:** The Suez Crisis (1956) had just embarrassed Britain and France, creating a power vacuum in the Middle East. Eisenhower is seizing the moment to insert U.S. influence.
> **Purpose:** To justify U.S. military intervention in the Middle East against Soviet-backed actors.
> **Audience:** Joint session of Congress — domestic political framing for military authorization.
> **Point of View:** Anti-colonial in rhetoric, interventionist in practice — reveals the contradiction in U.S. Cold War policy.

**Document 4 — Photograph: Non-Aligned Leaders at UN (1960)**
*Context:* September 1960 UN General Assembly. Nehru (India), Nkrumah (Ghana), Nasser (Egypt), Sukarno (Indonesia), Tito (Yugoslavia) — five major non-aligned leaders meeting.
*Key argument (visual):** The Non-Aligned Movement spanned continents and included the largest postcolonial states — demonstrating that decolonization produced a genuinely global political force that resisted Cold War binary.

> [!tip] Document 4 HAPP Analysis
> **Historical Situation:** 1960 was the "Year of Africa" — 17 African nations gained independence. The non-aligned bloc was rapidly growing.
> **Purpose:** Press photograph — meant to document and publicize the meeting of key non-aligned leaders.
> **Point of View:** The visual composition (five leaders of roughly equal prominence) emphasizes solidarity and equality — contrasting with the hierarchical Cold War bipolar system.

**Document 5 — U.S. State Department on Decolonization of Asia and Africa (1945–1960)**
*Point of View:* U.S. government historians; retrospective official account of U.S. Cold War policy toward newly independent states. Written with the benefit of hindsight.
*Key argument:* The U.S. feared that as colonial powers withdrew, Soviet-backed communist parties might gain power in new states. This fear drove U.S. aid, technical assistance, and military intervention. Confirms that the U.S. actively tried to steer decolonization outcomes.

> [!tip] Document 5 HAPP Analysis
> **Historical Situation:** Written retrospectively by U.S. government historians — acknowledges U.S. interventionism more candidly than contemporary government statements would have.
> **Purpose:** Official historical documentation of U.S. foreign policy — part of the public record, not classified.
> **Point of View:** U.S. institutional perspective; frames U.S. intervention as defensive (preventing Soviet gains) rather than as a new form of imperialism — the perspective that newly independent nations would dispute.

**Document 6 — Soviet Cartoon (1956): John Bull serving U.S. interests over Arab oil**
*Point of View:* Soviet state propaganda; depicts Britain as subordinate to the U.S. in extracting Arab resources.
*Key argument:* Soviet framing of U.S.-British imperialism in the Middle East as economic exploitation — resonant with anti-colonial sentiment across the Third World. Shows how both superpowers tried to use anti-colonialism as a propaganda tool.

> [!tip] Document 6 HAPP Analysis
> **Historical Situation:** 1956 Suez Crisis — Britain and France attacked Egypt after Nasser nationalized the canal. The U.S. forced them to withdraw.
> **Purpose:** Soviet propaganda targeting Third World audiences; paints the U.S. as the new imperial master replacing Britain.
> **Point of View:** Soviet government perspective; uses anti-colonial imagery to position the USSR as sympathetic to newly independent nations — though Soviet support was equally self-interested.

### Additional Evidence (Beyond Documents)

Use these outside examples to earn the complexity point or to support your argument:

- **Korean War (1950–1953):** U.S.-backed South Korea vs. Soviet/Chinese-backed North Korea — shows how decolonization-era nationalism in Asia intersected with Cold War superpower rivalry
- **Cuban Revolution (1959):** Castro's victory brought Cold War to Western Hemisphere; shows U.S. intervention when a Western Hemisphere state chose a communist path
- **Angolan Civil War (1975–2002):** Soviet/Cuban vs. U.S.-backed factions in a post-colonial state — shows proxy war extending decolonization's instability
- **Bandung Conference outcomes:** Led to G-77 coalition (1964) that tried to leverage Third World economic bargaining power against industrialized nations

### DBQ Thesis Models

> [!tip] Model DBQ Thesis — Extent Argument (Significant Impact)
> Decolonization significantly shaped the political actions of both former colonial powers and newly independent nations during the Cold War: colonial powers redefined their roles from direct rulers to Cold War-aligned patrons (Documents 3, 5), while newly independent nations leveraged their non-aligned status to resist Cold War pressure and assert sovereignty (Documents 1, 2, 4). However, decolonization's impact was limited by the superpowers' success in co-opting newly independent states into the Cold War framework through aid, intervention, and economic dependency.

> [!tip] Model DBQ Thesis — Complexity Modifier
> While decolonization empowered newly independent nations to challenge Cold War bipolarity through the Non-Aligned Movement, former colonial powers and the United States used anti-colonial rhetoric to disguise new forms of influence — suggesting that decolonization changed the form of external interference without eliminating it.

## SAQ Practice

**SAQ 1 — Causation**
a) Briefly describe ONE cause of the Cold War following World War II.
b) Briefly describe ONE political effect of the Cold War in Asia.
c) Briefly explain ONE way the economic effects of the Cold War differed between Western Europe and newly independent nations in the Third World.

> [!tip] Model Response — SAQ 1a
> One cause of the Cold War was the ideological incompatibility between American liberal capitalism and Soviet communism, combined with disagreements at the Yalta and Potsdam conferences over the future of Eastern Europe. After WWII, the Soviet Union refused to withdraw from the Eastern European territories it occupied and established communist satellite states, which the U.S. interpreted as evidence of Soviet expansionism. This mutual distrust led to the formal establishment of containment policy (Truman Doctrine, 1947) and the beginning of the Cold War.

> [!tip] Model Response — SAQ 1b
> One political effect of the Cold War in Asia was the Korean War (1950–1953), in which the U.S. intervened under a UN mandate to defend South Korea against a North Korean invasion backed by the Soviet Union and China. The war ended in an armistice that left the Korean peninsula permanently divided at the 38th parallel, demonstrating how Cold War ideological competition produced direct military conflicts in Asia that the superpowers managed without fighting each other directly.

> [!tip] Model Response — SAQ 1c
> While Western Europe received U.S. Marshall Plan aid and rebuilt prosperous free-market economies with welfare state protections, newly independent Third World nations remained trapped in neo-colonial economic dependency — exporting raw materials at disadvantageous terms of trade and importing manufactured goods, with their economies tied to former colonial powers. Western Europe's recovery represented a genuine structural shift; Third World economic independence was more political than economic, as the terms of the global trading system remained stacked against commodity exporters.

## Key Vocabulary for Causation Essays

| Cause language | Effect language |
|---|---|
| caused, produced, triggered | resulted in, led to, generated |
| accelerated, intensified | deepened, extended, worsened |
| facilitated, enabled | made possible, created conditions for |
| was rooted in, stemmed from | had the consequence of |
| contributed to, was a factor in | played a role in producing |

> [!exam-tip]
> **Avoid weak language** on the AP exam. "The Cold War affected the economy" earns no credit. "The Cold War's containment policy caused the U.S. to fund the Marshall Plan, which facilitated Western Europe's economic recovery" is specific enough to earn a point.
