# Connections

## Within Unit 8: The Cold War Synthesis

Topic 8.9 synthesizes all of Unit 8. Every topic feeds into its causation framework:

**[[Unit-8.1/Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview\|Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview]]**
The structural preconditions for Cold War: WWII's destruction, the U.S./USSR power vacuum, and the beginning of decolonization. 8.9 builds directly on this foundation — the "stage" explains why the Cold War started.

**[[Unit-8.2/Cold War Overview\|Cold War Overview]]**
The mechanics of Cold War competition: containment, nuclear deterrence, alliances (NATO/Warsaw Pact), proxy wars. 8.9 uses these mechanisms to argue *effects* across hemispheres.

**[[Unit-8.3/Effects of the Cold War Overview\|Effects of the Cold War Overview]]**
The specific regional effects in Asia, Africa, and the Americas. The comparative political/economic/social framework in 8.9 synthesizes 8.3's regional detail into a global argument.

**[[Unit-8.4/Spread of Communism after 1900 Overview\|Spread of Communism after 1900 Overview]]**
Communism's spread to China, Korea, Vietnam, Cuba, Angola — the specific cases that drove U.S. containment responses and Cold War proxy wars. 8.9's political effects analysis depends on understanding which states went communist and why.

**[[Unit-8.5/Decolonization after 1900 Overview\|Decolonization after 1900 Overview]]**
The processes of decolonization — peaceful (India), armed (Algeria, Angola, Vietnam). 8.9's comparison of hemispheres is inseparable from understanding where and how decolonization occurred.

**[[Unit-8.6/Newly Independent States Overview\|Newly Independent States Overview]]**
Economic and political challenges of newly independent states, including neo-colonialism and migration to metropoles. 8.9's economic effects section (Third World dependency) builds on 8.6.

**[[Unit-8.7/Global Resistance to Established Power Structures Overview\|Global Resistance to Established Power Structures Overview]]**
Resistance movements from civil rights to anti-apartheid to feminist movements. 8.9's social effects framework connects to the global resistance these movements represented.

**[[Unit-8.8/End of the Cold War Overview\|End of the Cold War Overview]]**
The causes of Soviet collapse and Cold War's end. 8.9 picks up the consequences: post-Cold War power vacuums, ethnic conflicts, and the rise of global institutions.

## Cross-Unit Connections

**Unit 7: World Wars and Their Aftermath**
The Cold War's origins lie in WWII's destruction and the ideological contest crystallized by the Russian Revolution (7.1). The post-WWI mandate system and failed Wilson's self-determination promises (7.3) set up the decolonization pressures the Cold War era had to manage.

**Unit 9: Globalization (1900–present)**
The end of the Cold War accelerates globalization: WTO, international institutions, transnational corporations, and the spread of liberal democratic capitalism. 8.9's global institutions section is the direct bridge to Unit 9. See: **Global Connections[[Unit-9.1/Advances in Technology & Exchange Overview|Advances in Technology & Exchange Overview]]**.

**Units 4–5: European Colonialism and Industrialization**
Neo-colonialism (8.9) is the continuation of the extractive economic relationships established under European colonialism (Unit 4) and industrialization (Unit 5). The argument that decolonization did not end economic dependency requires understanding why those dependencies were created.

## AP Exam: Cross-Topic Essay Connections

**LEQ combinations most likely to draw on 8.9:**

- "Evaluate the extent to which the effects of the Cold War were similar or different in the Eastern and Western Hemispheres" → Pure 8.9 argument
- "Evaluate the extent to which decolonization resulted in economic change or continuity" → Requires 8.5 (process) + 8.6 (new state challenges) + 8.9 (neo-colonial dependency)
- "Evaluate the extent to which superpower competition shaped political developments in the second half of the 20th century" → Requires 8.2 (Cold War mechanics) + 8.3 (regional effects) + 8.9 (synthesis argument)
- "Evaluate the extent to which the causes of the Cold War were economic rather than ideological" → 8.1 (origins) + 8.9 (economic effects analysis) with 8.2

> [!exam-tip]
> 8.9 is the one topic most likely to be the synthesis target of any Unit 8 LEQ. If you master the Eastern vs. Western Hemisphere comparison framework from this topic, you can scaffold an argument for almost any Cold War prompt.
