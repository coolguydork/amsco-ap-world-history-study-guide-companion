## Chronological Patterns

**1945–1955: First wave of communist victories.** The post-WWII power vacuum in Asia allows communist movements — in China, North Korea, and Vietnam — to consolidate control, either through civil wars or Soviet-backed installations.

**1953–1960: U.S. counter-pressure and Chinese radicalization.** The U.S. overthrows democratic governments in Iran and Guatemala to contain perceived communist spread. Meanwhile, Mao launches the Great Leap Forward, demonstrating communist states' capacity for self-destructive policy.

**1959–1975: Expansion into the Western Hemisphere and Southeast Asia.** Cuban Revolution and the Vietnam War mark communism's most dramatic geographic advances. Both trigger massive U.S. military and political responses.

**1966–1979: Internal crises and alternative revolutions.** Cultural Revolution paralyzes China from within. Iran's 1979 revolution shows the Soviet-U.S. binary was incomplete — religious nationalism was a third path to revolutionary restructuring.

**1974–1991: Africa's communist moment and its collapse.** Soviet-aligned Marxist regimes in Ethiopia, Angola, and Mozambique rise with Cold War patronage, then collapse as Soviet support evaporates after 1985.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam may ask about **continuity and change** across this period. The consistent thread: wherever colonial or feudal land systems concentrated wealth, mass movements — communist or otherwise — arose to challenge them. What changed was the ideological framing and the Cold War superpower that backed or opposed each movement.
