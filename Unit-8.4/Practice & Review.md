## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Questions 1–3 refer to the table below (AMSCO p. 576)*

**China's Economy During Its First Five-Year Plan**

| Economic Sector | Value 1952 | % of Economy 1952 | Value 1957 | % of Economy 1957 |
|-----------------|------------|-------------------|------------|-------------------|
| Agriculture, Forestry & Fishing | 43 billion | 58% | 48 billion | 48% |
| Industry & Construction | 11 billion | 16% | 24 billion | 24% |
| Government | 4 billion | 6% | 4 billion | 4% |
| Other | 14 billion | 20% | 23 billion | 24% |

---

**Question 1.** Which row in this table most clearly supports the argument that China's Five-Year Plan from 1952 to 1957 was successful?

A. Agriculture, Forestry, and Fishing
B. Industry and Construction
C. Government
D. Other

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Industry and Construction doubled in value (11 → 24 billion) and grew from 16% to 24% of the economy. This is the strongest evidence of success because China's Five-Year Plan deliberately prioritized heavy industry, following the Soviet model. Agriculture's share shrank even though absolute value rose slightly — evidence the plan shifted resources toward industry, not a sign of agricultural success.

---

**Question 2.** Which of the following statements best describes the changes in the Chinese government sector between 1952 and 1957?

A. It remained the same size, but it became a larger part of the economy.
B. It remained the same size, but it became a smaller part of the economy.
C. It grew in size and became a larger part of the economy.
D. It shrank in size and became a smaller part of the economy.

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Government output held constant at 4 billion yuan, but its share of the total economy fell from 6% to 4%. The economy grew overall (total rose from ~72 to ~99 billion), so the government sector's unchanged absolute size became proportionally smaller. This rules out C and D, which claim the government's absolute size changed.

---

**Question 3.** Which statement provides the context for understanding this table?

A. China was following the path of Soviet economic development.
B. China viewed the United States as an economic model to follow.
C. China wanted consumer spending to lead economic growth.
D. China believed that it would soon move away from communism.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> China's Five-Year Plan explicitly mirrored the Soviet model: emphasis on heavy industry over agriculture or consumer goods, centralized state planning, and rapid industrialization targets. The table shows industry's share rising while agriculture's falls — the Soviet pattern. B and C contradict this: the U.S. was an ideological enemy, and consumer spending was not a priority. D is anachronistic — in 1952–1957, China was moving *toward* deeper communism (the Great Leap Forward came in 1958).

## Short-Answer Questions

**SAQ 1** — Use the Zhou Enlai letter to Stalin, October 1950 (AMSCO p. 577)

*A. Identify ONE historical development that contributed to the Soviet Union's ability to maintain its status as a global power after 1945.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Context
> The Soviet Union's successful test of an atomic bomb in 1949 — ending the U.S. nuclear monopoly — allowed the USSR to present itself as a military equal to the United States, sustaining its superpower status and enabling it to provide credible security guarantees to allied communist states like China and North Korea.

*B. Explain ONE way the Soviet Union maintained influence in Korea based on the passage.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Evidence from Source
> Zhou Enlai's letter requesting Soviet jet fighter squadrons and air force support for Chinese forces in Korea demonstrates that the Soviet Union maintained influence by providing direct military assistance. By dispatching volunteer air forces and supplying weapons, the Soviets ensured that communist forces in Korea remained militarily competitive against U.S. and UN forces, allowing the USSR to shape the conflict's outcome without committing ground troops openly.

*C. Explain ONE aspect of the Chinese alliance with the Soviet Union after 1945 that is reflected in the passage.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Historical Context
> The passage reflects the patron-client relationship between the USSR and China in the early Cold War. Zhou Enlai's letter is essentially a requisition: requesting planes, steel plates, 15,000 vehicles, and guidance on command relationships. China relied on Soviet military technology and equipment, which it could not yet produce independently. This dependence illustrates why the eventual Sino-Soviet split (from 1961) was so significant — China had to develop its own military-industrial capacity.

**SAQ 2** — No source (AMSCO p. 577)

*A. Identify ONE political or economic policy of Vladimir Lenin that differed from those of Joseph Stalin.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Lenin's New Economic Policy (NEP, 1921–1928) permitted limited private trade and small-scale capitalism to revive the Soviet economy after the devastation of the Civil War. Stalin reversed this policy and launched forced collectivization and rapid industrialization through Five-Year Plans, eliminating private enterprise entirely and using state terror to enforce compliance. The NEP represented pragmatic compromise; Stalin's approach represented totalitarian command economics.

*B. Explain ONE way in which Vladimir Lenin's view of the proletarian class differed from Mao Zedong's view.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Lenin believed revolution must be led by and for the urban industrial *proletariat* — factory workers who, in Marxist theory, were the class with historical agency to overthrow capitalism. Mao adapted this to China's reality: he based revolution on the rural *peasantry*, arguing that China's massive agrarian population — not its small industrial working class — was the revolutionary force. This was a fundamental revision of Marxism, making Chinese communism a model for other peasant societies.

*C. Explain ONE way in which Lenin's and Mao's views of the proletarian class were similar.*

> [!tip] Model Response — Similarity
> Both Lenin and Mao believed that a revolutionary change required a disciplined, ideologically committed vanguard party to lead the masses — whether workers or peasants — toward socialism. Neither believed revolution would happen spontaneously; both emphasized party organization, ideological education, and centralized leadership as essential. This Leninist organizational principle was the common foundation even as they disagreed about which social class would carry out the revolution.

## Key Exam Arguments

**Causation — Why did communism spread?**
The most exam-relevant argument: communism spread because it offered land redistribution to peasant majorities in societies where colonialism and feudalism had concentrated land among small elites. Communist movements succeeded where governments failed to reform themselves (China, Vietnam, Ethiopia, Cuba) and were suppressed where the U.S. intervened militarily or through coups (Guatemala, Iran).

**Comparison — Communist states vs. non-communist reform states**
Use Iran and India as foils: both pursued land reform without communism, with mixed results. Iran's revolution produced a theocracy, not a communist state. India's democratic land reform succeeded partially only in Kerala. The comparison shows that peasant grievances were structural, not ideological — multiple paths led from the same cause.

**CCOT — Communism's trajectory across Period 4**
*Change:* Communism spread from the Soviet Union to Asia (China, Vietnam, Korea, 1945–1954), then Latin America (Cuba, 1959) and Africa (Ethiopia, 1974). *Continuity:* The underlying driver — land inequality and peasant grievances from colonial and feudal legacies — remained constant across all cases.
