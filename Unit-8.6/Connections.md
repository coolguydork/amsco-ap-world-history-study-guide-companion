# Connections

**Unit 8.6 — Newly Independent States** | AMSCO pp. 589–597

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-8.5/Decolonization after 1900 Overview\|Decolonization after 1900 (8.5)]]**
Topic 8.5 covers the independence movements that produced the new states examined here. The colonial structures dismantled in 8.5 created the structural problems — underdevelopment, artificial borders, weak institutions — that Topic 8.6 examines. The same movements (Indian National Congress, Zionism, African nationalism) appear in both topics.

**[[Unit-8.3/Effects of the Cold War Overview\|Effects of the Cold War (8.3)]]**
Cold War superpower competition deeply shaped the fate of newly independent states. The US supported Israel and various anti-communist regimes; the Soviet Union backed India in the 1971 war and supported socialist experiments in Africa. Cambodia became a casualty of Cold War proxy conflicts in Southeast Asia.

**[[Unit-7.8/Mass Atrocities Overview\|World War II & Mass Atrocities (7.8)]]**
The Holocaust directly accelerated Jewish immigration to Palestine and international support for Israeli statehood. The weakening of European colonial powers in World War II was the precondition for the wave of decolonization that produced Topic 8.6's new states.

**[[Unit-5.1/Enlightenment Overview\|Enlightenment & Reform (5.1)]]**
The Dreyfus Affair (1894) triggered Theodor Herzl's founding of political Zionism — the root cause of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. A rare case where a single event in one topic creates a direct causal chain spanning four units.

## Leads To

**Topic 8.7 — End of the Cold War**
The end of the Cold War changed the geopolitical environment for newly independent states. Without superpower patronage, some authoritarian regimes collapsed; others democratized. The Oslo Accords (1993) were partly products of this new environment.

**Topic 9.6 — Globalization and Cultural Exchange**
Post-independence migration from newly independent nations to former colonial metropoles (South Asians to London; Algerians to Paris) created multicultural urban societies that became sites of cultural exchange and tension — themes central to Unit 9.

**Topic 9.8 — Demographic and Environmental Challenges**
Population displacement (Palestinian refugees, partition refugees) and rapid urbanization in newly independent states connect to Unit 9's examination of global population and migration patterns.

## Compare With

**India/Pakistan Partition ↔ Israel/Palestine Partition**
Both 1947 partitions were designed by a European power (Britain, UN) to resolve ethnic/religious conflict; both produced mass displacement and immediate wars; both created territorial disputes (Kashmir, occupied territories) that remain unresolved. The AP exam frequently asks students to compare these cases.

**Ujamaa (Tanzania) ↔ Soviet Collectivization [[Unit-7.4/Economy in the Interwar Period Overview|Economy in the Interwar Period Overview]]**
Both were state-directed programs to transform agricultural societies into collectivized systems; both produced economic disruption and resistance; both achieved some social goals (literacy, literacy) while failing economically. Nyerere, unlike Stalin, did not use mass terror — a key distinction.

**Khmer Rouge Genocide ↔ [[Concept Notes/Holocaust\|Holocaust]] ↔ [[Concept Notes/Genocide\|Genocide]] (Rwanda)**
Three 20th-century genocides with different ideological bases: Nazi racial ideology, Maoist communist utopianism, Hutu ethnic nationalism. Compare: scale, methods, perpetrators' ideology, international response (or non-response), and long-term accountability.

**Female heads of state in South Asia ↔ Women's Political Rights in Western Democracies**
Bandaranaike (1960), Indira Gandhi (1966), Bhutto (1988) — all became heads of government before most Western democracies elected female leaders. Contrast with the US (no female president), UK (Margaret Thatcher, 1979). Challenges simple narratives about gender equality being more advanced in Western societies.

**Julius Nyerere ↔ [[Concept Notes/Pan-Africanism\|Pan-Africanism]]**
Nyerere's *ujamaa* was one of several African socialist/pan-Africanist experiments (compare Nkrumah's Ghana, Sékou Touré's Guinea). All sought Third World alternatives to both capitalism and Soviet communism; all faced economic difficulties; all achieved independence from formal colonial control while remaining economically dependent on former colonial powers.
