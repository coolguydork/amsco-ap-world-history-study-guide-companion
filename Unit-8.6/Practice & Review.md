## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: Camp David Accords, 1978*

> "After four wars during 30 years, despite intensive human efforts, the Middle East, which is the cradle of civilization and the birthplace of three great religions, does not enjoy the blessings of peace. The people of the Middle East yearn for peace so that the vast human and natural resources of the region can be turned to the pursuits of peace and so that this area can become a model for coexistence and cooperation among nations…Peace requires respect for the sovereignty, territorial integrity and political independence of every state in the area and their right to live in peace within secure and recognized boundaries free from threats or acts of force."
> — Camp David Accords, 1978

**Question 1.** According to the passage, what basic element is required for peace in the Middle East?

A. More natural resources
B. Secure borders
C. Economic cooperation
D. Territorial fluidity

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The passage explicitly states peace requires "secure and recognized boundaries free from threats or acts of force." Option C (economic cooperation) is mentioned as a benefit of peace, not a prerequisite. Option D (territorial fluidity) is the opposite of what the passage argues. Option A (natural resources) is referenced only as something peace would allow to be used productively.

**Question 2.** Who rejected the proposed peace treaty?

A. Anwar Sadat of Egypt
B. Menachem Begin of Israel
C. Yasser Arafat of the Palestinian Liberation Organization (PLO)
D. U.S. President Jimmy Carter

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Yasser Arafat and the PLO rejected the Camp David Accords because the agreement did not address Palestinian statehood — it was an Egypt-Israel bilateral deal. Sadat (A) and Begin (B) were the signatories; Carter (D) was the mediator. This is also a cross-reference test: Sadat was later *assassinated* for signing the treaty, not for rejecting it.

**Question 3.** Which was the intended consequence of the Camp David Accords?

A. An election defeat for Menachem Begin
B. An alliance between Egypt and Iran
C. A peace agreement between Israel and Egypt
D. An invasion of Gaza by the PLO

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The Accords produced the first Arab-Israeli peace treaty — Egypt recognized Israel and Israel returned the Sinai Peninsula. Begin (A) actually won the Nobel Peace Prize for the deal, not an election defeat. Egypt and Iran (B) had no such alliance — Iran under Khomeini opposed the Accords. The PLO (D) rejected the deal entirely.

## Short-Answer Questions

**SAQ 1** — Based on the passage about post-independence migration to France:

> "…the postwar labor migration occurred in the midst of changing mentalité attitudes. The attributes of colonialism had recently been challenged…when Antilleans and sub-Saharan Africans migrated to France in the 1960s, their former colonial status still determined their social conditions, especially in the labor and housing markets." — Wendy Pojmann, *Migration and Activism in Europe Since 1945*, 2008

**(A)** Identify ONE imperialist idea that continued in Europe after 1945.

> [!tip] Model Response — Continuity
> One imperialist idea that continued in Europe after 1945 was racial hierarchy in labor markets. As Pojmann's passage demonstrates, migrants from former French colonies were channeled into menial, low-wage work regardless of their qualifications or aspirations — "only offering people of African descent menial worker, household employee, or lower rank tertiary worker positions." This replicated colonial racial stratification in the metropole itself.

**(B)** Explain ONE way in which one European state developed migration policies after 1945.

> [!tip] Model Response — GOV
> France developed state-sponsored labor recruitment programs — such as the agency that recruited workers from the French Antilles mentioned in the passage — to address postwar labor shortages. This policy deliberately channeled colonial subjects into low-wage industrial and domestic service sectors rather than skilled professional positions, institutionalizing the racial-economic hierarchy of the colonial period in the metropole.

**(C)** Explain ONE piece of historical evidence that supports the claim that "the attributes of colonialism had recently been challenged" in Europe by 1960.

> [!tip] Model Response — Change
> By 1960, a wave of decolonization had fundamentally challenged colonial legitimacy: Ghana gained independence in 1957, the British Empire was visibly dissolving, and Algerian nationalists were fighting a brutal war for independence from France (1954–1962). The ideological basis for colonialism had also been challenged by the 1955 Bandung Conference, where Asian and African leaders explicitly rejected European imperialism and articulated a Third World non-aligned alternative. These developments created the "changing mentalité" that Pojmann describes — colonialism was no longer ideologically secure even as its economic patterns persisted.

**SAQ 2** — Answer all parts:

**(A)** Identify ONE event that caused a significant change in the movement to support the creation of Israel.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The Holocaust (1941–1945), in which Nazi Germany systematically murdered approximately six million Jews, dramatically accelerated international support for a Jewish state. Before World War II, Zionism was a minority Jewish movement; the Holocaust demonstrated catastrophically that assimilation in Europe had failed to protect Jews and generated worldwide sympathy for Jewish survivors seeking a homeland. This shifted the moral and political calculus of the United Nations, leading directly to the 1947 partition plan.

**(B)** Explain ONE event that caused a significant change in Israeli-Arab relations.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The 1967 Six-Day War fundamentally transformed Israeli-Arab relations by dramatically expanding Israeli-controlled territory. Before the war, Israel occupied roughly the territory allocated by the 1947 UN partition; afterward, it controlled the Sinai Peninsula, Gaza Strip, West Bank, Golan Heights, and East Jerusalem — tripling its territory. This occupation entrenched Palestinian displacement, radicalized Arab populations, and made a negotiated peace vastly more complicated, as Israel now held territory that Arab states and Palestinians demanded returned. The question of "occupied territories" became the central obstacle to all subsequent peace negotiations.

**(C)** Explain ONE example of a religion- or culture-based conflict in a former colony outside of the Middle East during the 20th century.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> The 1947 partition of British India provides a powerful example of religion-based conflict in a former colony. When Britain divided its South Asian colony into Hindu-majority India and Muslim-majority Pakistan based on religious demographics, it triggered catastrophic communal violence: between 500,000 and one million people were killed, and 10–20 million Hindus, Muslims, and Sikhs fled across the new borders. The ongoing dispute over Kashmir — a Muslim-majority region that India controls — reflects how colonial partition created enduring territorial and religious conflicts that neither state has been able to resolve, including after both nations developed nuclear weapons.

## Key Concept Review

**Comparing post-independence challenges across regions:**

| Region | New State | Primary Challenge | Outcome |
|--------|-----------|------------------|---------|
| South Asia | India | Religious partition violence; poverty; nuclear rivalry | World's largest democracy; ongoing Kashmir dispute |
| South Asia | Pakistan | Military vs. civilian rule; Kashmir; Islamic politics | Alternates democracy and military dictatorship |
| Middle East | Israel | Arab rejection; occupation of Palestinian lands | Multiple wars; unresolved Palestinian statehood |
| SE Asia | Cambodia | Cold War proxy war; communist takeover | Genocide; Vietnamese intervention; UN peace |
| Africa | Tanzania | Economic underdevelopment; socialist experiment | Political stability; economic stagnation |

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam often provides a primary source about one newly independent state and asks you to make connections to others. Practice seeing the **common patterns**: colonial boundaries causing conflict, political instability, economic underdevelopment, Cold War intervention, and migration to metropoles.
