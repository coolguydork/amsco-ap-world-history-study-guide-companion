# Themes & Historical Reasoning

**Unit 9.9 — Continuity & Change in a Globalized World | AMSCO pp. 707–720**

**AP Skill:** Continuity and Change Over Time (CCOT) | **AP Skill:** Argumentation

---

## AP Themes at Work in Topic 9.9

### TEC — Technology & Innovation

Technology is the primary driver of change in this topic. Every domain of change — population growth, economic shifts, cultural exchange, environmental transformation — traces back to scientific and technological breakthroughs.

**Change:** Vaccines, antibiotics, and birth control transformed medicine and extended life expectancy from ~35 to ~70 years. Jet aviation, containerized shipping, and the [[Concept Notes/Internet\|Internet]] compressed time and space, enabling the globalized economy and culture. The [[Concept Notes/Green Revolution\|Green Revolution]] fed billions. Nuclear and renewable energy diversified the global power supply.

**Continuity:** Technology's benefits remained unevenly distributed. The UNICEF vaccine report (1996) notes that vaccine protection only reached children in developing countries in the mid-1980s — decades after the vaccines existed. Access to technology tracked the developed/developing divide throughout the period.

> [!exam-tip]
> **TEC + unintended consequences** is a high-yield AP pattern. For each technology, know its unintended downside: Green Revolution → biodiversity loss; fossil fuels → climate change; global transportation → faster pandemic spread; internet → disinformation.

### ECN — Economic Systems

**Change:** The global economic center of gravity shifted. Asian Tigers, China, and India challenged Western economic dominance through export-led growth and knowledge-economy specialization. State economic intervention became normal — welfare states, Keynesian stimulus, and Five-Year Plans all represented departures from 19th-century laissez-faire.

**Continuity:** Core-periphery exploitation persisted. Developed nations continued to extract raw materials from and export finished goods to developing nations. Income inequality within countries increased under neoliberal policies (Reaganomics, Thatcherism).

| Economic Policy | Country | Approach | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| New Economic Policy | USSR (1921–28) | Market elements within communism | Recovery from civil war |
| First Five-Year Plan | USSR (1928–32) | Forced collectivization | Industrialization + mass famine |
| Great Leap Forward | China (1958–60) | Forced rural steel production | Mass famine |
| Four Modernizations | China (1970s) | Market opening | World's 2nd-largest economy |
| New Deal | USA (1933–41) | Keynesian stimulus | Jobs + regulation; slow recovery |
| Reagan Tax Act | USA (1981) | Supply-side cuts | Growth + rising inequality |
| Welfare State | UK (1945–51) | Universal public services | Health care, pensions, education |
| Thatcher Privatization | UK (1980s) | Deregulation + privatization | Growth + record unemployment |

### GOV — Governance

**Change:** Mass protest movements compelled political reform. Voting rights expanded globally. Decolonization created dozens of new states. Cold War ended with liberal-democratic optimism. Democratic transitions occurred across Eastern Europe, Latin America, East Asia, and parts of Africa.

**Continuity:** States persistently suppressed dissent through persecution, imprisonment, and violence. Authoritarianism resurged in the 21st century. State power over citizens remained robust throughout. The Arab Spring demonstrated that mass protest could topple regimes — but also that authoritarian restoration was equally likely.

> [!warning]
> Don't argue that democracy "won" in this period — the AP rewards complexity. The narrative explicitly states that "post-Cold War optimism gives way to new challenges to democratic governance" in the 21st century.

### CDI — Cultural Developments & Interactions

**Change:** The pace and scope of cultural exchange accelerated dramatically. Western popular culture spread globally via radio, TV, film, and the Internet. Cultural exchange became increasingly bidirectional — East Asian cuisine, anime, K-pop, and Bollywood found audiences in the West. The Internet enabled instantaneous global cultural diffusion.

**Continuity:** The flow of culture remained asymmetrically weighted toward Western (especially American) content. Cultural imperialism debates persisted. Resistance to homogenization continued, sometimes taking violent or political forms (religious fundamentalism as cultural self-assertion).

---

### ENV — Environment

**Change:** Human technological capacity to exploit and transform the environment reached unprecedented scale. Airborne and water pollution increased from carbon-based energy. Climate change emerged as an existential threat. Biodiversity declined from industrial agriculture. Space exploration broke Earth's environmental limits.

**Continuity:** The fundamental pattern of human exploitation of natural resources for economic growth — established in the Industrial Revolution — continued and intensified. Environmental damage tracked economic development: industrializing nations repeated the pattern of earlier industrializers.

> [!exam-tip]
> ENV questions often focus on the **tension between economic growth and environmental sustainability**. Use the climate change/fossil fuel connection as your anchor evidence: the same technology that enabled the 20th-century economic miracle is creating the 21st-century environmental crisis.

## CCOT Reasoning Framework

**What changed most rapidly?** Technology, medicine, communication, transportation, economic geography (Asia's rise), women's legal status in developed nations.

**What changed slowly?** Gender equality in developing nations, economic inequality between core and periphery, state authoritarianism.

**What stayed the same despite surface change?** Core-periphery economic exploitation (new actors, same structure), human tendency to suppress dissent, environmental exploitation for growth.

**Periodization question:** Is 1900–present one period, or should it be broken at 1945 (post-WWII) or 1991 (post-Cold War)? The AP expects you to identify turning points and justify them. Strong answer: argue that technology drove continuous acceleration throughout, but the Cold War (1945–1991) added an ideological overlay that shaped *which* economic models won.

## Think as a Historian: CCOT Exercise

**From the AMSCO text (p. 712):** Use Keynes's 1919 description of pre-WWI London as your baseline. He describes ordering goods from around the world by telephone, investing globally, and traveling anywhere affordably — a globalized world that already existed in 1914. Your task: identify what has changed *since then* and what continuities persist even into the present.

**Model CCOT claim:** "While the *scale and speed* of globalization accelerated dramatically after 1900 — due to jet aviation, the Internet, and containerized shipping — the fundamental *structure* of globalization, in which developed nations disproportionately benefit from global economic integration, remained a continuity throughout the period."
