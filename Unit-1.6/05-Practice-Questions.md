# Practice Questions — Unit 1.6: Developments in Europe (c. 1200–1450)

## Multiple Choice Questions (AP-Style)

---

**Question 1** *(Stimulus: excerpt from the Magna Carta, 1215)*

> *"No free man shall be seized, imprisoned, dispossessed, outlawed, exiled or ruined in any way, nor in any way proceeded against, except by the lawful judgement of his peers and the law of the land."*

Which of the following best describes the long-term historical significance of this document?

- A) It immediately abolished the feudal system in England
- B) It established that monarchs were subject to law, influencing later representative governments
- C) It granted voting rights to all English citizens
- D) It ended the conflict between England and France

**✅ Answer: B**
*Explanation: The Magna Carta's key significance is the precedent it set — rulers are not above the law. It directly influenced later documents like the English Bill of Rights and U.S. Constitution.*

---

**Question 2** *(Stimulus: map showing Black Death spread, 1347–1353)*

The spread of the Black Death along the routes shown in the map best illustrates which of the following?

- A) The negative consequences of increased Eurasian trade networks
- B) The success of medieval European medical practices
- C) The strength of feudal institutions during a crisis
- D) The role of the Mongols in deliberately spreading disease

**✅ Answer: A**
*Explanation: The Black Death traveled along the Silk Roads and Mediterranean trade routes — showing that increased trade connectivity had catastrophic unintended consequences.*

---

**Question 3**

Which of the following best explains why the Black Death led to the weakening of feudalism in Western Europe?

- A) Plague killed primarily the nobility, eliminating their authority
- B) Labor shortages gave surviving peasants greater bargaining power for wages and freedom
- C) The Church used the crisis to seize feudal estates from weakened lords
- D) Monarchs used plague mortality to justify dissolving feudal contracts

**✅ Answer: B**
*Explanation: With so many peasants dead, lords needed workers desperately — surviving serfs could now negotiate for better conditions, wages, or simply leave for higher-paying opportunities in towns.*

---

**Question 4**

The Crusades (1095–1291) most directly contributed to which of the following developments in Europe?

- A) The permanent conquest of the Holy Land by Christian forces
- B) The decline of Islamic civilization in the Middle East
- C) Increased European contact with Islamic learning and goods
- D) The political unification of Western European kingdoms

**✅ Answer: C**
*Explanation: Even though the Crusades ultimately failed militarily, they opened sustained contact between Europe and the Islamic world, bringing back mathematical, scientific, and philosophical advances.*

---

**Question 5**

The development of universities such as those at Bologna, Paris, and Oxford in the 12th–13th centuries most directly reflected:

- A) A decline in the Catholic Church's influence over intellectual life
- B) The growing need to train lawyers, physicians, and clergy for complex societies
- C) The influence of Mongol administrative practices on European institutions
- D) The spread of Islamic educational models via the Crusades

**✅ Answer: B**
*Explanation: Universities emerged from the growing complexity of European society — cities, trade, and Church administration all required educated professionals. They were largely Church-affiliated but served secular needs too.*

---

**Question 6**

The Western Schism (1378–1417), in which multiple men simultaneously claimed to be the legitimate Pope, most directly contributed to:

- A) The military defeat of the Holy Roman Empire
- B) The outbreak of the Hundred Years' War
- C) Growing skepticism about the authority and moral legitimacy of the Church
- D) The reunification of the Catholic and Orthodox churches

**✅ Answer: C**
*Explanation: When three popes simultaneously claimed authority, ordinary Christians lost confidence in the institution. This erosion of trust was a precursor to the Protestant Reformation a century later.*

---

**Question 7**

The signing of the Magna Carta (1215) and the gradual development of Parliament in England are best understood as examples of:

- A) The decline of the English monarchy's power to govern effectively
- B) The development of representative institutions that limited royal authority
- C) The influence of Islamic political thought on European governance
- D) The Church's effort to control secular government

**✅ Answer: B**
*Explanation: Both represent the slow, contested emergence of representative governance — the idea that rulers must govern with at least some consent of the ruled.*

---

**Question 8** *(Stimulus: excerpt from Thomas Aquinas, Summa Theologica)*

> *"Since human reason is not able fully to comprehend divine wisdom, it is necessary that divine truths surpass human reason. Nevertheless, they do not contradict it."*

Aquinas's argument in this passage most directly reflects which intellectual development of medieval Europe?

- A) The rejection of Christian theology in favor of classical philosophy
- B) The effort to harmonize faith and reason through Scholasticism
- C) The spread of Islamic rationalist philosophy into Europe
- D) The growing secularism of European university life

**✅ Answer: B**
*Explanation: Aquinas's whole project was Scholasticism — showing that rational inquiry and Christian faith were complementary. He used Aristotelian logic to defend Church teachings.*

---

**Question 9**

How did the Hundred Years' War (1337–1453) contribute to the development of centralized nation-states in Western Europe?

- A) It forced both England and France to develop stronger central governments to fund and organize warfare
- B) It caused both countries to adopt the Mongol model of bureaucratic rule
- C) The Church mediated the conflict, increasing papal authority over secular rulers
- D) Trade disruptions from the war encouraged feudal lords to consolidate power

**✅ Answer: A**
*Explanation: Long wars require consistent taxation, national armies, and administrative structures — all of which shifted power from feudal lords to monarchs in France and England.*

**Question 10**

The fall of Constantinople to the Ottoman Turks in 1453 most directly contributed to which of the following?

- A) The end of the Catholic Church's influence in Eastern Europe
- B) An influx of Greek scholars into Western Europe, stimulating the Renaissance
- C) The immediate launch of European overseas exploration
- D) The collapse of the Hanseatic League's trade networks

**✅ Answer: B**
*Explanation: Byzantine scholars fled to Italy carrying Greek manuscripts and classical knowledge, directly contributing to the humanist learning of the Italian Renaissance.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

---

### SAQ 1

**Prompt:** Using specific evidence, explain ONE cause and ONE effect of the Black Death on European society between 1347 and 1450.

**Top-scoring response would include:**

- **Cause:** Identify the plague's arrival via trade routes (Mediterranean/Silk Road connection); link to the Mongol Pax Mongolica that opened Eurasian trade
- **Effect (choose one strongly):**
  - Labor shortages → weakened serfdom / peasants gained bargaining power (cite Statute of Laborers as evidence lords tried to resist)
  - Loss of confidence in the Church (priests fled or died; prayers seemed ineffective) → Western Schism → later reform movements
  - Population decline → economic contraction followed by recovery and eventual expansion of merchant capitalism
- Use specific dates, terms, and a clear cause-and-effect sentence structure

---

### SAQ 2

**Prompt:** Briefly explain how the Crusades (1095–1291) reflect ONE continuity AND ONE change in the relationship between Europe and the Islamic world.

**Top-scoring response would include:**

- **Continuity:** Ongoing religious and political rivalry between Christian Europe and Islamic states (began with the early spread of Islam in the 7th–8th centuries; the Crusades are a continuation of this competition)
- **Change:** The Crusades opened direct, sustained cultural and economic exchange — Europeans encountered Islamic science, mathematics, philosophy, and goods in new ways, reshaping European intellectual life (12th-century renaissance)
- Cite specific examples: Arabic numerals, Aristotle's works preserved and transmitted by Islamic scholars, spice trade intensification

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline

---

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which the Black Death transformed European society and institutions between 1347 and 1450.

**What a top-scoring response would include:**

**Thesis:**
- Make a historically defensible claim that goes beyond restating the prompt
- Example: "The Black Death fundamentally transformed European society by destabilizing feudalism and undermining the Church's authority, though the pace and completeness of change varied significantly by region and social class."

**Contextualization:**
- Describe the pre-plague context: population growth, feudal stability, Church dominance c. 1200–1347
- Explain how trade networks (Silk Roads, Mediterranean) enabled plague's spread

**Body — Possible Document Groupings:**
1. **Social/Labor Transformation** — peasant revolts, labor legislation, serfdom decline
2. **Church Authority & Religious Response** — flagellant movements, scapegoating of Jews, Western Schism
3. **Economic Change** — wage increases, merchant capitalism, town growth

**Outside Evidence to Include:**
- English Statute of Laborers (1351) — lords' failed attempt to freeze wages
- Peasants' Revolt (1381) — direct social consequence
- Boccaccio's *Decameron* — source on social breakdown and skepticism
- Western Schism (1378) — institutional response to plague-era Church credibility crisis

**Complexity Point Options:**
- Argue the transformation was *uneven* — eastern Europe retained serfdom longer; Italy recovered faster due to merchant capital
- Connect to long-term causation: plague → labor shortage → motivation for Atlantic exploration to find new trade routes

> [!exam-tip]
> For the DBQ, always address **sourcing** (HAPP: Historical context, Audience, Purpose, Point of view) for at least 3 documents. And always write a thesis in the **intro AND conclusion**.
