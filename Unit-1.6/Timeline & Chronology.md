# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 1.6: Developments in Europe

## Core Period: c. 1200–1450

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| **962** | Otto I crowned Holy Roman Emperor | Revives Carolingian imperial tradition; begins HRE (962–1806) |
| **1054** | Great Schism | Christianity splits into Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox branches |
| **1066** | Norman Conquest of England | William the Conqueror fuses Norman & Anglo-Saxon cultures; spreads feudal system |
| **1095** | First Crusade launched by Pope Urban II | Beginning of Crusades era; only Crusade to achieve its military objective |
| **1099** | Crusaders capture Jerusalem | Peak of Crusade military success |
| **1122** | Concordat of Worms | Resolves lay investiture controversy; Church wins autonomy from secular rulers |
| **1141** | Peter Abelard's letters to Heloise | Emblematic of scholastic tension between faith and reason |
| **1180–1223** | Philip II rules France | First French royal bureaucracy; begins French centralization |
| **1187** | Saladin recaptures Jerusalem | Ends First Crusade gains; triggers subsequent Crusades |
| **1202–1204** | Fourth Crusade | Venice redirects Crusaders; Constantinople sacked; Crusade never reaches Holy Land |
| **1215** | Magna Carta signed | King John forced to recognize noble rights and limits on royal power |
| **1265** | First English Parliament | House of Lords + House of Commons; foundation of English constitutional government |
| **1271–1295** | Marco Polo's travels to East Asia | Sparks European curiosity about Asia; stimulates cartography and trade interest |
| **1285–1314** | Philip IV rules France; first Estates-General (1285) | Advisory body of three estates; limited power due to tax-exempt upper estates |
| **c. 1300** | Little Ice Age begins | Cooling reduces crop yields; fuels disease, unrest, scapegoating of minorities |
| **1337–1453** | Hundred Years' War (England vs. France) | Fosters national identity; spreads gunpowder weapons; England retains only Calais |
| **1347** | Black Death arrives in Europe | Kills ~1/3 of European population; transforms labor relations; weakens serfdom |
| **c. 1265–1321** | Life of Dante Alighieri | *Divine Comedy* (c. 1320); vernacular Italian; foundational Renaissance literature |
| **1290** | Jews expelled from England | First major expulsion; Jews pushed eastward across Europe |
| **c. 1343–1400** | Life of Geoffrey Chaucer | *Canterbury Tales* in Middle English; Northern Renaissance literature |
| **1394** | Jews expelled from France | Continuation of antisemitic expulsions across western Europe |
| **c. 1400** | Renaissance spreads to northern Europe | German, Flemish, and English humanists engage classical revival |
| **c. 1450** | Gutenberg's printing press | Mass-produced books; accelerates literacy and spread of ideas |
| **1453** | Fall of Constantinople to Ottoman Turks | End of Byzantine Empire; Greek scholars flee to Italy carrying manuscripts |
| **1462–1505** | Ivan the Great rules Moscow | Throws off Mongol rule; founds modern Russian state |
| **1492** | Reconquista completed; Jews expelled from Spain | Isabella and Ferdinand complete Christian reconquest of Iberia |

## Thematic Threads Through the Timeline

**Political decentralization → centralization**
962 (HRE founded, fragmented) → 1066 (Norman England, organized feudalism) → 1122 (Church wins investiture) → 1180s (French bureaucracy) → 1215 (Magna Carta limits crown) → 1265 (Parliament) → 1285 (Estates-General)

**Agricultural and demographic change**
Three-field system adoption (11th–12th c.) → Population growth (High Middle Ages) → Little Ice Age (c. 1300) → [[Concept Notes/Black Death\|Black Death]] (1347) → Labor scarcity, serf bargaining power → Decline of manorialism

**[[Concept Notes/Crusades\|Crusades]] and cross-cultural contact**
1095 (First Crusade) → 1099 (Jerusalem taken) → 1187 (Saladin retakes Jerusalem) → 1202–1204 (Fourth Crusade, Constantinople sacked) → Late 13th c. (Islamic forces prevail in Levant) → European demand for eastern goods surges

**Church power: rise and strain**
1054 (Schism) → 1095 (Urban II launches Crusade) → 1122 (Concordat of Worms) → 1198–1216 (Innocent III, peak papal power) → 1202–1204 (Fourth Crusade fiasco) → Corruption scandals → 16th c. Reformation (future)

**Cultural and intellectual revival**
Scholasticism (11th–13th c.) → First universities founded → Dante (c. 1320) → Chaucer (c. 1390s) → Gutenberg press (c. 1450) → Renaissance spreads north

---

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam often places events in sequence or asks which development came "as a result of" another. Know this chain cold: **Black Death (1347) → labor shortage → serf bargaining power → weakening of manorialism**. Also: **Crusades → contact with Islamic world → increased European demand for eastern goods → commercial revival → bourgeoisie grows → Renaissance patronage**.
