# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 1.6: Developments in Europe

Chronological anchors for medieval and early-Renaissance Europe, c. 400–c. 1450. The AP core period is c. 1200–c. 1450, but medieval Europe makes no sense without understanding the Roman collapse that set the starting conditions.

---

## Deep Background: The Post-Roman World

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **476 C.E.** | Fall of the Western Roman Empire | Power fragments into hundreds of competing Germanic kingdoms; trade collapses; Latin literacy narrows to clergy; sets the conditions for feudalism |
| **c. 500–900** | Early Middle Ages ("Dark Ages") | Economic contraction; political fragmentation; manorial self-sufficiency; the Roman Catholic Church is the only Europe-wide institution that survives |
| **768–814** | Charlemagne rules the Franks | Brief moment of European political unity; demonstrates the potential for centralization; Charlemagne crowned "Emperor of the Romans" by the Pope in 800 |
| **c. 793–1066** | Viking raids across Europe | Raiding from Scandinavia disrupts trade and settlement; forces coastal and river-valley communities to seek military protection → deepens feudal dependency |
| **962** | Otto I crowned Holy Roman Emperor | German king declared successor to Charlemagne; Holy Roman Empire becomes the dominant German political structure through the medieval period |

## AP Period Core: c. 1000–c. 1450

### Political Developments

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **1054** | The Great Schism | Christianity splits permanently into Roman Catholic (Western Europe) and Orthodox (Eastern Europe, Russia, Greece) branches (AMSCO p. 56) |
| **1066** | Norman Conquest of England | William the Conqueror invades from Normandy; creates a tightly organized feudal system with royal sheriffs; fuses Norman-French and Anglo-Saxon cultures into the modern English people (AMSCO p. 55) |
| **1095** | Pope Urban II launches the First Crusade | Seljuk Turks restrict Christian pilgrim access to Jerusalem; social pressures of primogeniture; Church and monarchs organize pan-European military campaign (AMSCO p. 57) |
| **1099** | Crusaders capture Jerusalem | The only clear Christian military victory; European Crusaders hold the city for nearly a century (AMSCO p. 58) |
| **1122** | Concordat of Worms | Resolves the lay investiture controversy — the Church wins formal autonomy from secular appointment of bishops; significant limitation on Holy Roman Emperor's authority |
| **c. 1180–1223** | Reign of Philip I of France | First French king to develop a true royal bureaucracy; groundwork for later French centralization (AMSCO p. 54) |
| **1187** | Saladin retakes Jerusalem | Muslim forces under Saladin end 88 years of Crusader control; triggers further Crusades that ultimately fail (AMSCO p. 58) |
| **1202–1204** | Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople | Venice diverts Crusaders to sack a Christian city — a major betrayal that permanently weakens the Byzantine Empire and embitters Eastern-Western Christian relations (AMSCO p. 58) |
| **1215** | Magna Carta signed | English nobles force King John to recognize that royal power has limits: jury trial before imprisoning a noble; consultation on taxation. First formal constraint on English royal authority — though it protected nobles, not commoners (AMSCO p. 55) |
| **1265** | First English Parliament formed | House of Lords (nobles + clergy) and House of Commons (wealthy townspeople); gradually becomes the most powerful legislative body in Europe (AMSCO p. 55) |
| **1337–1453** | Hundred Years' War | England and France fight for over a century; results in national identities hardening ("English" vs. "French"); gunpowder weapons (transmitted from China via Mongols) prove decisive (AMSCO p. 55) |

### Economic and Social Developments

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 1000–1200** | Three-field system spreads | Agricultural rotation (food crop + legume + fallow) increases yields; supports population growth; begins freeing some peasants from pure subsistence |
| **Late 1200s** | Marco Polo visits Kublai Khan's court | Italian merchant's accounts of Mongol customs and Chinese civilization spark European curiosity about Asia; stimulates cartography and the desire to establish direct trade routes (AMSCO p. 58) |
| **c. 1290** | Jews expelled from England | First major expulsion; Jews subsequently expelled from France (1394), Spain (1492), Portugal (1497); driven eastward into Poland and the Ottoman Empire (AMSCO p. 59) |
| **c. 1300 onward** | Little Ice Age begins | Five-century cooling reduces agricultural productivity; slows urban growth; increases disease and unemployment; fuels scapegoating of minorities (AMSCO p. 59) |
| **c. 1347–1351** | Black Death peaks in Europe | Bubonic plague kills up to one-third of Europe's population; increases surviving serfs' bargaining power; begins undermining the manorial system; accelerates the decline of feudalism |
| **c. 1300s onward** | Renaissance begins in Italy | Revival of classical Greek and Roman learning; emerges in city-states like Florence and Venice where trade wealth creates a merchant-patron class; spreads northward by 1400 (AMSCO p. 60) |
| **1394** | Jews expelled from France | Part of the broader pattern of anti-Jewish persecution accelerating in the late Middle Ages |
| **c. 1400** | Northern Renaissance spreads | Renaissance humanism and vernacular literature (Chaucer, Dante) spread across the Alps; represents the intellectual foundation of European modernity |
| **1439** | Gutenberg's printing press | Movable-type press enables mass production of manuscripts; promotes literacy; rapidly spreads ideas. Technology transfer chain: paper (China) → Islamic world → Europe (AMSCO p. 60) |
| **Late 15th century** | Ivan the Great leads Russia to independence | Moscow-based ruler ends Mongol overlordship of Kievan Rus; beginnings of the modern Russian state (AMSCO p. 61) |
| **1492** | Reconquista completed; Jews expelled from Spain | Christian kingdoms complete the seven-century effort to reconquer Iberia; same year Columbus sails — both events connected to Iberian consolidation and ambition |

## Causation Chain: From Feudal Fragmentation to Renaissance

```
Fall of Rome (476) → political fragmentation across Europe
                          ↓
Feudalism emerges: land-for-loyalty, protection-for-service
                          ↓
Manorial self-sufficiency → limits trade and cross-regional contact
                          ↓
Roman Catholic Church provides only pan-European institution
                          ↓
Crusades (1095–1200s): Europeans contact Middle Eastern civilization
                          ↓
European demand for Middle Eastern luxury goods increases trade
                          ↓
Trade growth → population growth (three-field system + agricultural improvements)
                          ↓
Growing towns → new bourgeoisie (merchants, craftspeople)
                          ↓
Black Death (c. 1347–1351): one-third of Europe dies
                          ↓
Labor shortage → serfs gain bargaining power → manorial system weakens
                          ↓
Agricultural surplus + middle-class wealth → cultural patronage
                          ↓
Renaissance (c. 1300s onward): revival of classical learning,
humanism, vernacular literature, printing press (1439)
                          ↓
European knowledge explosion → Age of Exploration (Units 3–4)
```

---

## Causation Chain: Rise of Stronger Monarchies

```
Political fragmentation under early feudalism
                  ↓
Pressure from below: nobles limit kings (Magna Carta, 1215)
Pressure from above: Church limits kings (lay investiture)
                  ↓
Monarchs respond: build their own bureaucracies and armies
                  ↓
Hundred Years' War (1337–1453): nations harden ("English" / "French")
                  ↓
Gunpowder weapons (Chinese innovation, Mongol transmission) reduce
value of knightly cavalry → further weakening noble military dominance
                  ↓
Centralized monarchies begin to dominate Europe by 1450
                  ↓
Sets stage for land-based gunpowder empires in Units 3–4
```
