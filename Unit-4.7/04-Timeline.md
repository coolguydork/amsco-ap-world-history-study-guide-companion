# Timeline — Unit 4.7: (4.7): Changing Social Hierarchies

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

> [!tip] Big Picture
> Social hierarchy changes in this period were *caused* by the economic and demographic developments of Topics 4.3–4.5. They set the stage for the social conflicts and independence movements of Unit 5. This is a topic about long-term structural change, not dramatic events.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1492 | Columbus's arrival begins demographic transformation of the Americas | Americas | First step in the racial mixing that produces the casta system |
| 1501 | Spain begins importing enslaved Africans to Caribbean | Caribbean | Racial chattel slavery introduced to the Americas |
| 1510s–1540s | Encomienda system established across Spanish Americas | Americas | Creates framework for racial labor hierarchy with Indigenous at bottom |
| 1542 | New Laws attempt to protect Indigenous peoples | Spain | Crown acknowledges abuses but largely fails to enforce protections |
| 1550s | Casta system formalized in Spanish colonial law | Americas | Race legally encoded as determinant of social status |
| c. 1600 | Mestizo population growing rapidly | Americas | Mixed-race peoples become numerically significant; casta categories multiply |
| 1619 | Enslaved Africans in English Virginia | North America | Foundation of race-based slavery in British colonies, different from Spanish casta model |
| c. 1648 | Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz born | New Spain (Mexico) | Colonial creole woman who achieves remarkable intellectual prominence despite gender restrictions |
| 1650–1750 | Free people of color growing as social category | Americas | Complexity of racial hierarchy increases; intermediate categories challenge binary slave/free |
| 1680–1700s | Casta paintings emerge as artistic genre | New Spain (Mexico) | Visual documentation of racial hierarchy; simultaneously descriptive and prescriptive |
| 1700s | Creole identity consolidates | Americas | Growing creole resentment of peninsulares sets stage for independence movements |

---

> [!warning] Continuity Note
> Social hierarchies based on ethnicity, class, and religion existed before 1450. The key *change* was the **racial character** of Atlantic colonial hierarchy — heritable, legally codified, and tied to permanent enslaved status in a way unprecedented in world history.
