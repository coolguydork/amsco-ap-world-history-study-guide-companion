# Practice & Review

## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: AMSCO AP World History, printed pp. 257–258*

**Questions 1–3 refer to the following passage:**

> "Learned men of various kinds and from every country, as well as adherents of many different religions and creeds, assembled at his court and were admitted to converse with him. Night and day people did nothing but inquire and investigate… His Majesty collected opinions of every one, especially of those who were not Mohammedans [Muslim]… and collected everything which people can find in books, with a talent of selection peculiar to him and a spirit of inquiry opposed to every Islamitic principle. Thus… as the result of all the influences which were brought to bear upon him, there grew… the conviction in his heart that there were sensible men in all religions and abstemious thinkers and men endowed with miraculous powers among all nations."
>
> — Badā'ūnī's History, section on the Mughal emperor Akbar

**Question 1.** Based on the passage and your knowledge of this unit, what were Akbar's policies toward religion?

A. Akbar favored strict Islamic practice and sometimes persecuted Catholics.
B. Akbar focused almost exclusively on the relatively new religious teachings of the Sikhs.
C. Akbar showed tolerance of and interest in a variety of religions.
D. Akbar was not interested in religion, pursuing philosophy instead.

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The passage describes Akbar welcoming scholars from many religions, collecting their ideas, and coming to believe there were "sensible men in all religions." This directly describes religious tolerance and curiosity. Option A is the opposite of the passage. Option B is too narrow — the passage describes many religions, not just Sikhism. Option D contradicts the passage, which shows deep engagement with religion.

**Question 2.** In the above passage, what is the author's attitude toward Akbar's behavior?

A. He disapproves of Akbar's lack of adherence to Islamic practice.
B. He is impressed by Akbar's curiosity and open-mindedness.
C. He wants to participate in the religious discussions in Akbar's court.
D. He believes Islamic thought has too much influence over Akbar.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Badā'ūnī was a conservative Muslim historian who was critical of Akbar's departures from orthodox Islam. The phrase "a spirit of inquiry opposed to every Islamitic principle" is a criticism, not praise — Badā'ūnī viewed Akbar's religious open-mindedness as a departure from Islamic practice. This is a point-of-view question: the author's disapproval is embedded in the language even though the facts he describes sound positive to modern readers.

**Question 3.** Which of the following had policies on religion that most resembled those of Akbar?

A. Spain under Ferdinand and Isabella
B. Qing dynasty
C. Ottoman Empire under Mehmed II
D. England under Edward I

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The Ottoman Empire under Mehmed II practiced relative religious tolerance, allowing Jews and Christians to live under the millet system as protected minorities. This parallels Akbar's tolerance, though Akbar went further by abolishing the jizya. Spain under Ferdinand and Isabella expelled Jews and pursued religious uniformity — the opposite of Akbar's policy. The Qing enforced cultural assimilation (queues), also unlike Akbar. England under Edward I expelled Jews in 1290.

## Short-Answer Questions

*Source: AMSCO AP World History, printed p. 258*

**SAQ 1** refers to this passage:

> "Nowadays, some 185 years after most of the Latin American nations obtained their independence, none of the Latin governments consider race to be an issue. All of these governments are firmly convinced that the racial caste system of colonial times has totally disappeared. This firmly held conviction is, however, not shared by academics and ordinary citizens who have noticed the distinct racial stratification of the Latin American societies. For these dissenters, the prevailing racial economic hierarchy and the easily uncovered attitudes that consider the dark-skinned unattractive and inferior clearly indicate that the racial caste system continues to operate."
>
> — Rutilio Martinez and Vish Iyer, *Latin America's Racial Caste System: Salient Marketing Implications* (2008)

**SAQ 1A.** Identify ONE way in which the casta system changed European economies in the period 1450–1750.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The casta system facilitated the extraction of coerced labor and tribute from indigenous and African populations in Spain's American colonies. This wealth — especially silver from mines like Potosí, worked by indigenous labor under the mit'a system — flowed back to Spain and fueled the European commercial economy, contributing to the Price Revolution and funding Spain's imperial ambitions. The casta system thus made colonial labor legally legible and taxable, directly supporting European capital accumulation.

**SAQ 1B.** Explain the authors' argument about the racial caste system in the passage.

> [!tip] Model Response — Argumentation
> Martinez and Iyer argue that despite official government denials, the colonial casta system has not actually disappeared from Latin American societies. They contend that persistent racial economic stratification — in which darker-skinned people occupy lower economic positions — and widespread cultural attitudes equating dark skin with inferiority demonstrate that the casta system's logic continues to operate, even without formal legal enforcement. The authors see the caste system as deeply embedded in social structures, surviving 185 years of political independence.

**SAQ 1C.** Explain ONE historical situation in the period 1450–1750, other than the one in the passage, in which imperial states adopted policies that limited the political power and influence of groups of people.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> The Qing Dynasty's enforcement of the queue requirement provides a clear example. After conquering China in 1644, the Manchu rulers required all men — regardless of ethnicity — to wear their hair in the braided queue style of the Manchu, on pain of death. This policy deliberately limited the ability of Han Chinese to maintain their cultural identity and practice traditional Confucian customs, using physical appearance as a marker of political submission. Han Chinese who refused — such as the residents of Jiading — were massacred by Han defectors working for the Qing, demonstrating how the Qing used both cultural coercion and brutal violence to suppress Han political agency.

**SAQ 2A.** Identify ONE way in which challenges to an elite class affected political or economic structures in the period 1450–1750.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The Fronde (1648–1653) in France, in which the French nobility rebelled against royal centralization, directly caused Louis XIV to restructure political power. After suppressing the Fronde, Louis XIV moved the royal court to Versailles and required nobles to reside there, removing them from their regional power bases and making them financially dependent on royal patronage. This effectively transformed the French nobility from an independent political class into a decorative court aristocracy, concentrating political power in the monarchy.

**SAQ 2B.** Explain ONE way in which social structures in 1450–1750 in the Americas compare to social structures in China during the Qing Dynasty.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Both the casta system in colonial Latin America and the Qing ethnic hierarchy in China used ancestry/ethnicity as the primary basis for social stratification, in contrast to earlier hierarchies based primarily on religious affiliation or economic class. In both systems, a minority group (Spanish-born peninsulares in the Americas; Manchu in China) occupied the top positions of political authority over a much larger subject population. Both also maintained the fiction of meritocracy while encoding ethnic privilege: Spain allowed criollos into some administrative roles, just as the Qing maintained the civil service examinations — but in practice, the top offices remained reserved for the ruling ethnic group. A key difference is that the Americas' casta system was more finely gradated, with multiple legally defined mixed-race categories, while the Qing system was primarily a binary Manchu/Han distinction.

**SAQ 2C.** Explain ONE way in which policies of monarchs and leaders withstood challenges in the period 1450–1750.

> [!tip] Model Response — Continuity
> Akbar the Great's policy of religious tolerance successfully withstood potential challenges from both his orthodox Muslim advisors and from the Hindu majority he ruled. By abolishing the jizya tax, granting land to both Hindus and Muslims, and integrating Hindu elites into his administration, Akbar built a cross-religious coalition that made rebellion against Mughal authority less attractive for any single religious group. The diversity of his court — Buddhist, Hindu, Jain, Christian, and Muslim scholars — gave the empire a broad base of support. This inclusive strategy allowed Mughal authority to persist through his reign despite the immense ethnic and religious diversity of the Indian subcontinent.

## Historical Reasoning Practice: Purposes of Evidence

*Source: AMSCO AP World History, printed p. 259 (Think As a Historian)*

**Context:** The following statements come from an account by an Italian Jew (1495) describing the expulsion of Jews from Spain in 1492.

**Claim to evaluate:** *"Although the King's promises sound respectful of the Jews' rights to safety, the very act of expulsion marked them as inferior and exposed them to relentless harms."*

**Statement 1:** Vessels from Genoa came to carry away the Jews; the crews "acted maliciously and meanly toward the Jews, robbed them, and delivered some of them to the famous pirate… To those who escaped and arrived at Genoa the people of the city showed themselves merciless… they took the infants from the mothers."

> [!tip] Purpose: **Supports**
> This statement directly supports the claim by showing that Jews were exposed to robbery, piracy, and violence — harms that occurred precisely because the expulsion forced them to leave on terms set by others. The suffering was a direct consequence of the expulsion, demonstrating that Ferdinand's promises of protection were not fulfilled in practice.

**Statement 2:** 120,000 Jews went to Portugal under a compact; after six months "he made slaves of all [who] remained in his country, and banished seven hundred children to a remote island to settle it, and all of them died."

> [!tip] Purpose: **Supports**
> This also supports the claim, showing that even when Jews found temporary refuge, they were enslaved and their children effectively killed. The expulsion set in motion a chain of harms that the Spanish king's promises did not prevent.

**Statement 3:** Ships with Jews from Sicily went to Naples; "The King of this country was friendly to the Jews, received them all, and was merciful towards them, and he helped them with money."

> [!tip] Purpose: **Modifies**
> This statement modifies the claim by showing that not all outcomes were harmful — some Jews found genuine refuge and mercy. The Naples case suggests that the claim is partially true (the expulsion generally exposed Jews to harm) but overstated if read as absolute (some rulers were merciful). A sophisticated AP essay would use this to argue that harms varied by context while the root cause — the expulsion itself — was the constant.

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP DBQ and SAQ, always name the purpose explicitly (support, modify, refute), then explain *why* the evidence serves that purpose using specific details. Do not just summarize the source.
