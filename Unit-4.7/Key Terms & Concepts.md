# Key Terms & Concepts

## Government & Power

**Timar**
The Ottoman system in which the sultan granted land or tax revenues to loyal soldiers and officials. Used as a tool of political control — rewarding loyalty and keeping the military dependent on the sultan. (AMSCO p. 252)

**Vizier**
A powerful Ottoman advisor who governed in the sultan's name as sultans became increasingly weak. The rise of powerful viziers reflects the tension between centralized royal authority and a bureaucratic elite. (AMSCO p. 252)

**Harem politics**
The competition among wives and concubines of the Ottoman sultan to position their sons as heirs. Though confined to private quarters, harem women could wield significant indirect political power, as the "sultanate of women" controversy illustrated. (AMSCO p. 252)

**Queue (pigtail)**
The [[Concept Notes/Manchu\|Manchu]] braided hairstyle that the [[Concept Notes/Qing Dynasty\|Qing Dynasty]] required all men in China to wear as a symbol of submission to Qing authority. Refusing the queue meant execution. The policy reveals how new rulers used cultural symbols to enforce political dominance. (AMSCO p. 254)

**Boyar**
The noble landowning class in Russia. Boyars were the top of the Russian social hierarchy, controlling land and serfs. Ivan IV confiscated boyar lands in Novgorod after a failed rebellion and forced them to Moscow — an early example of tsarist centralization. (AMSCO p. 255)

## Society & Social Structure

**Jizya**
A tax paid by non-Muslims (Christians and Jews) living in Muslim empires such as the Ottoman and, initially, the Mughal. Akbar's abolition of the jizya was a landmark act of religious inclusion. (AMSCO p. 252)

**Serf**
A peasant legally bound to the land and transferred with it when a noble sold or transferred his estate. Not technically enslaved, but with sharply limited personal freedom. Serfdom intensified in Russia during this period as a tool of elite control. (AMSCO p. 255)

**Casta system**
The formal racial hierarchy in Spanish colonial Latin America. Racial categories were assigned at baptism and determined legal status, tax obligations, and social mobility. The system codified race as the primary axis of social inequality — a genuinely new feature of global history. (AMSCO pp. 255–256)

**[[Concept Notes/Peninsulares\|Peninsulares]]**
Individuals born in Spain or Portugal (the Iberian Peninsula), who occupied the top of the colonial Latin American social hierarchy. They held the highest government and Church positions. (AMSCO p. 255)

**Criollos**
People of full European ancestry born in the Americas. Below peninsulares but above castas. Criollos often resented peninsulares' monopoly on top offices — a tension that would fuel independence movements in [[Unit-5.2/Nationalism & Revolutions Overview|Topic 5.2]].

**Castas**
The collective term for people of mixed racial ancestry in Spanish colonial Latin America, subdivided into mestizos, mulattos, and zambos. (AMSCO p. 256)

**[[Concept Notes/Mestizos\|Mestizos]]**
People of mixed European and indigenous ancestry. Occupied the upper tier of the castas. (AMSCO p. 256)

**Mulattos (mulattoes)**
People of mixed European and African ancestry. Occupied the middle tier of the castas. (AMSCO p. 256)

**Zambos**
People of mixed indigenous and African ancestry. At the lower end of the castas, above only indigenous peoples and enslaved Africans. (AMSCO p. 256)

## Religion & Ethnicity

**Sephardic Jews**
Jews expelled from Spain in 1492 (the Hebrew word for Spain is *Sepharad*) who resettled around the Mediterranean — many welcomed by the Ottoman Empire. They maintained a distinct cultural identity from Ashkenazi Jews. (AMSCO p. 255)

**Ashkenazi Jews**
Jews from central and eastern Europe. The term derives from the Hebrew word for Germany. Distinguished from Sephardic Jews by liturgical tradition and cultural origin. (AMSCO p. 255)

## Economy & Coercion

**Barbary pirates**
Pirates operating from North African ports along the Barbary Coast, who raided European ships and coastal towns. They captured and sold Europeans into slavery — as many as one million people were *impressed* into Ottoman naval service between the 16th and 17th centuries. (AMSCO p. 253)

**Impressed**
Forced into service. Refers specifically to Europeans captured by Barbary pirates and compelled to serve as galley workers in the Ottoman fleet. (AMSCO p. 253)

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam will test your ability to compare social hierarchies across regions and explain their causes and effects. Be prepared to argue: (1) how rulers *used* social hierarchy as a political tool, and (2) how the colonial encounter created *new* racial hierarchies unlike anything in earlier periods.

> [!warning]
> Don't confuse criollos and peninsulares. Criollos were of European descent but born in the Americas — they were below peninsulares in the casta system even though they were ethnically European. This distinction is tested frequently.
