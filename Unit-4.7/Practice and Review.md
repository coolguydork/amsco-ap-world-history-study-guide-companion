# Topic 4.7 – Practice and Review

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The casta system established in Spanish colonial Latin America most directly resulted from

- A) Spanish Catholic missionaries' need to track conversion among indigenous peoples
- B) the encounter of European, African, and indigenous American populations, which prompted colonizers to formalize race-based social stratification
- C) pre-existing racial hierarchies that the Spanish imported from their home country
- D) indigenous American social structures that the Spanish adapted for colonial administration

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The casta system was a new creation of the colonial Americas, not an import from Spain or an adaptation of indigenous systems. It developed because Spanish colonizers needed a legal framework to manage the new multiracial society that emerged from the encounter of three populations.

---

**2.** The Qing Dynasty's policy requiring all men in China to wear their hair in queues was most significant because it

- A) improved Chinese hygiene and public health
- B) served as a loyalty test and symbol of Manchu cultural dominance over Han Chinese
- C) demonstrated Qing respect for Han Chinese cultural traditions
- D) was quickly abandoned when it proved politically unpopular

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The queue requirement was a deliberate cultural imposition — a visible sign that the Manchu ruled China. Refusing could result in execution. It was simultaneously a loyalty test and a humiliation for Han Chinese men who valued Confucian norms about bodily integrity.

---

**3.** The Ottoman Empire's relative tolerance of Jewish and Christian minorities, compared to contemporary European treatment of religious minorities, is best explained by

- A) Ottoman adherence to Islamic principles requiring equal treatment of all faiths
- B) pragmatic policies that protected minority communities while maintaining Muslim supremacy through legal distinctions like the jizya
- C) the absence of any tension between Muslim, Jewish, and Christian communities in the empire
- D) Ottoman rulers' personal agnosticism about religious matters

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Ottoman tolerance was real but limited. Non-Muslims could practice their faith and were protected, but they paid a special tax (jizya), lived in designated areas, and could not hold top positions. It was tolerance structured by hierarchy, not equality.

**4.** Which of the following best explains the growing political ambitions of creoles (American-born people of European descent) in Spanish colonial America by 1750?

- A) Creoles had been denied the right to own property or engage in trade
- B) Creoles were politically dominant locally but resented their subordination to Spain-born peninsulares in the highest colonial offices
- C) Creoles had allied with indigenous and African populations against Spanish rule
- D) Creoles were denied access to Catholic education and religious institutions

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Creoles had achieved substantial local political and economic power by 1750, but the best positions (viceroy, archbishop, high court judges) were reserved for peninsulares. This glass ceiling — frustrated ambition, not fundamental oppression — would drive the independence movements of the early 19th century.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Question**: Explain how ONE social hierarchy in the period 1450–1750 was CONSTRUCTED (maintained or created) by the state AND provide ONE example of how individuals or groups challenged or adapted to it.

**Model Response (Casta System)**:

**Construction**: The casta system in Spanish colonial Latin America was actively constructed by the Spanish colonial state through legal decree and religious record-keeping. People were assigned to their casta level at baptism — making the church a key instrument of state social control. The hierarchy determined not only social status but also legal rights, taxation rates (lower castas paid higher taxes), and occupational access. The Spanish crown even commissioned casta paintings — elaborate visual representations of the different mixed-race groups — to reinforce the hierarchy culturally and legally. This was not a natural social evolution but a deliberate state construction of race as a legal category.

**Challenge/Adaptation**: Individuals adapted to the casta system by pursuing intermarriage to move their children up the hierarchy — a strategy that recognized the system's logic while exploiting its pathways for advancement. Creoles, despite their position below peninsulares, achieved enough local political power by 1750 that they were effectively the dominant class in daily colonial governance. Their growing political ambitions — frustrated by the peninsulares' monopoly on top positions — eventually drove them to challenge the entire system through independence movements in the early 19th century.

## Comparison: Social Hierarchies Across Regions

| Region | Primary Organizing Principle | Top | Bottom | Key Feature |
|--------|------------------------------|-----|--------|-------------|
| Ottoman Empire | Religion + military service | Sultan + warrior aristocracy | Enslaved peoples | Relative tolerance of minorities (jizya system) |
| Qing China | Ethnicity (Manchu over Han) | Manchu officials | Han peasants/enslaved | Queue requirement as loyalty test |
| Spanish Americas | Race (casta system) | Peninsulares | Indigenous/enslaved Africans | First formal legal race hierarchy |
| Europe | Birth + religion | Royalty + nobility | Serfs/enslaved | Nobility increasingly challenged by middle class |
| Russia | Birth + service | Tsar + boyars | Serfs | Serfdom legally codified 1649 |

> [!tip]
> Notice that every hierarchy in this period was justified by at least one of these three things: birth/ancestry, religion, or ethnic/racial identity. The colonial Americas added a fourth — visual physical appearance (skin color) as a proxy for ancestry. This was genuinely new. Before 1500, no legal system in the world used skin color as the primary marker of legal status.

## LEQ Connections

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 4.7:**
- "Evaluate the extent to which social hierarchies changed in the period 1450–1750."
- "Compare the methods used by ONE empire to maintain social hierarchy in the period 1450–1750 with the methods used by another empire in the same period."
- "Explain the causes of changing social structures in the Americas in the period 1500–1750."

**Key argument: Extent of change**

*What changed*: The casta system created entirely new racial categories that did not exist before 1500. The Qing imposed new ethnic hierarchies on China. The European middle class grew significantly, challenging the dominance of the nobility. Enslaved people's status formalized in law across multiple regions.

*What continued*: Hierarchies based on birth, religion, and wealth continued everywhere. Nobles continued to dominate European politics even as their power was occasionally challenged. The Ottoman jizya system of religious hierarchy continued unchanged. Serfdom persisted and was actually strengthened in Russia.

**Strong thesis**: "While the period 1450–1750 saw significant changes in social hierarchy — especially the creation of formal race-based hierarchies in colonial Latin America — in much of Afro-Eurasia, traditional hierarchies based on birth, religion, and ethnicity continued largely unchanged, demonstrating that European colonial expansion transformed the Americas more fundamentally than it transformed existing social structures in Asia and Europe."
