# Key People and Groups — Unit 4.7: (4.7): Changing Social Hierarchies

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

### Enslaved Africans and the African Diaspora
- **Region:** Americas, Caribbean, Brazil
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- Occupied the lowest tier of the colonial social hierarchy — legally property, not persons.
- Maintained African cultural traditions through oral history, music, dance, religion (Candomblé, Vodou, Santería), and language, creating hybrid Afro-American cultures.
- Forms of resistance — from foot-dragging to maroon communities to organized rebellion — demonstrate that enslaved people rejected their social position and actively challenged it.

---

### Mestizo and Mixed-Race Populations
- **Region:** Spanish and Portuguese Americas
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- Produced by the widespread sexual exploitation of Indigenous and African women by European men; over time became the demographic majority in many Latin American regions.
- Occupied ambiguous and contested social positions — sometimes able to "pass" into higher categories through wealth or documentation, sometimes harshly restricted.
- Their increasing numbers made the casta system progressively harder to administer and enforce.

---

### Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz (1648–1695)
- **Region:** New Spain (Mexico)
- **Period:** Late 17th century
- A creole nun, poet, and intellectual — one of the most significant literary figures of colonial Latin America and a rare woman who achieved public intellectual prominence.
- Her writings defended women's right to education and intellectual life in a deeply patriarchal colonial society.
- Her career illustrates both the limited possibilities for women within colonial hierarchies and the creative ways exceptional individuals navigated them.

---

### Creole Elites
- **Region:** Spanish Americas
- **Period:** 17th–18th century
- Wealthy, educated, American-born descendants of Spanish settlers; excluded from the highest colonial offices despite their education and wealth.
- Their resentment of peninsulares and mercantilist restrictions made them natural leaders of later independence movements.
- Demonstrated that social hierarchy was not only racial but also place-of-birth based.

---

### Indigenous Women in Colonial Society
- **Region:** Spanish and Portuguese Americas
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- Subject to domestic service, sexual exploitation, and forced religious conversion; their labor and bodies were sites of colonial power.
- Some Indigenous women navigated colonial society strategically: doña Marina (La Malinche), Cortés's translator and companion, wielded significant influence despite — and through — her ambiguous position.
- Their experiences illustrate the intersection of race, gender, and colonial power.

---

### Free People of Color
- **Region:** Spanish Americas, Caribbean
- **Period:** 17th–18th century
- Legally free individuals of African descent who occupied an intermediate social space — not enslaved, but subject to legal discrimination and restricted from the highest social positions.
- Could own property, run businesses, and in some contexts serve in colonial militias.
- Their existence demonstrated the casta system's complexity and the limits of a simple binary racial order.

> [!exam-tip]
> **Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz** is a sophisticated AP example of gender within colonial hierarchy. Her case shows that social constraints on women were real but not absolute — exceptional circumstances (religious vocation, extraordinary talent) could create limited space for female intellectual achievement.
