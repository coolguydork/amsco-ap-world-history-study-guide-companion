# Topic 4.7 – Key Terms and People

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Social Hierarchy Terms

### The Americas: Casta System

**Peninsulares**
People born on the Iberian Peninsula (Spain or Portugal) who lived in the Americas. Held the highest social status in colonial Spanish America; received preference for top government and church positions.

**Criollos / Creoles** (Latin American context)
People of European (Spanish or Portuguese) ancestry who were born in the Americas. Below peninsulares in social status despite similar racial heritage. *Significance*: By 1750, creoles dominated local politics in New Spain; their resentment of peninsulares' privilege would fuel independence movements in the early 19th century.

**Castas**
The collective term for people of mixed-race ancestry in colonial Spanish America, arranged in a sub-hierarchy:
- **Mestizos**: Mixed European and indigenous ancestry (the largest mixed-race group)
- **Mulattoes**: Mixed European and African ancestry
- **Zambos**: Mixed indigenous and African ancestry

*Significance*: The casta system formalized race as a legal category with profound social and economic consequences — higher taxes, restricted occupations, and limited rights for those lower in the hierarchy.

**Casta Paintings**
Elaborate paintings commissioned by Spanish colonial authorities to depict and categorize the different mixed-race groups of colonial society. *Significance*: The fact that the Spanish *painted* these hierarchies shows how deliberately they were constructed and enforced as social categories.

> [!warning]
> Race in the modern sense — as a formal legal and social category — was largely *invented* in the colonial Americas. Before 1492, social hierarchies in Afro-Eurasia were organized primarily around religion, class, and ethnicity, not race. The casta system is a key example of how colonialism created racial categories that shaped society for centuries.

### Ottoman Empire Terms

**Ulama**
Scholars and experts in Islamic law who played a central role in Ottoman governance and education. They competed with the warrior aristocracy for positions in the imperial bureaucracy.

**Janissaries**
Elite Ottoman infantry soldiers, originally recruited as children from Christian families through the devshirme (tribute children) system, then raised as Muslims and trained as soldiers. They gained great power and prestige — eventually attempting coups against ineffective sultans. *Significance*: Shows how the Ottoman system of forced conversion and elite military training created powerful actors who could threaten imperial authority.

**Viziers**
Powerful Ottoman ministers who spoke for the sultan and managed the empire when sultans were weak or ineffective. *Significance*: The real power in the empire often lay with viziers, not the sultan — a form of delegated authority that could undermine central control.

**Timar**
The Ottoman sultan's power to grant land or tax revenues to favored individuals — especially soldiers — as a reward and tool of loyalty. *Significance*: A key mechanism of Ottoman governance; comparable to European feudalism's land-for-service exchange but operating under a different legal framework.

**Harem**
The private residence of a powerful man's wives and concubines in the Ottoman world. *Significance*: Often portrayed as purely ornamental, the harem was in fact a site of serious political competition, as wives and concubines lobbied for their sons to become the sultan's heir. "Harem politics" (the "sultanate of the women") was a real phenomenon that viziers complained about.

**Jizya**
A tax required of non-Muslims (Jews and Christians) living in the Ottoman Empire. *Significance*: Represented the Ottoman approach to religious tolerance — non-Muslims could practice their faith and were protected, but they were not equal; the tax marked their subordinate status.

### Qing China Terms

**Qing Dynasty (1644–1912)**
The dynasty founded when the Manchu people from Manchuria conquered Han China. The last Chinese imperial dynasty. *Significance*: The Qing ruled a multiethnic empire in which the Manchu minority dominated the Han majority — a situation that created persistent social tensions.

**Manchu**
The ethnic group from Manchuria (northeast of China proper) that conquered China and founded the Qing Dynasty. Culturally distinct from the Han Chinese majority. *Significance*: Like the Mongols before them, the Manchu were foreign rulers of China who faced the challenge of governing a much larger population with a different culture.

**Queues**
The braided pigtail hairstyle mandated by the Qing for all men in China. Refusing to wear a queue could result in execution. *Significance*: The queue requirement was both a symbol of Manchu cultural dominance and a practical loyalty test. It was also a humiliation for Han Chinese men, who saw it as a violation of Confucian norms about physical integrity.

**Hanfu**
Traditional Han Chinese clothing. Non-official Han civilians were permitted to wear Hanfu, but all men had to adopt the Manchu queue hairstyle. *Significance*: The distinction shows the Qing's nuanced approach — cultural imposition (hair) combined with some tolerance (clothing).

### European Terms

**Boyars**
The Russian noble landowning class. Competed with tsars for power; occasionally resisted royal authority (e.g., boyars of Novgorod opposed Ivan IV). *Significance*: The boyar class was the Russian equivalent of European nobility — wealthy landowners whose power depended on serf labor and royal favor.

**Serf**
A peasant bound to the land they worked. In Russia, a 1649 law chained serfs to their birthplace and made them subject to landlord authority. *Significance*: Russian serfdom persisted long after it had ended in Western Europe (Russian serfs weren't formally freed until 1861). It was the foundation of the Russian agricultural economy and a source of chronic social tension.

**Sephardic Jews**
Jews who trace their heritage to Spain (Sepharad = Hebrew for Spain), expelled from Spain in 1492. Many resettled in North Africa, the Middle East, and especially the Ottoman Empire, which offered relative tolerance.

**Ashkenazi Jews**
Jews from central and eastern Europe (the term originally referred to Germany). Distinguished from Sephardic Jews by different traditions, languages (Yiddish), and migration history.

**Barbary Pirates**
Pirates operating near North Africa (the "Barbary Coast"), named for the Berber peoples who lived there. They captured European Christian slaves in the Mediterranean and sold them to the Ottoman sultan or other high-ranking officials. *Significance*: Slavery was not exclusively a trans-Atlantic African phenomenon — Mediterranean slave-raiding created a substantial population of enslaved Europeans in the Ottoman world.

## Key People

**Roxelana (c. 1502–1558)**
Enslaved woman from Eastern Europe who rose to become the wife of Ottoman Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent — an extraordinary achievement in a society where concubines did not become wives. She wielded significant political influence and commissioned major public works. *Significance*: Both an exception that proves the rule (social mobility was possible but rare) and an example of women's political influence operating through informal channels in the Ottoman world.

**Sultan Mehmed II**
Ottoman ruler who invited Jews expelled from Spain (1492) to settle in Istanbul. *Significance*: Illustrates Ottoman religious tolerance — a pragmatic policy that benefited the empire economically and intellectually.

**Ivan IV ("Ivan the Terrible") (r. 1547–1584)**
Russian tsar who defeated the boyars of Novgorod, confiscated their lands, and forced them to Moscow under his surveillance. Also murdered his own son. *Significance*: Represents the Russian tsar's absolute power — and its psychological instability; the boyar-tsar tension he embodied persisted throughout this period.

**Louis XIV (r. 1643–1715)**
French absolute monarch famous for declaring "I am the state." Used the memory of the Fronde rebellion to justify concentrating power away from nobles and common people. Built Versailles to keep nobles at court and under his eye. *Significance*: The archetype of European absolute monarchy; his model was imitated across Europe.

**Li Chengdong and Liu Liangzuo**
Han Chinese defectors to the Qing who carried out massacres of Han populations that resisted Qing authority. Li Chengdong orchestrated three massacres in Jiading within a month; Liu Liangzuo massacred the entire population of Jiangyin (74,000–100,000 people). *Significance*: Shows how the Qing used Han collaborators to enforce their rule — a pattern of divide-and-rule that characterized many imperial systems.
