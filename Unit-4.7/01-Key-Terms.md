# Key Terms — Unit 4.7: (4.7): Changing Social Hierarchies

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

1. **Casta system** — The Spanish colonial racial hierarchy that classified people by degree of European, Indigenous, and African ancestry, determining legal status and social position. (Formalized racial inequality; produced dozens of named categories for mixed-race individuals.)

2. **Peninsulares** — Persons born in Spain who immigrated to the Americas and held the highest social and political status in colonial society. (Occupied top positions in colonial government; source of growing creole resentment.)

3. **Creole (criollo)** — A person of European descent born in the Americas. (Occupied second tier of colonial hierarchy; excluded from highest offices; their resentments eventually fueled independence movements.)

4. **Mestizo** — A person of mixed European and Indigenous ancestry. (Occupied an intermediate social position; over time became the demographic majority in many Latin American regions.)

5. **Mulato** — A person of mixed European and African ancestry. (Occupied a lower intermediate position in the casta system; subject to more legal restrictions than mestizos.)

6. **Racial chattel slavery** — A system of slavery in which enslaved status is heritable, permanent, and determined by race. (Distinctive feature of the Atlantic slave system; unlike most earlier forms of slavery, it was race-based and absolute.)

7. **African diaspora** — The global dispersal of African peoples and cultures resulting from the Atlantic slave trade. (Created lasting African-descended communities in the Americas; preserved and transformed African cultural traditions.)

8. **Syncretism** — The blending of different cultural or religious traditions. (In the Americas, enslaved Africans and Indigenous peoples blended their traditions with Catholicism, producing new hybrid religious forms like Candomblé and Vodou.)

9. **Gender hierarchy** — The system of social inequality based on sex/gender, in which men typically have greater legal rights, economic resources, and political power than women. (Colonial encounters disrupted existing gender hierarchies in complex ways — sometimes creating new vulnerabilities for women, sometimes creating new spaces for female authority.)

10. **Two-spirit / muxe** — Indigenous gender categories that recognized gender identities beyond the European binary of male/female. (European colonizers often persecuted these identities; their suppression illustrates how colonialism imposed European gender norms on Indigenous societies.)

11. **Encomienda labor and women** — Indigenous women in encomienda households were subject to domestic service and sexual exploitation by Spanish men, producing the mestizo population. (Gender and racial exploitation were inseparable in the colonial system.)

12. **Free people of color** — Individuals of African descent who were legally free, existing in a complex middle space in colonial social hierarchies. (Could own property, enter certain occupations, sometimes served in colonial militias; their existence complicated the binary slave/free distinction.)

13. **Limpieza de sangre ("purity of blood")** — A Spanish concept requiring documentation of ancestry free from Jewish, Muslim, or African blood for entry into certain positions. (Extended racial categorization from the Reconquista into colonial society; linked religious and racial discrimination.)

> [!exam-tip]
> The key distinction between the **casta system** (Spanish Americas) and **slavery in British North America** is that the casta system was more fluid — free people of color and mestizos had intermediate legal statuses — while British colonial law increasingly moved toward a stark free/enslaved binary based on race.
