| 661–750 | Umayyad Caliphate | First major Islamic dynasty; expanded from Spain to Central Asia |
| 711 CE | Muslim forces invade Spain | Beginning of al-Andalus; Islamic rule in Iberia begins |

| 750 CE | Abbasid Caliphate begins | Replaces Umayyads; moves capital to Baghdad; Golden Age begins |

## Key Chronological Patterns

### The Abbasid Arc
The Abbasid Caliphate's history traces a classic rise-and-fall pattern: establishment (750) → Golden Age (8th–10th centuries) → gradual decline under Seljuk pressure (11th century) → economic decline as trade shifted (12th century) → final destruction by Mongols (1258). Understanding this arc is essential for answering causes-of-state-decline questions.

### The Turkic Succession
A consistent pattern across Period 1: **Turkic peoples from Central Asia** successively conquered Islamic territory and then adopted Islam, creating new states. Seljuk Turks (11th c.) → Mamluks in Egypt (13th c.) → Delhi Sultanate in South Asia (13th c.). This same pattern continues into Period 2 with the Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal Empires.

### The Knowledge-Transfer Timeline
The chain of Islamic intellectual influence on Europe unfolded over roughly 150 years:
- **12th century**: Ibn Rushd (Averroes) writes Aristotle commentaries in al-Andalus
- **c. 1135–1204**: Maimonides absorbs and adapts Averroes; synthesizes with Jewish tradition
- **1225–1274**: Thomas Aquinas absorbs Maimonides; applies Aristotelian logic to Christian theology
- **Late 15th–16th centuries**: This chain contributes to the Renaissance and Scientific Revolution

## Dates to Memorize for the AP Exam

| Date | What Happened | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1258** | Mongols destroy Baghdad | End of Abbasid Caliphate; most important political event in Islamic Period 1 |
| **732** | Battle of Tours | Limit of Islamic expansion into Europe; Spain remains Islamic |
| **1206** | Delhi Sultanate founded | Islam established in South Asia; precursor to Mughal Empire |
| **1325–1354** | Ibn Battuta travels | Primary source for Islamic cultural unity; common AP document stimulus |

> [!exam-tip]
> For the AP exam, you do not need to memorize all dates — but **1258** (fall of Baghdad) and **1206** (Delhi Sultanate) are the most likely to appear in MCQ stimulus context or FRQ prompts about Islamic state-building and the Mongol impact.
