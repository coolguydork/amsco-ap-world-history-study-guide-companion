# People, States & Movements

**Unit 8.3 — Effects of the Cold War | AMSCO pp. 562–570**

---

## Superpower Leaders

**Dwight D. Eisenhower**
US President (1953–1961) who continued the Truman containment doctrine; sent the first military advisers to South Vietnam; coined the term "military-industrial complex"; oversaw the formation of SEATO and CENTO; his "New Look" strategy relied on nuclear deterrence ("massive retaliation") rather than expensive conventional forces.

**John F. Kennedy**
US President (1961–1963) who presided over three defining Cold War crises: the Bay of Pigs invasion (1961), the Berlin Wall construction (1961), and the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962). Increased US military advisers in Vietnam from 1,000 to 16,000. Advocated a "flexible response" strategy beyond nuclear deterrence. Assassinated in Dallas in November 1963.

> [!exam-tip]
> Kennedy is the AP's go-to example for Cold War crisis management. His handling of the Cuban Missile Crisis — accepting a face-saving compromise rather than forcing military confrontation — is cited as a model of nuclear-age diplomacy.

**Lyndon B. Johnson**
US President (1963–1969) who massively escalated the Vietnam War, eventually deploying 500,000 US troops. Justified escalation through the domino theory. His failure to achieve victory in Vietnam destroyed his domestic political standing; he declined to seek re-election in 1968. Also authored landmark civil rights and social legislation (Great Society).

**Nikita Khrushchev**
Soviet Premier (1953–1964) who denounced Stalin's crimes in the "Secret Speech" (1956), oversaw the Sputnik launch (1957), authorized construction of the Berlin Wall (1961), and managed the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962). His perceived weakness in withdrawing missiles from Cuba contributed to his removal by Soviet hardliners in 1964.

**Douglas MacArthur**
US Army general who commanded UN forces in the Korean War (1950–1951); dramatically reversed North Korean gains with the Inchon landing; pushed toward the Chinese border, provoking Chinese intervention; was fired by President Truman after publicly advocating expanding the war into China. His dismissal established the principle of civilian control over military commanders.

## Cold War States

**West Germany (Federal Republic of Germany)**
Established 1949 from the US, British, and French occupation zones; democratic, capitalist; joined [[Concept Notes/NATO\|NATO]] in 1955; became an economic powerhouse (the "German economic miracle"); a frontline state of the Western bloc.

**East Germany (German Democratic Republic)**
Established 1949 from the Soviet occupation zone; communist, Soviet-aligned; Warsaw Pact member; built the Berlin Wall in 1961 to stop mass emigration; collapsed in 1989.

**South Korea / North Korea**
Divided at the 38th parallel after WWII (US/Soviet occupation zones). North Korea's 1950 invasion triggered the Korean War; the armistice restored the pre-war division. Both countries remained in a technical state of war, heavily armed, through the end of the Cold War and beyond.

**South Vietnam / North Vietnam**
Divided after the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu (1954). North Vietnam, led by Ho Chi Minh's communist government, sought to reunify the country; South Vietnam was backed by the US. The Vietnam War ended with North Vietnamese victory and reunification in 1975.

**Cuba**
Caribbean island nation that became a Cold War flashpoint after Fidel Castro's revolution (1959). Castro nationalized US-owned businesses; the US imposed an embargo and backed the failed Bay of Pigs invasion (1961). Cuba then became a Soviet ally — and the site of the 1962 missile crisis. Cuba also sent troops to support Soviet-aligned factions in Angola.

**Yugoslavia**
Balkan communist state that uniquely resisted Soviet domination under Tito; never joined the Warsaw Pact; pursued "non-aligned" socialism. After Tito's death (1980), ethnic divisions destabilized the country; Yugoslavia broke apart in the 1990s into Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia, Serbia, Montenegro, and North Macedonia.

**Angola**
Southwestern African nation; achieved independence from Portugal in 1975 after years of armed struggle; immediately fell into a Cold War proxy civil war between three ethnic factions backed by the USSR/Cuba, South Africa, and the US respectively; civil war finally ended in 2002.

## Movements

**Non-Aligned Movement**
Coalition of newly independent nations that refused to align with either superpower bloc; emerged from the Bandung Conference (1955) in Indonesia; key leaders: Jawaharlal Nehru (India), Josip Broz Tito (Yugoslavia), and Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana). Represented the political voice of the developing world during the Cold War.

> [!tip]
> The Non-Aligned Movement is crucial context for the MCQ map questions about the Cold War alliance system — the AP frequently includes a third category (non-aligned states) alongside NATO and Warsaw Pact, and asks students to identify which leaders or nations belonged to it.

**Antinuclear Weapons Movement**
Popular movement opposing nuclear weapons, originating in Japan in the 1950s; spread to the US and Western Europe by the late 1970s–early 1980s; reached its peak with a 1-million-person demonstration in New York City (June 1982) during the Reagan-era nuclear buildup. Demonstrates transnational civil society organizing (SIO theme).

**Sandinistas / Contras**
In Nicaragua: the Sandinistas (socialist revolutionaries who overthrew the Somoza dictatorship in 1979) vs. the US-backed Contra rebels (1981–1990). The Contra War raised major ethical and legal controversies about US covert foreign intervention.

## Key Individuals (non-US/Soviet)

**Ho Chi Minh**
Vietnamese communist leader who led independence movements first against France, then against the US-backed South Vietnamese government. Inspired by both nationalism and Marxism-Leninism; his death (1969) preceded the North Vietnamese victory (1975) that bore out the containment failure he represented.

**Fidel Castro**
Cuban revolutionary leader who overthrew dictator Fulgencio Batista in 1959; established a communist government in Cuba; nationalized US businesses; survived the Bay of Pigs invasion; aligned with the USSR; ruled Cuba until 2008. The Cuban Revolution transformed Cuba into a persistent Cold War flashpoint 90 miles from the US.

**Jawaharlal Nehru**
India's first Prime Minister (1947–1964); co-founder of the Non-Aligned Movement; sought to keep newly independent India out of both superpower blocs while pursuing socialist economic development. A major voice for the Global South during the early Cold War.

**Josip Broz Tito**
Yugoslav communist leader who broke with Stalin (1948) and refused Soviet domination; never joined the Warsaw Pact; co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement. His independence demonstrated that communism did not automatically mean Soviet control.
