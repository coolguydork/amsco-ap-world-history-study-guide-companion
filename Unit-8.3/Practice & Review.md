## Multiple-Choice Practice

*Questions 1–3 refer to a map of NATO and the Warsaw Pact, 1956, showing Western Europe (NATO), Eastern Europe (Warsaw Pact), communist non-aligned (Yugoslavia, Albania), and non-Communist non-aligned states.*

**Question 1.** The map most clearly reflects the context of the ongoing efforts of

A. the Marshall Plan to stimulate global economic growth
B. the United States to contain communism
C. the European Economic Union to unify the European economy
D. the United States to improve relations with the Soviet Union

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The map shows NATO (US-led Western alliance) surrounding the Soviet-dominated Warsaw Pact — the classic visual representation of containment strategy. The Marshall Plan (A) was an economic program, not shown by military alliance membership. The EEC (C) and US-Soviet détente (D) are not what this alliance division illustrates.

**Question 2.** Which best describes the Warsaw Pact bloc shown on the map?

A. A Soviet-led government based in Warsaw, the capital of Poland
B. A military alliance created in response to NATO by the Soviet Union and other communist nations
C. An economic union among Soviet-bloc countries created to counter the economic power of the European Economic Community
D. A military alliance of non-aligned nations of Europe, Asia, and Africa

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Warsaw Pact (1955) was a Soviet-led military alliance among Eastern European communist states, created explicitly as a counterpart to NATO. It was not a government (A), not an economic union (C — that was COMECON), and not non-aligned (D).

**Question 3.** Which countries represented most closely the declared position of Jawaharlal Nehru, Kwame Nkrumah, and Gamal Abdel-Nasser?

A. NATO
B. Warsaw Pact
C. Non-Communist, non-aligned
D. Communist, non-aligned

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Nehru (India), Nkrumah (Ghana), and Nasser (Egypt) were founders of the Non-Aligned Movement — newly independent nations that refused to join either superpower bloc. They were anti-colonial and often socialist, but explicitly not communist-bloc aligned.

## Short-Answer Practice

### SAQ 1 — Bay of Pigs (Document-Based)

*Passage:* A 2011 military history article on the April 1961 Bay of Pigs invasion describes it as a CIA-planned disaster in which 1,500 Cuban exiles were crushed by Castro's forces, with 114 killed and 1,200 captured. Kennedy famously asked: "How could I have been so stupid?"

**Part A:** Identify ONE immediate consequence of the failed invasion.

> [!tip] Model Response — Consequence
> One immediate consequence was the severe embarrassment and loss of credibility for the Kennedy administration. The failed invasion demonstrated that the CIA's operation was poorly planned and executed, undermining US prestige at the start of the Cold War's most dangerous period and emboldening Soviet Premier Khrushchev to take bolder actions — most directly, shipping nuclear missiles to Cuba in 1962.

**Part B:** Explain ONE piece of historical evidence for the failure of the invasion.

> [!tip] Model Response — Evidence
> The CIA's intelligence failures exemplify why the invasion failed. The agency's planners assumed a popular uprising would support the invaders — but the Cuban population largely did not rise against Castro. Additionally, Castro's air force retained four armed jet trainers that destroyed the invasion's air support, a capability the CIA had not anticipated. Both failures reflect the broader problem that CIA leadership was "so enamored of the plan they ignored its obvious flaws," as the passage states.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way the events in the passage reflected the relationship between Cuba and the United States prior to 1961.

> [!tip] Model Response — Prior Relationship
> Prior to the invasion, Cuba and the United States had a deeply antagonistic relationship rooted in Castro's 1959 revolution. After coming to power, Castro nationalized US-owned businesses and properties in Cuba, which cost American corporations significant investments. The Eisenhower administration had already begun planning a covert operation to remove Castro before Kennedy took office, reflecting US determination not to tolerate a communist government 90 miles from its shores. The Bay of Pigs invasion was thus the culmination of escalating hostility, not a sudden shift.

### SAQ 2 — Comparative US/Soviet Influence

**Part A:** Identify ONE way US policies to maintain global influence were **similar** to Soviet policies during the Cold War.

> [!tip] Model Response — Similarity
> Both the United States and the Soviet Union used military alliances to extend their influence globally. The US built NATO, SEATO, and CENTO; the Soviets built the Warsaw Pact and formed security agreements with Cuba, Vietnam, and Angola. In both cases, the superpower provided arms, financial support, and military advisers to allied states in exchange for political alignment, effectively using smaller nations as instruments of their own strategic competition.

**Part B:** Identify ONE way US policies to maintain global influence were **different** from Soviet policies during the Cold War.

> [!tip] Model Response — Difference
> The United States more extensively used covert operations through the CIA to overthrow unfriendly governments or support anti-communist movements, maintaining a degree of plausible deniability. Operations in Guatemala (1954), Cuba (1961), and Chile (1973) exemplify this approach. While the Soviets also used covert means, they were more willing to deploy Warsaw Pact forces openly against rebellious satellite states (Hungary 1956, Czechoslovakia 1968), reflecting a difference in how each superpower exercised control over its sphere of influence.

**Part C:** Explain ONE historical development in Latin America that contributed to furthering tensions between the United States and the Soviet Union during the Cold War.

> [!tip] Model Response — Latin America
> The Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962 was the single most tension-escalating development in Latin America during the Cold War. After the failed Bay of Pigs invasion convinced Khrushchev that Kennedy was weak, the Soviets secretly shipped nuclear missiles to Cuba. When US intelligence discovered the missiles in October 1962, it triggered a 13-day nuclear standoff — the closest the Cold War came to direct superpower military conflict. The crisis forced both sides to negotiate a settlement (Soviet withdrawal of missiles; US pledge not to invade Cuba) and ultimately led to the Hot Line and Nuclear Test-Ban Treaty, demonstrating how a regional development could push the superpowers to the brink of nuclear war.

## Review: Essential Question

**In what ways did both the Soviet Union and the United States seek to maintain influence during the Cold War?**

Both superpowers used the same basic toolkit — military alliances, arms transfers, economic aid, proxy wars, and covert operations — to build and maintain spheres of influence. The US organized NATO, SEATO, and CENTO to contain Soviet expansion; the Soviets built the Warsaw Pact and supported communist governments from Cuba to Vietnam to Angola. When client states were threatened, both powers intervened: the US in Korea, Vietnam, and Nicaragua; the Soviets in Hungary and Czechoslovakia.

The crucial constraint was nuclear deterrence: neither superpower could use its most powerful weapons without risking its own annihilation. This forced both to fight indirectly, through proxies, in the developing world — where millions died in conflicts the superpowers themselves survived unscathed.

> [!exam-tip]
> For the AP free-response, always connect US/Soviet methods of maintaining influence back to the central logic of containment (US) vs. expansion of the socialist bloc (USSR) — then show how proxy wars, alliances, and arms control all reflect that logic. Specific examples are essential: name the wars, the alliances, the treaties.
