# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 8.3 — Effects of the Cold War | AMSCO pp. 562–570**

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1945–1948 | Allied occupation zones in Germany established | Sets up the division of Germany and Berlin into superpower spheres |
| 1948–1949 | Berlin Blockade and Airlift | Soviets blockade West Berlin; US/UK airlift supplies; Soviets back down; confirms Cold War division of Europe |
| 1949 | West Germany (FRG) established | Democratic, capitalist West Germany joins Western bloc |
| 1949 | NATO founded | US, Canada, and 10 Western European nations form collective defense alliance; marks permanent US commitment to European security |
| 1949 | East Germany (GDR) established | Communist, Soviet-aligned East Germany; division of Germany formalized |
| 1950–1953 | Korean War | North Korea invades South; US-led UN forces intervene; China enters; stalemate at 38th parallel; ~4 million casualties |
| 1954 | SEATO founded | US-led Southeast Asia collective defense alliance; 8 members |
| 1954–1975 | Vietnam War | Longest proxy war; US supports South Vietnam; ends in communist victory and reunification |
| 1955 | Warsaw Pact established | Soviet-led military alliance; direct counterpart to NATO |
| 1955 | CENTO formed | Middle East anti-Soviet alliance; Britain, Turkey, Iran, Iraq, Pakistan |
| 1955 | Bandung Conference | 29 newly independent African and Asian states meet in Indonesia; seeds of Non-Aligned Movement |
| 1956 | Hungarian Revolution | Hungarian attempt to leave Warsaw Pact; crushed by Soviet tanks; demonstrates Soviet domination of Eastern Europe |
| 1959 | Cuban Revolution | Fidel Castro overthrows Batista; establishes communist Cuba; transforms Caribbean into Cold War flashpoint |
| 1961 | Bay of Pigs invasion | CIA-backed Cuban exile invasion fails; embarrasses Kennedy; emboldens Soviets |
| 1961 | Berlin Wall built | East Germany seals border with West Berlin; ~150 killed crossing it 1961–1989 |
| 1962 | Cuban Missile Crisis | 13-day nuclear standoff; resolved by mutual withdrawal of missiles from Cuba and Turkey |
| 1963 | Hot Line established | Direct Washington-Moscow communication link; first US-Soviet crisis management mechanism |
| 1963 | Nuclear Test-Ban Treaty | US, USSR, UK ban atmospheric, underwater, and space nuclear tests |
| 1965 | US ground troops to Vietnam | Johnson escalates with 500,000 troops; domino theory drives policy |
| 1968 | Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty | Nuclear states pledge not to help others acquire nuclear weapons |
| 1973 | US withdraws from Vietnam | Paris Peace Accords; US military withdrawal; South Vietnam falls 1975 |
| 1975 | Angola independence and civil war | Portuguese decolonization triggers Cold War proxy civil war (lasts until 2002) |
| 1979 | Sandinista revolution in Nicaragua | Socialists overthrow Somoza dictatorship |
| 1981–1990 | Contra War (Nicaragua) | US-backed Contra rebels fight Sandinista government; raises questions about US intervention |
| 1982 | NYC antinuclear demonstration | 1 million people march against nuclear weapons; peak of Western antinuclear movement |
| 1989 | Berlin Wall falls | November 9; effectively ends the Cold War |
| 2002 | Angola civil war ends | Cease-fire after 27 years; superpower competition long over but legacy violence continues |

## Chronological Patterns to Know

**Pattern 1 — Alliance building precedes proxy wars:** [[Concept Notes/NATO\|NATO]] (1949) → Korean War (1950); SEATO (1954) → Vietnam escalation (1965). The alliances created the framework; the proxy wars tested it.

**Pattern 2 — Crisis → arms control:** Cuban Missile Crisis (1962) → Hot Line (1963) → Nuclear Test-Ban Treaty (1963) → Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968). Each brush with nuclear war produced new guardrails.

**Pattern 3 — Decolonization creates proxy war opportunities:** Angola (1975), Nicaragua (1979) — superpower competition extended into newly independent states as colonial powers withdrew.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP frequently asks about the relationship between decolonization and the Cold War. The key insight: newly independent nations became battlegrounds for superpower competition, but many (Non-Aligned Movement) actively resisted being absorbed into either bloc.

> [!warning]
> Don't forget the dates: Korean War ends **1953**, not 1950. Vietnam War US withdrawal **1973**, South Vietnam falls **1975**. Cuban Missile Crisis **1962**, not 1961 (that's Bay of Pigs). These distinctions matter on MCQs.
