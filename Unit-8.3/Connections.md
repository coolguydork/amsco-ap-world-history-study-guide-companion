# Connections

**Unit 8.3 — Effects of the Cold War | AMSCO pp. 562–570**

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-8.1/Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview\|Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview]]**
Topic 8.1 establishes the post-WWII context: the US and USSR emerge as competing superpowers, each with incompatible political-economic systems. The ideological divide (democracy/capitalism vs. communism) and the physical division of Europe (Iron Curtain) explained in 8.1 are the preconditions for everything in 8.3.

**[[Unit-8.2/Cold War Overview\|Cold War Overview]]**
Topic 8.2 covers the origins of the Cold War and the early containment doctrine. The Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, and Berlin Crisis described in 8.2 are the direct predecessors of the alliance systems and proxy wars in 8.3.

**[[Unit-6.2/State Expansion Overview\|State Expansion Overview]]**
French colonialism in Indochina (Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia) is essential context for the Vietnam War. AMSCO explicitly directs students to trace foreign intervention in Southeast Asian affairs through Topic 6.2. Vietnam's anti-colonial war against France (Dien Bien Phu, 1954) transitioned directly into the Cold War proxy conflict with the US.

---

## Leads To

**Topic 8.4 — Economy and the Environment After 1900**
Cold War military spending, particularly the nuclear arms race and proxy wars, had significant economic and environmental consequences — including nuclear test contamination and the economic burden of the arms race that ultimately contributed to Soviet collapse.

**Topic 8.5 — Decolonization After 1900**
Proxy wars (Angola, Vietnam, Nicaragua) overlapped heavily with decolonization. The Cold War shaped which newly independent nations aligned with which bloc — and which chose non-alignment. The Non-Aligned Movement directly connects Topics 8.3 and 8.5.

**Topic 8.8 — Calls for Democracy and End of the Cold War**
The Cold War's end (Berlin Wall falls 1989, Soviet dissolution 1991) is the culmination of 8.3's dynamics: the arms race's economic burden on the USSR, the failure of Soviet-style communism to deliver prosperity, and popular resistance in Eastern Europe. The antinuclear movement from 8.3 feeds into the broader reform/democracy movements of 8.8.

---

## Compare With

**[[Unit-7.3/Conducting World War I Overview\|World War I Alliances]]**
NATO and the Warsaw Pact echoed the pre-WWI alliance system (Triple Entente vs. Triple Alliance) — interlocking defensive commitments that could pull great powers into conflicts started by smaller states. Key difference: nuclear deterrence prevented the escalation that WWI's alliances enabled.

**[[Unit-7.6/Causes of World War II Overview\|WWII & Occupation of Germany]]**
The division of Germany into occupation zones after WWII directly produced the Cold War division of Germany (8.3). The Berlin Blockade and Airlift emerged directly from WWII's settlement.

**Topic 9.3 — Spread of Disease After 1900 / Globalization**
The Cold War's proxy wars created long-term instability and humanitarian crises (Angola, Nicaragua) that connect to later topics on global inequality and political instability.
