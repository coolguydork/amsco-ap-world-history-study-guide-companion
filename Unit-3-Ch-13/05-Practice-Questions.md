**Question 1**

*Use the following excerpt to answer the question.*

> "A Christian who is truly contrite seeks and loves to pay penalties for his sins; the treasures of indulgences are nets with which one now fishes for the wealth of men."
> — Martin Luther, 95 Theses, 1517

Luther's argument in this passage most directly challenged which of the following?

A) The Catholic Church's authority to interpret scripture and define theological doctrine
B) The political power of German princes who taxed church property
C) The Ottoman Empire's use of the Sunni ulama to legitimate political authority
D) The spread of literacy through vernacular Bible translations

**Correct Answer: A**
*Luther's attack on indulgences was ultimately an attack on the church's claimed authority to mediate between humans and God — challenging papal and clerical power as intermediaries for salvation.*

**Question 2**

The Protestant Reformation's rapid spread across Europe in the 16th century was most directly facilitated by

A) The military campaigns of Protestant German princes who forced conversion of their subjects
B) The printing press, which allowed Luther's ideas to be reproduced and distributed cheaply across the continent
C) The Council of Trent's failure to address corruption in the Catholic Church
D) Widespread popular belief in predestination as taught by John Calvin

**Correct Answer: B**
*The printing press is the technology that explains the Reformation's speed and scale. Luther's 95 Theses were reproduced and distributed across Germany within weeks — a feat impossible before the printing press.*

**Question 3**

Sikhism, which emerged in the Punjab region of South Asia in the late 15th and early 16th centuries, is best understood as

A) A branch of Sufi Islam adapted for the Hindu majority population
B) A syncretic tradition that emerged from the cultural encounter between Hinduism and Islam in the Punjab — predating Mughal rule, though later shaped by it
C) A protest movement against Mughal taxation of non-Muslim communities
D) A revival of ancient Vedic religious traditions suppressed by Mughal rulers

**Correct Answer: B**
*Guru Nanak drew on both Hindu and Islamic elements — monotheism from Islam, rejection of caste from both traditions, devotional practices from Hinduism. Sikhism is a textbook example of religious syncretism.*

**Question 4**

*Use the following excerpt to answer the question.*

> "We believe it would be a great service to God our Lord to bring Our Holy Faith to these barbarous people [in Japan]."
> — Francis Xavier, letter from Japan, 1549

The missionary activities described in Xavier's letter most directly reflect which of the following?

A) The Protestant Reformation's emphasis on spreading Christianity beyond Europe
B) The Catholic Counter-Reformation's strategy of expanding Christianity into new regions as a response to Protestant challenges in Europe
C) The Ottoman Empire's use of Islamic missionaries to convert populations in newly conquered territories
D) The Jesuit order's belief that Asian religions were compatible with Christian practice

**Correct Answer: B**
*Xavier was a Jesuit — the primary missionary order of the Counter-Reformation. Catholic missionary expansion into Asia, Africa, and the Americas was explicitly connected to the Counter-Reformation's effort to strengthen the church after the Protestant challenge.*

**Question 5**

The "Chinese Rites Controversy" — the debate over whether Chinese Christian converts could continue practicing Confucian ancestral rites — resulted in the Pope condemning the Jesuit accommodation strategy. This controversy most directly reveals

A) Chinese Christians' unwillingness to abandon traditional practices despite official pressure
B) The tension between effective missionary practice (cultural accommodation) and the church's demand for doctrinal purity
C) The Kangxi Emperor's hostility to all forms of Christianity in China
D) The Jesuits' preference for Confucian philosophy over Christian theology

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Jesuits argued that Confucian rites were cultural, not religious, and therefore compatible with Christianity. The Pope disagreed. The controversy illustrates the fundamental tension in missionary work: how much can you adapt to local culture before you've compromised the faith itself?*

**Question 6**

The Ottoman Empire's practice of welcoming Jewish refugees expelled from Spain in 1492 is best understood in the context of

A) Ottoman religious devotion to protecting all Abrahamic faith communities equally
B) The millet system, which allowed the Ottomans to govern non-Muslim communities as productive, taxpaying subjects without forcing conversion
C) A diplomatic agreement between the Ottoman sultan and the Spanish monarchs to transfer the Jewish population peacefully
D) Jewish merchants' unique skills in the silk trade, which the Ottomans needed for their economy

**Correct Answer: B**
*The millet system made the Ottoman Empire relatively hospitable to religious minorities compared to Catholic Spain — not out of pure religious tolerance but because the system was designed to extract tax revenue and administrative value from non-Muslim communities.*

**Question 7**

The Jesuit missionary strategy of "accommodation" — learning local languages, adopting local dress, and presenting Christianity through local intellectual frameworks — most closely parallels which other historical pattern from AP World History?

A) The Mongol policy of religious tolerance in conquered territories (Unit 2)
B) The spread of Buddhism along Silk Road trade routes, where Buddhist monks adapted their message to local cultural contexts (Unit 1–2)
C) The Ottoman devshirme system of incorporating non-Muslims into imperial service (Unit 3, Ch 11)
D) The spread of Islam through conquest in the early Abbasid period (Unit 1)

**Correct Answer: B**
*Both Buddhist missionaries along the Silk Roads and Jesuit missionaries in Asia used cultural adaptation — presenting their religion in ways that resonated with local traditions — to spread their faith. Both demonstrate that successful religious diffusion often requires accommodation, not just assertion.*

**Question 8**

The Thirty Years' War (1618–1648) and its resolution in the Peace of Westphalia most directly contributed to which of the following long-term developments?

A) The permanent reunification of Catholic and Protestant Christianity under a reformed papacy
B) The emergence of the principle of state sovereignty, in which each ruler could determine the religion of their own territory
C) The collapse of the Holy Roman Empire and the unification of Germany under Protestant leadership
D) The Ottoman Empire's expansion into Central Europe as Catholic and Protestant states weakened each other

**Correct Answer: B**
*The principle that rulers determine the religion of their territory was first established at the Peace of Augsburg (1555), which applied to Catholic and Lutheran states within the Holy Roman Empire. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) confirmed and extended this settlement — adding recognition of Calvinist states — and more broadly strengthened the concept of state sovereignty. On the AP exam, Westphalia is the key term for the development of the modern state system, but be aware that "rulers determine religion" traces directly to Augsburg.*

**Question 9**

The spread of Islam into sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia in the period 1450–1750 was primarily facilitated by

A) Military conquest by Ottoman armies establishing Islamic governance across both regions
B) Sufi missionaries and Muslim merchants who traveled along trade routes and established communities that introduced Islam gradually and syncretically
C) Forced conversion policies similar to the Safavid conversion of Persia to Shia Islam
D) Christian missionary resistance that made local rulers turn to Islam as an alternative

**Correct Answer: B**
*Islam's spread into sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia (unlike the Safavid case in Persia) was generally peaceful, gradual, and syncretic. Sufi missionaries and traders were the primary agents, and local converts typically blended Islamic and existing religious practices.*

**Question 10**

A historian argues that "the Protestant Reformation and the emergence of Sikhism both represent religious responses to perceived corruption or injustice within existing religious establishments." Which of the following evidence best supports this argument?

A) Both Luther and Guru Nanak rejected belief in God and promoted secular governance
B) Luther attacked Catholic clerical corruption and indulgences; Guru Nanak rejected the caste system and ritualism of both Hinduism and Islam as human-made barriers to God
C) Both movements originated in response to Ottoman Islamic expansion into their respective regions
D) Both Luther and Guru Nanak relied on gunpowder armies to defend their new religious communities

**Correct Answer: B**
*This is the best evidence: both reformers directed their criticism at specific perceived corruptions — Luther at Catholic financial corruption and clerical mediators, Nanak at caste hierarchy and empty ritual. Both argued for more direct, egalitarian access to God.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Syncretic religious traditions that emerged in the period 1450–1750 reflected the cultural encounters of that era."**

**(a)** Describe **ONE** specific example of a syncretic religious tradition that emerged from cultural encounter in this period.

**(b)** Explain **ONE** way in which this tradition reflected the specific cultural encounter from which it emerged.

**(c)** Explain **ONE** way in which the emergence of this tradition challenged OR reinforced existing social or political hierarchies.

> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Sikhism (Hindu + Islamic encounter in the Punjab; emerged before Mughal rule, later shaped by it); Vodou (African + Catholic in the Caribbean); Candomblé (African + Catholic in Brazil); Japanese Christianity (Catholic + Shinto/Buddhist)
> - (b) For Sikhism: Guru Nanak's monotheism reflects Islamic influence; rejection of caste reflects response to South Asian social stratification; devotional poetry echoes both Hindu bhakti and Sufi traditions
> - (c) Sikhism challenged Mughal orthodoxy and the caste system (both); Vodou helped enslaved Africans maintain cultural identity, challenging the complete cultural destruction intended by slaveholders

**(a)** Briefly explain **ONE** way in which the Protestant Reformation challenged existing political authority in Europe.

**(b)** Briefly explain **ONE** way in which rulers used the Protestant Reformation to *expand* their own political authority.

**(c)** Explain **ONE** similarity between the Protestant Reformation's challenge to Catholic authority and a challenge to religious authority in a non-European context in this period.

> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Protestant princes in the Holy Roman Empire used Lutheranism to justify breaking from the Emperor's authority; the Reformation challenged the Pope's claim to universal religious authority

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which religious change in the period 1450–1750 was primarily driven by political factors rather than spiritual or intellectual ones."**

> **Outline for a top-scoring response:**

**Thesis:** State a historically defensible claim — e.g., *While spiritual and intellectual concerns genuinely motivated key reformers, political factors were equally or more important in determining which religious changes spread and survived, as rulers adopted or rejected religious movements based on their political utility.*

**Body Paragraph 1 — Political factors driving religious change:**
- Henry VIII's break with Rome driven by marriage politics, not theology
- German princes using Lutheranism to break from imperial authority and seize church property
- Safavid shahs using Shia conversion to define Persian identity against Ottoman rivals

**Body Paragraph 2 — Spiritual/intellectual factors:**
- Luther's genuine theological conviction (justification by faith) drove his original protest
- Guru Nanak's spiritual vision of equality before God preceded any political context
- Jesuit scientists in China were genuinely motivated by faith even as they used intellectual strategy

**Body Paragraph 3 — Complexity:**
- The printing press (technology) was a third factor that neither political nor spiritual leaders fully controlled
- Religious change that lacked political support rarely survived — the Tokugawa suppression of Christianity in Japan shows what happens when rulers oppose a movement
- Some religious changes were genuinely popular (Sufism in West Africa) and spread without significant political patronage

**Contextualization:** Connect to the medieval period — the long-standing tension between church and state authority in Europe (Investiture Controversy, Crusades) provided the context within which Luther's challenge resonated so powerfully.
