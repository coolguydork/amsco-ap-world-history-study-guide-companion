# Connections

**Topic 5.5 — Technology in the Industrial Age**

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-5.3/Industrial Revolution Begins Overview\|Industrial Revolution Begins Overview]]** (5.3)
The Industrial Revolution's origins — textile factories, early steam applications, Britain's coal and iron resources — provide the direct foundation for Topic 5.5's technologies. The steam engine improvements in 5.5 are the mature version of machinery introduced in 5.3.

**[[Unit-5.4/Industrialization Spreads Overview\|Industrialization Spreads Overview]]** (5.4)
The spread of industrialization to other nations (US, Germany, France, Japan) in 5.4 is the economic context that made the Second Industrial Revolution's scale possible. Multiple competing industrial economies drove investment in new technologies.

**[[Unit-4.1/Technological Innovations Overview\|Technological Innovations Overview]]** (4.1)
An earlier wave of technological change (maritime technologies, gunpowder, printing) provides a comparative framework. AP essays may ask you to compare how different eras of technology drove global integration.

## Leads To

**New [[Concept Notes/Imperialism\|Imperialism]]** [[Unit-6.1/Rationales for Imperialism Overview|Rationales for Imperialism Overview]] — *folder not yet built*
Industrial technologies directly enabled the imperial scramble of the late 19th century. Railroads penetrated continental interiors; steamships projected naval power; the telegraph coordinated colonial administration. Without the technologies of 5.5, New Imperialism at its historical scale would have been impossible.

## Compare With

**[[Unit-4.1/Technological Innovations Overview\|Technological Innovations Overview]]** (4.1) — Earlier era of technology
Both periods feature technology driving global integration, but the scale differs enormously. Compare: printing press (spreads ideas slowly over decades) vs. telegraph (spreads information globally in seconds). Maritime exploration (months per voyage) vs. steamships (weeks, on reliable schedules).

## Cross-Cutting Themes

**Technology → Imperialism chain:** The clearest through-line in this topic connects to Unit 6. Every technology in 5.5 — railroads, steamships, telegraphs, steel — made the late-19th-century imperial expansion more effective. When writing about New Imperialism, use 5.5 technologies as evidence for *how* imperial powers projected power over vast distances.

**Technology → Migration:** Railroads and steamships enabled mass migration at unprecedented scale. This connects to the broader Unit 5 and Unit 6 themes of labor migration, indentured workers, and demographic transformation.

**Continuity — resource extraction:** The fundamental dynamic of core regions extracting resources from peripheral regions was not new in 1800 (see Silk Roads, Atlantic System). What was new was the *technological efficiency* with which it occurred. Use this as a CCOT argument in essays.
