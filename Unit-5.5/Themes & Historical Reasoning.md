# Topic 5.5 — Themes & Historical Reasoning

---

## AP Themes

### Technology & Environment
The second industrial revolution deepened the environmental consequences of the first. Steel production required massive coal burning; oil wells tapped ancient fossil reserves; factories dumped waste into rivers. The telegraph, telephone, and radio shrank the world culturally. Coaling stations planted imperial flags across the Pacific and Africa. Technology both created wealth and reshaped global power in ways that are still unfolding.

### Economic Systems
New technologies lowered the cost of production and transportation, expanding markets globally. Railroads opened continental interiors (U.S., Russia) to extraction. Steamships made transoceanic trade fast and reliable. The telegraph synchronized global markets in near-real-time. Together, these technologies created the infrastructure of a truly **global capitalist economy** for the first time.

### Governance
Railroads required massive capital investment — governments heavily subsidized them (U.S. Transcontinental Railroad; Trans-Siberian Railroad). Control of coaling stations and telegraph cables became **instruments of imperial power**. States that could harness the second industrial revolution (Germany, Japan, U.S.) rose; those that couldn't (Ottoman Empire, Qing China) declined.

## Historical Reasoning: Causation

**How did the second industrial revolution cause globalization?**

1. **Railroads** opened interior regions previously inaccessible — cotton, rubber, minerals could be extracted cheaply
2. **Steamships** made transoceanic shipping fast, reliable, and cheap — ended the era when geography determined trade patterns
3. **Telegraph** made instantaneous communication possible across continents and oceans — synchronized global markets
4. Together: **industrial nations extracted raw materials from colonized regions** and sold finished goods back — the foundation of New Imperialism (Unit 6)

> [!exam-tip]
> A strong LEQ or DBQ argument about imperialism in Period 3 should mention at least one specific technology (railroad, steamship, or telegraph) and explain exactly HOW it enabled imperial control — not just that "technology was important."

## Historical Reasoning: CCOT

**What changed with the second industrial revolution?**
- Energy shifted from coal/steam → petroleum/electricity (mobile, even more powerful)
- Communications collapsed distance — for the first time, distant events became immediately knowable
- Steel replaced iron as the structural material of civilization — stronger, more versatile
- Automobiles and airplanes (early 20th c.) transformed transportation again

**What continued?**
- Coal remained dominant well into the 20th century even as oil rose
- Global inequality between industrial and non-industrial nations deepened (not reversed)
- Labor exploitation continued — new technologies did not automatically improve workers' lives
- Imperial competition intensified (coaling stations, railroad concessions, telegraph routes)

## Historical Reasoning: Comparison

**First vs. Second Industrial Revolution:**

| | First (c. 1760–1850) | Second (c. 1850–1914) |
|--|---------------------|----------------------|
| Energy | Coal → steam | Coal + petroleum + electricity |
| Materials | Iron, textiles | Steel, chemicals |
| Leaders | Britain | Britain, U.S., Germany |
| Communication | None (then telegraph) | Telegraph, telephone, radio |
| Transportation | Canals, early steam trains | Railroads spanning continents, steamships |
| Labor | Unskilled factory work | More technical/skilled work + continued unskilled |
