# Topic 5.5 — Key Terms & Concepts

**AMSCO:** pp. 311–313

---

## Energy and Materials

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Coal** | Fossil fuel burned to produce steam power | Foundation of the first Industrial Revolution |
| **Coke** | Refined form of coal | Enabled larger iron-producing furnaces |
| **Cast iron** | Iron alloy — strong but brittle | Useful for some structures; limited for shaping |
| **Wrought iron** | Workable form of iron (Henry Cort, 1794) | Could be stretched and shaped; valuable for construction and machinery |
| **Steel** | Alloy of iron and carbon — strong and versatile | Backbone of industrial society after Bessemer Process |
| **Bessemer Process** | Method (1856) of blasting molten metal with air to remove impurities | Made mass production of steel economical; enabled massive infrastructure |
| **Petroleum** | Fossil fuel (oil) from ancient plant/animal remains | Led to kerosene, internal combustion engine, gasoline |
| **Kerosene** | Product extracted from petroleum (1847) | First major petroleum product; used for lighting and heating |
| **Fossil fuel** | Energy source from ancient organic material (coal, petroleum, natural gas) | Powered both industrial revolutions; laid groundwork for modern climate change |

---

## Steam and Transportation

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Steam engine** | Engine using coal-fired steam to generate mechanical power (improved by James Watt, 1765) | Mobile, scalable energy that replaced waterpower and wind |
| **Coaling stations** | Ports where steam-powered ships could refuel with coal | Strategically vital; shaped imperial competition for Pacific islands, Cape Colony |
| **SS Savannah** | First steam-powered ship to cross the Atlantic Ocean (1819) | Symbol of steam's transformation of ocean travel |
| **Transcontinental Railroad** | U.S. railroad connecting Atlantic and Pacific coasts (completed 1869) | Opened interior to resource extraction; accelerated U.S. industrialization |
| **Trans-Siberian Railroad** | Russian railroad from Moscow to the Pacific | Enabled Russian trade with East Asia; supported industrialization |

---

## The Second Industrial Revolution

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Second industrial revolution** | Late 19th–early 20th century wave of innovation in steel, chemicals, electricity, and electronics | Led by U.S., Britain, Germany; transformed global economy again |
| **Internal combustion engine** | Engine burning fuel inside the engine (not in a separate boiler) | Enabled automobiles and airplanes |
| **Electricity** | Harnessed as a power source late 19th century | Lit streets, powered factories, enabled communications |
| **Telephone** | Device transmitting voice via electricity; patent to Alexander Graham Bell (1876) | Revolutionized business communication |
| **Radio** | Wireless communication via electromagnetic waves; Guglielmo Marconi crossed the Atlantic (1901) | First mass media; transformed public culture |
| **Telegraph** | Device transmitting coded messages via electrical signals | Near-instant long-distance communication; essential for railroads and global trade |

## Key People

| Person | Contribution |
|--------|-------------|
| James Watt | Improved steam engine (1765) — cheap, mobile coal power |

| Thomas Edison | Refined voice transmitter (1886); also improved electrical generator |
