# Timeline — Unit 5.5: Technology in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** Between 1750 and 1900, the pace of technological change accelerated with each decade. The First Industrial Revolution (steam, textiles, coal) was followed by the Second (steel, electricity, chemicals, internal combustion). By 1900, the world's transportation, communication, and production systems had been fundamentally remade — and the gap between industrialized and non-industrialized regions was wider than at any previous point in history.

> [!tip] Continuity vs. Change
> **Continuity:** The basic energy principle (heat → mechanical work → movement) underlying steam, internal combustion, and electrical generators remained constant; what changed was the *fuel* (coal → oil → natural gas) and *scale*.
> **Change:** The *speed* of technological change itself accelerated — inventions that took generations in the 18th century were commercialized within years by the 1880s, as organized research laboratories replaced individual tinkerers.
