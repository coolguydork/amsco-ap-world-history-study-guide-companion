## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Questions 1–3 refer to the image of a girl working in a textile factory during the Industrial Revolution (AMSCO p. 314).*

**1. Which statement provides the best context for interpreting this photo?**

A. Machines used in factories during the Industrial Revolution were similar to the ones used in cottage industries.
B. One effect of the industrialization was that girls and boys worked side by side rather than in separate jobs.
C. As the Bessemer Process improved, textile machines became faster and more sophisticated.
D. The Industrial Revolution led to a division of labor that meant employees began to do highly specialized work.

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> The image shows a factory worker performing a single, repetitive task — the hallmark of industrial division of labor. Option A is wrong because factory machines were fundamentally different from cottage industry tools. B is a distractor (the photo shows only a girl). C incorrectly links the Bessemer Process (which makes steel) to textile machinery.

---

**2. For textile production as seen in the photo, which of the following relationships would have existed between an industrialized country and a colony during the Second Industrial Revolution?**

A. The colony would have provided resources for the industrialized country to make textiles.
B. The colony would have sold finished textiles to the industrialized country.
C. The industrialized country would have sent its resources to the colony to be made into finished textiles.
D. The industrialized country would have located its textile-manufacturing headquarters in the colony.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The classic industrial-era relationship: colonies supplied raw materials (cotton, wool) to the industrialized country, which processed them into finished goods and sold them back. B, C, and D reverse the direction of this relationship.

---

**3. The photo can best be used to demonstrate which of the following long-term effects of the Industrial Age?**

A. The growth in international trade
B. The problem of water pollution
C. The expansion of the workforce
D. The increase in the population

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The image depicts workers — including children — drawn into factory labor, illustrating the expansion of the industrial workforce beyond traditional adult male workers. The photo alone doesn't demonstrate A (trade patterns), B (environmental pollution), or D (demographic growth).

## Short-Answer Questions

*Question 1 refers to the image of breaker boys at Woodward Coal Mines, Kingston, PA (AMSCO p. 315).*

**Part A:** Identify ONE technological development in the period 1750–1900 represented in the image.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> The image represents coal mining, a foundational technological development of the Industrial Revolution. Coal was the primary energy source that powered steam engines in factories, locomotives, and steamships. The industrialization of coal extraction — with mechanized machinery and organized labor — was central to sustaining the energy demands of the period 1750–1900.

**Part B:** Explain ONE way the image above reflects changes in workplaces in the period 1750–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain (Change)
> The image reflects the shift from small-scale, artisanal labor to large-scale, industrial labor organization. Before industrialization, most production occurred in homes or small workshops (cottage industries) where workers controlled their own pace and tools. The coal mine depicted is an industrial workplace where workers — including young boys — performed specialized, repetitive tasks under the direction of factory owners, illustrating the new hierarchical, wage-labor structure that industrialization imposed.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way new business practices affected workers like these in the period 1750–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain (Causation)
> The rise of large corporations and joint-stock companies concentrated capital in the hands of investors, which drove owners to minimize labor costs and maximize output. Workers in coal mines and factories were paid low wages for long hours, and child labor was common because children could be paid even less than adults. This exploitation of the workforce was a direct effect of the profit-maximizing business practices of industrial capitalism, which treated labor as a cost to be minimized rather than a craft to be respected.

## Reflect on the Essential Question

**In one to three paragraphs, explain how technology shaped economic production during the period from 1750 to 1900.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Synthesis
> Industrial technologies fundamentally transformed how goods were produced, distributed, and traded between 1750 and 1900. James Watt's improved steam engine (1765) replaced human and animal power with coal-driven mechanical energy, enabling factories to produce goods at scales and speeds previously impossible. The introduction of steam-powered railroads and steamships dramatically reduced the time and cost of moving raw materials to factories and finished goods to markets, integrating regional economies into national and global networks for the first time.
>
> The Second Industrial Revolution (c. 1870–1914) deepened and diversified these changes. The Bessemer Process (1856) made steel cheap enough to build transcontinental railroads, ocean-going vessels, and urban infrastructure. Oil wells provided new energy sources; the internal combustion engine created the automobile industry. Communication technologies — the telegraph, telephone, and radio — made it possible to coordinate economic activity across vast distances instantaneously, accelerating the pace of investment and trade.
>
> The cumulative effect was a global economic system organized around industrial production and resource extraction. Industrialized nations — Britain, the United States, Germany — used these technologies to establish colonies and trade networks that supplied raw materials and absorbed finished goods. Technology thus shaped not just production within factories but the entire structure of the global economy.
