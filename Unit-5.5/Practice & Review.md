# Topic 5.5 — Practice & Review

---

## Key Facts to Know Cold

- Steam engine (Watt, **1765**) → factories, then trains
- SS Savannah: first steam-powered transatlantic crossing (**1819**)
- Bessemer Process (**1856**): mass steel production — blast molten iron with air
- First commercial oil wells: **mid-1800s**; kerosene extracted **1847**
- First public power station: **London, 1882**
- Telephone patent: **Alexander Graham Bell, 1876**
- First transatlantic radio signal: **Guglielmo Marconi, 1901**
- Transcontinental Railroad (U.S.) completed: **1869**
- **Coaling stations** = strategically vital for steam-powered imperial navies

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The development of the Bessemer Process (1856) most directly contributed to industrialization by:

A) making steam-powered ships faster and more reliable
B) enabling the mass production of affordable steel for construction and railways
C) replacing coal with petroleum as the primary industrial energy source
D) allowing factories to be built without access to rivers or coal deposits

*Answer: B — The Bessemer Process made steel cheap enough to use for railroads, bridges, and buildings on a massive scale, driving the second industrial revolution.*

---

**2.** The construction of coaling stations across the Pacific and Africa in the late 19th century was most directly connected to:

A) the development of the internal combustion engine
B) the spread of the telegraph network
C) the replacement of sailing ships with steam-powered vessels
D) the growth of the railroad industry in Europe

*Answer: C — Steam-powered ships needed coal to operate; coaling stations allowed navies and merchant vessels to refuel at strategic points worldwide, enabling imperial expansion.*

---

**3.** Which of the following best describes the significance of the telegraph in the late 19th century?

A) It replaced the railroad as the primary means of transporting goods across continents
B) It enabled near-instant communication across long distances, linking global markets
C) It allowed factory owners to coordinate production with steam engines more efficiently
D) It provided the energy source for the second industrial revolution

*Answer: B — The telegraph allowed businesspeople, governments, and military commanders to communicate almost instantly across continents and oceans, fundamentally transforming commerce and imperial coordination.*

## Short Answer Question (SAQ)

**Prompt:** Explain ONE way that new technologies of the second industrial revolution (c. 1850–1900) changed economic production or global trade.

**Strong response example:** The development of the Bessemer Process (1856) transformed economic production by making mass steel manufacturing affordable for the first time. Steel, stronger and more versatile than iron, became the structural backbone of industrial civilization — used for railroads, bridges, ships, and buildings. This drove a new wave of railroad construction globally, including the U.S. Transcontinental Railroad (completed 1869) and Russia's Trans-Siberian Railroad, which in turn opened vast interior regions to resource extraction and linked distant markets to the global economy.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> The second industrial revolution was NOT just "more of the same." Steel, electricity, oil, and mass communications represented genuinely new technologies, not just improved versions of steam and iron. Know the distinction.

> [!warning]
> Railroads didn't just help industrialized nations — they were also tools of **imperial control**. Britain, France, and Belgium built railroads in Africa and Asia primarily to extract resources, not to develop local economies.
