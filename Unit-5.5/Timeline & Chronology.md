# Timeline & Chronology

**AMSCO pp. 310–316 | AP Period 3 (1750–1900)**

---

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 1765 | James Watt improves the steam engine | Makes coal power efficient and mobile; launches industrial-era energy |
| 1825 | First steam-powered railway in England | Railroads begin transforming land transportation and trade |
| 1856 | Bessemer Process introduced | Mass production of steel becomes possible; Second Industrial Revolution begins |
| 1869 | Transcontinental Railroad completed (Utah) | Opens American interior to industry, resources, and settlement |
| 1869 | Suez Canal opens | Cuts sailing distance between Europe and Asia; accelerates global trade |
| c. 1840s | Steamships widely replace sailing ships | Predictable ocean schedules; coaling stations become strategic |
| c. 1840s–1870s | Second Industrial Revolution: steel, chemicals, electricity | New industries accelerate and diversify industrial production |
| 1876 | Alexander Graham Bell patents the telephone | Voice communication over electrical wires; transforms business and society |
| 1879 | Thomas Edison develops practical electric light bulb | Enables electrification of homes and factories |
| 1882 | First public electrical power station (London) | Lays foundation for urban electrification |
| c. 1880s | Electricity and internal combustion engine widely adopted | Define the Second Industrial Revolution's mature phase |
| 1886 | Edison refines voice transmitter | Makes telephone commercially practical at scale |
| 1886 | Karl Benz patents first automobile | Internal combustion engine enters transportation |
| 1901 | Marconi transmits transatlantic radio signal | Wireless communication without physical infrastructure becomes possible |

---

> [!tip]
> For AP essays, you don't need to memorize every date — but you should be able to place technologies in the **First** (pre-1850, centered on steam/textiles/iron) vs. **Second** (post-1850, centered on steel/oil/electricity) Industrial Revolution.

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Suez Canal (1869)** and **telegraph cables** are frequently tested as examples of how communication and transportation technology accelerated economic globalization in the second half of the 19th century.
