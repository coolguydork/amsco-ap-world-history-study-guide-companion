# Chapter 12: Timeline — Administering Land-Based Empires

#apwh #unit-3 #timeline

> **Big Picture:** Chapter 12 focuses on the internal workings of empires at their height. The timeline here tracks the key administrative, architectural, and cultural milestones that defined how these empires governed and legitimized their power. Note how many of the empire's most lasting cultural monuments were created during periods of *relative stability* — the connection between political security and cultural flourishing is a key AP theme.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. 1453–1520 | Ottoman devshirme system matures into primary source of military/administrative elite | Ottoman Empire | Institutionalization of a slave-soldier bureaucracy; key governance innovation — **CHANGE** from tribal military recruitment |
| 1520–1566 | Reign of Suleiman the Magnificent; legal reforms codify Ottoman law | Ottoman Empire | Peak of Ottoman cultural and administrative achievement; Suleiman's legal code (kanun) harmonizes secular and Islamic law — **continuity** with Islamic legal tradition |
| 1558 | Süleymaniye Mosque complex completed in Istanbul (architect: Sinan) | Ottoman Empire | Visual statement of imperial and Islamic grandeur; Sinan's greatest early work |
| 1556–1605 | Akbar's reign; mansabdar system and religious tolerance policies | Mughal Empire | Most sophisticated administrative innovation in Mughal history; Din-i-Ilahi; Ibadat Khana — **CHANGE** from previous Muslim-Hindu relations |
| c. 1570s | Todar Mal designs Mughal land revenue (bandobast) system | Mughal Empire | Comprehensive land survey enables accurate taxation; financial backbone of the empire |
| 1588–1629 | Shah Abbas I reforms Safavid military; moves capital to Isfahan | Safavid Empire | Ghulam slave army replaces Turkic tribal forces; Isfahan becomes world-class city — administrative and cultural peak |
| c. 1600 | Naqsh-e Jahan Square completed in Isfahan | Safavid Empire | Royal mosque, bazaar, and palace in one complex; the empire's identity expressed architecturally |
| 1605–1627 | Reign of Jahangir; Nur Jahan effectively co-rules | Mughal Empire | "Sultanate of Women" equivalent in Mughal context; demonstrates women's political agency within patriarchal structures |
| 1628–1658 | Shah Jahan's reign; Taj Mahal (completed 1648) and Red Fort built | Mughal Empire | Peak of Mughal architectural achievement; enormous expense contributes to later fiscal strain |
| 1644 | Qing conquer China; establish dyarchy and impose queue order | China | Manchu minority governance of Han majority; queue as visible marker of submission — **CHANGE** in ruling dynasty, **continuity** in Confucian governance |
| 1661–1722 | Kangxi Emperor; standardizes Qing administration; compiles Kangxi Dictionary | China | Longest-reigning emperor; stabilizes Qing rule; manages Manchu-Han balance through Confucian patronage |
| 1718–1730 | "Tulip Period" in Ottoman Empire; cultural flourishing, European influence | Ottoman Empire | Ottoman elites adopt European fashions; signal of increasing cultural exchange with Europe — early sign of later Westernization pressures |
| 1658–1707 | Aurangzeb reverses Akbar's tolerance; reinstates jizya; expands empire | Mughal Empire | Religious orthodoxy alienates Hindu population; Maratha resistance grows; empire overextended — **CHANGE** from Akbar era, leads to fragmentation after 1707 |
| c. 1700s | Ottoman Janissaries block military reform; become political liability | Ottoman Empire | Institutional ossification: the devshirme's creation becomes the empire's internal threat — a classic example of how solutions become problems |
