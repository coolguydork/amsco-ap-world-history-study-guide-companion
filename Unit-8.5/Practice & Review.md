## Multiple-Choice Practice

**Source:** Ho Chi Minh, *Vietnamese Declaration of Independence*, 1945

> "For more than 80 years, the French imperialists, abusing the standard of Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity, have violated our Fatherland and oppressed our fellow-citizens. They have acted contrary to the ideals of humanity and justice. In the field of politics, they have deprived our people of every democratic liberty. They have enforced inhuman laws; they have set up three distinct political regimes in the North, the Center, and the South of Vietnam in order to wreck our national unity and prevent our people from being united. They have built more prisons than schools . . . To weaken our race they have forced us to use opium and alcohol. In the fields of economics, they have fleeced us to the backbone, impoverished our people, and devastated our land."

---

**Question 1.** Which words in the passage most directly reflect Marx's fundamental criticism of capitalism?

A. "They set up three distinct political regimes."
B. "They have built more prisons than schools."
C. "They have forced us to use opium and alcohol."
D. "They have fleeced us to the backbone, impoverished our people."

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Marx's core critique of capitalism was economic exploitation — the extraction of wealth from workers/subjects for the benefit of the owning class. "Fleeced to the backbone, impoverished" is the economic exploitation argument. (A) is a political grievance about divide-and-rule, (B) is a cultural/educational grievance, and (C) is a social control grievance — all valid anti-colonial arguments but not specifically Marxist economic critique.

---

**Question 2.** Which movement would have most likely influenced Ho Chi Minh's sentiments expressed in the passage?

A. The Enlightenment
B. Abolitionism
C. Mercantilism
D. Social Darwinism

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Ho Chi Minh explicitly invokes "Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity" — the slogan of the French Revolution and core Enlightenment ideals — then accuses France of violating the very principles it claims to stand for. This is a classic anti-colonial rhetorical move: turning colonial powers' own stated values against them. Ho Chi Minh studied in France and was deeply influenced by both Enlightenment liberalism and Marxism.

---

**Question 3.** Ho Chi Minh's eventual declaration of Vietnam's independence most directly resulted in:

A. His decision to abandon communism and form an alliance with the United States.
B. An election in France in which the majority voted for Vietnam to be granted independence.
C. A war between South Vietnam and the United States, after which France granted independence to North Vietnam only.
D. A war for independence from France, followed by an armed conflict involving the United States.

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> This is a two-stage process: (1) the First Indochina War (1945–1954) against France, ending with the Geneva Accords and partition; (2) the Vietnam War (1955–1975) with US involvement supporting South Vietnam against North Vietnam. (A) is wrong — Ho remained communist throughout. (B) is wrong — no such election. (C) reverses the sequence: the war against the US came after the war against France.

*Ten Countries with the Largest Number of Muslims* (World Factbook, CIA)

| Iran | 80,032,000 | 99% | 5% |
| Algeria | 38,424,000 | 99% | 2% |
| Morocco | 32,657,000 | 99% | 2% |

**A.** Identify ONE region with large Muslim populations based on the table.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> South/Southeast Asia is a region with large Muslim populations, as demonstrated by Indonesia (221 million), Pakistan (189 million), India (165 million), and Bangladesh (148 million) — together representing over 40% of the world's Muslims.

**B.** Explain ONE reason for the global distribution of Islam in the countries listed.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> Islam spread to South and Southeast Asia primarily through Indian Ocean trade networks. Arab and Persian Muslim merchants established trading posts and communities in port cities across the Indian Ocean basin beginning in the 8th century. Over time, local rulers adopted Islam to facilitate trade relationships, and Sufi missionaries made Islam accessible to broader populations. This explains why Indonesia — the world's largest Muslim country by population — is geographically distant from Islam's Arabian origins.

**C.** Explain ONE example that supports the argument that Islam is stronger outside the Middle East than in the Middle East.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> Indonesia supports this argument with 221 million Muslims — more than any Middle Eastern country — representing 87% of its population and 13% of all Muslims globally. The Middle Eastern nations on the list (Egypt, Iran, Algeria, Morocco) together account for only about 17% of world Muslims. This demonstrates that Islam's center of gravity, in terms of raw population, lies in South and Southeast Asia rather than in its Arabian heartland.

**Question 2.** Answer all parts of the question.

**A.** Identify ONE example of a colony that achieved independence through armed struggle after 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Identify
> Algeria achieved independence through armed struggle. The FLN (National Liberation Front) fought an eight-year war (1954–1962) against France, involving guerrilla tactics against 500,000 French troops and resulting in hundreds of thousands of Algerian deaths before France accepted independence in 1962.

**B.** Explain ONE way in which nationalist leaders in Asia sought independence differently from nationalist leaders in Africa after 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> Nationalist leaders in Asia more frequently combined independence movements with Cold War ideological alignment. Ho Chi Minh explicitly adopted communism as part of his nationalist program, framing Vietnam's independence struggle as both anti-colonial and anti-capitalist, which shaped his strategy (drawing Soviet and Chinese support) and the US response (military intervention). By contrast, many African nationalist leaders — including Nkrumah and Nasser — adopted non-alignment rather than committing to either Cold War bloc, preserving flexibility to receive aid from both superpowers while maintaining independence from both.

**C.** Explain ONE example of a regional, religious, or ethnic movement that challenged colonial rule and imperial boundaries after 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Explain
> The Muslim League's campaign for Pakistan is an example of a religious movement that challenged both colonial rule and imperial boundaries. Founded in 1906, the Muslim League argued that British India's Muslim minority could not trust a Hindu-majority independent state to protect their rights. By 1947, the Muslim League had successfully pressured the British to partition India, creating Pakistan as a separate Muslim homeland. This movement challenged not only British colonial rule but also the territorial boundaries of British India, producing a new state whose borders were drawn along religious rather than ethnic or geographic lines — with consequences including massive communal violence and the enduring India-Pakistan rivalry.

## Essential Question Response

**How did various peoples pursue independence after 1900?**

Peoples across Asia and Africa pursued independence through a range of strategies shaped by colonial context, Cold War dynamics, and internal divisions. In India, Gandhi's non-violent civil disobedience — boycotts, hunger strikes, mass protests — built a movement Britain could not suppress without undermining its own moral authority, resulting in negotiated independence in 1947, though accompanied by the traumatic partition into India and Pakistan along religious lines. In Ghana, Nkrumah similarly negotiated independence from Britain in 1957, building a nationalist movement around Pan-African ideals and Ghana became the model for sub-Saharan African independence. Where colonial powers refused to negotiate — as in Algeria, where France considered the colony part of metropolitan France — nationalist movements turned to armed struggle. The FLN fought an eight-year war (1954–1962) that cost hundreds of thousands of lives before France accepted independence. In Vietnam, Ho Chi Minh's communist nationalist movement defeated France and then fought the US-backed South Vietnamese government for two decades, achieving reunification in 1975 after 1–2 million deaths. Egypt's Nasser used political and economic leverage — nationalizing the Suez Canal — to assert Egyptian sovereignty, winning an international confrontation with Britain, France, and Israel through Cold War diplomacy rather than armed struggle. In all cases, independence was a beginning, not an end: new states faced ethnic violence, military coups, one-party states, and economic dependency that tested whether political independence could translate into genuine self-determination.
