# Connections

*How Topic 8.5 connects backward, forward, and laterally across the vault.*

---

## Builds On

**[[Unit-8.1/Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview\|Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview]]**
WWII is the proximate cause of decolonization's acceleration — European empires were exhausted, Japan destroyed the myth of white invincibility, and the [[Concept Notes/Atlantic Charter\|Atlantic Charter]] produced [[Concept Notes/Self-Determination\|self-determination]] language that independence movements weaponized. Without WWII, the timetable would have been much longer.

**[[Unit-8.2/Cold War Overview\|Cold War Overview]]**
The superpower rivalry shaped which independence movements received support and which were crushed. Ho Chi Minh's communist Vietnam drew US military intervention; Lumumba's Congo was destabilized by CIA involvement. The Cold War was the external frame within which decolonization played out.

**[[Concept Notes/Nationalism\|Nationalism]]**
The ideological engine of decolonization. Pan-Africanism, Pan-Arabism, Vietnamese nationalism, Indian nationalism — all are applications of the broader nationalist idea that peoples defined by shared culture, language, or history deserve self-governing states. This concept traces back to 19th-century European nationalism and its global diffusion.

**[[Concept Notes/Indian National Congress\|Indian National Congress]]**
Founded 1885; the organizational vehicle for Indian independence. Its tactics and internal debates (Gandhi's non-violence vs. other approaches; Congress vs. Muslim League) shaped the form of Indian independence and partition.

**[[Concept Notes/Mohandas Gandhi\|Mohandas Gandhi]]**
Non-violent resistance as a decolonization strategy. Gandhi's methods influenced the US civil rights movement, the anti-apartheid movement in South Africa, and countless other liberation struggles — making him one of the most globally influential figures of the 20th century.

## Connects Laterally (Same Period)

**[[Unit-8.3/Effects of the Cold War Overview\|Effects of the Cold War Overview]]**
Cold War proxy conflicts overlapped directly with decolonization — Vietnam, Korea, Angola, and Congo all involved both decolonization struggles and Cold War superpower intervention. The two processes were inseparable.

**[[Unit-8.4/Spread of Communism after 1900 Overview\|Spread of Communism after 1900 Overview]]**
Communist ideology shaped many anti-colonial movements. Ho Chi Minh explicitly combined nationalism and communism. Nkrumah and Nasser both drew on socialist economic models. The Chinese Communist Revolution provided a model of peasant-based revolutionary nationalism that influenced movements across Asia and Africa.

## AP Exam Cross-Topic Arguments

**Decolonization as evidence for:** nationalism as a force for political change; the role of WWII as a turning point; Cold War as a cause of instability; the gap between Enlightenment ideals and colonial practice; causes and consequences of state formation.

**Comparison arguments using 8.5:**
- Compare Indian independence (non-violent, negotiated) with Algerian independence (armed, brutal) → why the difference? (Colonial settler populations; French vs. British attitudes toward empire)
- Compare Nkrumah (Pan-Africanism, one-party state) with Nehru (secular democracy, Non-Aligned Movement) → different visions for post-colonial governance
- Compare the Suez Crisis with earlier colonial crises → demonstrates the shift in global power from Europe to US/USSR

**Causation arguments using 8.5:**
- WWII caused decolonization (most immediate and significant cause)
- Cold War shaped the form of decolonization (which paths were viable depended on superpower support)
- Colonial borders caused post-independence ethnic violence (structural cause of Biafra, India-Pakistan partition)
