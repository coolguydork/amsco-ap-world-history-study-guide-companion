# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 1.3: South and Southeast Asia

Chronological anchor points for South and Southeast Asia, c. 500 B.C.E.–c. 1450. Pre-period dates provide essential context; the AP core period is c. 1200–c. 1450.

---

## Deep Background: Roots of the Period

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 500 B.C.E.** | Indian merchants begin contact with Southeast Asia | Hinduism and Buddhism carried by trade voyages; Indian cultural influence on SE Asia begins |
| **3rd c. B.C.E.** | Buddhism arrives in Sri Lanka | Sinhala dynasties become Buddhist; priests eventually advise monarchs |
| **c. 320–550 C.E.** | Gupta Empire ("Golden Age" of India) | Sanskrit culture, Hindu art, and mathematics flourish; collapse in 550 marks start of South Asian disunity |

> [!tip]
> The Gupta Empire is South Asia's closest parallel to China's Han Dynasty. Both were "classical" peaks whose collapse defined the political fragmentation of the next period. On AP essays, you can use the Gupta the same way you use the Han — as the "before" in a CCOT argument.

---

## Sea-Based Southeast Asian Kingdoms

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **550 C.E.** | Gupta Empire collapses | South Asia enters a millennium of decentralization; Rajput kingdoms fill the vacuum in the north |
| **670–1025** | Srivijaya Empire (Sumatra) | Hindu maritime kingdom; controls the Strait of Malacca; charges fees on ships between India and China |
| **802–1431** | Khmer Empire / Angkor Kingdom (Cambodia) | Land-based kingdom; complex irrigation enables multiple rice harvests per year; Angkor Thom built as capital |
| **850–1267** | Chola Dynasty (Southern India) | Hindu kingdom; naval power extends to Ceylon (Sri Lanka) in the 11th century; stable over 400 years |

---

## AP Period Core: c. 1200–c. 1450

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **8th century** | First Islamic armies invade present-day Pakistan | Minimal daily impact; region on the eastern fringe of Dar al-Islam; Rajput princes limit Muslim influence |
| **11th century** | Islamic forces plunder Hindu temples and Buddhist shrines | Temples looted, mosques built on holy sites; Buddhism severely weakened; major Hindu resentment |
| **12th century** | Bhakti Movement begins in southern India | Hindu devotional movement emphasizing emotion over ritual; not discriminatory by caste or gender |
| **Early 13th century** | Delhi Sultanate established | Islamic forces conquer Delhi; first major Islamic state in South Asia; rules for ~300 years (to 1526) |
| **1200s** | Low-caste Hindu conversions to Islam | Hoping for social mobility; most do not achieve real improvement; parallel to Rome's Christian conversions |
| **1200s–1300s** | Urdu language emerges | Hindi grammar + Arabic/Farsi vocabulary; product of Islamic-Hindu cultural synthesis |
| **1200s–1300s** | Indo-Islamic architecture flourishes in Delhi | Qutub Minar built; mosques over Hindu temples; geometric + intricate artistic styles blend |
| **12th–13th centuries** | Khmer rulers convert to Buddhism | Hindu art at Angkor Thom preserved; Buddhist sculptures added; Angkor Wat built as ornate temple complex |
| **1293–1520** | Majapahit Kingdom (Java) | Buddhist maritime kingdom; 98 tributaries at its height; largest SE Asian maritime power |
| **1336–1646** | Vijayanagara Empire (Southern India) | Hindu kingdom founded by brothers Harihara and Bukka who reconverted from Islam; name = "victorious city" |
| **c. 1300s–1400s** | Islam spreads into SE Asia | Local merchants convert to facilitate trade; Sufi missionaries adapt to local faiths; Indonesia becomes Muslim |
| **1431** | Sukhothai Kingdom invades; Khmer flee Angkor | End of the Khmer Empire's golden age; Angkor Thom and Angkor Wat abandoned |
| **1526** | Mughals defeat the Delhi Sultanate | New empire (rooted in Mongol ancestry) replaces the Sultanate; Indo-Islamic synthesis enters a new phase |

## Causation Chain: Islam Arrives in South Asia

```
Muhammad's death (632) → rapid Islamic expansion
          ↓
8th century: Islamic armies reach Pakistan (minimal impact)
          ↓
11th century: Raids on Hindu temples and Buddhist shrines
          ↓ (Buddhism collapses in its birthplace)
Early 13th century: Delhi Sultanate conquers northern India
          ↓
Jizya tax on non-Muslims → resentment but also accommodation
          ↓
Bhakti Movement (12th–13th c.) → Hindu mystical response, parallels Sufism
          ↓
Urdu language + Indo-Islamic architecture → permanent cultural synthesis
          ↓
1526: Mughal Empire replaces Delhi Sultanate → deeper synthesis continues
```

> [!exam-tip]
> The key CCOT for South Asia: **Islam's arrival changed political structures and produced cultural synthesis, but did NOT end Hinduism, did NOT destroy the caste system, and did NOT transform gender relations.** What changed and what stayed the same are both testable.

## Causation Chain: Southeast Asian Kingdoms Rise and Fall

```
Indian merchants reach SE Asia (c. 500 B.C.E.)
          ↓
Hinduism and Buddhism introduced via trade voyages
          ↓
Srivijaya (670): Controls Strait of Malacca → charges fees → wealth without conquest
          ↓
Khmer Empire (802): Irrigation-based rice surplus → prosperity without maritime trade
          ↓
Majapahit (1293): Controls sea routes as Srivijaya's successor
          ↓
Islam arrives through Indian Ocean merchants (700s–1300s)
          ↓
Sufi missionaries adapt to local traditions → comfortable conversion
          ↓
Sukhothai invades Angkor (1431): Land-based power shifts
          ↓
By 1450: SE Asia transitioning to Islamic-majority urban areas, Buddhist rural heartlands
```

## Chronological Comparison: South Asia vs. Southeast Asia

| Period | South Asia | Southeast Asia |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-1000 | Gupta Golden Age (320–550); Rajput kingdoms in north; Chola in south | Srivijaya controls Malacca Strait; Khmer irrigation builds prosperity |
| 1000–1200 | Islamic raids; Buddhist monasteries weakened; Islam expands | Khmer at its height; Majapahit not yet born |
| 1200–1300 | Delhi Sultanate established; Bhakti Movement begins | Majapahit founded (1293); Islam entering coastal cities |
| 1300–1450 | Vijayanagara founded (1336); Indo-Islamic culture matures; Urdu emerges | Khmer falls (1431); Islam spreads via Sufis; Majapahit at peak |
| After 1450 | Mughals replace Delhi Sultanate (1526) | Indonesia becomes most Muslim-populated country on Earth |
