# Unit 1.3: Developments in South and Southeast Asia (1200–1450)

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 1 — The Global Tapestry
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (1200–1450)
**Key Regions:** South Asia (Indian subcontinent), Southeast Asia (mainland and archipelago)

## Summary

South and Southeast Asia in 1200–1450 were among the world's most culturally dynamic and commercially vital regions, positioned at the crossroads of Indian Ocean trade networks linking China, the Middle East, and East Africa. In South Asia, the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) established Muslim political rule over northern India, initiating a complex process of Hindu-Muslim cultural interaction that shaped Indian civilization for centuries. In Southeast Asia, great Hindu-Buddhist kingdoms like the Khmer Empire and Majapahit maintained sophisticated states based on wet-rice agriculture and Indian Ocean commerce, while coastal city-states like Malacca increasingly converted to Islam as it spread via trade. The Indian Ocean was the central artery connecting all these regions, making South and Southeast Asia central nodes in a genuinely global commercial network.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how South Asian states developed and changed under the Delhi Sultanate
- Analyze the diversity of political and cultural forms in Southeast Asian states
- Explain the role of the Indian Ocean trade network in connecting South and Southeast Asia to the wider world
- Evaluate the nature of Hindu-Muslim interaction in the Delhi Sultanate

## AP World History Key Concepts

- **KC-1.2.I** — The spread of Islam and Hinduism/Buddhism shaped political and cultural development in South and Southeast Asia
- **KC-1.3.II** — The Indian Ocean trade network connected South and Southeast Asia to Afro-Eurasia
- **KC-1.2.II** — South and Southeast Asian states used religious ideology to legitimize political authority
- **KC-2.3.II** — Cross-cultural interactions along trade networks produced new forms of cultural synthesis
