# Practice & Review

**AMSCO Pages:** 375–387

## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: James Monroe, annual presidential message to Congress, 1823*

> "The American continents, by the free and independent condition which they have assumed and maintain, are henceforth not to be considered as subjects for future colonization by any European powers. . . . We should consider any attempt on their [Europeans'] part to extend their system to any portion of this hemisphere as dangerous to our peace and safety. With the existing colonies or dependencies of any European power we have not interfered and shall not interfere. But with the Governments who have declared their independence and maintain it, and whose independence we have, on great consideration and on just principles, acknowledged, we could not view any interposition for the purpose of oppressing them, or controlling in any other manner their destiny, by any European power in any other light than as the manifestation of an unfriendly disposition toward the United States."

**Question 1.** Which statement summarizes the main idea stated in the passage?

A. Many former colonies of European nations in the Western Hemisphere desired a return to colonial status.
B. The United States intended to interfere in the administration of existing European colonies.
C. The European powers must respect the sovereignty of independent nations in the Western Hemisphere.
D. The United States must remain neutral in any attempt of a European nation to claim land in the Western Hemisphere.

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Monroe explicitly states that European attempts to extend influence to independent nations in the Western Hemisphere would be considered "an unfriendly disposition toward the United States." The doctrine protects the independence of newly formed nations (like Mexico and Argentina), not existing colonies (he explicitly says the U.S. will not interfere with *existing* colonies). A is wrong — there is no suggestion colonies want to return to colonial status. B is wrong — Monroe explicitly says the U.S. will *not* interfere with existing European colonies. D is wrong — the doctrine is explicitly *not* neutral; it is a warning.

**Question 2.** Which event indicates that the Monroe Doctrine might have included a desire of U.S. leaders to engage in economic imperialism?

A. The War of 1812
B. The Spanish-American War
C. The Civil War
D. The Seven Years' War

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Spanish-American War (1898) gave the United States control of Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines — demonstrating that American hemispheric interest included economic and military domination, not just keeping Europe out. The Roosevelt Corollary (1904) extended this explicitly. A, C, and D are either irrelevant to Western Hemisphere expansion or predate the Monroe Doctrine's imperial application.

**Question 3.** Which aspect of the Spanish-American War most directly demonstrated that the United States wanted to become an imperial power?

A. Declaring war against a country in Europe
B. Seizing territories from Mexico
C. Helping Cuba become independent of Spain
D. Taking control of the Philippines

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> Acquiring the Philippines — a territory in the Pacific, far from the Western Hemisphere — was the clearest evidence of imperial ambition beyond the Americas. The U.S. fought a brutal guerrilla war against Filipino independence fighters after the war, demonstrating it intended to *rule* the Philippines, not liberate it. A is wrong — Spain was not a European theater of operations. B is wrong — the war was against Spain, not Mexico. C is a partial truth but misleading — Cuba became a U.S. protectorate, not genuinely independent.

## Short-Answer Questions

### SAQ 1 — Imperialism in China (cartoon-based)

*The question references a cartoon showing Queen Victoria (Britain), Kaiser Wilhelm II (Germany), Tsar Nicholas II (Russia), Marianne (France), and a Japanese samurai carving up China while a Qing official throws up his hands.*

**Part A:** Identify ONE reason imperial powers had an interest in China.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> Imperial powers sought spheres of influence in China because of its vast potential market for manufactured goods and its resources. Britain, France, Germany, Russia, and Japan used superior military force to carve out exclusive trading zones (spheres of influence) in which they held monopoly trading rights and access to raw materials. China's enormous population also represented a potentially massive consumer market for European industrial output.

**Part B:** Explain ONE response by Chinese people to the actions of people from imperial countries represented in the cartoon.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> The Boxer Rebellion (1899–1901) represents a direct Chinese response to the imperial domination shown in the cartoon. The Boxers — martial arts practitioners who believed their training gave them supernatural protection — attacked Chinese Christians and foreign missionaries whom they viewed as agents of Western cultural imperialism. Empress Cixi encouraged the rebellion and ordered all foreigners killed in 1900. The rebellion reflected deep Chinese resentment of foreign encroachment on Chinese sovereignty, culture, and economic life, even as it ultimately failed when a multinational foreign force crushed it.

**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why China was unable to withstand the development represented in the cartoon.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> China was unable to resist foreign domination because internal crises severely weakened the Qing Dynasty's governing capacity. The Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864), which killed over 20 million people, destroyed the dynasty's military strength and economic base. Compounded by the Yellow River flooding that devastated agriculture and bubonic plague outbreaks, the Qing government lacked the resources and political legitimacy to mount effective military resistance to Western powers armed with modern industrial weapons. China's failure to industrialize — unlike Japan's Meiji Restoration — meant it had no means to match European military technology.

### SAQ 2 — Comparative Imperialism

**Part A:** Identify ONE way in which imperialism in Africa was similar to imperialism in East Asia in the period 1850–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Both Africa and East Asia experienced exploitation of their natural resources for the benefit of European industrial economies. In Africa, European powers extracted rubber (Congo), diamonds (South Africa), ivory (East Africa), and palm oil (West Africa). In China, European powers used spheres of influence to gain monopoly access to Chinese raw materials and markets. In both cases, indigenous peoples bore the human cost of this extraction through forced labor, displacement, or economic marginalization.

**Part B:** Identify ONE way in which imperialism in Africa and imperialism in Southeast Asia differed in the period 1850–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Imperialism in Africa was marked by the Berlin Conference (1884–85), which divided the continent through negotiated European agreement without any African input, creating formal colonial administrations. Imperialism in Southeast Asia, by contrast, developed more gradually and relied more heavily on corporate intermediaries — the Dutch East India Company controlled Indonesia until 1800, when the Dutch government took over; the British East India Company controlled much of the Malay Peninsula. The African case involved a more sudden, formalized partition, while Southeast Asian imperialism evolved through a longer transition from corporate to state control.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which imperialism in state-run colonies was similar to imperialism in settler colonies in the period 1850–1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Both state-run colonies and settler colonies involved the exploitation of indigenous labor and the displacement of indigenous culture. In the Belgian Congo (state-run), King Leopold II forced Congolese workers to harvest rubber under threat of mutilation and death. In settler colonies like British South Africa and Australia, indigenous peoples (black Africans, Aboriginal Australians) were displaced from their lands, forced into poverty, and subjected to coercive labor practices. In both colonial types, Western institutions (law, language, religion) were imposed on indigenous populations, resulting in the systematic destruction of pre-colonial cultural practices.

## Key Review Themes

**Three types of imperialism** — know examples for each and their distinguishing features (see Key Terms & Concepts note and AMSCO p. 383 table).

**Enabling technologies** — quinine, steamship, industrial weapons. These are causation answers waiting to happen.

**The Berlin Conference** — what it was, what it did (divided Africa), what it didn't do (no Africans invited; created artificial borders).

**Japan's dual role** — victim of imperialism (Perry, 1853) and perpetrator (Meiji industrialization → empire in East Asia). High-value comparison material.

**Monroe Doctrine → Roosevelt Corollary** — a continuity argument: U.S. goes from "keep Europe out of the Americas" (1823) to "we'll intervene whenever we want" (1904). The economic imperialism behind both is the connective thread.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam likes SAQs that ask you to compare two regions of imperialism. The three-type framework (state-run / settler / economic domination) is your analytic tool — use it to organize your comparisons quickly and precisely. Every response needs a specific historical example with detail, not just a general claim.
