## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: Nelson Mandela, statement on receiving the Truth and Reconciliation Commission report, October 1998*

> "If the pain has often been unbearable and the revelations shocking to all of us, it is because they indeed bring us the beginnings of a common understanding of what happened and a steady restoration of the nation's humanity. The TRC . . . was established by an Act of Parliament with overwhelming support. . . . [W]e are confident that it has contributed to the work in progress of laying the foundation of the edifice of reconciliation. . . . The wounds of the period of repression and resistance are too deep to have been healed by the TRC alone, however well it has encouraged us along that path."

**1. What is the "terrible past" that Mandela refers to in the passage?**

(A) The system of apartheid
(B) The imprisonment of Mandela
(C) The Boer Wars
(D) The Zulu massacre

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Mandela's statement is directly about the TRC's work exposing apartheid-era human rights violations. The TRC was established specifically to address the "period of repression" — South Africa's apartheid system (1948–1994). The Boer Wars (19th–early 20th century) and the Zulu conflicts are from earlier eras not addressed by the TRC.

**2. What was the goal of the South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission?**

(A) To prevent the white South Africans from retaking political control
(B) To establish a new system of governing South Africa
(C) To establish an atmosphere of multiracial trust
(D) To punish the South African Boers for implementing apartheid

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The TRC's explicit mandate was reconciliation — building trust in a fragile new multiracial democracy. Unlike the Nuremberg Trials, it did NOT seek punishment (eliminating D). It was not a governance body (eliminating B), and preventing white political resurgence was not its stated purpose (eliminating A). Mandela's statement emphasizes "reconciliation" and "restoration of the nation's humanity."

**3. How does the TRC differ from the Nuremberg Trials?**

(A) The Nuremberg trials sought retribution.
(B) The TRC forgave all apartheid implementers.
(C) The Nuremberg trials prevented future ethnic injustice.
(D) The TRC was international in scope.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> The key distinction is the justice model. Nuremberg (1945–46) was a criminal tribunal that prosecuted Nazi leaders and executed many of them — retributive justice. The TRC offered amnesty in exchange for truthful testimony — restorative justice. B is false (amnesty was conditional on testifying; not everyone was forgiven). C is false (Nuremberg did not prevent future atrocities — the Holocaust's lessons failed in Rwanda 1994). D is false (the TRC was a South African domestic body, while Nuremberg was international).

## Short-Answer Questions

*Source: Chai Ling, Interview at Tiananmen Square, 1989*

> "I believe that democracy is a natural desire. It should guarantee human rights and independence, and foster self-respect — all of which people are entitled to. Unfortunately, the basic human instinct for independence has been greatly inhibited and degraded among the Chinese. . . . The square is our last stand. If we lose it, China will retreat into another dark age. . . . If a nation's own people don't stay and help it to grow and develop, who will? But I will not be there to protect the square because I'm different from the others: my name is on the blacklist. I don't want to die."

**SAQ 1 (Parts A–C):**

(A) Identify ONE historical event that affected Ling's argument in the passage.
(B) Explain ONE historical situation that supports Ling's argument.
(C) Explain ONE way in which struggles for democracy in China are similar to struggles for democracy in South Africa.

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation/Context
> **(A)** The May Fourth Movement (1919) is a historical event that shaped Ling's argument. Chinese students and intellectuals protested foreign imperialism and demanded democratic reforms, establishing a tradition of student-led protest against authoritarian government that directly preceded the 1989 movement.
>
> **(B)** The CCP's control of China since 1949 supports Ling's argument that democracy has been "inhibited" in China. After the Communists took power, opposition parties were eliminated, media was censored, and dissenters were imprisoned or executed — the Great Leap Forward and Cultural Revolution both illustrate this pattern of political suppression.
>
> **(C)** Both movements used peaceful protest and civil disobedience to demand political rights from authoritarian governments. In South Africa, ANC members engaged in non-violent resistance against apartheid, while at Tiananmen, students held peaceful sit-ins and hunger strikes. Both governments ultimately responded with violence (Tiananmen massacre; Sharpeville massacre in South Africa), but in South Africa the movement eventually succeeded in a negotiated transition, while in China the CCP crushed the movement entirely.

**SAQ 2 (Parts A–C):**

(A) Identify ONE way in which access to education affected social structures after 1900.
(B) Explain ONE way in which people challenged old assumptions about race, class, gender, or religion after 1900.
(C) Explain ONE way in which the development of the United Nations affected global integration after 1900.

> [!tip] Model Response — CCOT/Causation
> **(A)** Increased access to education for women changed social structures by enabling greater female participation in professional and political life. As female literacy rates rose in the early 20th century, women organized suffrage movements and entered professions previously closed to them, directly contributing to the passage of women's voting rights in country after country between 1893 and 2015.
>
> **(B)** The anti-apartheid movement in South Africa challenged centuries-old assumptions about racial hierarchy by demonstrating through sustained international pressure and domestic resistance that racial segregation was not a natural or inevitable social arrangement. The ANC's campaign, combined with the global divestment movement, forced the South African government to abandon apartheid and hold multiracial elections in 1994 — proving that racially hierarchical systems could be dismantled through organized resistance.
>
> **(C)** The United Nations' adoption of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) affected global integration by establishing a shared international standard for human rights that transcended national sovereignty. This created a framework through which the international community could collectively pressure individual states to reform discriminatory practices — as seen in the UN's expulsion of apartheid South Africa in 1974 and the subsequent international sanctions campaign.

## Long Essay Question Practice

**LEQ Prompt (Causation):** Evaluate the extent to which calls for reform after 1900 caused changes in political and social structures worldwide.

> [!tip] Model Thesis
> While calls for reform after 1900 produced significant legal and institutional changes — including the dismantling of apartheid, the passage of civil rights legislation, and the establishment of the international human rights framework — the depth of these changes was limited by states' persistent resistance to reform and the ongoing gap between legal equality and social reality.

**Outline:**
- **Change:** UDHR (1948) + international pressure → end of apartheid (1994); Civil Rights Acts (1964–65) → desegregation in the U.S.; CEDAW (1979) → global women's rights framework
- **Continuity:** Caste discrimination persisted in India despite constitutional reform; racial inequality persisted in the U.S. after Civil Rights Acts; China resisted political reform entirely at Tiananmen
- **Complexity/Nuance:** The TRC model shows that reform also involved new institutional innovations — not just laws but new processes of accountability and healing
