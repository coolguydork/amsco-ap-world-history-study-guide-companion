# Timeline & Chronology

**AP Themes:** GOV, CDI, SIO | **AMSCO pp. 667–678**

---

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1893 | New Zealand grants women's suffrage | First country in the world to do so |
| 1911 | First International Women's Day | One million demonstrators across Europe for women's rights |
| 1912 | African National Congress (ANC) founded | South Africa's primary anti-apartheid organization |
| 1915 | First International Congress of Women | Representatives from 12 nations; early feminist international coordination |
| 1930s–50s | Negritude movement emerges | African/Caribbean intellectual resistance to colonial assimilation |
| 1946 | UNICEF established by UN | Humanitarian aid to children in post-WWII Europe; then globally |
| 1948 | Universal Declaration of Human Rights adopted | Foundation of international human rights law; Eleanor Roosevelt led drafting |
| 1948 | Apartheid formally instituted in South Africa | Legal racial segregation by National Party government |
| 1949 | India's Constitution outlaws caste discrimination | Dalits (untouchables) gain legal protections; caste reservation system established |
| 1950s–60s | Liberation Theology spreads in Latin America | Combines Catholicism with socialism; challenges military dictatorships |
| 1953 | Pakistan outlaws discrimination against Dalits | Parallel to India's 1949 constitutional reform |
| 1955 | Bandung Conference | Afro-Asian solidarity; 29 nations assert anti-colonialism and self-determination |
| 1960 | Senegal gains independence; Senghor becomes first president | Intellectual Negritude movement leader transitions to political leadership |
| 1964 | Nelson Mandela imprisoned for life | Sparks global anti-apartheid movement; serves 27 years |
| 1964 | U.S. Civil Rights Act passed | Outlaws racial discrimination in employment and public accommodations |
| 1965 | U.S. Voting Rights Act passed | Bans discriminatory voting practices; completes major civil rights legislative push |
| 1970 | First Earth Day (United States) | 1970 — now observed in 174 countries on April 22 |
| 1971 | Greenpeace founded | Direct-action environmental NGO; grows to 55+ countries |
| 1974 | South Africa expelled from UN | International community rejects apartheid as a pariah state |
| 1975 | UN First World Conference on Women (Mexico City) | 133 nations plan decade of advancement for women |
| 1977 | Wangari Maathai founds Green Belt Movement (Kenya) | Tree-planting movement linking environment, women's empowerment, postcolonial repair |
| 1979 | CEDAW adopted by UN | "International bill of rights for women"; outlines voting, education, marriage rights |
| 1986 | Mandela begins secret negotiations with apartheid government (while in prison) | Signals beginning of negotiated end to apartheid |
| 1989 | Tiananmen Square massacre (June 4) | CCP kills hundreds–thousands of pro-democracy protesters; event censored in China |
| 1989 | World Fair Trade Organization founded | Counterweight to WTO; emphasizes fair wages, no child labor, environment |
| 1990 | Nelson Mandela released from prison | F. W. de Klerk's pivotal reform; ANC legalized |
| 1993 | Mandela and de Klerk share Nobel Peace Prize | International recognition of South Africa's peaceful transition |
| 1994 | South Africa's first multiracial elections | ANC wins; Mandela elected president May 10, 1994 |
| 1994 | South Africa rejoins the United Nations | Readmitted after apartheid ends |
| 1995 | Truth and Reconciliation Commission established | Chaired by Desmond Tutu; amnesty for truth model of post-conflict justice |
| 1995 | Fourth International Congress of Women (Beijing) | Clinton: "Women's rights are human rights" |
| 2002 | International Criminal Court established | Permanent court to prosecute genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity |
| 2004 | Wangari Maathai wins Nobel Peace Prize | First African woman; environmental rights as human rights |
| 2013 | Pope Francis elected | First Latin American pope; reverses Vatican opposition to Liberation Theology |
| 2014 | Malala Yousafzai wins Nobel Peace Prize | Youngest laureate; global symbol of girls' right to education |
| 2017 | Women's March on Washington | ~5 million participants globally; largest single-day protest in U.S. history |
