# Unit 3.1: Timeline — Land-Based Empires Expand

#apwh #unit-3 #timeline

> **Big Picture:** This chapter sits at the opening of AP Period 3 (1450–1750). While Europeans were beginning to sail outward in search of oceanic trade routes (partly because Ottoman control of land routes made them expensive), the real power centers of the early modern world were the gunpowder land empires of Afro-Eurasia. The fall of Constantinople (1453) and the founding of the Mughal dynasty (1526) bracket the core events of this chapter.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1368 | Ming Dynasty founded after expelling Mongols | China | Restored Han Chinese rule; rebuilt the Great Wall; established the tributary system — **continuity** with earlier Chinese governance |
| 1405–1433 | Zheng He's voyages | Indian Ocean | Ming China briefly projected naval power outward but then withdrew — a major **discontinuity** that left the Indian Ocean open to later Portuguese entry |
| 1453 | Mehmed II conquers Constantinople | Eastern Mediterranean | End of the Byzantine Empire; Ottoman control of key trade routes; **watershed moment** in gunpowder warfare — signal event of Period 3 |
| 1464–1492 | Sunni Ali builds Songhai Empire | West Africa | Largest empire in West African history; controls trans-Saharan gold/salt routes; peak of Islamic West African political power |
| 1480 | Ivan III rejects Mongol tribute | Russia | Marks Russian independence from the Golden Horde; Moscow positioned as "Third Rome" — beginning of Russian imperial ambition |
| 1516–1517 | Ottoman-Mamluk War; Ottomans take Syria (1516) and Egypt (1517) | Middle East/N. Africa | Ottomans become guardians of Mecca and Medina; claim leadership of the Sunni Islamic world |
| 1501 | Shah Ismail founds Safavid Empire; converts Persia to Shia Islam | Persia | Creates permanent Sunni-Shia geopolitical divide; triggers Ottoman-Safavid rivalry lasting centuries |
| 1514 | Battle of Chaldiran | Persia/Anatolia border | Ottomans defeat Safavids using artillery; Safavids lack cannons; demonstrates decisive role of gunpowder technology — **CHANGE** |
| 1526 | Babur defeats Delhi Sultanate at Panipat; Mughal Empire founded | Indian subcontinent | Gunpowder artillery defeats larger Delhi cavalry force; new era of Mughal rule begins; **continuity** in Muslim governance of northern India |
| 1556–1605 | Reign of Akbar | Indian subcontinent | Mughal Empire expands and stabilizes; mansabdar system; religious tolerance policies — high point of Mughal governance |
| 1547 | Ivan IV crowned first Tsar of Russia | Russia | Formal centralization of Russian state; begins Cossack expansion into Siberia |
| 1588–1629 | Reign of Shah Abbas I | Persia | Isfahan becomes cultural/commercial center; Safavid military reformed; diplomatic ties with Europe |
| 1591 | Moroccan army defeats Songhai at Battle of Tondibi | West Africa | Gunpowder-equipped Moroccan force destroys the Songhai army; empire fragments — stark example of gunpowder's military power |
| 1600 | Tokugawa Ieyasu unifies Japan after Battle of Sekigahara | East Asia | Japan briefly centralized; Tokugawa shogunate issues seclusion edicts (sakoku) limiting European access — contrast with open empires |
| 1644 | Qing Dynasty established; Manchu conquer China | China | Manchu minority rules Han majority; dyarchy system; queue order imposed — **CHANGE** in dynasty, **continuity** in governance structures |
| 1661–1722 | Reign of Kangxi Emperor | China | Consolidates Qing rule; incorporates Taiwan (1683), intervenes in Mongolia and Tibet; tolerates Jesuits at court — note: greatest *territorial* expansion came under Qianlong (r. 1735–1796), who added Xinjiang |
