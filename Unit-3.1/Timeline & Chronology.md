# Timeline & Chronology

*Source: AMSCO pp. 141–151 (curated key dates)*

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1299 | Ottoman Empire founded in Anatolia | Starting point for the most powerful gunpowder empire; expands steadily toward Constantinople |
| 1368 | Ming Dynasty established in China | Replaces the Mongol Yuan Dynasty; 300 years of stability; rebuilds Great Wall |
| 1453 | Mehmed II conquers Constantinople; Byzantine Empire ends | Ottoman Empire controls key East–West trade corridor; signals gunpowder empire dominance |
| 1501 | Shah Ismail I founds Safavid Empire; declares Iran officially Shia Muslim | Creates Sunni-Shia fault line with Ottomans; fuels Ottoman-Safavid rivalry for two centuries |
| 1520–1566 | Suleiman the Magnificent rules the Ottoman Empire | Ottoman peak: codified law, expanded into Hungary and North Africa, navy controls Mediterranean |
| 1526 | Babur founds Mughal Empire in India; Ottomans defeat Hungary | Two gunpowder empires expand simultaneously — Mughal in South Asia, Ottomans into Europe |
| 1547 | Ivan IV ("the Terrible") becomes first Tsar of Russia | Russia begins transformation from Muscovy principality into centralized tsardom; expands east and south |
| 1556–1605 | Akbar rules the Mughal Empire | Mughal golden age: religious tolerance, centralized administration, mansabdar system |
| 1571 | Battle of Lepanto: Spain/Venice defeat Ottoman navy | First major Ottoman military defeat; signals limits of Ottoman naval power vs. European states |
| 1588–1629 | Shah Abbas I rules the Safavid Empire at its height | Modernizes military with European mercenaries; moves capital to Isfahan; recaptures territory from Ottomans |
| 1603 | Tokugawa Ieyasu declared shogun; Edo period begins in Japan | Japan unified under centralized military rule; long era of stability and controlled isolation |
| 1640 | Russia reaches the Pacific Ocean | Completes transcontinental expansion from Moscow to Pacific; missionaries follow traders |
| 1644 | Manchu forces invade China; Qing Dynasty established | End of Ming Dynasty; Manchu (foreign) rulers adopt Chinese governance while maintaining ethnic distinction |
| 1658–1707 | Aurangzeb rules the Mughal Empire | Reverses Akbar's tolerance; imposes jizya on Hindus; overextension weakens empire; decline begins |
| 1661–1722 | Emperor Kangxi rules the Qing Dynasty | Qing golden age: stability, territorial expansion, Confucian governance |
| 1683 | Ottomans fail second Siege of Vienna | Turning point: Ottoman westward expansion halted permanently; "Sick Man of Europe" era begins |
| 1722 | Afghan forces sack Isfahan; Safavid Empire collapses | End of Safavid power; demonstrates vulnerability of gunpowder empires to internal revolt and external pressure |
| 1736–1796 | Emperor Qianlong rules the Qing Dynasty | Territorial expansion into Xinjiang; later reign marked by corruption and White Lotus Rebellion |

## Chronological Patterns to Know

**The overlap of peak reigns (1520s–1560s):** Suleiman I (Ottoman), Shah Ismail/Tahmasp (Safavid), Akbar (Mughal beginning), and Ivan IV (Russia) all ruled in roughly the same generation. This concentration of powerful rulers reflects a broader 16th-century pattern of state consolidation across Eurasia.

**The cascade of declines:** The Safavids fell first (1722), followed by the Mughals (fragmented after 1707, British takeover 19th century), then the Ottomans (WWI, 1922). Note that declines were gradual and overlapping, not sudden — a common AP essay trap.

**China's parallel timeline:** Ming (1368–1644) → Qing (1644–1912) runs parallel to the [[Concept Notes/Gunpowder Empires|Gunpowder Empires]] but follows a different arc: the Qing was a *foreign* (Manchu) dynasty that adopted Chinese institutions, rather than a Turkic warrior state reshaping a conquered culture.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam rarely asks you to memorize exact dates — but you should know the **sequence and relative timing** of key events: (1) Constantinople 1453 → gunpowder empires dominant; (2) Akbar's tolerance vs. Aurangzeb's intolerance (before/after 1658); (3) Safavid collapse 1722 → Ottoman/Russian opportunism. These sequences support causation and CCOT arguments.

> [!tip]
> **Memory trick for the three Islamic empires:** "**O**nly **S**ultan **M**ehmed" → Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal. Their founding dates follow in order: Ottoman (1299), Safavid (1501), Mughal (1526).
