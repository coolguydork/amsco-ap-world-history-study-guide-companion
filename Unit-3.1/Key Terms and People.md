# Topic 3.1 — Key Terms and People

> [!exam-tip]
> Know **who ruled each empire, what they accomplished, and why it matters for the AP exam**. Don't just memorize names — connect each ruler to a theme (expansion, legitimacy, decline).

---

## Empires and Dynasties

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **[[Concept Notes/Gunpowder Empires\|Gunpowder Empires]]** | Large multiethnic states — Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, and Russia — that used firearms to conquer and govern | The organizing concept for Period 2 land-based empires; all trace to Turkic nomadic origins |
| **Ming Dynasty** | Chinese dynasty (1368–1644) that overthrew the Mongol Yuan Dynasty | Restored Chinese cultural traditions; rebuilt the Great Wall; governed for 300 years |
| **Qing Dynasty** | Manchu-founded dynasty (1644–1911) that succeeded the Ming | Expanded China to its greatest territorial extent; eventually weakened by corruption and European pressure |
| **Ottoman Empire** | Sunni Islamic empire founded by Osman dynasty in 1300s; lasted until 1918 | Longest-lasting of the Gunpowder Empires; controlled key Silk Roads chokepoints at Istanbul |
| **Safavid Empire** | Shi'a Islamic Persian empire (1501–1760) | Institutionalized Shi'a Islam in Iran; rival of the Ottomans; declined after 1722 |
| **Mughal Empire** | Muslim empire in India (1520s–19th century) | United much of India under one rule; produced the Taj Mahal; declined under Aurangzeb |
| **Romanov Dynasty** | Russian dynasty that ruled from 1613 following Ivan IV's death | Produced Peter the Great; modernized Russia along European lines |

## Key Concepts and Vocabulary

**ghazi ideal** — A model for warrior life blending nomadic cooperative values with willingness to fight as a holy warrior for Islam. Shaped the armies of the Gunpowder Empires. Tamerlane exemplified this ideal.

**shah** — Title equivalent to king or emperor, used in Persia/Iran. Ismail was proclaimed shah in 1501.

**castes (jatis)** — Rigid social groupings in Hindu society, designated at birth. The four varnas are Brahmins (priests/scholars), Kshatriyas (warriors/rulers), Vaishyas (merchants/farmers), Shudras (laborers). Outside the system: the Dalits (untouchables). The caste system structured Mughal India's social and economic life.

**Cossacks** — Fierce peasant warriors of the Russian steppes, hired by the Stroganov landowners to fight indigenous tribes and the Siberian khan during Russian eastward expansion under Ivan IV.

**oprichnina** — Ivan IV's paramilitary force, dressed in black, drawn from lower bureaucrats and merchants (not nobles) to assure loyalty to the tsar. Prefigured later Russian secret police.

**Gutenberg printing press** — Invented c. 1450; dramatically increased literacy in Europe; enabled the Protestant Reformation by making the Bible widely accessible.

**White Lotus Rebellion (1796–1804)** — Peasant uprising against Qing Dynasty's high taxes and desire to restore the Ming Dynasty; suppressed brutally.

> [!tip]
> The **caste system** is an important continuity from earlier Indian history. It persisted throughout Mughal rule (Topic 3.1) into the colonial period (Topic 6.3) and beyond.

## Key Places

**Istanbul (Constantinople)** — Capital of the Ottoman Empire; fell to Mehmed II in 1453; controls the Bosporus Strait between the Aegean and Black Seas; western terminus of overland Silk Roads.

**Samarkand** — Central Asian city (modern Uzbekistan) and base of Tamerlane's power; cultural center of art, literature, and architecture; crucial Silk Roads hub.

**Isfahan** — Safavid capital; sacked by Afghan rebels in 1722, marking the Safavid decline.

**Delhi** — Mughal capital under Akbar; center of Mughal government and justice.

**Guangzhou (Canton)** — Port city where Qing China confined European traders; focus of future Opium War tensions.
