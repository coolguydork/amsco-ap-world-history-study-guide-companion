## Causation Chain: Why These Empires Rose When They Did

1. **Mongol collapse** (13th–14th centuries) left power vacuums across Central Asia, Persia, and India
2. **Tamerlane** exploited those vacuums but couldn't build lasting government
3. **Gunpowder technology** (originating in China, spreading via trade routes) gave military advantage to those who adopted it first
4. **Turkic nomadic warriors** — with the ghazi ideal — were ideally suited to exploit this technology
5. **Three Islamic empires** filled the vacuum in overlapping regions: Ottoman (Middle East/Balkans), Safavid (Persia), Mughal (India)
6. **Russia and China** expanded separately but by similar mechanisms — organized state power backed by firearms

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP exam, **causation questions** about the rise of empires in Period 2 should mention: (1) post-Mongol power vacuum, (2) gunpowder technology, (3) strong military leadership. All three need to be in your answer for full credit.

## Continuity and Change Over Time: 1450–1750

**What changed:**
- Gunpowder replaced cavalry-only warfare as the key to empire
- New, larger, more ethnically diverse empires replaced the fragmented post-Mongol states
- European monarchies centralized dramatically (new monarchies)
- Russia expanded across Siberia to the Pacific in just ~200 years

**What continued:**
- Trade along Silk Roads and Indian Ocean routes (see [[Unit-2.1/Silk Roads Overview]])
- Islam as the unifying ideology of the three major Asian empires
- Tribute and tax systems structuring economic relationships within empires
- The ghazi warrior tradition linking nomadic values to Islamic conquest
