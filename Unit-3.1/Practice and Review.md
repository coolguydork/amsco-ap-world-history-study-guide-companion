## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

Which of the following best explains why the Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal empires are grouped together as "Gunpowder Empires"?

A) They were all founded by the same Mongol dynasty  
B) They all originated from Turkic nomads who used firearms to conquer and control territory  
C) They all adopted Sunni Islam as their state religion  
D) They all gained power by defeating the Ming Dynasty of China

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> A is wrong — they descend from Turkic, not the same Mongol lineage.
> **B is correct** — the key shared traits are Turkic origins, post-Mongol power vacuum exploitation, and gunpowder weapons.
> C is wrong — the Safavids were Shi'a, not Sunni.
> D is wrong — none of these empires defeated the Ming.

**Question 2**

The fall of Constantinople in 1453 was significant primarily because it

A) ended the Islamic presence in Europe  
B) gave the Ottomans control of a key trade chokepoint between the Aegean and Black Seas  
C) destroyed the eastern terminus of the Silk Roads  
D) proved that land armies were superior to naval forces

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> A is wrong — it *expanded* Islamic presence in Europe.
> **B is correct** — Istanbul's location on the Bosporus Strait made it a crucial trade hub.
> C is wrong — Istanbul *became* the terminus, reinforcing rather than destroying it.
> D is wrong — the fall used cannons (technology), not strictly land vs. sea dynamics.

**Question 3**

Tamerlane's significance for AP World History lies primarily in the fact that he

A) founded the Ottoman Empire and established Istanbul as its capital  
B) created a lasting bureaucratic structure that governed Central Asia for centuries  
C) set the stage for the rise of the Turkic Gunpowder Empires by exploiting post-Mongol power vacuums  
D) unified China under a single dynasty for the first time since the Song period

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> A is wrong — Mehmed II established Istanbul; the Osman dynasty founded the Ottomans.
> B is wrong — Tamerlane *failed* to create lasting government.
> **C is correct** — Tamerlane's conquests cleared the path; his descendant Babur founded the Mughals.
> D is wrong — Tamerlane was Central Asian, not Chinese; the Ming unified China.

**Question 4**

Which of the following best represents a continuity in Russia from the period of Ivan IV through Peter the Great?

A) Russia's rulers increasingly reduced the power of the boyar noble class  
B) Russia expanded its territory eastward using gunpowder and professional warriors  
C) Russia developed a strong naval power in the Baltic Sea  
D) Russia modeled its government on Western European parliamentary systems

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> A is partially true but misleading — both rulers fought the boyars, but the trend wasn't consistent.
> **B is correct** — the eastward expansion using gunpowder and Cossacks/professional soldiers is the through-line from Ivan IV to Peter.
> C is wrong — the Baltic port (St. Petersburg) was Peter's achievement, not a continuity.
> D is wrong — Peter admired Western Europe but created autocracy, not parliaments.

**Question 5**

The conflict between the Ottoman and Safavid Empires is best understood as

A) primarily a racial conflict between Arab and Persian peoples

## Short Answer Questions

**SAQ 1 — Causation**

Using specific evidence, explain ONE cause of the expansion of the Gunpowder Empires in the period 1450–1750.

*Sample Response:* One major cause of Gunpowder Empire expansion was the **power vacuum left by the collapse of the Mongol khanates** in the 14th century. After Tamerlane's invasions destabilized Central Asia and Persia, there was no dominant power capable of resisting well-organized, gunpowder-armed forces. The Ottomans exploited this vacuum in Anatolia and the Balkans, the Safavids in Persia, and the Mughal Empire under Babur (a descendant of Tamerlane) in India. All three empires rose in regions where the Mongol-established order had collapsed, and their military superiority — particularly the use of artillery cannons — allowed them to defeat fragmented opponents who lacked comparable firepower.

---

**SAQ 2 — Comparison**

Briefly explain ONE similarity and ONE difference between the expansion of the Ottoman Empire and the expansion of Russia in the period 1450–1750.

*Sample Similarity:* Both the Ottoman Empire and Russia expanded by exploiting post-Mongol political disorder and relied on gunpowder weapons to defeat opponents. The Ottomans used artillery to breach Constantinople's walls in 1453, while Ivan IV used gunpowder weapons to defeat the Mongol successor khanates of Kazan and Astrakhan.

*Sample Difference:* The Ottoman Empire expanded primarily **westward into Christian Europe and southward into the Arab world**, while Russia expanded **eastward across Siberia** toward Asia. The Ottomans controlled key Mediterranean and Balkan territories; Russia's expansion was through sparsely populated Siberian tundra toward the Pacific.

---

**SAQ 3 — Continuity and Change**

Explain ONE continuity and ONE change in the political or economic structures of the Gunpowder Empires from c. 1450 to c. 1750.

*Sample Continuity:* Throughout the period 1450–1750, all three Islamic Gunpowder Empires continued to use **tribute and taxation systems** as the primary means of financing military expansion. Whether through Ottoman tax farming, Mughal zamindar collection, or Safavid levies, the pattern of extracting agricultural surplus from peasant populations remained constant — and the corruption of these systems contributed to all three empires' eventual decline.

*Sample Change:* While the empires began the period as militarily dominant forces capable of expanding against all rivals, by 1750 they were **declining relative to Western European powers** who had developed stronger navies and economies. The Ottoman Empire that besieged Vienna in 1529 and 1683 was a very different power from the "Sick Man of Europe" of the 18th century.

## LEQ / DBQ Guidance

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 3.1:**

- "Evaluate the extent to which the Gunpowder Empires of the period 1450–1750 were similar in their methods of expansion."
- "Evaluate the extent to which gunpowder technology caused the rise of large land-based empires in the period 1450–1750."

**Strong evidence to use:**
- Fall of Constantinople (1453) — Ottoman artillery breach
- Mehmed II's expansion into the Balkans
- Safavid conquest of Persia using land-based military might
- Babur's founding of Mughal India (Tamerlane's descendant filling the power vacuum)
- Russia's eastward expansion using Cossacks and firearms
- Ivan IV's defeat of Mongol khanates

**Common mistakes to avoid:**
- Confusing the three Islamic Gunpowder Empires (they are separate states — different religion branches, different regions)
- Saying the Safavids were Sunni (they were **Shi'a**)
- Forgetting that the Qing Dynasty was *Manchu*, not Han Chinese
- Claiming that the Gunpowder Empires were unified — they frequently fought each other

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: The Silk Roads ended when the Gunpowder Empires rose.
> **Reality**: Istanbul (Constantinople) was the western terminus of the overland Silk Roads and *remained* a major trade hub under Ottoman control. The Gunpowder Empires *competed* over trade routes; they didn't destroy them. European maritime expansion (Topic 4) eventually reduced the Silk Roads' importance.

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: Mughal India was entirely Muslim.
> **Reality**: The Mughal Empire governed a **majority Hindu population**. Under Akbar, this was managed through tolerance and inclusion. Under Aurangzeb, forced Islam led to rebellions and ultimately weakened the empire.

## Quick Review: Three Islamic Gunpowder Empires at a Glance

| | Ottoman | Safavid | Mughal |
|--|---------|---------|--------|
| **Religion** | Sunni Islam | Shi'a Islam | Sunni Islam (but Akbar tolerant) |
| **Location** | Turkey, Balkans, N. Africa, Middle East | Persia (Iran) | India |
| **Peak ruler** | Suleiman I (1520–1566) | Shah Abbas I (1588–1629) | Akbar (1556–1605) |
| **Key achievement** | Fall of Constantinople (1453) | Shi'a state religion; Isfahan as cultural center | Religious tolerance; rich trade |
| **Cause of decline** | Weak sultans, harem politics, European naval power | Ineffectual leaders; Pashtun rebellion (1722) | Aurangzeb's intolerance; treasury drain |
