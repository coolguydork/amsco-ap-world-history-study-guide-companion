# Topic 1.4 — Key Terms, Concepts & Practice

## Core Vocabulary

**Mississippian culture**
The first large-scale civilization in North America, emerging in the 700s–800s in the Mississippi River Valley. Known for enormous earthen mounds. Matrilineal. Declined around 1450.

**Cahokia**
The largest Mississippian mound site, located in southern Illinois. A major urban and ritual center — abandoned around 1450.

**Matrilineal society**
A social system where status and inheritance pass through the mother's line. The Mississippians were matrilineal — succession passed to a sister's son, not the chief's own son. This contrasts with the patrilineal systems common in Eurasia.

**Mayan city-states**
The dominant Mayan political unit — a city and its surrounding territory, each ruled by a king. Wars were fought for tribute and captives, not territorial control. Represents political **decentralization** in Mesoamerica.

**Tenochtitlan**
Aztec capital city, founded 1325, on an island in Lake Texcoco (modern Mexico City). Population ~200,000. Featured the Great Pyramid, aqueducts, and chinampas. One of the largest cities in the world.

**Chinampas**
Aztec "floating gardens" built on Lake Texcoco to expand agricultural land. Ditches irrigated the gardens. An example of environmental adaptation enabling urban food supply.

**Theocracy**
Government by religious leaders. The Aztec state was a theocracy — the emperor was both political ruler and divine representative of the gods.

**Pochteca**
Aztec special merchant class that traded in luxury goods. An important distinction — the Aztec state had a more developed merchant class than some other societies.

**Tribute system (Aztec)**
Conquered peoples paid tribute (food, cloth, luxury goods) to the Aztec emperor. Local rulers remained in place as tribute collectors. Effective but generated deep resentment among subject peoples.

**Pachacuti (r. 1438–1471)**
Incan leader whose name means "transformer/shaker of the earth." His military conquests founded the Incan Empire. One of the most successful state-builders in pre-Columbian American history.

**Mit'a system**
The Incan system of mandatory public service (labor). Instead of tribute in goods, conquered men aged 15–50 provided labor for agriculture, road construction, and military service. More integrative than the Aztec tribute model.

**Carpa Nan**
The Incan road system — 25,000 miles of roads used mainly by government and military. Essential in mountainous terrain where pack animals (llamas) couldn't carry heavy loads. Made imperial administration possible.

**Quipu**
Incan system of knotted strings used to record numerical information (trade data, census, engineering specifications, government messages). The Inca had no written script — the quipu was their information management system.

**Royal ancestor veneration**
Incan practice of mummifying dead rulers who continued to "own" their wealth and possessions. New emperors inherited no property, motivating constant conquest to accumulate resources.

**Huaca**
Incan animistic belief that elements of the physical world (mountains, rivers, stones) could have supernatural powers.

**Waru waru**
Incan agricultural technique: raised beds with water channels that captured rainfall, prevented erosion, and stored water for dry periods. Made high-altitude farming productive.

**Human sacrifice**
Practiced by Maya, Aztecs, and Inca — but to different degrees. Aztecs used it extensively (gods sacrificed themselves to create the world; humans must repay). Inca used it more rarely. Mayans used war captives.

> [!warning]
> Most detailed descriptions of Aztec human sacrifice come from Spanish sources. Spanish conquerors had political motives to portray Aztec practices as extreme to justify conquest. Use this as your HAPP (Historical situation, Audience, Purpose, Perspective) point in a DBQ.

## Key People

**Pachacuti (r. 1438–1471)**
See above. Founded the Incan Empire through military conquest. His name reflects his self-perception as a transformer of the world.

**Nezahualcoyotl (1402–1472)**
Aztec poet quoted in AMSCO. "But more than all I love my brother, man!" — This quote from an Aztec intellectual challenges stereotypes about Aztec culture being exclusively focused on war and sacrifice.

**Francisco Pizarro**
Spanish conquistador who arrived in the Incan Empire in 1532, during a civil war. Conquered the core of the empire by 1533. (More in Unit 4.)

## AP Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1.** The Aztec and Incan empires both used which of the following methods to control conquered peoples?

A) Full assimilation of conquered peoples into Aztec or Incan culture  
B) Systems that extracted resources or labor while allowing some local autonomy  
C) The imposition of a single universal religion on all conquered peoples  
D) The creation of democratic councils in each conquered territory

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Aztecs used tribute (goods) while allowing local rulers to remain. Inca used mit'a (labor) and rewarded loyal conquered leaders. Both extracted resources but neither fully dismantled local structures. A, C, and D are incorrect.

---

**Question 2.** A historian studying the Inca would most likely point to the quipu and the Carpa Nan road system as evidence of:

A) The Inca's advanced maritime trade capabilities  
B) The Inca's ability to administer a large empire without a writing system  
C) The Inca's dependence on tribute from coastal peoples  
D) The Inca's use of religious authority to suppress conquered populations

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The quipu recorded administrative data; the road system allowed rapid communication and movement of goods and troops across an enormous mountain empire. Together they enabled governance over vast territory without a written script.

---

**Question 3.** The decline of both the Aztec and Incan empires in the 16th century was most directly caused by:

A) Internal collapse due to agricultural failure before Spanish arrival  
B) A combination of Spanish military conquest, European diseases, and internal tensions  
C) Conquest by other indigenous civilizations in the Americas  
D) The Mongol invasions, which spread from Asia to the Americas

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Aztecs faced resentment from subject peoples (many joined the Spanish); the Inca faced a civil war of succession. European diseases devastated populations in both empires. D is factually wrong — the Mongols never reached the Americas.

## SAQ: Aztec vs. Inca Comparison

**Prompt:** Compare ONE similarity and ONE difference between the Aztec Empire and the Incan Empire in the period c. 1200–c. 1450.

**Similarity Model:**
Both the Aztecs and the Inca used their imperial power to extract resources from conquered peoples to support the state. The Aztecs required tribute in goods (food, cloth, luxury items), while the Inca required mit'a (labor). In both cases, conquered peoples maintained some local autonomy — Aztec rulers kept local leaders as tribute collectors, while Inca governors rewarded loyal conquered leaders. This parallel shows that effective imperial control in the Americas, like in Eurasia, depended on balancing extraction with accommodation.

**Difference Model:**
The Aztec empire generated more resentment among subject peoples than the Inca did. The Aztec tribute system — including the demand for human sacrifice victims from conquered peoples — bred deep hostility. When the Spanish arrived in 1519, many subject tribes were ready to rebel and allied with the conquistadors. The Incan mit'a system, though demanding, was not as openly coercive, and the Inca faced internal instability primarily from a dynastic civil war (1532), not widespread subject rebellion.
