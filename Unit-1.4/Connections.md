# Connections — Topic 1.4

*The Americas: Backward, Forward & Lateral Links*

## Builds On

### Classical Olmec and Maya (c. 1500 BCE–900 CE)
The Olmec established the foundational cultural patterns later civilizations inherited: step pyramids, the feathered serpent deity, ritual ball courts, and human sacrifice. The Classic Maya (250–900 CE) developed writing, the calendar, and astronomical observation to levels of sophistication not matched in the Americas. The post-classic Maya city-states (active during AP Period 1) are direct successors.

### Classical Inca Predecessors (Wari and Tiwanaku, c. 600–1000 CE)
Before the Inca, the **Wari** and **Tiwanaku** civilizations in the Andes developed early forms of terrace agriculture, road networks, and political centralization. Pachacuti's conquests built on a pre-existing tradition of Andean state-building.

## Leads To

### Topic 4.4 — Spanish Conquest of the Americas
The Aztec Triple Alliance falls to Hernán Cortés in 1521; the Inca Empire falls to Francisco Pizarro in 1533. Both conquests exploited structural weaknesses documented in Topic 1.4: Aztec tribute resentment that drove subject-people alliances with Spain; Inca civil war that split the army. The **mit'a system** continued under Spanish colonial rule — one of the most direct labor-system continuities in AP World History.

### Topic 4.3 — The Columbian Exchange
The Americas' geographic isolation ends when Columbus arrives in 1492. American crops — maize, potatoes, tomatoes, cacao — transform global agriculture and population growth. American populations, having no exposure to Eurasian diseases, suffer catastrophic mortality from smallpox, measles, and influenza. The exchange was deeply asymmetrical in its immediate human costs.

### Topic 4.7 — Casta System
Spanish colonial society imposed a racial hierarchy (the casta system) on the indigenous peoples of the Americas, placing them below European-born settlers. The pre-existing Aztec and Inca social hierarchies were both destroyed and partially co-opted into this new colonial order.

## Compare With

### Topic 1.1 — East Asia
[[Unit-1.1/East Asia Overview|East Asia Overview]] offers productive comparisons:
- **Record-keeping:** Inca quipu vs. Chinese writing. Both record-keeping systems supported complex administration, but Chinese script enabled literary and philosophical culture that quipu could not.
- **Agricultural innovation:** American terrace farming and chinampas vs. Chinese wet-rice cultivation and irrigation. Both required sophisticated environmental engineering.
- **State legitimacy:** Aztec/Inca divine rulers vs. Chinese Mandate of Heaven — both used religious concepts to justify political power.

### Topic 1.5 — Africa
[[Unit-1.5/Africa Overview|Africa Overview]] offers several parallel and contrasting patterns:
- **Oral tradition:** Inca quipu-runners carried messages without writing; West African **griots** preserved history through oral performance. Both were sophisticated information systems that didn't rely on text.
- **Stateless vs. centralized:** West African kin-based networks (stateless) alongside centralized Mali Empire — parallel to Mississippian clan societies alongside centralized Inca Tawantinsuyu.
- **Environmental decline:** Maya deforestation and Mississippian flooding parallel Great Zimbabwe's collapse from overgrazing. Environmental limits as a cause of civilizational decline is a cross-regional pattern.

### Topic 1.6 — Europe (Feudalism)
Inca mit'a and European feudal serfdom are both **coercive labor systems** in which subject populations owed labor obligations to a ruling elite. Both systems created networks of obligation and dependency. Key differences: mit'a was a state-managed public service system with reciprocal benefits (food, goods from state storehouses); feudal serfdom was a private relationship between lord and serf with fewer state-level reciprocal obligations.

## AP Threads Running Through Topic 1.4

- **State-building under diverse constraints** — How do civilizations build complexity without iron, wheel, or horses? The Americas answer this question directly.
- **Agricultural innovation** — Chinampas, terrace farming, waru waru: all examples of adapting agriculture to extreme environments.
- **Coercive labor systems** — Mit'a (Inca), tribute (Aztec) — connect forward to colonial encomienda/mit'a, backward to other coercive systems.
- **Religion and political legitimacy** — Divine rulers across all three major civilizations; human sacrifice as a state institution.
- **Oral tradition and non-text record-keeping** — Quipu without writing; connects to African griot tradition.
