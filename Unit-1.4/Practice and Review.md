# Topic 1.4 — Practice and Review

## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1.** The Aztec and Incan empires both used which of the following methods to control conquered peoples?

A) Full assimilation of conquered peoples into Aztec or Incan culture
B) Systems that extracted resources or labor while allowing some local autonomy
C) The imposition of a single universal religion on all conquered peoples
D) The creation of democratic councils in each conquered territory

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Aztecs used tribute (goods) while allowing local rulers to remain. Inca used mit'a (labor) and rewarded loyal conquered leaders. Both extracted resources but neither fully dismantled local structures. A, C, and D are incorrect.

---

**Question 2.** A historian studying the Inca would most likely point to the quipu and the Carpa Nan road system as evidence of:

A) The Inca's advanced maritime trade capabilities
B) The Inca's ability to administer a large empire without a writing system
C) The Inca's dependence on tribute from coastal peoples
D) The Inca's use of religious authority to suppress conquered populations

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The quipu recorded administrative data; the road system allowed rapid communication and movement of goods and troops across an enormous mountain empire. Together they enabled governance over vast territory without a written script.

---

**Question 3.** The decline of both the Aztec and Incan empires in the 16th century was most directly caused by:

A) Internal collapse due to agricultural failure before Spanish arrival
B) A combination of Spanish military conquest, European diseases, and internal tensions
C) Conquest by other indigenous civilizations in the Americas
D) The Mongol invasions, which spread from Asia to the Americas

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Aztecs faced resentment from subject peoples (many joined the Spanish); the Inca faced a civil war of succession. European diseases devastated populations in both empires. D is factually wrong — the Mongols never reached the Americas.

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Prompt:** Compare ONE similarity and ONE difference between the Aztec Empire and the Incan Empire in the period c. 1200–c. 1450.

> [!tip] Similarity Model
> Both the Aztecs and the Inca used their imperial power to extract resources from conquered peoples to support the state. The Aztecs required tribute in goods (food, cloth, luxury items), while the Inca required mit'a (labor). In both cases, conquered peoples maintained some local autonomy — Aztec rulers kept local leaders as tribute collectors, while Inca governors rewarded loyal conquered leaders. This parallel shows that effective imperial control in the Americas, like in Eurasia, depended on balancing extraction with accommodation.

> [!tip] Difference Model
> The Aztec empire generated more resentment among subject peoples than the Inca did. The Aztec tribute system — including the demand for human sacrifice victims from conquered peoples — bred deep hostility. When the Spanish arrived in 1519, many subject tribes were ready to rebel and allied with the conquistadors. The Incan mit'a system, though demanding, was not as openly coercive, and the Inca faced internal instability primarily from a dynastic civil war (1532), not widespread subject rebellion.

## LEQ/DBQ Thinking: Causation Prompt

**Prompt:** Evaluate the most important cause of the growth of large-scale states in the Americas in the period c. 1200–c. 1450.

**Brainstorm outline:**

- **Thesis angle:** Military conquest was the primary cause of state expansion for both the Aztec and Incan empires, but each relied on distinct systems of resource extraction and legitimization that shaped the sustainability of their power.
- **Aztec evidence:** Tribute system funded the state and military; theocracy and human sacrifice legitimized conquest; chinampas supported urban food supply; alliance system (Triple Alliance) enabled territorial expansion.
- **Inca evidence:** Pachacuti's military campaigns rapidly expanded the empire after 1438; mit'a system integrated conquered peoples into the state economy; road network and quipu made vast empire administrable; ancestor veneration created permanent incentive for new emperors to conquer.
- **Complexity:** Both empires also relied on religion (Aztec theocracy; Inca sun worship and huaca) to justify political authority — military and ideological causes worked together.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently compares the Americas to Eurasia in this period. The Aztec and Inca are your go-to examples for showing that **complex, tributary empires developed independently** in the Americas — without the Silk Roads, gunpowder, or horses that shaped Eurasian empires.
