# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 1.4: Developments in the Americas

Chronological anchor points for the Americas, c. 700s–c. 1533. The AP core period is c. 1200–c. 1450, but deep background is essential context for understanding these civilizations.

---

## Deep Background: Roots of the Period

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 2500–400 B.C.E.** | Olmec civilization (Mesoamerica) | The foundational culture of Mesoamerica; feathered serpent deity, ritual sacrifice, pyramids, and ball courts adopted by later Maya, Aztec cultures |
| **c. 800–200 B.C.E.** | Chavín civilization (Andes) | Early Andean civilization; later replaced by the cultures that gave rise to the Inca |
| **250–900 C.E.** | Classic Maya period | Mayan civilization at its height; 40+ city-states; accurate calendar; concept of zero; astronomical observatories; peak population ~2 million |

> [!tip]
> The **Olmecs** are to Mesoamerica what the Yellow River civilization is to China — the foundational "mother culture." Knowing this allows you to make the argument that later Mesoamerican civilizations built on a shared cultural inheritance, even without centralized political unity.

## North America

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **700s–800s C.E.** | Mississippian Culture emerges | First large-scale civilization in North America; Mississippi River Valley; earthen mounds (Cahokia) replace stone pyramids |
| **c. 900** | Chaco Canyon culture develops (Southwest) | Elaborate multi-room stone housing; water management in an arid environment |
| **c. 900–1300** | Mesa Verde culture flourishes (Southwest) | Cliff-side multi-story dwellings of sandstone brick; adapts to scarce wood and dry climate |
| **c. late 13th c.** | Chaco and Mesa Verde decline | Climate became drier; agricultural failure forces abandonment |
| **c. 1450** | Cahokia abandoned | Theories: flooding/crop failure from climate change; European-introduced diseases (arriving via trade networks before direct contact) |
| **By 1600** | Other major Mississippian cities abandoned | End of North America's first large-scale urban civilization |

## AP Period Core: c. 1200–c. 1450

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **1200s** | Aztecs (Mexica) migrate from north to central Mexico | Originally hunter-gatherers; settle near modern Mexico City; begin building their empire |
| **1200s** | Maya in decline but continuing | City-states persist into the 1500s, though the Classic peak has passed |
| **1200s** | Inca tribes near Cuzco, Peru | Small, competing tribes — not yet an empire |
| **1325** | Aztecs found Tenochtitlan | Capital on island in Lake Texcoco; strategic defensive location; grows to ~200,000 people — one of world's largest cities |
| **1325–1425** | Aztecs conquer central Mexico | Build empire from Gulf of Mexico to Pacific Ocean through military conquest and tribute system |
| **1438** | Pachacuti begins Inca conquests | "Transformer of the earth" launches campaigns from Cuzco; founds what becomes the largest pre-Columbian empire in the Americas |
| **c. 1450** | Incan Empire stretches from Ecuador to Chile | Carpa Nan road system under construction; mit'a labor system integrating conquered peoples |
| **1471–1493** | Topa Inca and Huayna Capac rule | Son and grandson of Pachacuti consolidate and manage the vast empire |

---

## Decline and Contact: Post-1450

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **c. late 1400s** | Aztec Empire shows signs of strain | Agricultural inefficiency (no wheels/pack animals); overextension; resentment among tribute-paying subjects |
| **1519** | Hernán Cortés arrives in Mexico | Many Aztec subject tribes ally with Spaniards to rebel; empire falls by 1521 |
| **1521** | Spanish conquer Tenochtitlan | End of Aztec Empire; beginning of New Spain |
| **1532** | Francisco Pizarro arrives in Peru | Incan Empire already in civil war of succession after Huayna Capac's death |
| **1533** | Spanish conquer core of Incan Empire | Civil war + European diseases weakened resistance; Incan outposts hold out until 1572 |

## Causation Chain: Rise of the Aztec Empire

```
Aztecs migrate from the north to central Mexico (1200s)
          ↓
Found Tenochtitlan on island in Lake Texcoco (1325)
          ↓
Defensive location enables survival and growth
          ↓
Military conquest of surrounding peoples (next 100 years)
          ↓
Tribute system extracts goods from conquered peoples
(local rulers kept in place as collectors)
          ↓
Theocracy + human sacrifice legitimizes imperial power
("gods sacrificed themselves; we repay them")
          ↓
Chinampas + aqueducts support urban population of ~200,000
          ↓
Overextension + resentment from subject peoples
          ↓
Spaniards arrive (1519) → subject tribes ally with Cortés → Empire falls (1521)
```

---

## Causation Chain: Rise and Fall of the Incan Empire

```
Small tribes near Cuzco, Peru (pre-1438)
          ↓
Pachacuti's military conquests begin (1438)
          ↓
Mit'a system: labor (not tribute) integrates conquered peoples
          ↓
Provincial governors + bureaucracy administer four regions
          ↓
Carpa Nan road system (25,000 miles) enables communication and control
          ↓
Quipu system records administrative data without writing
          ↓
Royal ancestor veneration: dead rulers own wealth →
new rulers must conquer to accumulate resources
          ↓
Empire stretches Ecuador to Chile — largest in pre-Columbian Americas
          ↓
Huayna Capac dies → civil war of succession (1527–1532)
          ↓
Pizarro arrives during civil war (1532)
European diseases + weakened army → Spanish conquest (1533)
```

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Aztec and Inca declines** follow a parallel AP structure: internal weakness (resentment/civil war) + external shock (Spanish + disease). The AP loves asking you to evaluate which cause was "most important." A sophisticated thesis acknowledges both internal and external factors rather than attributing collapse to Spain alone.

## Chronological Comparison: Three American Civilizations

| Period | Maya | Aztec | Inca |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-1200** | Classic peak (250–900); city-states continue | Hunter-gatherers migrating south | Small competing tribes in Andes |
| **1200–1325** | Declining but surviving city-states | Migration and settlement near Lake Texcoco | Tribes near Cuzco |
| **1325–1438** | City-states persist; tribute wars | Tenochtitlan founded; empire building begins | Local conflicts; no unified state |
| **1438–1493** | Continuing but weakened | Empire at peak; tribute system mature | Pachacuti → Topa Inca; rapid expansion |
| **1493–1521** | City-states persist until Spanish contact | Signs of overextension; strain | Huayna Capac consolidates empire |
| **1521–1533** | Spanish conquest begins (1519–1524) | Empire falls (1521) | Empire falls (1533) |
