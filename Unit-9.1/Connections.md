# Connections

**Unit 9.1 — Advances in Technology and Exchange After 1900 | AMSCO pp. 633–640**

---

## Builds On (Prerequisites)

**[[Unit-5.5/Technology in the Industrial Age Overview\|Technology in the Industrial Age Overview]]**
The Industrial Revolution established the pattern of technology driving economic and social transformation. Steam power, the telegraph, and railroads compressed distance and accelerated exchange in Period 3 — the internet, shipping containers, and air travel did the same at vastly greater scale in Period 4. CCOT essays comparing the two periods should emphasize continuity of the *pattern* (technology → globalization → inequality) and change in *speed and scale*.

**[[Unit-4.1/Technological Innovations Overview\|Technological Innovations Overview]]**
Maritime technology and gunpowder weapons enabled the early-modern age of exploration and conquest. Topic 9.1's technology serves a similar structural role: enabling unprecedented movement of people, goods, and ideas across distances — but now at near-global instantaneous scale.

## Cross-Unit AP Connections

**[[Unit-8.1/Setting the Stage for the Cold War & Decolonization Overview|Topic 8.1]] — The Cold War** — The Space Race and ARPANET/internet emerged directly from Cold War state competition. Technology (Sputnik, Apollo, nuclear energy) was a primary arena of U.S.-Soviet rivalry. This is a classic example of geopolitical competition producing civilian technological spin-offs.

**[[Unit-8.5/Decolonization after 1900 Overview|Topic 8.5]] — Decolonization** — The Green Revolution was partly designed to prevent newly independent developing nations from turning to communism by addressing food insecurity. U.S. agricultural aid was as much Cold War strategy as humanitarian effort.

**[[Unit-9.5/Calls for Reform & Responses Overview|Topic 9.5]] — Calls for Reform** — Birth control and women's roles in the workforce connect directly to feminist movements. The oral contraceptive pill is one of the material technological causes of the women's rights movements analyzed in 9.5.

**[[Unit-9.6/Globalized Culture Overview|Topic 9.6]] — Globalized Culture** — The internet and social media are the primary mechanisms by which cultural globalization (the spread of music, film, fashion, ideas across borders) occurred in Period 4.

**[[Unit-9.8/Institutions Developing in a Globalized World Overview|Topic 9.8]] — International Institutions** — WHO vaccine campaigns and agricultural research programs (Rockefeller Foundation, CGIAR) illustrate how international institutions channeled technology to address global problems.

## AP Essay Combination Strategies

> [!exam-tip]
> **LEQ on causes of globalization after 1900:** Use three 9.1 technologies as your three body paragraphs — (1) communication/internet for cultural/political globalization, (2) shipping containers for economic globalization, (3) Green Revolution for agricultural integration. Each eliminates a different type of barrier (informational, logistical, nutritional).

> [!exam-tip]
> **DBQ with Borlaug's Nobel lecture:** Frame Borlaug as a complex source — he's both celebrating and warning. Use his warning about population growth as evidence that technology alone cannot solve structural problems, which opens the door to discussing 9.3 (environmental limits) and 9.5 (inequality).

> [!tip]
> The AP loves asking about *unintended consequences* of technology — antibiotic resistance, Green Revolution environmental damage, social media enabling authoritarianism as well as protests. Always have a "costs and limits" point ready for any technology argument.
