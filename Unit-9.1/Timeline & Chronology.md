# Timeline & Chronology

**Unit 9.1 — Advances in Technology and Exchange After 1900 | AMSCO pp. 633–640**

---

## Master Timeline

| Date | Event | AP Significance |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| 1901 | Marconi transmits first transatlantic radio signal | Beginning of mass communication technology; first step toward globally connected media audiences |
| 1928 | Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin | First antibiotic; transformed treatment of bacterial infections; contributed to 20th-century population growth |
| c. 1950s | Shipping container standardization begins | Revolutionized global freight; enabled intercontinental supply chains; key material driver of economic globalization |
| 1952–1960 | Gregory Pincus develops birth control pill; U.S. approves it (1960) | Declining fertility rates; women's agency over reproduction; demographic transformation in developed world |
| 1957 | Soviet Union launches Sputnik | First artificial satellite; opens Space Age; intensifies Cold War technological competition |
| 1961 | Yuri Gagarin becomes first human in space | Peak of Soviet space program; demonstrates reach of state-directed R&D in Cold War context |
| c. 1960s–1970s | Green Revolution transforms agriculture in Asia and Latin America | High-yield wheat and rice varieties reduce famine risk; Norman Borlaug's crossbreeding credited; but also displaces small farmers |
| 1969 | Apollo 11 moon landing | U.S. claims Cold War space victory; demonstrates transformative potential of massive state investment in science |
| c. 1970s–1980s | Antibiotic resistance emerges as public health concern | Validates Fleming's 1945 warning; illustrates unintended consequences of technological progress |
| 1980 | WHO declares smallpox eradicated | First (and so far only) human disease eradicated by vaccine; landmark of international health cooperation |
| 1989 | Tim Berners-Lee invents the World Wide Web | Makes internet publicly accessible; enables the information economy and modern globalization |
| c. 1990s | Internet and WWW spread globally | Most transformative communication technology since printing press; accelerates all forms of globalization |
| 2010s | Arab Spring — social media amplifies anti-government protests | Technology enables rapid political mobilization; demonstrates internet's impact on governance |

## Cause-and-Effect Chain

```
Cold War competition
        ↓
U.S. military funds ARPANET
        ↓
Tim Berners-Lee invents WWW (1989)
        ↓
Internet goes public (1990s)
        ↓
Social media, e-commerce, knowledge economies
        ↓
Arab Spring / global cultural diffusion / economic globalization
```

```
Fleming discovers penicillin (1928)
        ↓
Antibiotics deployed in WWII (saves soldiers)
        ↓
Post-war civilian antibiotic use
        ↓
Dramatic drop in bacterial mortality
        ↓
Population explosion (20th century)
        ↓
Overuse → antibiotic resistance (threat)
```

```
Borlaug crossbreeds high-yield wheat (1940s–60s)
        ↓
Green Revolution: rice + wheat yields surge
        ↓
Famine averted in India, Pakistan, Mexico
        ↓
BUT: small farmers displaced, soil degraded
        ↓
Genetic engineering replaces crossbreeding (late 20th c.)
        ↓
New debates: food sovereignty, biodiversity, GMOs
```

> [!exam-tip]
> Chronological precision matters on the AP: know that the birth control pill was **approved 1960** (not invented then — Pincus worked in the early 1950s), that penicillin was **discovered 1928** but widely deployed in **WWII**, and that the internet was **public by the 1990s** but its military roots (ARPANET) go back to the Cold War.
