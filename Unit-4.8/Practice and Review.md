# Topic 4.8 – Practice and Review (Synthesis)

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** Which of the following best explains why the period 1450–1750 is considered a turning point in global economic history?

- A) European states replaced all existing regional trade networks with a single unified global market
- B) The incorporation of the Western Hemisphere into global trade created the first truly worldwide economic connections, integrating American silver, African labor, and Asian luxury goods into one system
- C) China emerged as the dominant global economic power, replacing Arab and Indian merchants
- D) The decline of land-based empires led to the rise of purely market-based economies across Afro-Eurasia

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The key is the *integration of the Americas*. Before 1450, Afro-Eurasia and the Americas were isolated. By 1750, American silver was circulating in China, African labor was producing sugar for European markets, and Asian goods were sold in the Americas. This was genuinely global in a way no previous trade network had been.

---

**2.** Which of the following represents the MOST significant continuity in labor systems between 1450 and 1750?

- A) The expansion of free wage labor as the dominant form of labor globally
- B) The persistence of coercive labor systems — from serfdom in Russia to chattel slavery in the Americas — as the foundation of economic production
- C) The continued use of guild systems in European manufacturing
- D) The preservation of indigenous American communal farming systems

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Whether old (serfdom) or new (chattel slavery, encomienda, mit'a), coercive labor systems remained the foundation of agricultural and extractive economies throughout the period. The *forms* changed — new systems were created — but the fundamental pattern of forced labor continued.

---

**3.** The development of joint-stock companies and capitalism in this period was most directly a consequence of

- A) the failure of state-sponsored mercantilist policies in France and England
- B) the need to pool investor capital and distribute risk for expensive, dangerous long-distance trading ventures
- C) the decline of the Ottoman and Mughal empires
- D) religious reforms that made lending money at interest morally acceptable

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Joint-stock companies emerged because individual investors or even governments often couldn't afford the costs and risks of overseas trading expeditions alone. Pooling capital and limiting individual liability made these ventures possible.

---

**4.** The spread of syncretic religions like Santería, Vodun, and the cult of the Virgin of Guadalupe in the Americas most directly resulted from

- A) deliberate missionary policies that encouraged blending of religious traditions
- B) the isolation of indigenous and African communities from Christian influence
- C) sustained contact between different religious traditions under conditions of colonial power that required outward conformity to Catholicism while allowing space for cultural adaptation
- D) the failure of Catholic missionaries to convert indigenous and African peoples

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> Syncretic religions emerged because enslaved Africans and indigenous peoples were required to practice Catholicism publicly but retained elements of their own spiritual traditions, blending them with Christianity to create new forms. This was not missionary-approved — it was cultural survival.

## Long Essay Question (LEQ) Practice

### Prompt 1 (Causation)
**"Explain the causes of the dramatic expansion of the transatlantic slave trade in the period 1500–1750."**

**Outline for a strong response:**

**Thesis**: The transatlantic slave trade expanded dramatically due to the convergence of three primary causes: the collapse of indigenous American populations through disease, which eliminated the original colonial labor force; the enormous profitability of sugar cultivation, which demanded intensive labor; and the commercial and military systems that made African enslavement logistically feasible and economically rational for European colonizers.

**Body paragraphs**:
1. *Indigenous population collapse*: Disease (especially smallpox) killed 50–90% of indigenous Americans within a century, eliminating the original labor force. Survivors could escape into familiar territory and social networks. Europeans could not use indigenous labor reliably.

2. *Sugar's labor demands*: Sugar cultivation was uniquely labor-intensive. Processing sugarcane required year-round work, dangerous conditions, and constant supervision. Plantation owners lost 5–10% of their labor force per year. The profitability of sugar in European markets made massive labor investment worthwhile.

3. *The logistics of African enslavement*: Africans transported across the Atlantic found themselves in unfamiliar territory without social networks for escape, unable to blend into the indigenous population. Some African coastal states (Dahomey, Kongo) participated in slave raiding and trading, providing a supply chain. The legal infrastructure of chattel slavery made enslaved people permanent, heritable property — more economically efficient (from the colonizer's perspective) than time-limited indentured servitude.

### Prompt 2 (CCOT)
**"Evaluate the extent to which the economic systems of maritime empires changed in the period 1450–1750."**

**Outline for a strong response:**

**Thesis**: Maritime empires transformed European economic systems significantly during this period, shifting from state-directed mercantilism toward investor-driven capitalism through joint-stock companies, triangular trade, and the integration of American silver — while traditional regional markets and coercive labor systems continued alongside the new global economy.

**Change arguments**:
- Joint-stock companies (EIC 1600, VOC 1602) democratized investment and distributed risk, creating new financial institutions
- The Atlantic System created the first truly global trade circuit, connecting three continents
- Silver tripling the global money supply was unprecedented in scale
- The Dutch stock exchange (1602) and Bank of Amsterdam (1609) were genuinely new financial institutions

**Continuity arguments**:
- Coercive labor systems continued — serfdom persisted in Russia; new forms were added in the Americas
- Regional markets in Afro-Eurasia continued functioning alongside global trade
- Mercantilism remained the dominant state economic theory throughout most of the period
- States continued to intervene heavily in trade through monopolies and charters

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Strategies for Unit 4

DBQs for this period often use documents about:
- The Columbian Exchange and its effects on different groups
- Labor systems (defenses of slavery, indigenous testimony about encomienda)
- European exploration and its justifications
- Responses to European expansion (Japanese edicts, indigenous accounts, African rulers' letters)

**Key skills for Unit 4 DBQs**:

1. **Identify perspective and purpose**: A conquistador's account of conquest has a different bias than an Aztec poem about the same events. Spanish administrators writing about the encomienda have different interests than an indigenous community subject to it.

2. **Explain historical context**: Always explain *why* the document was written — what was happening in the world that made this document necessary or relevant?

3. **Use outside evidence**: Know specific facts from beyond the documents — populations, dates, key figures — that you can use to support your argument.

4. **Make a sophisticated argument**: Go beyond "some documents support X, some support Y." Argue about the extent of change, the causes of events, or comparisons between groups.

> [!exam-tip]
> The most common mistake on Unit 4 DBQs is writing a document "grocery list" — summarizing each document without making an argument. Always state your thesis in the first paragraph and connect every document to that thesis. The documents are evidence, not the argument itself.
