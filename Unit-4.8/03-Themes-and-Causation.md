# Themes and Causation — Unit 4.8: (4.8): Continuity and Change

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

## What Changed Most Significantly

The most fundamental change of the period was the **integration of the Western Hemisphere** into the Afro-Eurasian world for the first time, creating a genuinely global network of exchange, disease, and migration. This single development produced cascading consequences: demographic collapse in the Americas, the Atlantic slave trade, the global silver economy, new racial hierarchies, and new forms of commercial capitalism. No previous period of AP World History had produced comparable disruption across as many regions simultaneously.

A second major change was the **racialization of slavery** — the development of heritable, race-based chattel slavery that was qualitatively different from the coerced labor systems of earlier periods. This created social structures whose legacies persist into the present.

A third major change was the **emergence of new institutional forms** — the joint-stock company, the stock exchange, double-entry accounting, and proto-capitalist financial practices — that would eventually evolve into modern capitalism.

---

## What Remained Continuous

Several features of the world remained substantially unchanged across this period. **Long-distance trade** had existed for millennia before 1450 and continued afterward; what changed were the routes, the commodities, and the degree of global integration. **Coerced labor** — slavery, serfdom, tribute labor — was not new in 1450 and continued in various forms after 1750. **State competition** for resources, territory, and prestige drove events in this period as in all others. **Gender inequality** persisted across all societies, though its specific forms were transformed by colonial encounter.

---

## Comparing Unit 4 to Adjacent Periods

Unit 4 is best understood in relation to what came before and after. Compared to Unit 3 (1200–1450), Unit 4 added the Americas to the existing Afro-Eurasian exchange system — a qualitative change in kind, not just degree. Compared to Unit 5 (1750–1900), Unit 4 was the *foundation* for industrialization, revolution, and imperialism: the capital, the labor systems, and the commercial networks of Unit 4 made the transformations of Unit 5 possible.

> [!exam-tip]
> A strong CCOT thesis for Unit 4 might read: *"While long-distance trade networks and coerced labor systems showed significant continuity from earlier periods, the period 1450–1750 witnessed transformative changes including the integration of the Western Hemisphere, the racialization of slavery, and the emergence of proto-capitalist institutions that collectively created the foundations of the modern global economy."*
