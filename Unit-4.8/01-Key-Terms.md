# Key Terms — Unit 4.8: (4.8): Continuity and Change

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

1. **CCOT (Continuity and Change Over Time)** — An AP essay format asking students to analyze what changed and what stayed the same across a given time period. (Core skill for Unit 4 essays; requires identifying *both* continuity and change, not just listing changes.)

2. **Globalization (early modern)** — The process by which the world's regions became increasingly interconnected through trade, migration, and cultural exchange. (Period 4 saw the first genuinely *global* connections — including the Americas — though earlier networks (Silk Roads, Indian Ocean) already connected much of the Afro-Eurasian world.)

3. **World-systems theory** — The analytical framework (developed by Immanuel Wallerstein) describing the global economy as divided into a dominant "core" (Western Europe) and an exploited "periphery" (colonies). (Useful lens for understanding how colonial trade created lasting global inequality, though the theory has limitations in explaining African and Asian agency.)

4. **Periodization** — The practice of dividing history into distinct eras based on significant changes. (Understanding *why* historians mark 1450 and 1750 as boundaries for this period is itself an AP skill — what was new enough in 1450 to mark a break? What changed after 1750 enough to begin a new period?)

5. **Continuity** — Historical elements that persist across a period of apparent change. (Examples for Unit 4: long-distance trade continued; coerced labor persisted; gender inequality persisted; states competed for power.)

6. **Transformation** — A change so significant it creates a qualitatively different situation. (Examples for Unit 4: integration of the Western Hemisphere, racial chattel slavery, global silver economy, joint-stock capitalism.)

7. **Agency** — The capacity of individuals or groups to make choices and shape their own historical circumstances. (Non-European peoples are not just victims of this period — Indigenous peoples resisted, African rulers made choices, Asian merchants adapted. AP essays earn complexity credit for demonstrating agency.)

8. **Comparative advantage** — The economic principle that states/regions should specialize in what they produce most efficiently. (Europe's "advantage" in this period was geographic and institutional, not cultural or racial — a key corrective to Eurocentric historical interpretations.)

9. **Columbian Exchange (as CCOT concept)** — The biological transformation of the globe as a result of 1492, the most significant *change* in this period's environmental history. (Continuity: disease had always traveled with humans; what changed was the scale and the total biological isolation of the Americas before contact.)

10. **Eurocentrism** — The tendency to interpret history from a primarily European perspective, overemphasizing European agency and underestimating the contributions and agency of non-European peoples. (AP essays that demonstrate understanding of non-European perspectives earn higher scores; avoid framing the period as simply "Europeans did things to the world.")

11. **Proto-capitalism** — The early forms of capitalist practice (joint-stock companies, global trade, wage labor) that developed in this period before the full industrialization of capitalism in the 19th century. (Unit 4 is when capitalist institutions emerge; they mature in Units 5–6.)

> [!exam-tip]
> For any CCOT essay on this period, identify your **turning point** — the single most significant moment of change. Strong essays argue that 1492 (Columbian Exchange), 1602 (VOC founding), or 1619 (chattel slavery in Virginia) was the critical threshold, and explain *why*.
