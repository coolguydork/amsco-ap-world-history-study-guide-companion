# Topic 2.7 — Practice and Review

## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

Which of the following best describes a similarity among the Silk Roads, the Indian Ocean trade network, and the trans-Saharan trade routes during c. 1200–c. 1450?

A) All three networks were controlled by a single imperial power that standardized trade regulations  
B) All three networks relied primarily on maritime technology such as the lateen sail and astrolabe  
C) All three networks depended on stable political entities to protect merchants and routes, and all generated wealthy trading cities as nodes of exchange  
D) All three networks spread exclusively the same set of luxury goods — silk, spices, and gold

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> This is the core similarity tested in Topic 2.7: all three networks required political stability (Mongols, Mali, Malacca) and all generated major trading cities (Samarkand, Calicut, Timbuktu) as hubs of exchange. A is wrong — no single empire controlled all three. B is wrong — maritime tech applied only to the Indian Ocean (and partially to Silk Roads sea extensions), not trans-Saharan. D is wrong — each network had distinct goods.

**Question 2**

Which of the following best represents a key difference between the Silk Roads and the Indian Ocean trade network during c. 1200–c. 1450?

A) Only the Silk Roads spread Islam to new regions  
B) The Indian Ocean network moved heavier bulk goods using monsoon-dependent maritime technology, while the Silk Roads specialized in lighter luxury goods transported overland by camel caravan  
C) Only the Indian Ocean network was protected by a stable political authority  
D) The Silk Roads relied entirely on Chinese merchants, while the Indian Ocean was dominated by Arab traders

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> This captures the most important structural difference between the two networks: the Indian Ocean moved bulk goods (cotton, iron, grain) that overland routes could not practically carry, using maritime technology timed to monsoon winds. The Silk Roads used camels for lighter luxury goods. A is wrong — Islam spread via both networks (and trans-Saharan routes). C is wrong — both had political patrons. D overstates exclusivity; Chinese merchants were active in both.

**Question 3**

The presence of cowrie shells (from the Indian Ocean) as currency in the Mali Empire during the 14th century most directly suggests that:

A) Mali was a maritime power with direct access to Indian Ocean trade  
B) West African trade networks were indirectly linked to the Indian Ocean trade system through trans-Saharan routes  
C) The trans-Saharan trade routes were controlled by Indian Ocean merchants  
D) West African kingdoms had abandoned gold as a currency in favor of imported goods

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Cowrie shells originated in the Indian Ocean but circulated in West Africa as currency — evidence that goods flowed between the Indian Ocean world and sub-Saharan Africa through the trans-Saharan network. Mali was not a maritime power (A). C misidentifies who controlled trans-Saharan routes (Arab/Berber merchants, not Indian Ocean traders). D is wrong — gold remained West Africa's primary export commodity.

**Question 4**

Which of the following social patterns was common to all three major trade networks during c. 1200–c. 1450?

A) Women dominated commercial exchange in markets across Afro-Eurasia  
B) The demand for goods generated demand for labor, which included enslaved people as one form among several  
C) All societies in contact with trade networks adopted similar caste-based social hierarchies  
D) Trade networks eliminated traditional social distinctions by creating new merchant classes equal in status to nobles

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The slave trade was active on both the Indian Ocean routes and the trans-Saharan routes in this period, and the Silk Roads also facilitated some slave movement. The demand for labor — including coerced labor — was a common social feature of expanding trade. A is wrong — this applied mainly to Southeast Asian women, not universally. C is wrong — social hierarchies varied widely. D is wrong — merchants typically ranked below aristocrats in most societies (especially Confucian China).

## LEQ Practice

**Prompt:** "Evaluate the extent to which the three major Afro-Eurasian trade networks of c. 1200–c. 1450 were similar in their causes, organization, and effects."

**Planning your response:**

A high-scoring LEQ on this prompt would:

1. **Write a defensible thesis** that makes a historically specific claim about the *degree* of similarity — not just "they were both similar and different" but an actual judgment. Example: "Although the three networks differed significantly in their transportation technologies and regional goods, they were fundamentally similar in their dependence on political stability, their generation of wealthy trading cities, and their role as conduits for the spread of Islam."

2. **Provide at least two pieces of specific evidence** for similarities and at least one for a meaningful difference (to show complexity).

3. **Use historical reasoning** — here, *comparison* is the primary skill, but you can also use *causation* (what caused the similarity?) and *CCOT* (did similarities increase or decrease over time?).

**Evidence bank for similarities:**

- Mongol Empire → Pax Mongolica on Silk Roads; Mali Empire → taxation on trans-Saharan routes; Malacca → navy protecting Indian Ocean strait. All show political stability enabling trade.
- Samarkand, Kashgar (Silk Roads); Calicut, Kilwa, Malacca (Indian Ocean); Timbuktu, Cairo (trans-Saharan). All show trading city growth.
- Islam spread via all three networks through merchant activity, not primarily by conquest.

**Evidence bank for differences:**

- Transport: camels vs. dhows/junks vs. camels (again, but Sahara-specific)
- Goods: silk/porcelain vs. spices/cotton/gold vs. gold/salt/ivory
- Currency: paper money (China) vs. tin ingots (SE Asia) vs. cowrie shells (West Africa)
- Scale: Indian Ocean was intercontinental; trans-Saharan was regional; Silk Roads were intermediate

**Complexity point options:**

- Note that the networks were *interconnected*, not isolated. Cowrie shells from the Indian Ocean circulated in West Africa. Chinese porcelain appears in Swahili city ruins. Zheng He's voyages touched the East African coast. This interconnection complicates a clean comparison.
- Acknowledge that similarities increased during the Pax Mongolica (c. 1250–c. 1350) as Mongol stability temporarily linked the Silk Roads more tightly to both the Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan networks.

## CCOT Prompt

**"Describe ONE continuity and ONE change in the social consequences of Afro-Eurasian trade networks between c. 1200 and c. 1450."**

**Continuity**: Across the entire period, trade networks continued to reinforce and depend on forms of coerced labor — including enslaved people traded along both the Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan routes. The use of enslaved people as one form of labor in long-distance commerce persisted without fundamental change.

**Change**: The volume and cultural reach of trade created new diasporic communities that gradually altered the social composition of trading cities. Port cities like Calicut, Malacca, and the Swahili city-states shifted from relatively homogeneous communities to cosmopolitan ones with resident Muslim, Chinese, Hindu, and African populations — a significant social change driven by the intensification of trade after 1200.

## Common Misconceptions

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: The three trade networks were entirely separate systems. In reality, they were interconnected. Goods, people, and ideas flowed between them. The Swahili Coast was a node connecting the Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan systems. Zheng He's voyages linked Chinese merchants to East African ports. Think of the three networks as overlapping circles, not separate boxes.

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: Trade networks only moved luxury goods. The Indian Ocean moved bulk goods (cotton, iron, grain) that were too heavy for overland transport. The trans-Saharan routes moved salt — a dietary necessity, not a luxury. Only the Silk Roads specialized predominantly in lighter luxury items.

> [!warning]
> **Misconception**: The biggest difference between the networks was which religion they spread. In fact, all three spread Islam. The more meaningful differences are transportation technology, goods, and geographic scope.
