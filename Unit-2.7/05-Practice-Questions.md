# Unit 2.7: Practice Questions — Comparison of Economic Exchange

#apwh #unit-2 #period-1

## Multiple Choice Questions

**Question 1**
*"The city of Kilwa, which the Moroccan traveler Ibn Battuta visited in 1331, was one of the most beautiful and well-constructed cities in the world. Its inhabitants were black-skinned, pious Muslims… engaged in commerce with merchants from India and Arabia."*
*(Adapted from Ibn Battuta, Rihla, c. 1355)*

Which of the following best explains why Kilwa was a thriving commercial center in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Kilwa was located along the overland Silk Roads connecting China to the Mediterranean
B) Kilwa's position on the East African coast made it a key node in the Indian Ocean monsoon trade network
C) The Mongol Empire had extended its political control to East Africa, providing security for merchants
D) The Portuguese had established Kilwa as a trading post to facilitate their African exploration

**Correct Answer: B**
*Kilwa's wealth derived from its role as an entrepôt in the Indian Ocean network, exporting gold and ivory from the interior while importing Indian textiles and Chinese porcelain — driven by monsoon wind patterns, not overland routes or Mongol or European control.*

**Question 2**
A historian studying long-distance trade networks in the period c. 1200–1450 notes that the Indian Ocean network was "self-organizing" while the Silk Roads were "politically dependent." Which of the following best supports this distinction?

A) Indian Ocean merchants used no financial instruments, while Silk Road merchants relied on state-issued currency
B) The Indian Ocean network continued functioning after the Mongol decline, while Silk Road commerce collapsed when Mongol political authority fragmented
C) Only the Indian Ocean network transmitted Islam, while the Silk Roads were dominated by Buddhist merchants
D) Indian Ocean trade was controlled by Chinese state-sponsored fleets, while Silk Road caravans were private enterprises

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Indian Ocean network was sustained by decentralized merchant communities and Islamic commercial law rather than state authority; the Silk Roads required Mongol political stability and declined sharply when the Mongol Empire fragmented after the mid-14th century.*

**Question 3**
The Trans-Saharan trade network and the Indian Ocean trade network were similar in that both:

A) Relied primarily on maritime technology to move goods
B) Were controlled by a single dominant empire throughout the period c. 1200–1450
C) Served as primary corridors for the spread of Islam into new regions
D) Connected China directly to sub-Saharan Africa

**Correct Answer: C**
*Islam spread into West and East Africa primarily through the Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean networks respectively — via Muslim merchants and Sufi missionaries, not conquest.*

**Question 4**
Which of the following most directly explains why merchants on the Indian Ocean network could trade effectively without political protection from a powerful empire?

A) Indian Ocean merchants carried weapons that deterred piracy
B) Islamic commercial law provided a shared legal framework that allowed merchants of different origins to enforce contracts
C) Chinese naval power policed the Indian Ocean and guaranteed merchant safety
D) The monsoon winds limited competition by restricting which ports could participate

**Correct Answer: B**
*Islamic commercial law (sharia) provided enforceable contract standards across the Indian Ocean world, substituting for the state authority that the Silk Roads depended on — a key structural difference between the two networks.*

**Question 5**
*[Map showing Silk Roads, Indian Ocean routes, and Trans-Saharan routes, with arrows indicating direction of luxury goods flow]*

A student analyzing this map argues that "all three networks primarily moved the same categories of goods." Which of the following best challenges this argument?

A) Only the Silk Roads carried silk; the other networks did not trade textiles of any kind
B) The Trans-Saharan network was uniquely built around two essential commodities (gold and salt) rather than prestige luxury goods alone
C) The Indian Ocean network moved only spices, while the Silk Roads moved only porcelain
D) The Trans-Saharan network was the only one that transported enslaved people

**Correct Answer: B**
*Gold and salt were functional necessities (currency metal; food preservation) rather than prestige luxuries like silk or spices — the Trans-Saharan network had a distinct commodity structure. Option D is wrong because enslaved people moved on all three networks.*

**Question 6**
The decline of the Silk Roads after c. 1350 was most directly caused by:

A) The Black Death, which killed all Central Asian merchants
B) Chinese naval power redirecting all trade to the Indian Ocean
C) The fragmentation of the Mongol Empire, which had provided the political security that overland trade required
D) The discovery of the Americas, which created new trade opportunities

**Correct Answer: C**
*The Pax Mongolica was the political foundation of sustained Silk Road commerce; when the four khanates fragmented and competed, the safety of overland routes collapsed and merchants shifted to maritime alternatives.*

**Question 7**
Which of the following best explains why the social status of merchants differed between China and the Islamic world in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Chinese merchants were wealthier than Islamic merchants, which created social envy
B) Confucian philosophy ranked merchants at the bottom of the social hierarchy, while Islamic tradition honored the merchant profession
C) Chinese merchants controlled the Indian Ocean network, giving them political power that Islamic merchants lacked
D) Islamic merchants were mostly enslaved people with no social standing

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Prophet Muhammad was a merchant, and Islamic tradition explicitly values trade; Confucianism placed merchants below scholars, farmers, and artisans — this ideological difference shaped how each civilization organized commercial activity.*

**Question 8**
Zheng He's seven voyages (1405–1433) were significant in the context of comparing trade networks because they demonstrated that:

A) China intended to permanently dominate the Indian Ocean and replace Arab merchants
B) The Chinese state had the maritime capacity to project power throughout the Indian Ocean but chose political retrenchment over commercial expansion
C) Chinese merchants had been absent from the Indian Ocean before the voyages
D) The Indian Ocean network had collapsed and needed Chinese intervention to restart

**Correct Answer: B**
*The voyages proved China's maritime capacity was immense, but the decision to stop them was political (Confucian officials opposed the expense and commercial orientation) — a key comparison with contemporary European states that were expanding rather than contracting their maritime ambitions.*

**Question 9**
A historian argues that "diasporic merchant communities were the essential human infrastructure of Indian Ocean trade." Which of the following best supports this argument?

A) Arab, Indian, and Chinese merchants settled permanently in foreign ports, maintaining trade networks through shared culture and legal systems rather than state authority
B) The Mongol Empire required all Indian Ocean merchants to register with state authorities
C) Diasporic communities replaced local merchants, eliminating indigenous participation in Indian Ocean trade
D) Indian Ocean trade would have collapsed without Chinese imperial sponsorship of merchant settlements

**Correct Answer: A**
*The Indian Ocean's decentralized commercial model depended on trust networks within diasporic communities — Arab merchants in Indian ports, Indian merchants in Southeast Asian cities, Chinese merchants in the Spice Islands — held together by shared commercial law and family ties, not state control.*

**Question 10**
Which of the following is the most significant similarity between the Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean trade networks in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Both were dominated by Chinese state-sponsored trading fleets
B) Both relied exclusively on maritime technology rather than overland routes
C) Both transmitted Islam into new regions primarily through the activities of Muslim merchants and missionaries
D) Both were disrupted by the Mongol Empire's expansion

**Correct Answer: C**
*Islam spread into West Africa via Trans-Saharan Muslim merchants and into East Africa and Southeast Asia via Indian Ocean Muslim merchants — the single most important parallel between the two networks. The Mongols disrupted the Silk Roads but had limited effect on Trans-Saharan or Indian Ocean commerce.*

## Short Answer Questions

### SAQ 1

**Prompt:** Use the following evidence to answer parts A, B, and C.

*"In the lands of Mali, the traveler is secure from theft. The king does not allow anyone to oppress others. Gold and salt are the chief articles of commerce; the salt comes from Taghaza in the desert and the gold from Bambuk and Bure."* — Al-Umari, describing Mali c. 1337

**A)** Explain ONE way the Trans-Saharan trade network shaped political authority in West Africa in the period c. 1200–1450.

**B)** Explain ONE similarity between the Trans-Saharan trade network and the Indian Ocean trade network in the same period.

**C)** Explain ONE difference between how the Trans-Saharan network and the Silk Roads related to state power in the period c. 1200–1450.

**Top-score guidance:**
- A: Focus on how gold tax revenue funded Mali's military and administration; how Islam spread along the network and gave rulers religious legitimacy through connection to the wider Islamic world
- B: Both transmitted Islam; both relied on merchant diasporas; both moved luxury goods; both were decentralized relative to the Silk Roads
- C: Trans-Saharan — state (Mali) taxed but didn't control the crossing; Silk Roads — entirely dependent on Mongol political security and infrastructure

### SAQ 2

**Prompt:** Answer parts A, B, and C.

**A)** Explain ONE way the physical environment shaped how each of the three major trade networks (Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, Trans-Saharan) functioned differently.

**B)** Explain ONE way cultural diffusion along trade networks operated similarly across all three networks in the period c. 1200–1450.

**C)** Explain ONE reason why the Indian Ocean network was more resilient than the Silk Roads during periods of political disruption in the period c. 1200–1450.

**Top-score guidance:**
- A: Silk Roads — steppe and desert terrain required camels and Mongol political security; Indian Ocean — monsoon winds created predictable seasonal sailing; Trans-Saharan — Sahara required specialized dromedary technology and Berber geographic knowledge
- B: Islam spread along all three; merchants served as cultural brokers; diasporic communities created hybrid cultures at commercial nodes
- C: Indian Ocean was self-organizing through Islamic commercial law and diasporic trust networks — no single political entity could collapse the whole system; contrast with Silk Roads' Mongol dependence

## Long Essay Question

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which the three major trade networks of c. 1200–1450 (Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, Trans-Saharan) were more similar than different in their causes, characteristics, and consequences.

**Top-score guidance:**
- **Thesis:** Take a clear, defensible position on the extent of similarity vs. difference — don't just say "both similar and different." A strong thesis might argue: "While the three networks shared important characteristics — all transmitted Islam, all relied on luxury goods, all produced diasporic communities — they differed more fundamentally in their physical mechanisms, their relationship to state power, and their vulnerability to political disruption."
- **Evidence (at least three specific examples per side):**
  - Similarities: all spread Islam; all moved luxury goods; all produced cultural synthesis; all were vulnerable to plague
  - Differences: physical mechanisms (camel vs. dhow vs. camel); state involvement (Mongol-dependent vs. decentralized vs. Mali-taxed); post-1350 trajectories (Silk Roads collapsed; Indian Ocean continued; Trans-Saharan continued under Songhai)
- **Complexity:** Address how the networks were interconnected — plague traveled from Silk Roads into Mediterranean maritime networks; gold from Trans-Saharan financed Mediterranean commerce connected to Silk Roads; no network was truly independent
- **Causation:** Explain WHY differences emerged — geography determined mechanisms; ideology (Confucian vs. Islamic merchant attitudes) shaped organization; political structures (Mongol empire vs. Islamic commercial law) determined resilience
