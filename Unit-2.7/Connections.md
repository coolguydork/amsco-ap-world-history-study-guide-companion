# Connections — Topic 2.7

> [!tip]
> Topic 2.7 is the **capstone of Unit 2** — it synthesizes every prior topic in the unit into a comparative framework. Every Unit 2 topic feeds directly into 2.7, and every Unit 3–4 topic builds on the trade networks established here.

---

## Builds On (Unit 2 Topics)

Topic 2.7 is explicitly a comparison of Topics 2.1–2.6. For each network, the corresponding topic note is the source of detail:

- [[Unit-2.1/Silk Roads Overview|Silk Roads Overview]] — The overland luxury-goods network; camels, caravanserais, Buddhism, [[Concept Notes/Neo-Confucianism|Neo-Confucianism]], and Islam. The Pax Mongolica is its high point.
- [[Unit-2.2/Mongol Empire Overview|Mongol Empire Overview]] — How the Mongols created the security environment that made peak Silk Roads trade possible; the historiographical debate about Genghis Khan's legacy.
- [[Unit-2.3/Indian Ocean Overview|Indian Ocean Overview]] — The monsoon-powered maritime network; dhows, junks, stern rudders, lateen sails, astrolabes; the spread of Islam to Southeast Asia; Swahili city-states on the East African coast.
- [[Unit-2.4/Trans-Saharan Trade Routes Overview|Trans-Saharan Trade Routes Overview]] — The gold-salt exchange; camel caravans; Mali Empire; the spread of Islam to sub-Saharan Africa; Timbuktu as intellectual center.
- [[Unit-2.5/Cultural Consequences Overview|Cultural Consequences Overview]] — How travel across all three networks spread religions, artistic styles, technologies, and ideas; [[Concept Notes/Syncretism|Syncretism]] as a result.
- [[Unit-2.6/Environmental Consequences Overview|Environmental Consequences Overview]] — How trade amplified environmental pressures: the [[Concept Notes/Black Death|Black Death]] as trade's catastrophic downside; deforestation and soil erosion from growing populations.

## Leads To (Units 3–4)

The trade networks of 1200–1450 directly shape the empires and conflicts of Units 3–4. The key causal chains are:

- **Maritime shift → European exploration.** As the Indian Ocean's volume surpassed overland routes, and as Ottoman expansion disrupted access for European merchants, European states funded oceanic exploration to find direct sea routes to Asian markets. This is the direct origin of the Age of Exploration in Unit 4.
- **Silk Roads decline → Ottoman Empire's rise.** The Ottoman Empire (Unit 3) positioned itself to control what remained of the overland routes, making Istanbul one of the world's great commercial cities.
- **Trans-Saharan gold → West African empires.** The Mali Empire (Unit 2.4) gives way to the Songhai Empire (Unit 3) along the same trade corridor; the gold-salt trade continues to fund state-building in West Africa.
- **Indian Ocean network → early modern empires.** Portuguese, Dutch, and eventually English trading empires in Unit 4 are built on top of the Indian Ocean network's existing commercial infrastructure — routes, ports, and trading relationships that had operated for centuries.
