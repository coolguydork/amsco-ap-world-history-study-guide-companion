# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 2.7

> [!tip]
> This timeline covers the *comparative* arc of all three networks. Use it to build CCOT arguments about which routes dominated when, and why they rose or fell.

---

## Deep Roots (Before c. 1200)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| c. 500 BCE | Silk Roads infrastructure established (Persian and Han foundations, caravanserais, mountain passes) | Overland network predates the period by over a millennium; 1200–1450 represents a revival, not an origin |
| c. 200 BCE | Indian Ocean trade established | Oldest of the three networks in terms of sustained use; connects India, Arabia, and East Africa long before 1200 |
| c. 800 CE | Trans-Saharan gold trade reaches peak visibility | West African gold enters Afro-Eurasian monetary systems; Ghana's wealth documented by Arab geographers |
| 7th–8th c. CE | Muslim merchants come to dominate all three networks | Islam becomes the common commercial thread linking Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, and trans-Saharan trade |

---

## The Core Period: c. 1200–1450

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1206–1368 | Pax Mongolica — Mongol unification of the Silk Roads | Overland network peaks; safer than any time since the Han–Roman era; trade volume surges |
| 1235–1468 | Mali Empire controls the trans-Saharan corridor | Islamic state-building organized around and funded by gold and salt trade; Timbuktu becomes a commercial and intellectual hub |
| c. 1200–1450 | Swahili city-states flourish (Kilwa, Mombasa) | Indian Ocean's East African terminus; parallel function to Silk Roads' oasis cities; connected to gold from Great Zimbabwe |
| c. 1296–1297 | Zhou Daguan observes Cambodian trade (Khmer Empire) | Chinese account documenting women's dominance of local markets in Southeast Asia |
| 1330s–1350s | Black Death spreads along all three networks | China suffers tens of millions of deaths; plague reaches Europe via the Silk Roads and Mediterranean; connectivity's catastrophic cost |
| 1347 | Black Death reaches Europe | At least one-third of Europe's population dies; Constantinople and other trading cities decline; demonstrates all three networks are linked |
| 1352 | Ibn Battuta visits Mali | His *Rihla* documents matrilineal inheritance and women's relative freedom in West Africa; primary source on trans-Saharan society |
| 1368 | Mongol Yuan dynasty falls in China | End of the Pax Mongolica; overland Silk Roads lose their security umbrella; maritime routes begin their ascent |
| 1402–1424 | Ming Emperor Yongle's reign | Context for Empress Xu's *Instructions for the Inner Quarters*; also the period of Zheng He's voyages |
| c. 1400 | Malacca founded | Sultanate controls Strait of Malacca; levies fees on passing ships; clearest example of trade → centralization → state power |
| 1417–1422 | Ulugh Beg Madrasa built in Samarkand | Trade wealth funds intellectual culture; Samarkand as Silk Roads center of learning |
| 1442 | Abd al-Razzaq visits Calicut | *Description of Calicut* opens Topic 2.7; documents the legal and commercial security of Indian Ocean trading cities |

---

## The Transition: c. 1450 Onward

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| c. 1450 | Ottoman expansion disrupts overland Silk Roads for European merchants | Accelerates European demand for oceanic routes; stage set for Portuguese and Spanish exploration |
| c. 1450 | Maritime Indian Ocean trade surpasses overland Silk Roads in volume | Key CCOT endpoint: the shift from land to sea that defines the early modern period |
| Post-1450 | European oceanic exploration begins | Directly connects to Unit 3's topics; explains why European powers sought sea routes to Asia |
