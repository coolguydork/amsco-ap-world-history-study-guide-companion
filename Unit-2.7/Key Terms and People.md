# Topic 2.7 — Key Terms and People

## Economic Concepts

**Proto-industrialization** — A period in which rural workers produce goods beyond local consumption using simple equipment, before the development of factory-based industry. China underwent proto-industrialization to meet growing demand for iron, steel, and porcelain. See [[Unit-1.1/Song Dynasty and East Asia]].

**Money economy** — An economic system in which transactions are conducted in currency rather than barter. All three trade networks eventually shifted toward metal coin-based money economies, with China leading the way through flying cash and paper money.

**Commodity currency** — When a widely valued good (silk, cowrie shells, salt) serves as a medium of exchange before standardized coins emerge. West Africa used cowrie shells; East Asia used silk; Southeast Asia used tin ingots.

**Standardized currency** — Coins or paper with a set value, accepted across a wide area. The shift toward standardized currency was a common feature of expanding trade networks — it sped up transactions and gave merchants a common way to measure value.

**Partnership** — Business arrangement for sharing investment risk; emerged along trade routes as a new commercial practice to manage the financial dangers of long-distance trade.

**Customs** — Taxes imposed on imported goods at ports and trading cities; a key revenue source for states like Malacca, Gujarat, and Mali. Gujarat's customs revenue was reportedly many times greater than the total wealth of some European states.

## Social Concepts

**Social hierarchy / caste** — Rigid social stratification systems that defined most societies during 1200–1450. Continued largely unchanged despite trade expansion; merchants often ranked low in formal hierarchies (Confucian China) even when they were economically powerful.

**Patriarchy** — A social system in which men hold primary power. Nearly universal across Afro-Eurasia in this period, though with notable exceptions (Mongol women, Southeast Asian marketplace women, European guild women).

**Debt bondage** — A form of coerced labor in which people work to pay off debts. One of the labor forms that persisted in this period alongside slavery and free farming.

**Large-scale labor organization** — The coordination of thousands of workers for infrastructure projects (irrigation, military defenses, buildings). Kinship networks often played a role in organizing such projects.

## Abdu Razzak's Calicut (1442)

> *"Wealthy merchants bring in big cargoes, which they unload and unhesitatingly send into the markets without thinking in the meantime of any security, or checking the account, or keeping watch over the goods."*

This quote from Abdu Razzak's "Description of Calicut" (1442) illustrates the **commercial trust** created by political stability. Merchants could leave goods unguarded because the state and social norms guaranteed security — a striking example of how governance enables commerce.

## Comparison: Currencies Across Trade Networks

| Region/Network | Currency Used |
|---------------|--------------|
| China | Paper money, flying cash, copper coins |
| East/Central Asia | Silk (sometimes as currency); later metal coins |
| Southeast Asia | Tin ingots |
| West Africa (Mali) | Cowrie shells, cotton cloth, gold, glass beads, salt |
| Mediterranean/Europe | Silver and gold coins; bills of exchange (later) |

The global trend during c. 1200–c. 1450 was toward standardized metal coin currencies, with China leading innovation via paper money. This shift enabled faster, more flexible long-distance trade.

## Key Comparison: Labor Systems

| Labor Type | Description | Where Common |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Free peasant farming | Independent farmers paying taxes/rents | Everywhere |
| Artisan/craft labor | Skilled producers in cottage industries | China, India, Islamic world |
| Debt bondage | Work to pay off debts | Widespread |
| Enslaved labor | Coerced labor through purchase/capture | Indian Ocean routes, trans-Saharan routes |
| Organized mass labor | Large-scale projects (irrigation, construction) | China, India, Islamic world |

> [!exam-tip]
> AP prompts sometimes ask about "continuity in labor systems" — the point is that all these labor forms *persisted* through the period, with slavery remaining significant on both Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan routes. The Atlantic slave trade after 1500 was qualitatively different in scale, but not a sudden invention.
