# Unit 2.7: Comparison of Economic Exchange

#apwh #unit-2 #period-1

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 2 — Networks of Exchange
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (c. 1200–1450)
**Key Theme:** Synthesis — Comparing the Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, and Trans-Saharan Networks

## Summary

Unit 2.7 is a synthesis topic that asks students to compare the three major trade networks of c. 1200–1450: the overland Silk Roads, the Indian Ocean maritime network, and the Trans-Saharan routes. Each network had distinct goods, participants, physical mechanisms, and relationships with states — yet all three shared common features: they transmitted Islam, spread luxury goods, enabled cultural diffusion, and were vulnerable to the same disruptions (Mongol conquest, plague, political collapse). Understanding these networks comparatively is essential for AP essay writing, as comparison is one of the most frequently tested historical thinking skills.

> [!exam-tip]
> Comparison questions on the AP exam often ask about similarities AND differences. For trade networks, a strong thesis might argue: "While all three networks transmitted Islam and luxury goods, they differed fundamentally in their physical mechanisms and the degree of state control — the Silk Roads depended on Mongol political security, the Indian Ocean on monsoon winds and decentralized merchant communities, and the Trans-Saharan on Saharan geography and West African state patronage."

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Compare the causes, characteristics, and consequences of the three major trade networks
- Explain what made each network distinctive in terms of goods, participants, and mechanisms
- Analyze how states interacted with each network differently
- Evaluate the relative importance of each network in facilitating global connectivity c. 1200–1450

## AP World History Key Concepts

- **KC-2.1.I** — The Silk Roads facilitated the exchange of goods, technologies, and diseases across Eurasia
- **KC-2.2.I** — The Mongol Empire facilitated Eurasian trade and cultural exchange
- **KC-2.3.I** — The Indian Ocean network transmitted goods, peoples, and ideas across a vast maritime system
- **KC-2.4.I** — The Trans-Saharan network connected West Africa to the Mediterranean world through commerce and Islam

## Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Silk Roads | Indian Ocean | Trans-Saharan |
|---------|-----------|--------------|---------------|
| **Primary goods** | Silk, porcelain, spices, paper | Spices, cotton textiles, porcelain, gold, ivory | Gold, salt, enslaved people, cloth |
| **Physical mechanism** | Overland; camel/horse caravans | Maritime; monsoon winds; dhows/junks | Overland; camel caravans across Sahara |
| **Key participants** | Central Asian nomads, Chinese merchants, Muslim traders | Arab, Indian, Malay, Chinese, Swahili merchants | Berber/Tuareg traders, West African merchants, North African Muslims |
| **State involvement** | High (Mongol protection) | Low (decentralized; Islamic commercial law) | Medium (Mali state-sponsored) |
| **Religion transmitted** | Buddhism, Islam, Christianity | Islam (primary), Hinduism, Buddhism | Islam (primary) |
| **Vulnerability** | Political — depended on Mongol stability | Climate — monsoon reliability | Geographic — Sahara crossing danger |
