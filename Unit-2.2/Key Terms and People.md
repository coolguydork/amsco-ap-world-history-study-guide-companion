# Topic 2.2 — Key Terms and People

## Key People

**Genghis Khan (Temujin, 1162–1227)** — Unified Mongol clans in 1206 at the *kuriltai*; launched conquests reaching from the North China Sea to eastern Persia by 1227. Known for brutal warfare, but also religious tolerance and infrastructure-building in peacetime. Arguably the most consequential conqueror in human history by territory gained.

**Batu (d. 1255)** — Genghis Khan's grandson; leader of the **Golden Horde** that invaded Russia in 1236 and reached Poland and Hungary in 1241. His withdrawal to attend Ogodei Khan's funeral may have saved Western Europe from Mongol conquest.

**Hulegu (c. 1217–1265)** — Genghis Khan's grandson; destroyed the Abbasid Caliphate (Baghdad, 1258); established the **Il-khanate** in Persia. Stopped by the Mamluks at the Battle of Ain Jalut (1260). Eventually converted to Islam.

**Kublai Khan (1215–1294)** — Greatest grandson of Genghis Khan; Great Khan from 1260; conquered Song Dynasty China (completed 1271); established the **Yuan Dynasty**; patronized trade and welcomed foreigners including Marco Polo. His reign represents both the peak and beginning decline of Mongol power.

**Zhu Yuanzhang (Hongwu Emperor, 1328–1398)** — Buddhist monk from a peasant family who overthrew the Yuan Dynasty in 1368 and founded the Ming Dynasty — a reminder that the mightiest empires can fall to unexpected challengers.

## Key Places

**Karakorum** — Capital of the Mongol Empire, established by Genghis Khan in central Mongolia. Center of governance, scholarship, and religious diversity during the Pax Mongolica.

**Gobi Desert** — Arid desert north of China; the geographic homeland of the Mongols and a formidable barrier that shaped their nomadic culture.

**Moscow** — Russian city-state that collected extra Mongol tributes to build an anti-Mongol army; eventually led the coalition that defeated the Golden Horde at the Battle of Kulikovo (1380). Foundation of the modern Russian state.

**Dadu (Beijing)** — Rebuilt Mongol capital in China under Kublai Khan, on the ruins of Zhongdu.

## Key Concepts

**Khan** — A Mongol (or Central Asian Turkic) word for ruler or king.

**Kuriltai** — A gathering of Mongol clan leaders to elect a new Great Khan; the democratic legitimation ritual that Temujin used in 1206 to become Genghis Khan.

**Khanate** — A kingdom or territory ruled by a khan. The Mongol Empire eventually fragmented into four major khanates: the Golden Horde (Russia/Western steppe), the Il-khanate (Persia/Middle East), the Chagatai Khanate (Central Asia), and the Yuan Dynasty (China).

**Pax Mongolica** (c. 1250–c. 1350) — "Mongolian Peace." The period of relative stability across Eurasia under Mongol rule, during which trade flourished along the Silk Roads and cultural exchange intensified. The same roads that enabled trade also transmitted the bubonic plague.

**Il-khanate** — Mongol khanate in Persia/Central Asia under Hulegu and his descendants; stretched from Byzantium to the Oxus River. Eventually became Islamic.

**Golden Horde** — Batu's khanate in Russia and the western steppes; ruled Russia through indirect means for ~250 years.

**Yuan Dynasty (1271–1368)** — Kublai Khan's Mongol-ruled China; replaced Song Dynasty. Fell to the Ming Dynasty in 1368.

**Ming Dynasty (1368–1644)** — Ethnic Chinese dynasty that replaced the Yuan; associated with Zheng He's voyages and eventually a turn toward isolationism. See [[Unit-2.3/Indian Ocean Exchange]].

## Technology: Warfare

**Siege weapons** — Technologies for attacking walled cities; the Mongols adopted and adapted these from conquered Chinese and Persian engineers. Included portable towers, catapults, and early cannon-like devices.

**Cannon** — Some historians argue the cannon was a Mongol synthesis: Chinese gunpowder + Muslim flamethrower technology + European bell-casting techniques. A product of the cultural mixing the Mongol conquests enabled.

---

## Culture: Writing and Society

**Uyghur alphabet** — The script system adapted (from a captured Uyghur scribe in 1204) to write the Mongolian language. Though a single unified script for the empire was never achieved, this alphabet is still used in Mongolia today.

**White Lotus Society** — Chinese secret religious society that organized resistance to Mongol rule in the 1350s; a key catalyst of the rebellion that brought down the Yuan Dynasty.

---

## Environment

**Gobi Desert** — Originating homeland of the Mongols; the arid steppes shaped their pastoral nomadic culture and bred the horsemanship and toughness that powered their conquests.

**Bubonic plague** — Bacterium (*Yersinia pestis*) spread by fleas on rodents; transmitted along Mongol trade and conquest routes from southern China to Central Asia and then Europe. See [[Unit-2.6/Environmental Consequences of Connectivity]].

## Person Comparison: Mongol Rulers

| Ruler | Region Conquered | Key Legacy |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| Genghis Khan | North China, Central Asia, eastern Persia | Unified Mongols; founded empire; Pax Mongolica |
| Batu | Russia, eastern Europe | Golden Horde; 250 years of Russian subjugation |
| Hulegu | Abbasid Caliphate, Persia | Destroyed Baghdad; Il-khanate; later converted to Islam |
| Kublai Khan | Song Dynasty China | Yuan Dynasty; trade patronage; received Marco Polo |

> [!tip]
> On the AP exam, if asked to compare Mongol rulers, focus on *what they did differently* as rulers (not just conquerors) — Kublai adopted Chinese practices, Hulegu converted to Islam. This shows how the Mongols were absorbed by the cultures they conquered over time.
