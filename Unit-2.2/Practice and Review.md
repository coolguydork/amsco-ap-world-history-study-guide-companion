# Topic 2.2 — Practice and Review

## Multiple Choice Practice

**Question 1**

Which of the following best describes the long-term impact of the Mongol conquest of Russia in the 13th century?

A) Russia rapidly modernized its economy by adopting Mongol banking and financial practices  
B) Russia's isolation from Western Europe for three centuries contributed to a distinct Russian cultural identity and the eventual rise of a centralized Russian state  
C) The Mongols directly governed Russia by replacing all existing rulers with Mongol administrators  
D) Russia joined the Mongol Empire as a willing partner, sharing in the benefits of the Pax Mongolica trade network

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Mongols used indirect rule in Russia — governing through existing princes who paid tribute. This isolation from Western Europe is the key long-term consequence. Russian identity and the centralized Muscovite state emerged partly *in response* to resisting the Mongols. A is wrong (no Mongol financial innovations transferred to Russia). C is wrong (indirect rule, not direct). D is wrong (Russia was a conquered, tribute-paying subject).

**Question 2**

The Pax Mongolica (c. 1250–c. 1350) is significant in world history primarily because it:

A) Established a lasting period of peace in which no major wars were fought across Eurasia  
B) Created conditions for greatly expanded trade and cultural exchange across Eurasia, while also enabling the spread of the bubonic plague  
C) Demonstrated that nomadic peoples could develop complex systems of writing and philosophy  
D) Prevented the spread of Islam by establishing religious tolerance across the Mongol Empire

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> The Pax Mongolica is significant for both its positive effects (trade revival, cultural exchange) and its catastrophic unintended consequence (plague transmission). A is wrong — there were still conflicts; the "peace" refers to the Silk Roads being safe, not zero warfare. C is not the primary historical significance. D is incorrect — Islam actually *spread* during this period.

**Question 3**

The primary reason Kublai Khan's Yuan Dynasty eventually lost power in China was:

A) A military defeat by a Japanese invasion force that exposed the dynasty's weakness  
B) Economic collapse caused by overextension of the Silk Roads trade network  
C) Growing Chinese alienation from Mongol rule, including the dismantling of the civil service exam and favoritism toward foreign officials  
D) The Mongols voluntarily chose to return to their nomadic lifestyle in the steppes

> [!tip] Answer: **C**
> The Yuan Dynasty alienated the scholar-gentry class (who depended on the civil service exam for status) by dismantling the exam and hiring foreigners. This, combined with failed military campaigns and the Black Death, eroded Chinese support for Mongol rule. A is partly true (Japan repelled Mongol invasions) but not the primary cause of the *fall* of the dynasty. B is not supported. D is incorrect.

## SAQ Practice

**SAQ Prompt:**

> *"Swarming like locusts over the face of the earth, they have brought terrible devastation to the eastern parts, laying it waste with fire and carnage."*
> — Matthew Paris, *Chronica Majora*, 1240

**Part A**: Briefly explain ONE way in which Matthew Paris's account of the Mongols is limited as historical evidence.

**Part B**: Briefly explain ONE positive consequence of Mongol expansion for Eurasian trade and communication in the 13th–14th centuries.

**Part C**: Briefly explain ONE negative consequence of Mongol expansion beyond the immediate military destruction.

> [!tip] Model Responses
> **Part A**: Matthew Paris was a Benedictine monk writing from England who had no firsthand contact with the Mongols. His account reflects Western European fear and propaganda, not direct observation. He cannot reliably describe events he did not witness, and his religious perspective would incline him toward portraying non-Christian conquerors as demonic.
>
> **Part B**: The Mongol Empire created the Pax Mongolica (c. 1250–1350), a period during which the Silk Roads from China to Persia were unified under a single political authority. The Mongols improved roads, punished bandits, and established a messenger relay system. As a result, trade volume and interregional cultural exchange — including the transfer of paper-making and printing technology from China to Europe — reached levels unseen since the Roman/Han era.
>
> **Part C**: The Mongol trade routes transmitted the bubonic plague (Black Death) from southern China to Central Asia and then to Europe, where it killed approximately one-third of the population between 1347 and 1351. The same networks that facilitated trade also provided pathways for the most devastating pandemic in premodern history.

## Comparison Prompt

**Compare and contrast the Mongol impact on Russia and on China.**

| Dimension | Russia | China |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Type of rule | Indirect — through Russian princes paying tribute | Direct — Yuan Dynasty; Kublai Khan as emperor |
| Cultural impact | Isolated Russia from Western Europe; shaped distinct Russian identity | Dismantled civil service exam; patronized Buddhism/Daoism; welcomed foreign merchants |
| Duration | ~250 years (1240–~1480s) | ~97 years (1271–1368) |
| How it ended | Moscow-led coalition defeat (Battle of Kulikovo, 1380) | White Lotus Society rebellion; Zhu Yuanzhang's Ming Dynasty (1368) |
| Long-term legacy | Foundation of centralized Russian state | Ming Dynasty's turn toward Chinese traditions and eventual isolationism |

## Causation Practice

**"The Mongol Empire had both positive and negative consequences for Eurasian societies between 1200 and 1450." Explain this claim using specific evidence.**

Strong responses should discuss:
- **Positive**: Pax Mongolica, road system, trade revival, cultural transfers (Islamic science to China; Arabic numerals and paper to Europe), religious tolerance
- **Negative**: Destruction of Baghdad and the Abbasid Caliphate, mass civilian deaths, Russia's isolation, Black Death transmission
- **Complexity**: The *same mechanisms* (trade routes, caravanserai) caused both trade expansion and plague spread — this is the kind of historical complexity that earns top scores

> [!exam-tip]
> On DBQ or LEQ prompts about the Mongols, always note that historians disagree about how to weigh the Mongols' destructive versus constructive legacies. Showing awareness of this historiographical debate demonstrates sophisticated historical thinking.
