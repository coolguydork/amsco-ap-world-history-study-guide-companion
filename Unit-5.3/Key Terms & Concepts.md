# Topic 5.3 — Key Terms & Concepts

**AMSCO:** pp. 297–301

---

## Technology

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Spinning jenny** | Machine (James Hargreaves, 1760s) that allowed spinning multiple threads simultaneously | Increased textile production speed; began mechanizing cottage industry |
| **Water frame** | Machine (Richard Arkwright, 1769) using waterpower to spin thread | More efficient than hand labor; moved textile production to large factories |
| **Factory system** | Centralized production in large buildings with machinery and paid wage laborers | Replaced cottage industry; Richard Arkwright called "father of the factory system" |
| **Interchangeable parts** | System (Eli Whitney, 1798) in which identical parts could replace broken ones | Led directly to division of labor and mass production |
| **Division of labor** | Workers specialize in one specific task in production | Lowered need for skilled labor; increased speed; basis of assembly line |
| **Specialization of labor** | Each worker performs one type of task | Synonym for division of labor |
| **Assembly line** | Production method where each worker performs one task as a product moves past | Henry Ford expanded this to manufacture Model T automobiles |
| **Steam engine** | Engine (improved by James Watt, 1765) using coal-fired steam to generate power | Replaced waterpower; mobile energy source; powered factories and eventually trains and ships |

## Agriculture

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Agricultural revolution** | Early 1700s improvements in farming productivity | Produced food surplus; freed labor for factories |
| **Crop rotation** | Rotating different crops in a field each year | Prevented soil exhaustion; increased yields |
| **Seed drill** | Device that placed seeds efficiently in designated spots | Reduced waste; increased food production |

## Economy & Society

| Term | Definition | Significance |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Industrial Revolution** | Dramatic shift from hand production to mechanized factory production, c. 1750–1900 | Reshaped society, economy, environment, and global power structures |
| **Industrialization** | The process of increasing mechanization of production and associated social changes | Originated in Britain; spread globally |
| **Cottage industry** | System in which merchants provided raw materials to workers (usually women) who produced goods at home | Gave women some independence; slow; replaced by factory system |
| **Putting-out system** | Synonym for cottage industry | Same as above |
| **Enclosure movement** | Government fencing off traditional shared farmland ("commons") for private use | Displaced small farmers → forced migration to cities → provided factory labor |
| **Capital** | Money available to invest in businesses | Essential for building factories; British capitalists had it from colonial profits and slave trade |
| **Fossil fuel** | Energy source derived from plant and animal remains (coal, petroleum) | Coal powered the first Industrial Revolution; petroleum powered the second |
