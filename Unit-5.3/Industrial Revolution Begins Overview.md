# Topic 5.3 — Industrial Revolution Begins: Overview

**AP Unit:** 5 — Revolutions | **Period:** 3 (c. 1750–c. 1900) | **AMSCO:** pp. 297–301

**Essential Question:** What factors contributed to and characterized industrialization in the period 1750–1900?

---

## What Was the Industrial Revolution?

The **Industrial Revolution** was a dramatic shift from hand production methods to machines, from rural agricultural economies to urban factory-based economies. It began in **Britain** in the mid-to-late 18th century and spread across Europe, North America, and eventually the world. Its effects — urbanization, new social classes, environmental degradation, and global economic inequality — shape the modern world.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam loves asking WHY the Industrial Revolution began in Britain specifically. Know at least four British advantages: coal, geography, capital (from slave trade), enclosure movement (labor supply), and legal protections for private property.

---

## Why Britain First?

Britain had a unique combination of advantages that no other country matched in the mid-1700s:

### Geographic Advantages
- Located on the Atlantic Ocean → easy to import raw materials and export finished goods
- Extensive natural network of rivers + publicly funded canals and harbors → cheap internal transport
- World's strongest naval and commercial fleet

### Mineral Resources
- Sitting atop **immense coal deposits** (South Wales, Yorkshire, Lancashire)
- Coal powered the steam engine → energy for factories, iron/steel production
- When the U.S. industrialized, coal-mining developed in West Virginia, Pennsylvania, and Kentucky

### Capital from Empire and Slave Trade
- British colonists had access to colonial resources (timber for ships, raw cotton from slave-labor plantations in the Americas)
- Accumulated wealth from the trans-Atlantic slave trade gave British capitalists **excess capital** (money to invest in new businesses)

> [!warning]
> Don't sanitize this: Britain's Industrial Revolution was partially funded by the profits of the Atlantic slave trade. This is a crucial connection the AP exam may test — and it connects to the decline of India's manufacturing (see [[Unit-5.4/Industrialization Spreads Overview]]).

### Legal Protection of Private Property
- Entrepreneurs needed assurance their businesses wouldn't be seized by government or rivals
- Britain offered these legal guarantees; not all nations did

### Labor Supply: The Enclosure Movement
- Traditional English farming allowed peasants to cultivate **"the commons"** (shared government land)
- **Enclosure movement**: government fenced off commons; exclusive use sold or given to wealthy landowners
- Small farmers became landless → forced to move to cities like Manchester and Liverpool
- These displaced farmers became the **urban industrial workforce**

## Pre-Industrial to Industrial: The Transition

Before factories, Britain used the **cottage industry** (putting-out system):
- Merchants provided raw cotton to women who spun and wove cloth in their own homes
- Gave women weavers some independence; kept them close to their children
- But: production was too slow to meet investor demand for cotton cloth

This demand for faster production drove **technological innovation**.

---

## Key Technologies

| Invention | Inventor | Date | Impact |
|-----------|----------|------|--------|
| Spinning jenny | James Hargreaves | 1760s | Allowed spinning multiple threads at once |
| Water frame | Richard Arkwright | 1769 | Used waterpower to spin; more efficient than hand labor; ended cottage industry |
| Interchangeable parts | Eli Whitney (U.S.) | 1798 | Broken parts could be replaced identically; led to division of labor |
| Steam engine (improved) | James Watt | 1765 | Harnessed coal to generate steam power for factories; within 50 years, powered trains |

**Richard Arkwright** is considered the "father of the factory system" — his water frame required large factories to house the machines, moving production from homes to centralized facilities.

**Division of labor** (from Whitney's interchangeable parts): workers specialize in one task rather than crafting an entire product. Henry Ford later expanded this with the **moving assembly line** (Model T automobiles).

---

## Agricultural Revolution: The Foundation

Before the Industrial Revolution could happen, Britain needed more food AND surplus labor. The early-1700s **agricultural revolution** provided both:

- **Crop rotation**: rotating different crops yearly → prevented soil exhaustion → increased yields
- **Seed drill**: efficiently placed seeds in designated spots → less waste → more food
- **Potato** (introduced from South America): more calories per acre → sustained larger populations
- Result: population grew (more food + improved medical care → lower infant mortality); **fewer people needed in agriculture** → surplus labor for factories

---

## Urbanization

The combination of the enclosure movement + factory jobs drew millions from rural areas to cities. Manchester and Liverpool grew explosively. By c. 1800, Britain had a network of industrial cities with factories, tenements, and pollution.
