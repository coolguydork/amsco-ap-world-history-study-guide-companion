## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the image description below for Questions 1–2.**
> *[Stimulus: A casta painting from 18th-century New Spain showing labeled family groups illustrating racial mixing: "De Español e India, Mestizo"; "De Español y Mestiza, Castizo"; "De Negro e India, Lobo," etc.]*

**1.** The casta paintings depicted above most directly reflect which of the following features of Spanish colonial society?

- A) The equal status of all racial groups under Spanish colonial law
- B) The Spanish attempt to categorize and legally regulate racial mixing
- C) The celebration of cultural diversity within Spanish colonial culture
- D) The dominance of Indigenous culture in colonial Latin American society

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Casta paintings were a visual representation of the Spanish colonial project of racial classification — they mapped and ranked racial mixtures, reflecting the legal and social hierarchy the casta system created.*

---

**2.** Which of the following best explains why the casta system became increasingly difficult to administer over time?

- A) The Spanish Crown lost interest in regulating colonial society
- B) The growing mestizo and mixed-race population blurred the racial categories the system was built on
- C) The Catholic Church opposed racial classification as incompatible with Christian equality
- D) Indigenous peoples successfully resisted being classified by Spanish authorities

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*As racial mixing produced ever more people of ambiguous heritage, the dozens of casta categories became harder to assign and enforce — the system's complexity reflected its own instability.*

---

**3.** The development of racial chattel slavery in the Americas differed most significantly from earlier forms of slavery in that:

- A) Earlier forms of slavery did not involve physical coercion
- B) Atlantic slavery was permanent, heritable, and determined entirely by race
- C) Earlier forms of slavery were more economically productive
- D) Atlantic slavery was limited to agricultural work, unlike earlier forms

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The combination of permanent status, hereditary transmission, and racial determination was new — earlier forms of slavery (Roman, Islamic, African) were more varied in how enslavement was determined and whether it could be escaped.*

---

**4.** Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's career is best understood as evidence of which of the following?

- A) Women in colonial Latin America had equal access to education and intellectual life
- B) The colonial social hierarchy allowed limited exceptions for exceptionally talented women in religious contexts
- C) The Catholic Church actively promoted women's intellectual development
- D) Creole women occupied the same social position as creole men

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Sor Juana's case shows that the convent offered a rare space for female intellectual life in colonial society — but this was exceptional, not typical, and her later years saw her silenced by church authorities.*

---

**5.** The emergence of creole identity in Spanish colonial society most directly resulted from:

- A) Creoles' cultural affinity with Indigenous peoples
- B) Resentment of the privileges granted to peninsulares and restrictions of mercantilist trade policy
- C) Creoles' rejection of Catholicism in favor of Enlightenment rationalism
- D) Military threats from English and Dutch colonial powers

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Creoles were excluded from the top colonial offices (reserved for peninsulares) and economically restricted by mercantilist trade laws that funneled colonial profits to Spain — these concrete grievances, not abstract identity politics, drove creole identity formation.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain how the development of the Atlantic slave trade transformed social hierarchies in the Americas in the period 1500–1750."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE feature of the racial hierarchy created by the Atlantic slave trade.
**Part B:** Explain ONE way enslaved Africans maintained cultural identity within this hierarchy.
**Part C:** Explain ONE long-term social consequence of the racial hierarchy created in this period.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Chattel slavery's hereditary, race-based character; casta system categories; legal codes restricting enslaved people's rights.
> - **Part B:** Syncretism (Candomblé, Vodou), oral tradition, music, language preservation.
> - **Part C:** Long-term racial inequality in Latin America and the Caribbean; foundation of modern racial categories.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare how gender hierarchies were transformed in TWO different colonial societies in the period 1450–1750."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE way gender hierarchies changed in the first society.
**Part B:** Describe ONE way gender hierarchies changed in the second society.
**Part C:** Explain ONE similarity in how colonial encounters transformed gender hierarchies across both societies.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - Good comparison societies: Spanish Americas vs. Portuguese Brazil; Americas vs. West Africa; Spanish Americas vs. British North America.
> - Similarity: colonial patriarchy generally increased women's vulnerability to exploitation, whether Indigenous women in encomienda systems or enslaved African women on plantations.
