# Cross-Chapter Connections — Chapter 20 (4.7): Changing Social Hierarchies

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Atlantic Slave Trade]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-18]]) — The racial hierarchy of 4.7 is a direct social consequence of the economic system of 4.5.
- **[[Columbian Exchange]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-16]]) — Demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans is the demographic precondition for the racial mixing that produces the casta system.
- **[[Social Hierarchies in Aztec and Inca Empires]]** (Unit 3) — Pre-colonial social hierarchies in the Americas were based on class and ethnicity, not race — the racialization is the key change introduced by colonialism.
- **[[Gender in Pre-Colonial African and Indigenous Societies]]** (Units 1–3) — Colonial encounters transformed existing gender orders, sometimes dramatically increasing women's vulnerability.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Atlantic Revolutions]]** (Unit 5) — Creole identity and resentment (4.7) directly drives independence movements. The Haitian Revolution is a slave revolt against the racial hierarchy of 4.7.
- **[[Abolition Movements]]** (Unit 5) — The racial hierarchy of chattel slavery is exactly what abolitionists target; Equiano's narrative challenges the dehumanization embedded in the casta system.
- **[[Social Reform Movements]]** (Unit 6–9) — The racial hierarchies created in this period are challenged by civil rights movements, decolonization, and social justice movements through the 20th century.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Casta system (Spanish Americas) vs. racial binary (British North America)** — Spanish system had dozens of intermediate categories; British colonial law moved toward stark free/enslaved binary. Different outcomes for free people of color.
- **Colonial gender hierarchy vs. pre-colonial gender structures** — Were women better or worse off under colonial rule? The answer varies by region, class, and race.
- **Race-based slavery vs. non-racial slavery** — Compare Atlantic chattel slavery (racial, heritable, permanent) with Islamic or Indian Ocean slavery (less racially exclusive, more varied social mobility).

> [!exam-tip]
> A sophisticated AP argument about social hierarchy notes that the **casta system both reflected and reinforced** the economic order — it wasn't just ideology, it was a labor management system that determined who did what kind of work and under what legal conditions.
