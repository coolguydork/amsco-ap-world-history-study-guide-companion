# Key People and Groups — Unit 4.6: (4.6): Internal and External Challenges to State Power

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

---

### Pueblo Peoples of New Mexico
- **Region:** North America (present-day New Mexico)
- **Period:** 17th century
- A coalition of Pueblo peoples (Hopi, Zuni, Tiwa, Tewa, and others) launched the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 under the leadership of Popé, driving ~2,000 Spanish settlers out of New Mexico for 12 years.
- The revolt was caused by Spanish forced labor, destruction of Pueblo religious practices, and severe drought.
- After Spanish reconquest (1692), Spain moderated some of the most oppressive policies — demonstrating that resistance could produce real concessions.

---

### Shivaji and the Maratha Confederacy
- **Region:** Deccan Plateau, India
- **Period:** Mid-to-late 17th century
- Shivaji (1630–1680) founded the Maratha kingdom in 1674, challenging Mughal authority through guerrilla warfare, strategic use of mountain forts, and appeals to Hindu identity and regional pride.
- His tactics — mobile cavalry raids, fortified hilltop strongholds, refusal of pitched battle against superior Mughal forces — were devastatingly effective against Aurangzeb's armies.
- The Maratha Confederacy became the most significant internal challenge to Mughal power in the 1450–1750 period, weakening the empire structurally and foreshadowing its post-1707 disintegration.

> [!tip] **Chronological note:** Tupac Amaru II (1780–1781) is outside AMSCO Unit 4's 1450–1750 scope and belongs in Units 5–6. Do not use it as a Unit 4 resistance example.

### Nzinga of Ndongo and Matamba (c. 1583–1663)
- **Region:** West Central Africa (present-day Angola)
- **Period:** 17th century
- Queen who resisted Portuguese slave raids and colonial expansion for four decades through military resistance, diplomatic negotiations, and strategic alliances.
- Converted to Christianity as a diplomatic tool while maintaining traditional practices.
- One of the most effective African rulers in resisting the Portuguese slave trade.

---

### Francis Drake (c. 1540–1596) and English Privateers
- **Region:** England / Atlantic / Pacific
- **Period:** Late 16th century
- English privateers like Drake attacked Spanish treasure fleets and ports with royal authorization, disrupting the flow of American silver to Spain.
- Drake's circumnavigation (1577–1580) demonstrated England's challenge to Spanish oceanic dominance.

---

### Quilombo dos Palmares Leadership
- **Region:** Brazil (Pernambuco)
- **Period:** c. 1600–1694
- The maroon community of Palmares was governed by a king (Ganga Zumba, then Zumbi) and organized as a quasi-state, resisting Portuguese and Dutch military attacks for nearly a century.
- Zumbi, its last major leader, became a symbol of Black resistance and is celebrated as a national hero in Brazil (November 20 = National Black Consciousness Day).

---

### Creole Elites in Spanish America
- **Region:** Spanish Americas
- **Period:** 17th–18th century
- Born in the Americas to European parents, creoles were excluded from the highest colonial offices and increasingly resentful of metropolitan control and mercantilist trade restrictions.
- Their grievances accumulated throughout the 17th–18th centuries; by 1750 the structural conditions for independence movements were forming, with the full rupture coming in Units 5–6.

> [!exam-tip]
> **Nzinga** and **Zumbi of Palmares** are excellent examples for AP essays requiring evidence of resistance *outside* the Spanish Americas. They demonstrate that challenges to colonial power occurred across multiple regions and contexts.
