# Unit 4.7: Changing Social Hierarchies from 1450 to 1750

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

## AP Exam Period
**Period 2: 1450–1750**

## Summary

The period 1450–1750 produced significant transformations in social hierarchies across the globe — not only in the Atlantic world but also in Eurasia's great land-based empires. AMSCO 4.7 explicitly covers social hierarchies across **gunpowder empires, Manchu/Qing China, European states, Russian society, and the Americas**, not solely the colonial Atlantic context.

In the **Atlantic colonial world**, new race-based social categories emerged — the casta system in Spanish and Portuguese America — translating racial ancestry into legal rights and social status. The Atlantic slave trade embedded racial chattel slavery at the foundation of Atlantic economies.

In **Qing China**, the Manchu conquest created a new ethnic hierarchy: the Manchu-dominated Eight Banner system gave Manchu nobles privileged military and administrative positions over the Han Chinese majority, while the Qing courts used Chinese Confucian ideology to legitimize their rule and co-opt Han elites. The tension between Manchu identity (preserved through language, dress, and marriage prohibitions) and the need to govern a vast Han Chinese population is one of the period's key social dynamics.

In the **Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal empires**, social hierarchies were structured around religion as much as ethnicity. The Ottoman **millet system** organized non-Muslim subjects (Christians, Jews, Armenians) into semi-autonomous religious communities with their own legal and social institutions. The Safavid conversion of Persia to Shia Islam created a new hierarchy privileging Shia Muslims over Sunni and other communities. The Mughal Empire under Akbar pursued deliberate religious pluralism; under Aurangzeb, reinstated jizya taxation and temple destruction shifted the hierarchy in favor of Sunni orthodoxy.

In **Russia**, Peter the Great's reforms created a new service nobility ranked by the Table of Ranks (1722) — merit-based military and civil service replacing purely hereditary status. At the same time, Russian serfdom intensified: peasants became more tightly bound to the land and to noble landowners, creating a social structure that in some ways resembled the feudalism western Europe was abandoning.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how the casta system functioned in Spanish colonial society and how it differed from social hierarchies in other colonial contexts.
- Analyze social hierarchies in the Qing, Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, and Russian empires during this period.
- Evaluate how the Atlantic slave trade reshaped social structures in the Americas and Africa.
- Compare how gender roles and social mobility operated differently across the gunpowder empires, colonial Americas, and European states.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.2.II** — The Atlantic slave trade led to the development of new forms of racial hierarchy.
- **KC-4.2.III** — New social and economic relationships were forged in colonial societies.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently asks about the **casta system** and the **racialization of slavery** as connected phenomena. Be ready to explain both and to distinguish the social structures of colonial Latin America from those of North American British colonies.
