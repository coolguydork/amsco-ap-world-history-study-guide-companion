# Unit 3.4: Comparison in Land-Based Empires

#apwh #unit-3 #period-2

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Compare the political, economic, cultural, and social features of the major land-based empires from c. 1450 to c. 1750.
- Explain similarities and differences in how the Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, Qing, Russian, and Songhai empires organized administration, legitimated authority, and managed diverse populations.
- Use historical reasoning skills — especially comparison and argumentation — to construct evidence-based claims about how land-based empires expanded and consolidated power in this period.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.1:** Globalizing Networks of Exchange (gunpowder technology enables empire-building)
- **KC-4.2:** New Forms of Social Organization and Modes of Production
- **KC-4.3:** State Consolidation and Imperial Expansion
- **Key Concept 4.3.I:** Empires expanded and conquered new peoples using new military technologies, and the rulers of these empires used a variety of methods to legitimize and consolidate their power.

## Summary

Topic 3.4 is a **synthesis and comparison topic** drawing on the regional content of Topics 3.1–3.3, which covered the Ottoman, Safavid, Mughal, Qing, Russian, and Songhai empires individually. The period from c. 1450 to c. 1750 is defined by an unprecedented wave of imperial expansion, made possible by **gunpowder weapons** (cannons and firearms), **new administrative techniques**, and **religious legitimation** that helped rulers manage vast, ethnically diverse populations. These empires are sometimes called "gunpowder empires" because firearms technology gave them a decisive advantage over neighboring peoples. The AP exam expects you to compare how different empires solved the same fundamental problems: How do you conquer new territory? How do you govern people who are different from you? How do you make your rule seem legitimate? This topic is most commonly tested through **LEQ prompts** asking you to compare two or more empires on a specific dimension.

## Comparison Matrix: Six Land-Based Empires (c. 1450–1750)

| Empire | Region | Religion Used | Admin Method | Expansion Method | Key Ruler |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ottoman** | Anatolia, Middle East, North Africa, SE Europe | Sunni Islam; millet system tolerates minorities | Devshirme (slave-soldiers as officials); provincial governors | Gunpowder armies; siege cannons; naval power | Suleiman the Magnificent (r. 1520–1566) |
| **Safavid** | Persia (modern Iran) | Shia Islam (imposed as state religion) | Shah as religious and political head; bureaucracy of Persian elites | Military conquest; conversion campaigns | Shah Ismail I (r. 1501–1524); Shah Abbas I (r. 1588–1629) |
| **Mughal** | Indian subcontinent | Islam (ruling minority); Hindu majority tolerated | Mansabdar system (ranked military-administrative officials); jagir land grants | Conquest; diplomatic marriages; religious tolerance under Akbar | Akbar (r. 1556–1605); Aurangzeb (r. 1658–1707) |
| **Qing** | China, Manchuria, Central Asia | Confucianism (for Chinese subjects); Tibetan Buddhism (for Mongols); shamanism (Manchu) | Dyarchy (paired Manchu and Chinese officials); civil service exam retained | Military conquest of Ming; strategic incorporation of Mongol and Tibetan elites | Kangxi Emperor (r. 1661–1722) |
| **Russian** | Eastern Europe, Siberia, Central Asia | Russian Orthodox Christianity | Tsar as autocrat; boyar nobility; expanding bureaucracy | Military expansion east and south; Cossack frontiersmen | Ivan the Terrible (r. 1547–1584); Peter the Great (r. 1696–1725) |
| **Songhai** | West Africa (Niger River region) | Islam (court elite); indigenous practices (rural majority) | Emperor with appointed provincial governors; Islamic scholars in administration | Military conquest under Sunni Ali; cavalry-based expansion | Sunni Ali (r. 1464–1492); Askia Muhammad (r. 1493–1528) |

## How to Use This Topic

- **For MCQs:** Look for questions asking which two empires share an administrative technique, or which empire's method of legitimation best matches a given source.
- **For SAQs:** Be ready to compare two empires on a specific dimension (e.g., treatment of religious minorities, use of gunpowder) with concrete evidence.
- **For LEQs:** Practice building a thesis that compares at least two empires on a shared challenge (legitimation, administration, expansion) and supports it with at least three specific pieces of evidence.

> [!exam-tip]
> The six empires in this topic are NOT equally emphasized on the AP exam. The **Ottoman, Mughal, and Qing** appear most frequently. Know at least two specific facts about each — one administrative technique and one method of legitimation — and you will be well prepared.

> [!warning]
> Do not treat all six empires as identical just because they are all "land-based empires." The AP exam rewards students who identify **meaningful differences** — such as the Safavid's aggressive Shia conversion policy versus the Mughal's general religious tolerance under Akbar — rather than simply listing similarities.
