# Timeline — Unit 5.8: Reactions to the Industrial Economy

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** The reactions to industrial capitalism — from Luddite machine-breaking to Marxist theory to parliamentary reform — unfolded over a century (1810s–1900s) and produced both revolutionary ideologies and practical reforms. By 1900, most Western industrial nations had begun extending political rights, restricting the worst labor abuses, and building the foundations of welfare states — evidence that reform, not revolution, was the dominant strategy succeeding in the short term.

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| 1811–1816 | Luddite uprisings smash machinery in textile districts | Britain | **Change:** First organized worker resistance to industrialization; violently suppressed; illustrates early form of labor reaction |
| 1819 | Peterloo Massacre; cavalry charge working-class reform rally | Britain | State violence against labor organizing; radicalizes reform movement |
| 1824 | Combination Acts repealed in Britain — unions become legal | Britain | **Change:** Workers can now legally organize; beginning of modern union movement |
| 1825 | Robert Owen establishes New Harmony cooperative community (Indiana) | USA | Utopian socialism in practice; fails financially within 2 years; illustrates limits of utopian approach |
| 1832 | Reform Act (Britain) expands suffrage — but not to workers | Britain | **Continuity:** Workers still excluded; drives working-class demand for Chartism |
| 1833 | Factory Act (Britain) limits child labor; creates inspectors | Britain | **Change:** First significant legislative response to industrial conditions |
| 1834 | Grand National Consolidated Trades Union formed (Owen) | Britain | First attempt at national union federation; collapses quickly |
| 1838 | Chartist movement begins | Britain | Working-class political movement demanding democratic reforms |
| 1842 | Mines Act (Britain) — women and boys under 10 banned underground | Britain | Reform response to documented exploitation; public horror at conditions drives legislation |
| 1844 | Engels writes *Condition of the Working Class in England* | Britain | Systematic documentation of industrial workers' conditions; influences Marx |
| 1848 | Marx and Engels publish *Communist Manifesto* | Europe | **Change:** Revolutionary socialism's founding document; "Workers of the world, unite!" |
| 1848 | European Revolutions ("Springtime of Nations"); workers' demands raised | Europe | Workers join nationalist revolts; mostly suppressed; Marx writes about lessons |
| 1859 | Darwin publishes *Origin of Species* | Britain | Evolutionary science influences both Social Darwinism (anti-reform) and socialist ideas about historical progress |
| 1864 | First International (International Workingmen's Association) founded | Europe | First international labor organization; Marx leads; includes socialists and anarchists |
| 1867 | Marx publishes *Capital*, Vol. I | Britain | Systematic economic analysis of industrial capitalism; surplus value theory |
| 1871 | Paris Commune (March–May) | France | **Change:** First workers' government; violently suppressed; ~10,000 killed; traumatizes French left |
| 1876 | First International dissolves (Marxist-anarchist split) | Europe | Internal divisions prevent international labor solidarity |
| 1878 | Bismarck's Anti-Socialist Laws suppress German socialist movement | Germany | **Continuity:** State repression of socialist organizing; but SPD survives underground |
| 1883 | Bismarck introduces health insurance (Germany) | Germany | **Change:** State welfare as alternative to socialist revolution; reform strategy validated |
| 1886 | Haymarket Affair (Chicago) — bomb kills police at labor rally | USA | Labor movement set back; anarchism associated with violence in American public opinion |
| 1886 | American Federation of Labor (AFL) founded (Gompers) | USA | Pragmatic, non-revolutionary trade unionism; craft union model |
| 1888 | Matchgirls' Strike (London) — women workers win | Britain | Unskilled/female workers organize successfully; expands union movement beyond crafts |
| 1889 | Second International founded | Europe | Socialist parties coordinate internationally; German SPD largest and most organized |
| 1890 | Bismarck's Anti-Socialist Laws expire; SPD becomes Germany's largest party | Germany | Reform socialism succeeds; Bismarck's strategy partially fails |
| 1899 | Bernstein publishes *Evolutionary Socialism* | Germany | **Change:** "Revisionism" — reform vs. revolution debate within socialism formalized |

---

> [!tip] Continuity vs. Change
> **Continuity:** The fundamental power imbalance between capital owners and wage laborers persisted throughout this period; workers gained rights incrementally but capitalism's basic structure remained unchanged.
> **Change:** The *rights and protections available to workers* improved dramatically from 1820 to 1900 — from illegal unions and child labor in mines to Factory Acts, legal unions, and Bismarck's welfare state. Reform achieved real gains.
