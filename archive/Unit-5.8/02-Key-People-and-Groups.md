# Key People and Groups — Unit 5.8: Reactions to the Industrial Economy

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## Karl Marx (1818–1883) and Friedrich Engels (1820–1895)
**Region:** Germany/Britain | **Period:** 1840s–1880s

- Co-authored *The Communist Manifesto* (1848); Marx wrote *Capital* (Vol. I, 1867).
- Argued that capitalism is historically contingent (not eternal) and that class struggle drives history toward proletarian revolution and communist society.
- Engels, son of a German factory owner, had firsthand knowledge of industrial conditions (*The Condition of the Working Class in England*, 1845) that grounded Marxist theory in concrete social observation.

> [!exam-tip]
> Know these three core Marxist concepts for the AP exam: (1) **historical materialism** (economics drives history), (2) **class struggle** (bourgeoisie vs. proletariat), (3) **inevitable revolution** → socialism → communism. Be able to explain why Marx thought capitalism would inevitably collapse.

## Robert Owen (1771–1858)
**Region:** Britain | **Period:** Early 19th century

- Wealthy textile manufacturer who created a model factory community at New Lanark, Scotland: decent housing, schools, limited hours, no child labor under 10.
- Proved that humane treatment of workers was compatible with industrial profitability — challenging the laissez-faire argument that regulation would destroy business.
- Later advocated cooperative communities (*Villages of Cooperation*) as alternatives to capitalism; founded the Grand National Consolidated Trades Union (1834), briefly the largest union in Britain.

---

## The Luddites (1811–1816)
**Region:** England | **Period:** 1811–1816

- Skilled textile workers who organized to smash labor-saving machinery threatening their trades.
- Represent an early, visceral reaction to industrialization — not anti-technology in principle, but protective of their skilled craft livelihoods.
- Violently suppressed; their failure demonstrated that direct action against machinery could not succeed; labor organizing shifted toward collective bargaining.

---

## The Chartist Movement (Britain, 1838–1857)
**Region:** Britain | **Period:** 1838–1857

- British working-class political movement demanding six political reforms: universal male suffrage, secret ballot, annual parliaments, no property requirement for MPs, payment of MPs, and equal electoral districts.
- Most Chartist demands eventually became law (by 1918), demonstrating that reformism could achieve significant change over time.
- Chartism is significant for being a *political* rather than *economic* labor movement — workers understood that political power was prerequisite to economic reform.

---

## Mikhail Bakunin (1814–1876)
**Region:** Russia | **Period:** Mid-19th century

- Russian anarchist who competed with Marx within the First International; rejected Marxist state-based socialism in favor of immediate abolition of all authority.
- His conflict with Marx over whether to use the state as a revolutionary tool led to the First International's dissolution (1876).
- Anarchism had particular influence in southern Europe (Spain, Italy) and Russia; Spanish anarcho-syndicalism became a major force in the early 20th century.

---

## Eduard Bernstein (1850–1932)
**Region:** Germany | **Period:** Late 19th–early 20th century

- German socialist politician who argued in *Evolutionary Socialism* (1899) that capitalism was not producing the inevitable collapse Marx had predicted; workers' living standards were improving; gradual reform through democratic politics was preferable to revolution.
- His "revisionism" split the socialist movement between reformists (evolutionary socialists) and revolutionaries (Marxist-Leninists).
- Historically important because the reform-vs-revolution debate remains unresolved and shapes political alternatives to this day.

---

## Samuel Gompers (1850–1924)
**Region:** United States | **Period:** 1880s–1920s

- Founded and led the American Federation of Labor (AFL), the first durable national labor federation in the USA.
- Advocated "pure and simple" trade unionism: focus on wages, hours, and working conditions — not socialist politics.
- His pragmatic, non-revolutionary approach represented the dominant American labor strategy — in contrast to the more politically radical European socialist parties.

---

## The Paris Commune (1871)
**Region:** France | **Period:** 1871

- A revolutionary working-class government that briefly controlled Paris (March–May 1871) after the Franco-Prussian War, implementing worker-friendly policies.
- Violently suppressed by the French national government (10,000–30,000 Communards killed in "Bloody Week").
- Marx celebrated it as the first example of the "dictatorship of the proletariat"; conservatives used it as a horror story about socialist revolution; its suppression traumatized the French left for a generation.
