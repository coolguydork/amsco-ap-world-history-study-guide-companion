## Multiple Choice Questions

**Questions 1–2 refer to the following passage:**

> "In the year of our Lord 1348, the deadly pestilence arrived in the noble city of Florence from the East, whether through the influence of the heavenly bodies or because God's just wrath had been kindled against wicked men... not only did it cause disease in those who spoke to the sick, but the very touching of the clothes seemed to communicate the disease."
>
> — Giovanni Boccaccio, *Decameron*, c. 1353

**Question 1.** Boccaccio's explanation of the plague's origin ("from the East... through the influence of the heavenly bodies or because God's just wrath") best illustrates:
- A) Accurate medieval scientific understanding of disease transmission
- B) The limits of pre-germ-theory medical knowledge and the role of religious interpretation in explaining catastrophe
- C) Evidence that medieval Europeans correctly identified the plague's Central Asian origin
- D) The effectiveness of miasma theory in preventing plague spread

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Boccaccio's two explanations — astral influence and divine punishment — reflect the pre-scientific frameworks available to 14th-century Europeans. He had no knowledge of bacteria or fleas; religious and astrological explanations were the available tools for understanding catastrophe.*

---

**Question 2.** Boccaccio's observation that "the very touching of the clothes seemed to communicate the disease" most accurately describes:
- A) The pneumonic form of plague, which spread through respiratory droplets
- B) The septicemic form, spread through blood contact
- C) Flea transmission via clothing and bedding
- D) Miasma spreading through textile fibers

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Plague-infected fleas could indeed survive in clothing and bedding, transmitting the disease through contact — Boccaccio was accidentally correct about this observation even without understanding the mechanism.*

---

**Questions 3–4 refer to the following description:**

> "When the mortality was at its height in Venice in 1348, the city council required that all ships arriving from infected ports anchor at a distance from the city for a period of forty days before passengers or cargo could be unloaded."

**Question 3.** The policy described above represents:
- A) The first use of biological warfare in European history
- B) An early example of state-sponsored public health intervention
- C) The application of miasma theory to regulate air quality
- D) A religious response to divine punishment

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Venice's quarantine (from Italian *quarantina*, "forty days") is the classic early example of government public health policy — isolating potentially infected arrivals to prevent disease spread. It was accidentally effective because plague can have an incubation period.*

---

**Question 4.** The most significant long-term consequence of the policy described above was:
- A) The complete prevention of plague's spread in Venice
- B) The establishment of a precedent for state authority over commerce and movement in the name of public health
- C) The decline of Venetian trade due to excessive port restrictions
- D) The spread of miasma theory throughout the Mediterranean

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Venice's quarantine established the principle — still used today — that states can restrict individual movement and commerce to protect public health. This is a foundational development in the history of governance and public health.*

**Questions 5–6 refer to the following data:**

> *Estimated European population: c. 1300 — approximately 75 million; c. 1400 — approximately 45–50 million.*

**Question 5.** The demographic change reflected in the data above most directly resulted from:
- A) Warfare between European kingdoms during the Hundred Years' War
- B) Deliberate population control policies by the Catholic Church
- C) The Black Death and its recurring outbreaks between 1347 and c. 1400
- D) Emigration to Asia along the Silk Roads

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: The 30–40% population decline between 1300 and 1400 is primarily explained by the Black Death and its recurring waves. The Hundred Years' War contributed marginally but was not the primary cause.*

---

**Question 6.** Which of the following social changes was MOST directly caused by the demographic shift shown in the data?
- A) The establishment of feudalism in Western Europe
- B) Labor shortages that gave surviving workers increased economic leverage
- C) The consolidation of papal authority over European monarchs
- D) Increased demand for luxury goods from Asian trade networks

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The death of 30–40% of the workforce created labor shortages, allowing surviving workers to demand higher wages and better conditions — a direct causal link between demographic collapse and social change.*

**Questions 7–8 refer to the following passage:**

> "In city after city, Jews were accused of poisoning the wells that spread the plague. In Strasbourg in February 1349, about two thousand Jews were burned alive. In Mainz and Cologne, thousands more were killed. The flagellants spread these accusations as they traveled from town to town."

**Question 7.** The violence described above is best understood as an example of:
- A) Accurate epidemiological investigation into plague transmission
- B) Scapegoating a minority community as a response to unexplained mass catastrophe
- C) Islamic jihad spreading into European cities via trade routes
- D) Church-sponsored inquisition against heretical communities

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Medieval Jewish communities were falsely accused of causing the plague. This is a classic historical pattern: when communities face inexplicable catastrophe, vulnerable minority groups become scapegoats. The AP exam uses this to illustrate how environmental crises produce social persecution.*

---

**Question 8.** The role of flagellants in spreading anti-Jewish violence (as described in the passage) most directly illustrates:
- A) How religious responses to disaster can amplify social harm
- B) The effectiveness of flagellant self-punishment in preventing plague
- C) How the Catholic Church directed responses to the Black Death
- D) The connection between Judaism and trade networks

**Correct Answer: A**
*Explanation: Flagellant movements spread accusations and violence as they traveled — showing how a religious response to catastrophe (self-flagellation, searching for human sin to blame) could produce additional social harm beyond the original disaster.*

**Questions 9–10 refer to this prompt:** *[Map showing plague transmission routes from Central Asia → Black Sea → Mediterranean → Northern Europe, 1346–1353]*

**Question 9.** The pattern shown on the map most directly supports which argument?
- A) Plague spread randomly, with no connection to human movement patterns
- B) Plague spread along trade routes, demonstrating that commercial connectivity enabled disease transmission
- C) Plague was deliberately spread by Mongol armies across Eurasia
- D) European cities were more vulnerable to plague because of their distance from Central Asia

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The map shows plague following Silk Road and Mediterranean trade routes — from Central Asia to Black Sea ports to Mediterranean cities to inland Europe. This geographic pattern is the key evidence for the argument that trade network connectivity caused the pandemic.*

---

**Question 10.** Which development MOST directly created the conditions for the rapid spread of plague shown on the map?
- A) The decline of Islamic trade networks in the 13th century
- B) The Pax Mongolica, which intensified trade movement across Central Asia and connected previously isolated populations
- C) European agricultural expansion that pushed farmers into forested plague reservoir areas
- D) The decline of the Byzantine Empire's quarantine systems

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The Mongol Empire's road networks and the Pax Mongolica connected populations across Eurasia that had previously been relatively isolated, creating the conditions for rapid, wide-scale plague transmission. This is the key causal argument linking 2.2 (Mongols) to 2.6 (environmental consequences).*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1 — Environmental Consequences of Trade
**"Trade networks during c. 1200–1450 produced significant environmental and demographic consequences for Afro-Eurasian populations."**

**Part A:** Explain ONE way trade networks contributed to demographic collapse in this period.

**Part B:** Explain ONE way trade networks contributed to demographic growth in this period.

**Part C:** Explain why the same mechanisms that produced the positive consequence in Part B also created vulnerability to the negative consequence in Part A.

*Strong answers for Part A: Black Death transmission via Silk Road merchant networks.*
*Strong answers for Part B: Crop diffusion (Champa rice, citrus) increasing agricultural productivity.*
*Strong answers for Part C: Connectivity that moves crops also moves pathogens; dense, well-fed populations are also well-connected populations vulnerable to epidemic disease.*

## DBQ Outline: The Black Death

**Prompt:** "Evaluate the extent to which the Black Death transformed European society in the period c. 1347–1450."

**Thesis Framework:** The Black Death was the most transformative event in medieval European history, producing cascading changes in economic structures (labor markets), social hierarchies (erosion of serfdom), religious authority (Church credibility challenged), and cultural production (death-obsessed art, individualism) — changes that collectively created the conditions for the Renaissance and Reformation of the 15th–16th centuries.

**Document Categories to Expect:**
1. Eyewitness accounts of plague mortality (Boccaccio, *Decameron*) — perspective: educated survivor; purpose: literary/documentary
2. Government decrees about wages and labor (Statute of Laborers, 1351) — perspective: English crown; purpose: restore pre-plague economic order
3. Church responses to plague — perspective: institutional Church; purpose: maintain religious authority
4. Evidence of flagellant movements or anti-Jewish violence — perspective: popular religious belief; purpose: find explanation/scapegoat
5. Evidence of artistic/cultural change (dance of death imagery, tomb sculpture) — perspective: artistic community; purpose: process mass mortality
6. Evidence of labor market changes (wage records, manorial accounts) — perspective: economic; purpose: document

**Body Paragraph Structure:**
- **P1:** Economic transformation — labor shortage → rising wages → erosion of serfdom
- **P2:** Religious and cultural transformation — Church authority challenged → individualist piety → art focused on mortality
- **P3 (Complexity):** Not all regions changed equally — Eastern Europe saw serfdom intensify, not decline; Church recovered much of its authority by c. 1400

**Required Complexity:** Address regional variation OR the limits of transformation OR the long-term vs. short-term distinctions.

> [!exam-tip]
> For the DBQ, remember to use documents as EVIDENCE for your argument, not just summaries. Identify the document's sourcing (who wrote it, why, for whom) and explain how that context affects what it can tell us. A Statute of Laborers tells us what the government *wanted* — not necessarily what actually happened.
