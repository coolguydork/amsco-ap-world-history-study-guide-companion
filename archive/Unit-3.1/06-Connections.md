# Unit 3.1: Connections

#apwh #unit-3 #connections

---

## Builds On (Earlier Chapters)

- [[Unit-1.1/00-Overview|Unit 1.1: Developments in East Asia]]: The Ottoman and Mughal empires inherited the tradition of Islamic governance; the Abbasid Caliphate (Unit 1) established the model of a universal Islamic empire that Suleiman and the Mughals consciously evoked
- [[Unit-1.2/00-Overview|Unit 1.2: Developments in Dar al-Islam]]: The Mongol legacy is direct — Babur descended from Timur and Genghis Khan; the Timurid cultural tradition heavily influenced Mughal court culture; post-Mongol power vacuums across Eurasia created the space gunpowder empires filled
- [[Unit-1.3/00-Overview|Unit 1.3: Developments in South and Southeast Asia]]: The Qing Dynasty built on Ming administrative structures (civil service examination system, Confucian bureaucracy); the Great Wall expanded under the Ming was a defensive response to the same steppe threats the Qing eventually absorbed
- [[Unit-2.1/00-Overview|Unit 2.1: The Silk Roads]]: Ottoman control of overland Eurasian trade routes was a direct cause of European maritime exploration (Unit 4) — the Silk Road connection links Chapters 7 and 11 directly
- [[Unit-2.2/00-Overview|Unit 2.2: The Mongol Empire and the Modern World]]: The Mughal Empire's wealth derived partly from its position as a subcontinent connecting Indian Ocean trade; Persian Gulf trade under Safavid control

## Sets Up (Later Chapters)

- [[Unit-3.2/00-Overview|Unit 3.2: Empires: Administrations]]: The empires introduced in Unit 3.1 are analyzed in depth in Unit 3.2 — their bureaucratic systems, architecture, and methods of cultural legitimation
- [[Unit-3.3/00-Overview|Unit 3.3: Empires: Belief Systems]]: The Ottoman-Safavid Sunni-Shia divide, the Protestant Reformation (which happened while Suleiman besieged Vienna), and the Mughal religious accommodation policies all feed directly into Unit 3.3
- [[Unit-4/00-Overview|Unit 4 — Transoceanic Expansion]]: Ottoman control of eastern Mediterranean trade routes is frequently cited as a *cause* of Portuguese and Spanish exploration; the Qing later faced European contact pressure via the Canton System
- [[Unit-5/00-Overview|Unit 5 — Revolutions]]: The later decline of Mughal, Ottoman, and Qing empires sets up the context for colonialism and revolution in Unit 5–6

## Comparative Civilizations and Contrasts

| Compare | Contrast |
|---|---|
| Ottoman devshirme vs. Chinese examination system | Both created loyal officials; devshirme used enslaved converts, examination used educated elites |
| Mughal Akbar vs. Qing Kangxi | Both were "great" rulers who expanded territory and patronized culture; Akbar promoted religious pluralism, Kangxi promoted Confucian orthodoxy |
| Safavid Shah Abbas vs. Ottoman Suleiman | Both built brilliant capitals (Isfahan vs. Istanbul); both reformed their militaries; divided by Sunni/Shia |
| Russian expansion into Siberia vs. European colonial expansion (Unit 4) | Similar pattern of gunpowder-armed state subjugating indigenous peoples for resources; different geography (land vs. ocean) |
| Songhai Empire vs. later West African states (Unit 4–5) | Songhai's fall opened West Africa to increased trans-Saharan and eventually Atlantic contact |
