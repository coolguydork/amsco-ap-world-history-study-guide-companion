# Key People and Groups — Unit 5.5: Technology in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## Thomas Edison (1847–1931)
**Region:** United States | **Period:** 1870s–1900s

- Developed the practical incandescent light bulb (1879), the phonograph (1877), and the first commercial electrical power distribution system (Pearl Street Station, New York City, 1882).
- Created the Menlo Park research laboratory — the first industrial research lab — pioneering systematic, team-based invention aimed at commercial products.
- His DC (direct current) electrical system was ultimately displaced by Tesla's AC system, but his organizational model of the industrial research laboratory became standard practice for 20th-century technology development.

---

## Nikola Tesla (1856–1943)
**Region:** Serbia/United States | **Period:** 1880s–1900s

- Developed the AC (alternating current) motor and transformer systems, enabling long-distance electrical transmission — essential for electrifying cities, not just individual buildings.
- Worked for and then competed with Edison in the "War of Currents" — AC ultimately won because it could transmit electricity over hundreds of miles; DC could not.
- His patents were purchased by George Westinghouse, whose Westinghouse Electric Company built the AC electrical system that became the global standard.

---

## Henry Bessemer (1813–1898)
**Region:** Britain | **Period:** 1850s–1870s

- Invented the Bessemer converter (1856), which produced steel by blasting air through molten pig iron — reducing the cost of steel by ~80% compared to earlier processes.
- Made steel production viable at industrial scale; by the 1870s, steel was replacing iron in railroads, bridges, and eventually buildings.
- His invention is a key example of the Second Industrial Revolution's characteristic pattern: applying scientific principles to solve production cost problems.

---

## George Stephenson (1781–1848)
**Region:** Britain | **Period:** 1810s–1830s

- Built the *Rocket* locomotive (1829) and designed the Liverpool-Manchester Railway (1830) — the first commercially successful intercity steam railroad.
- Established the standard railway gauge (4 feet, 8.5 inches) still used in most of the world today.
- His success launched the global railroad age; within a decade, railroad construction was underway across Europe, and British engineers and capital were building railroads on every continent.

---

## Samuel Morse (1791–1872)
**Region:** United States | **Period:** 1830s–1840s

- An artist who became an inventor; developed the electromagnetic telegraph and Morse code (a system of dots and dashes representing letters and numbers) in the 1830s.
- Demonstrated the first long-distance telegram in 1844 (Washington to Baltimore: "What hath God wrought!").
- The commercial telegraph system he helped establish transformed communication first within the USA, then globally; the first transatlantic cable (1866) used Morse's system.

---

## Karl Benz (1844–1929) and Gottlieb Daimler (1834–1900)
**Region:** Germany | **Period:** 1880s

- Karl Benz built the first true gasoline-powered automobile (Benz Patent-Motorwagen, 1885); Daimler independently developed high-speed gasoline engines around the same time.
- Their work launched the automobile industry; Benz and Daimler's companies eventually merged to form Mercedes-Benz.
- The internal combustion engine they developed would ultimately replace steam as the dominant transportation technology in the 20th century.

---

## Cyrus Field (1819–1892)
**Region:** United States | **Period:** 1850s–1860s

- American businessman who organized and financed multiple attempts to lay a transatlantic telegraph cable, finally succeeding in 1866.
- The successful cable reduced transatlantic communication time from weeks (by ship) to minutes (by telegraph) — one of the most dramatic compression of distance in communication history.
- Field's project required enormous capital mobilization and coordination between American and British interests, illustrating how technology diffusion was also a business and financial enterprise.

---

## Louis Pasteur (1822–1895) and Robert Koch (1843–1910)
**Region:** France/Germany | **Period:** 1860s–1890s

- Pasteur developed germ theory — the idea that microorganisms cause disease — and invented pasteurization and early vaccines.
- Koch identified the bacteria causing tuberculosis (1882) and cholera (1884), establishing the germ theory on a firm experimental basis.
- Their work transformed medicine and public health; the connection to industrialization is that industrial cities created the disease environments (cholera, tuberculosis) that made medical science urgent and socially valued.
