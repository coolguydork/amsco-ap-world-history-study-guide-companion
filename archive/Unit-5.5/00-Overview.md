# Unit 5.5: Technology in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

The Industrial Revolution produced not one wave of technology but two. The **First Industrial Revolution** (c. 1760–1850) was centered on textiles, coal, and iron, powered by steam, and associated primarily with Britain. The **Second Industrial Revolution** (c. 1870–1914) was centered on steel, chemicals, electricity, and the internal combustion engine — more science-based, more capital-intensive, and more associated with Germany and the United States than with Britain. Together, these two waves transformed every dimension of material life: how goods were made (**factory production**), how people and things moved (**railroads, steamships**), how information traveled (**telegraph, telephone**), and how energy was generated and distributed (**electricity**). The railroad is the single most consequential technology of the 19th century for AP World History purposes — it integrated national markets, enabled industrial-scale production, drove demand for steel, carried colonial goods and armies, and reshaped the spatial organization of human settlement. The **telegraph** transformed communication by enabling messages to cross continents and oceans in minutes rather than weeks, with major consequences for commerce, military coordination, and journalism. By 1900, **electricity** was transforming urban life through electric lights, streetcars, and the first industrial motors — pointing toward the full electrification of the 20th century.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Identify the key technologies of the First and Second Industrial Revolutions.
- Explain how railroads and the telegraph transformed transportation, communication, and commerce.
- Analyze how technological change drove further industrialization and global economic integration.
- Evaluate the environmental and social consequences of 19th-century technological change.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.1.I** — Industrialization fundamentally changed how goods were produced using new energy sources and technologies.
- **KC-5.2.I** — Railroads and steamships transformed economic production and global exchange.

> [!exam-tip]
> For AP stimulus questions on technology, always connect the technology to its *consequences* — not just what it is but what it changed. Railroad → lower transportation costs → integrated national markets → industrial-scale viable → urban growth → environmental impact. Practice this chain for each key technology.
