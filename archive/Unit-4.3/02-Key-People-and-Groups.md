# Key People and Groups — Unit 4.3: (4.3): The Columbian Exchange

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

---

### Indigenous American Peoples
- **Region:** Americas (Caribbean, Mesoamerica, Andes, North America)
- **Period:** Post-1492
- Suffered a demographic collapse of 50–90% within a century of European contact, primarily from epidemic diseases (smallpox, measles, typhus) for which they had no prior immunity.
- Survivors were subjected to coerced labor systems (encomienda, mita) and cultural suppression, though Indigenous traditions survived in syncretic forms.
- Their demographic catastrophe was the single largest cause of the Atlantic slave trade: the labor vacuum left by dying Indigenous peoples was filled by enslaved Africans.

---

### Bartolomé de las Casas (1484–1566)
- **Region:** Spanish Caribbean / Mexico
- **Period:** 16th century
- Dominican friar who documented the devastation of Indigenous populations and advocated for their legal protection before the Spanish Crown.
- His writings are primary sources for the scale of demographic collapse and the violence of the encomienda system.
- Ironically, his advocacy contributed to increased African enslavement as an "alternative" labor source — he later recanted this position.

---

### Alfred W. Crosby (1931–2018)
- **Region:** United States (modern historian)
- **Period:** 20th century
- American historian who coined the term "Columbian Exchange" in his 1972 book of the same name and developed the concept of "ecological imperialism."
- His work transformed how historians understand the role of biology and ecology in historical change.
- Relevant for AP students understanding the *historiographical framework* behind the topic.

---

### West African Peoples
- **Region:** West and Central Africa
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- Became the primary source of enslaved labor for American plantations, directly because the demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans created demand for alternative workers.
- Received American crops (maize, cassava, sweet potato) via the Columbian Exchange, which supplemented traditional diets and contributed to population resilience even as the slave trade removed millions.

---

### European Settlers and Colonists
- **Region:** Americas, primarily Spanish and Portuguese
- **Period:** 16th–17th century
- Carried pathogens to the Americas — often unknowingly — that caused virgin soil epidemics.
- Introduced European livestock, which transformed American landscapes.
- Adopted American crops (potato, tomato, maize) for consumption, though full integration into European diets took time.

---

### Comanche, Lakota, and Great Plains Peoples
- **Region:** North American Great Plains
- **Period:** 17th–18th century
- Dramatically transformed their societies after acquiring horses (reintroduced from Spain via the Columbian Exchange), shifting from semi-sedentary agricultural to fully mobile bison-hunting cultures.
- The Comanche in particular became a dominant military power on the southern plains using horse-based warfare.
- A clear example of Indigenous adaptation and resilience in response to the exchange.

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Comanche horse culture** is a sophisticated AP example of Indigenous *agency* — rather than simply being victims of the Columbian Exchange, some Indigenous peoples adapted creatively to new animals and tools. Use this to add complexity to essays about the exchange's impact.
