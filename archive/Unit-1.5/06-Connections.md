# Unit 1.5: Cross-Topic Connections — Developments In Africa

## Concepts This Topic Builds On

- [[Unit-1.2/00-Overview|Unit 1.2: Developments in Dar al-Islam]] — Islam's spread to Africa via trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean routes; Mali's Islamic connections to the Abbasid world; the hajj as a mechanism of exchange; Ibn Battuta documenting both chapters
- [[Unit-1.3/00-Overview|Unit 1.3: Developments in South and Southeast Asia]] — The Indian Ocean trade network connecting East Africa to India and Southeast Asia; Indian textiles and Chinese porcelain found in Swahili archaeological sites
- Pre-1200 Bantu migrations — The Bantu expansion across sub-Saharan Africa created the demographic and linguistic foundation for the states studied in this chapter

## Concepts This Topic Sets Up

- Unit 2 (Networks of Exchange) — Trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean trade are the core topics of Unit 2; Unit 1.5 shows the African nodes in those networks
- Unit 3 (Land-Based Empires) — The Songhai Empire (West Africa) and the Mutapa state (southern Africa) succeed Mali and Great Zimbabwe respectively; Ottoman-Safavid competition affects North Africa
- Unit 4 (Transoceanic Connections) — Portuguese arrival on the African coast (1400s–1500s) disrupts existing African trade networks; Swahili cities are Portuguese targets
- Atlantic Slave Trade (Unit 4–5) — The transformation of African domestic slavery into the massive, externally-driven Atlantic slave trade is one of history's most consequential changes; this chapter shows what African slavery looked like *before* European intervention

## Comparative Civilizations and Contrasts

- **Mali vs. Mongol Empire:** Both were steppe/savanna-based empires that controlled vast territories through military power and control of trade routes — compare governance models and how each related to the settled agricultural civilizations they dominated
- **Swahili Coast vs. Italian city-states:** Both were commercial city-states dependent on long-distance trade rather than agricultural hinterlands; both adopted the religion and practices of their dominant trading partners
- **Great Zimbabwe vs. Angkor (Khmer):** Both built impressive stone monuments demonstrating state power; both relied on agricultural surplus and control of trade revenues; both declined as trade routes shifted
- **Mali's "elite Islam" vs. Mongol conversion:** Both represent cases of a ruling minority adopting Islam while governing populations with different religious traditions — compare how sincerely and completely each adopted Islamic practice
- **Ethiopian Christianity vs. Eastern Orthodox Christianity:** Both maintained forms of Christianity outside the Roman Catholic tradition; compare how geographic isolation and external pressure shaped each tradition's development

> [!tip]
> Africa is the region where **all three** major AP World History trade networks (trans-Saharan, Indian Ocean, and Mediterranean) intersect. Mastering African geography — West Africa (gold-salt-Sahara), East Africa (Indian Ocean), North Africa (Mediterranean-Islamic) — will help you on virtually every APWH network question.
