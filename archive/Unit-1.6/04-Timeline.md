# Timeline — Unit 1.6: Developments in Europe (c. 1200–1450)

#apwh #unit-1 #period-1

> [!tip]
> **Big Picture:** Europe in 1200–1450 transformed from a feudal, Church-dominated backwater into a region primed for global expansion. This chapter sits between the Classical civilizations (Units before) and the Age of Exploration (Unit 4). The key pivot is the **Black Death** — everything before it shows a rising medieval world; everything after shows its fractures.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1054 | Great Schism splits Christianity | Rome / Constantinople | Permanent divide between Catholic and Orthodox churches; weakens universal Christian unity |
| 1066 | Norman Conquest of England | England | Introduced feudalism to England; mixed Anglo-Saxon and French cultures to create English identity |
| 1095 | Pope Urban II launches First Crusade | Western Europe | Begins 200 years of Christian-Muslim military conflict; opens Europe to cultural exchange with Islamic world |
| 1099 | First Crusade captures Jerusalem | Middle East | Short-lived Christian control of Holy Land; intensified religious motivation for future Crusades |
| 1150s–1200s | Rise of European universities | France, England, Italy | Bologna (1088), Paris (~1150), Oxford (~1167) established; center of scholastic learning |
| 1204 | Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople | Byzantine Empire | Crusaders attack fellow Christians; accelerates Byzantine decline; deepens Catholic-Orthodox rift |
| 1215 | Magna Carta signed by King John | England | First legal limit on royal power; precedent for constitutional government and rule of law |
| c. 1225–1274 | Life of Thomas Aquinas | Italy / France | *Summa Theologica* reconciles faith and reason; becomes foundation of Catholic intellectual tradition |
| 1241 | Founding of the Hanseatic League | Northern Europe | Major commercial alliance boosting Baltic trade; symbol of growing merchant class power |
| 1291 | Fall of Acre — end of Crusades | Middle East | Final Crusader state falls; Europe withdraws from Holy Land but retains trade networks with the East |
| 1309–1377 | Avignon Papacy ("Babylonian Captivity") | France | Popes move to Avignon under French influence; undermines papal independence and credibility |
| 1337 | Hundred Years' War begins | France / England | Century-long conflict forges French and English national identities; weakens feudal nobility |
| 1347–1353 | Black Death devastates Europe | All of Europe | ~25–30 million dead (⅓ of population); labor shortages, social upheaval, decline of feudalism and Church prestige |
| 1351 | Statute of Laborers (England) | England | Crown attempts to freeze wages after Black Death; shows lords' panic at peasant bargaining power — largely unsuccessful |
| 1378–1417 | Western Schism (multiple popes) | Western Europe | Three men simultaneously claim papacy; catastrophic blow to Church authority — **CHANGE** in religious confidence |
| 1381 | English Peasants' Revolt | England | Lower-class uprising sparked by poll tax and post-plague labor tensions; suppressed but signals new social pressures |
| 1415 | Battle of Agincourt | France | English victory under Henry V in Hundred Years' War; later reversed by Joan of Arc |
| c. 1429–1431 | Joan of Arc leads French forces | France | Turns tide of Hundred Years' War; symbol of national identity; burned at stake by English-allied court |
| 1453 | Fall of Constantinople to Ottomans | Byzantine Empire | End of Eastern Roman Empire; Greek scholars flee to Italy, sparking the Renaissance — **major turning point** |
| 1453 | End of Hundred Years' War | France / England | France consolidates territory; both nations develop stronger centralized monarchies |

---

> [!exam-tip]
> Know these as **continuity vs. change** markers:
> - **Continuity (1200–1347):** Feudalism, Church authority, agrarian economy — all essentially unchanged
> - **CHANGE (1347–1450):** Black Death fractures everything — feudalism weakens, Church questioned, towns grow, merchants rise
