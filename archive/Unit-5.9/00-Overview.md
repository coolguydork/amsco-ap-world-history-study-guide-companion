# Unit 5.9: Society and the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

Industrialization did not merely transform production — it remade social life from top to bottom. The most visible change was **urbanization**: industrial cities grew with explosive speed, pulling in rural migrants and international immigrants but providing them with overcrowded housing, contaminated water, and no sanitation infrastructure. **Manchester** grew from 25,000 in 1772 to 316,000 in 1850; **Chicago** from 30,000 in 1850 to 1.1 million in 1890. A new **middle class** — managers, professionals, clerks, shopkeepers — emerged between the industrial bourgeoisie and the proletariat, defining itself through education, domestic virtue, and respectability. The **"separate spheres" ideology** redefined gender roles around the middle-class ideal of the domestic woman and the breadwinning man — though working-class women continued to labor in factories, domestic service, and sweatshops. **Child labor** in factories, mines, and textile mills was pervasive and brutal until legislative reform restricted it from the 1830s onward. **Public health** crises — cholera epidemics in 1832, 1848, and 1854; tuberculosis ("white plague") as a chronic industrial killer — eventually produced municipal sanitation reform (clean water, sewage systems) and germ theory that transformed medicine. By 1900, the outlines of modern urban industrial society — with its class structure, gender divisions, public health infrastructure, and consumer culture — were visible in Western Europe and North America.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Analyze how industrialization transformed social structures, including the emergence of new social classes.
- Explain how gender roles changed in industrializing societies and evaluate the "separate spheres" ideology.
- Describe the public health challenges of industrial urbanization and the responses they generated.
- Evaluate child labor as both an economic practice and a social reform target.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.9.I** — Industrialization produced new social classes and transformed gender relations, family structure, and daily life.
- **KC-5.9.II** — Urbanization created public health crises that eventually produced new municipal institutions and medical advances.

> [!exam-tip]
