# Unit 4.4: Maritime Empires Link Regions

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

## AP Exam Period
**Period 2: 1450–1750**

## Summary

Topic 4.4's organizing question is not simply *how* European maritime empires were built, but *what they connected* — and what those connections changed. For the first time in history, all major inhabited regions of the globe were brought into sustained contact through European maritime networks. The **Atlantic system** linked Western Europe, West Africa, and the Americas in a triangular exchange of manufactured goods, enslaved people, and colonial commodities (sugar, silver, tobacco). The **Indian Ocean system**, already ancient, was partly restructured as Portugal inserted itself into existing networks and forced trade through its fortified feitorias; later the Dutch VOC and English EIC competed for nodes in the same network. The **Pacific** was crossed for the first time by Magellan's circumnavigation (1519–1522) and then regularly by the Manila Galleons carrying American silver to China in exchange for silk and porcelain — connecting the Americas to Asia for the first time.

The critical AP frame for this topic is **continuity and change in trade networks and commercial systems**. The Indian Ocean had operated for millennia and overland connections did not vanish — what changed was *who controlled key nodes* and *what commodities moved at what unprecedented scale*. Portuguese feitorias inserted European power into existing Indian Ocean networks rather than replacing them; the Dutch VOC and English EIC later pushed this further by seizing physical control of production zones. The labor systems that extracted colonial commodities — encomienda, mita, chattel slavery, plantation agriculture — are covered in depth in [[Unit-4.5/00-Overview|Unit 4.5: Maritime Empires Develop]].

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how maritime empires linked previously separate regions across the Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and Pacific.
- Analyze continuity and change in trade networks and commercial systems as maritime empires expanded.
- Evaluate how political and commercial interests shaped the organization of maritime empires.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.2.I** — European states established maritime empires that linked regions across the Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and Pacific.
- **KC-4.2.II** — The economic systems of maritime empires showed both continuity (long-distance trade, existing Indian Ocean networks) and change (new commodities, new routes, new scales of exchange).
