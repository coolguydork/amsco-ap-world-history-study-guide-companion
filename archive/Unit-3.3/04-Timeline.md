# Unit 3.3: Timeline — Belief Systems in the Early Modern World

#apwh #unit-3 #timeline

> **Big Picture:** Unit 3.3 spans the full arc of AP Period 3 (1450–1750) and connects directly to Period 4 (1750–1900). The Protestant Reformation, the Islamic Sunni-Shia institutionalization, the emergence of Sikhism, and the beginning of global missionary activity all unfold simultaneously — a reminder that religious change was a *global* phenomenon in this era, not just a European story.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1469 | Birth of Guru Nanak; Sikhism begins to develop | Indian subcontinent (Punjab) | Syncretic religious tradition emerging from Hindu-Muslim encounter in the Punjab — predates Mughal rule (which began 1526); later developed and was shaped by the Mughal context — **CHANGE** in religious landscape of South Asia |
| 1492 | Expulsion of Jews (and Moors) from Spain | Iberian Peninsula/Mediterranean | Forced migration of Jewish communities to Ottoman Empire and Netherlands; Spanish religious uniformity through violence — **CHANGE** |
| 1501 | Safavid Empire converts Persia to Shia Islam | Middle East | Institutionalization of Sunni-Shia divide as geopolitical reality; creates Ottoman-Safavid religious rivalry |
| 1517 | Martin Luther's 95 Theses | Holy Roman Empire (Germany) | Start of the Protestant Reformation; printing press enables rapid spread — **watershed moment** in Western Christian history |
| 1534 | Henry VIII breaks with Rome; Church of England founded | England | Politically motivated Reformation; monarch becomes head of national church — **CHANGE** in church-state relations |
| 1536 | John Calvin publishes Institutes of the Christian Religion | Geneva/France | Systematic Calvinist theology; predestination; Geneva becomes model Reformed city-state |
| 1540 | Society of Jesus (Jesuits) founded by Ignatius of Loyola | Rome | Most important Catholic missionary and intellectual order; begins global expansion of Counter-Reformation Catholicism |
| 1542–1552 | Francis Xavier missions in India, Japan, East Asia | Asia | Catholic Christianity reaches Japan and East Asia for the first time; ~300,000 Japanese converts |
| 1545–1563 | Council of Trent | Italy (Catholic Church) | Catholic doctrinal clarification; internal reform; launches Counter-Reformation — **CHANGE** within Catholicism, **continuity** in core doctrine |
| c. 1550s–1580s | Matteo Ricci's Jesuit mission in China (arrives 1582) | China | Cultural accommodation strategy; Christianity presented to Chinese elites through Confucian framework; later condemned by Pope |
| 1562–1598 | French Wars of Religion (Huguenot Wars) | France | Catholic vs. Calvinist civil war; St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre (1572); Edict of Nantes (1598) grants limited tolerance |
| 1571 | Battle of Lepanto: Christian coalition defeats Ottoman fleet | Mediterranean | Signals limits of Ottoman naval expansion; Ottoman Empire remains a land empire; major psychological victory for Catholic Europe |
| 1597 | Tokugawa Japan begins suppressing Christianity | Japan | 26 martyrs crucified in Nagasaki; Christianity banned; shows limits of Jesuit missions when rulers view foreign religion as political threat |
| 1618–1648 | Thirty Years' War | Central Europe | Deadliest religious war in European history; ends with Peace of Westphalia establishing principle of state sovereignty and limited religious tolerance |
| 1685 | Louis XIV revokes Edict of Nantes; Huguenot exodus | France/Europe | ~200,000 skilled Protestants flee to England, Prussia, Dutch Republic — transferring economic and intellectual capital |
| 1699 | Guru Gobind Singh founds Sikh Khalsa | Indian subcontinent | Sikhism becomes a martial community; response to Mughal (Aurangzeb's) persecution — religion transformed by imperial oppression |
| 1744 | Wahhabi movement allied with Saudi tribe in Arabia | Arabian Peninsula | Foundation of modern Islamic puritan reform tradition; long-term consequences for global Islam — bridge to Units 7–9 |
