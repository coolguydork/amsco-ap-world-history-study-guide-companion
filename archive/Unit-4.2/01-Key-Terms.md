# Key Terms — Unit 4.2: (4.2): Exploration

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

1. **Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)** — A papal-mediated agreement dividing the non-European world between Spain (west) and Portugal (east) along a line in the Atlantic. (Attempted to prevent conflict between the two Iberian powers; gave Portugal Brazil and Asia, Spain the Americas.)

2. **Reconquista** — The centuries-long Christian campaign to retake the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rulers, completed in 1492. (Trained a warrior class of conquistadors and created religious fervor that spilled directly into overseas exploration and conversion campaigns.)

3. **Feitoria (trading post / factory)** — A Portuguese fortified trading station established along coastlines in Africa and Asia. (Allowed Portugal to control key nodes of Indian Ocean trade without large territorial conquest — the trading-post empire model.)

4. **Circumnavigation** — The act of sailing completely around the world. (Magellan-Elcano expedition, 1519–1522, proved Earth's spherical shape and the enormous size of the Pacific Ocean.)

5. **Northwest Passage** — A sought-after sea route through the Arctic from Europe to Asia via North America. (Its pursuit drove English and French exploration of North America; it was never found in this period.)

6. **Conquistador** — Spanish military leaders who conquered Indigenous empires in the Americas using small forces, firearms, Indigenous allies, and epidemic disease. (Their campaigns gave Spain control of the most populous and wealthy regions of the Americas.)

7. **Encomienda** — A labor grant giving Spanish settlers control over Indigenous people's labor in exchange for "protection" and Christian instruction. (The primary mechanism of Indigenous exploitation in early Spanish colonies; later modified under pressure from Las Casas.)

8. **Viceroy** — A royal representative governing a colonial territory on behalf of the Spanish Crown. (Viceroyalties of New Spain and Peru administered Spain's vast American empire.)

9. **Papal Bull** — A formal decree issued by the Pope carrying the authority of the Catholic Church. (*Inter Caetera* (1493) granted Spain rights over newly discovered lands and obligated it to Christianize their inhabitants.)

10. **Mercantilism** — The economic theory that national wealth is accumulated through exports, colonial extraction, and hoarding of bullion, with the state actively directing commerce. (Drove the competitive nature of European exploration and imperial policy throughout the period.)

11. **Privateers** — Privately owned ships authorized by a government to attack enemy ships. (England used privateers like Francis Drake to harass Spanish treasure fleets; blurred the line between commerce, piracy, and war.)

12. **Line of Demarcation** — The meridian established by the Treaty of Tordesillas dividing Spanish and Portuguese spheres of influence. (Ignored by the Dutch, English, and French, who challenged Iberian monopolies in the 17th century.)

13. **Strait of Magellan** — The passage at the southern tip of South America connecting the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, navigated by Magellan in 1520. (Opened the Pacific to European navigation and demonstrated the scale of the globe.)

14. **Carreira da India** — The Portuguese annual fleet system connecting Lisbon to Goa (India) via the Cape of Good Hope. (Institutionalized Portuguese control of the Cape route and the spice trade for nearly a century.)

> [!exam-tip]
> Know the difference between **motives** (God, glory, gold) and **methods** (feitoria vs. conquest vs. joint-stock company). The AP exam often asks you to distinguish between Portuguese and Spanish approaches to empire — trading-post vs. territorial.
