# Key People and Groups — Unit 4.5: (4.5): Maritime Empires Develop

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

---

### Dutch Republic / VOC
- **Region:** Netherlands / Southeast Asia / Atlantic
- **Period:** 17th century
- The VOC displaced Portugal as the dominant power in the Southeast Asian spice trade after 1600, seizing Malacca (1641) and controlling the Banda Islands (nutmeg and cloves).
- Pioneer of the joint-stock model and modern financial capitalism (Amsterdam Stock Exchange, 1602).
- Also operated in the Atlantic, controlling Brazil briefly and establishing New Amsterdam (New York) in North America.

---

### British East India Company (EIC)
- **Region:** England / South Asia / Caribbean
- **Period:** 17th–19th century
- Chartered in 1600; began as a trading company competing with the VOC in Asia.
- Gradually built military power in India, eventually governing the subcontinent on behalf of the British Crown.
- Also managed plantation colonies in the Caribbean using enslaved African labor.

---

### West African Kingdoms (Dahomey, Ashanti, Kongo)
- **Region:** West and Central Africa
- **Period:** 16th–18th century
- Actively participated in the Atlantic slave trade — some kingdoms (Dahomey, Ashanti) built their power through slave raiding and selling captives to European traders.
- Kingdom of Kongo saw its population devastated by the trade despite attempts by its rulers to negotiate limits with Portugal.
- Demonstrate that the slave trade was not imposed solely from outside; African rulers made calculated decisions about participation.

---

### Olaudah Equiano (c. 1745–1797)
- **Region:** West Africa / Atlantic
- **Period:** 18th century
- Enslaved Igbo man who wrote a famous memoir (*The Interesting Narrative*, 1789) describing his capture, the Middle Passage, and his eventual self-purchase and freedom.
- His narrative is a primary source for the AP exam's DBQ exercises and humanizes the statistics of the slave trade.

---

### Enslaved Africans in the Americas
- **Region:** Caribbean, Brazil, North America
- **Period:** 16th–19th century
- Built the plantation economies that generated Atlantic wealth through their forced labor on sugar, tobacco, and cotton plantations.
- Maintained African cultural traditions (music, religion, language) and resisted slavery through individual acts, organized rebellions, and the formation of maroon communities.
- Their labor was the foundation of the Atlantic economy.

---

### Spanish Silver Merchants and the Crown
- **Region:** Spain / Americas / Asia
- **Period:** 16th–17th century
- Coordinated the extraction and shipment of American silver through the Casa de Contratación; silver flowed from Potosí and Mexico to Seville, then to Asia via the Philippines.
- Spain's reliance on silver imports rather than domestic production contributed to long-term economic weakness ("resource curse" before the concept existed).

> [!exam-tip]
> **Olaudah Equiano's narrative** is one of the most frequently used AP DBQ documents. Know: his perspective (formerly enslaved, now free), his purpose (abolitionist argument), and how his positionality shapes what he includes and emphasizes.
