# Practice Questions — Unit 4.8: (4.8): Continuity and Change

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

---

## Long Essay Questions (LEQ) — Unit 4 Synthesis

These prompts require a full essay with a defensible thesis, contextualizing argument, supporting evidence, and complexity.

---

### LEQ 1 — CCOT

**"Evaluate the extent to which the period 1450–1750 represents a significant turning point in global trade networks."**

> [!tip] Essay Guidance
> **Context:** Establish what trade networks looked like before 1450 — Silk Roads, Indian Ocean, Trans-Saharan. These were extensive but did not include the Americas.
>
> **Thesis options:**
> - Strong "change" argument: The integration of the Western Hemisphere was so significant that it created a qualitatively new global system — not just more of the same.
> - Nuanced argument: While trade networks expanded dramatically in geographic scope, their fundamental character (luxury goods, unequal exchange, state competition) showed important continuity.
>
> **Evidence — Change:** Columbian Exchange; global silver trade; VOC/EIC joint-stock model; Manila Galleon transpacific route.
>
> **Evidence — Continuity:** Indian Ocean trade continued with same participants; luxury goods (spices, silk, silver) remained dominant; coercion continued to structure labor.
>
> **Complexity:** Compare the impact of trade network changes on different regions — transformative for the Americas, less disrupting for China and India in the short term.

### LEQ 2 — Causation

**"Evaluate the most significant cause of the Atlantic slave trade's development in the period 1500–1750."**

> [!tip] Essay Guidance
> **The main causes to evaluate:**
> 1. Demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans (labor vacuum)
> 2. European demand for plantation crops (sugar, tobacco)
> 3. African political conditions enabling slave raiding and trade
> 4. Racial ideology justifying enslavement of Africans
>
> **Strong thesis:** Argues that economic demand (plantation agriculture) was the primary structural cause, while demographic collapse was the immediate trigger, and racial ideology was a justifying mechanism rather than a root cause.
>
> **Complexity:** Explain that the causes were mutually reinforcing — no single cause is sufficient; the trade required all four factors operating together.

### LEQ 3 — Comparison

**"Compare the social hierarchies of TWO colonial societies in the period 1450–1750."**

> [!tip] Essay Guidance
> **Best comparisons:**
> - Spanish Americas vs. British North America (casta system vs. binary racial slavery)
> - Spanish Americas vs. Portuguese Brazil (similar but not identical racial hierarchies)
> - Colonial Americas vs. colonial India under Portuguese/Dutch influence
>
> **Similarity:** Both used race as a primary organizing principle; both embedded inequality in legal codes.
>
> **Difference:** Spanish casta system had dozens of intermediate categories; British colonies moved toward stark free/enslaved binary. Long-term consequence: more fluid racial mobility in Latin America vs. more rigid binary in North America.

## DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which economic motivations explain European maritime expansion in the period 1450–1600."**

> [!tip] DBQ Guidance
> **Thesis:** Acknowledge economic factors (spice trade, silver) as central while arguing that religious mission and political rivalry were also significant and often inseparable from economic motives.
>
> **Document groupings:**
> 1. Economic motivation documents (merchant accounts, trading records, price data)
> 2. Religious motivation documents (papal bulls, missionary accounts, Requerimiento)
> 3. Political rivalry documents (Treaty of Tordesillas, privateering authorizations)
> 4. Documents showing economic and religious motives intertwined
>
> **Complexity argument:** Economic, religious, and political motives were not separate — they reinforced each other. The Crown funded exploration for profit but needed religious legitimacy; missionaries needed imperial protection to operate. A sophisticated argument shows how these motivations interacted.
>
> **Outside evidence:** Specific statistics (Potosí silver output), specific events (Reconquista → Columbus voyage), specific individuals (Las Casas's critique of economic exploitation cloaked in religious language).

## Short Answer Questions — Synthesis

### SAQ: Unit 4 Synthesis

**"Explain how the period 1450–1750 both changed and continued earlier patterns of long-distance exchange."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE significant way that global trade networks *changed* in the period 1450–1750.
**Part B:** Identify ONE significant *continuity* in long-distance trade from the period before 1450 into this period.
**Part C:** Explain which you consider more historically significant — the change or the continuity — and why.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Integration of the Americas; global silver economy; joint-stock companies.
> - **Part B:** Indian Ocean trade continued with similar participants; luxury goods (spices, silk) remained dominant; coercion in labor systems persisted.
> - **Part C:** Strong answers are *arguable* — either choice can earn full credit if supported with specific evidence and a clear historical argument.
