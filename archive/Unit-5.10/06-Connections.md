# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 5.10: Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## 1. Builds On (All Earlier Topics in Unit 5)

- **[[The Enlightenment]]** ([[Unit-5.1]]) — Ideological change (natural rights, secular politics); limited social change (women, enslaved peoples excluded from proclaimed universalism). Perfect CCOT example.
- **[[Nationalism and Revolutions]]** ([[Unit-5.2]]) — Political change (popular sovereignty, new nation-states); social continuity (class and racial hierarchies preserved in most revolutions). The Haitian exception proves the rule.
- **[[Industrial Revolution Begins and Spreads]]** ([[Unit-5.3]], [[Unit-5.4]]) — Economic change (fossil fuel energy, factory production); economic continuity (private ownership, market exchange, profit-seeking — capitalism persists through transformation).
- **[[Technology in the Industrial Age]]** ([[Unit-5.5]]) — Genuine technological rupture (fossil fuels, railroad speed, electric communication); but environmental continuity (human environmental exploitation intensifies rather than begins).
- **[[Society and the Industrial Age]]** ([[Unit-5.9]]) — Social change (new classes, urban life, public health); social continuity (gender hierarchy, class inequality, racial subordination).

---

## 2. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Unit 4 Overview]]** — The commercial capitalism, colonial trade networks, and Atlantic slave trade of Unit 4 are *directly continued* in Unit 5 — industrialization intensifies rather than replaces these systems.
- **[[Unit 3 Silk Roads and Indian Ocean Trade]]** — The long-distance trade networks of Unit 3 are the earliest form of the global commodity networks that industrial capitalism scaled up in Unit 5; the core-periphery pattern is as old as long-distance trade itself.
- **[[Unit 2 Agricultural Civilizations]]** — The agricultural majority that persisted through Unit 5 represents the longest and most fundamental continuity in human history — most people farmed throughout all of Units 1–5.

---

## 3. Sets Up (for Later Units)

- **[[New Imperialism]]** (Unit 6) — Industrial military and economic power + persistent core-periphery trade structure = formal colonization of Africa and Asia. Unit 5 creates the conditions; Unit 6 is the consequence.
- **[[World War I]]** (Unit 7) — Nationalist conflicts within and between industrial great powers, socialist class tensions, imperial rivalries — all seeded in Unit 5.
- **[[Russian Revolution]]** (Unit 7) — Witte's industrialization without democratization + Marxist ideology from Unit 5.8 = revolutionary pressure that explodes in 1917.
- **[[Decolonization]]** (Unit 8) — The Atlantic Revolutions' natural rights language and nationalist ideology of Unit 5.2 are the ideological tools anti-colonial movements use in the 20th century.
- **[[Climate Change]]** (Unit 9) — Fossil fuel combustion beginning in Unit 5.3 is the origin of the carbon emissions driving 21st-century climate change.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases for CCOT

- **Unit 4 Atlantic Revolutions vs. Unit 5 outcomes** — The promise of natural rights (Unit 5.1) vs. the reality of social hierarchies preserved through revolutionary change (Unit 5.2/5.10): the gap between revolutionary ideals and social reality is the central CCOT tension.
- **Britain in 1750 vs. Britain in 1900** — Every dimension of life changed: energy, production, urbanization, class structure, political rights, gender ideologies, medicine, communication. Yet capitalism, empire, patriarchy, and extreme inequality persisted. Britain is the ideal single-country CCOT study.
- **Non-industrialized regions in 1750 vs. 1900** — Continuity of subordination to European commercial and then industrial power; change in the *mechanism* (colonial administration, railroad extraction, commodity markets) rather than the *fact* of subordination.

> [!exam-tip]
> The single most powerful CCOT argument for AP essays on Period 3: **"The Industrial Revolution changed the *mechanism* of capitalism (from commercial to industrial) and the *basis* of political legitimacy (from divine right to popular sovereignty), but left the underlying structures of class inequality, gender hierarchy, and Western global dominance largely intact — changed in form but not in substance."** This thesis addresses change, continuity, and complexity simultaneously.
