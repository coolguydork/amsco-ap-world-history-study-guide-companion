# Key People and Groups — Unit 5.10: Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> This file focuses on figures who *illustrate* continuity and change patterns across Unit 5, rather than new figures introduced here for the first time. Use these entries to practice synthesis across the unit.

---

## Toussaint Louverture (continuity/change in slavery)
**Illustrates:** The *change* from legalized slavery to abolition and the *continuity* of racial hierarchy even after formal emancipation.

- The Haitian Revolution abolished slavery (1804) — the most radical legal change of the era.
- But Haiti was forced to pay France 150 million francs in "reparations" for former slaveholders' "losses" (paid until 1947!) — illustrating the economic continuity of colonial exploitation even after political independence.
- The persistence of racial discrimination, economic underdevelopment, and foreign intervention in Haiti after independence illustrates that legal abolition did not produce racial equality.

---

## Mary Wollstonecraft and Elizabeth Cady Stanton (continuity in gender hierarchy)
**Illustrates:** The *continuity* of patriarchal gender hierarchy despite Enlightenment natural rights principles.

- Wollstonecraft (1792) and Stanton (1848) both argued for women's equality using Enlightenment logic — demonstrating that the ideological tools for challenging gender hierarchy had existed for decades.
- Yet women's suffrage was not achieved in the USA until 1920, Britain until 1928 — illustrating that ideological argument alone cannot overcome structural power.
- The persistence of the voting rights struggle across 130 years (1792–1928) is itself a powerful CCOT example.

---

## Adam Smith and Karl Marx (continuity/change in capitalism)
**Illustrates:** The *continuity* of capitalism across the commercial-to-industrial transition, and the *change* in its specific form.

- Smith (1776) theorized commercial capitalism; Marx (1867) analyzed industrial capitalism — but both were analyzing versions of the same system of private ownership and market exchange.
- The *continuity*: private property, wage labor, and profit-seeking persisted from commercial to industrial capitalism.
- The *change*: the *source* of profit (production rather than trade), the *scale* (industrial vs. workshop), and the *class structure* (bourgeoisie vs. proletariat more clearly defined than merchant vs. peasant).

---

## Otto von Bismarck (continuity/change in governance)
**Illustrates:** The *change* in the form of governance (welfare state, national industry) and the *continuity* of elite control.

- Bismarck built a modern welfare state not to empower workers but to preserve aristocratic and bourgeois power — illustrating how institutions can change form while preserving underlying power relations.
- German unification (1871) changed the political map dramatically but transferred power from many princes to one kaiser — continuity of authoritarian governance in new national form.

---

## Japanese Factory Women (Meiji era) (continuity/change in gender and labor)
**Illustrates:** The *change* in women's economic roles (factory wage labor) and the *continuity* of their social subordination.

- Meiji industrialization employed women extensively in textile mills — a genuine change in their economic role.
- But their wages were lower than men's, their social status did not improve, and they were expected to return to domestic roles after factory work — continuity of patriarchal structure underneath economic change.

---

## Non-Industrialized Colonial Peoples (continuity of global inequality)
**Illustrates:** The *continuity* of European global dominance and the *new form* it took (industrial imperialism replacing commercial colonialism).

- The peoples of India, Africa, and Southeast Asia had been subject to European commercial and colonial power before industrialization; industrialization intensified this power rather than creating it.
- What *changed*: the mechanism of domination (industrial manufacturing advantage + railroad/telegraph military logistics, rather than just maritime commercial advantage).
- What *continued*: European extraction of resources and labor from non-European peoples; the basic core-periphery economic structure.
