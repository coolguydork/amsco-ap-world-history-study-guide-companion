# Unit 5.10: Continuity and Change in the Industrial Age

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

Topic 5.10 is the synthesis and CCOT (Continuity and Change Over Time) capstone for the entire unit. The Industrial Revolution and the Age of Revolutions (c. 1750–1900) produced some of the most rapid and consequential transformations in world history — but alongside dramatic change, important continuities persisted. **What changed:** The energy source of production (fossil fuels replacing human/animal power), the scale and organization of production (factory replacing cottage), the political basis of legitimate authority (popular sovereignty replacing divine right), the dominant social class (industrial bourgeoisie partially displacing landed aristocracy), and the geographic scope of economic integration (global commodity markets replacing regional trading networks). **What persisted:** Capitalism itself (industrial capitalism evolved from, not replaced, commercial capitalism), social hierarchy and inequality (new forms replaced old), patriarchal gender relations (separate spheres reconfigured but did not abolish gender hierarchy), Western dominance (industrial power extended European global reach rather than displacing it), and the fundamental patterns of global trade (core manufacturing regions and peripheral raw material suppliers — a pattern established in the 16th century). The AP exam's CCOT essays for Period 3 ask students to assess this balance of change and continuity across political, economic, social, technological, and environmental dimensions.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Synthesize the major changes produced by the Industrial Revolution and the Age of Revolutions.
- Identify significant continuities that persisted despite revolutionary change.
- Evaluate the relative significance of changes across different thematic lenses.
- Practice CCOT essay argument construction for Period 3.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.10.I** — In the period c. 1750–1900, industrialization and political revolution produced both dramatic transformations and important continuities across economic, political, social, and environmental dimensions.

> [!exam-tip]
> CCOT essays for Period 3 are among the most frequently assigned AP prompts. The key to a strong argument is: (1) a clear thesis that identifies BOTH change AND continuity (not just one); (2) specific evidence for each; and (3) a "complexity" argument that explains *why* something changed or what *drove* continuity. Practice writing topic sentences of the form: "While X changed dramatically, Y persisted because..."
