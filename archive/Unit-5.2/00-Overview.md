# Unit 5.2: Nationalism and Revolutions

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

Between 1750 and 1830, a wave of revolutions transformed the political map of the Atlantic World, all drawing in various ways on Enlightenment ideas of natural rights, popular sovereignty, and national self-determination. The **American Revolution** (1776) established the first modern republic, but preserved slavery and excluded women and the propertyless from full citizenship. The **French Revolution** (1789) was more radical — abolishing feudalism, declaring universal rights, and eventually executing the king — but its Terror and Napoleon's empire revealed the dangers of unchecked popular sovereignty. The **Haitian Revolution** (1791–1804) was the most radical of all: the only successful slave revolt in history to produce an independent state, it exposed the hypocrisy of Atlantic revolutionary idealism and produced the first Black republic in the Western Hemisphere. **Latin American independence movements** (1810s–1820s), led by creole elites like Simón Bolívar and José de San Martín, freed much of Spanish America but generally preserved existing social hierarchies. In Europe, **nationalism** — the idea that peoples sharing language, culture, or history should govern themselves — drove independence movements in Greece, Hungary, and Poland, and eventually produced the unified nation-states of Italy (1861) and Germany (1871). Together, these movements made nationalism the dominant political force of the 19th century.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how Enlightenment ideas inspired Atlantic Revolutions.
- Compare the causes, processes, and outcomes of the American, French, Haitian, and Latin American revolutions.
- Analyze how nationalism reshaped political boundaries in Europe and the Americas.
- Evaluate the extent to which revolutions fulfilled or failed their stated ideals.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.3.I** — Enlightenment ideas inspired resistance to existing political authorities and the development of new political ideologies and institutions.
- **KC-5.3.II** — Peoples around the world developed a new sense of commonality based on shared culture, history, or political aspirations.

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Haitian Revolution** is the single most tested revolution in this topic. Know: its connection to the French Revolution, Toussaint Louverture, why it terrified slaveholders worldwide, and why it represents the fullest application of Enlightenment ideals. The AP exam frequently uses the Haitian Revolution to test whether students understand both the reach and the limits of revolutionary ideology.
