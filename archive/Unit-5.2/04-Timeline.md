# Timeline — Unit 5.2: Nationalism and Revolutions

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** Between 1776 and the 1870s, the Atlantic World was swept by a chain of political revolutions and nationalist movements that collectively dismantled the old regime of hereditary monarchy and aristocratic privilege — and erected new political orders based (imperfectly) on popular sovereignty and national identity. This era is the political parallel to the Industrial Revolution happening simultaneously in Britain.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| 1776 | American Declaration of Independence | North America | **Change:** First modern republic; Lockean natural rights embedded in founding document; slavery preserved |
| 1781 | Battle of Yorktown; British surrender | North America | American Revolution's decisive military victory; French financial and military support critical |
| 1787 | U.S. Constitutional Convention | North America | Separation of powers, federalism established; compromise on slavery (3/5 clause); Montesquieu's influence visible |
| 1789 | French Revolution begins; Estates-General convened | France | **Change:** Absolute monarchy challenged; commoners assert political power |
| 1789 | Declaration of Rights of Man and Citizen | France | Universal rights of liberty, equality declared; women and colonized peoples excluded |
| 1791 | Haitian Revolution begins in Saint-Domingue | Caribbean | **Change:** Enslaved people rise against French colonialism; most radical Atlantic Revolution begins |
| 1791 | Olympe de Gouges writes Declaration of Rights of Woman | France | Feminist challenge to revolutionary exclusions; she is later guillotined |
| 1793 | Reign of Terror begins under Robespierre | France | Revolutionary violence; ~17,000 executed; illustrates dangers of unchecked popular sovereignty |
| 1794 | France abolishes slavery in all colonies | France/Caribbean | **Change:** First major emancipation; Napoleon later reverses this (1802) in most colonies |
| 1799 | Napoleon seizes power; Consulate established | France | Revolution ends; military authoritarianism replaces democratic republic |
| 1801 | Toussaint Louverture negotiates autonomy for Saint-Domingue | Caribbean | Brief recognition of self-governance; Napoleon undermines it the following year |
| 1803 | Toussaint Louverture dies in French prison | France | Martyr figure; Haitian resistance continues under Dessalines |
| 1804 | Haiti declares independence | Caribbean | **Change:** First Black republic in Western Hemisphere; only successful slave revolt producing independent state |
| 1804 | Napoleon crowns himself Emperor | France | **Continuity:** Revolutionary France reverts to empire; liberal ideals subordinated to imperial ambition |
| 1808 | Napoleon invades Spain; triggers Latin American revolts | Latin America | Removal of Spanish authority creates power vacuum; creole elites seize opportunity |
| 1810 | Mexican independence movement begins (Hidalgo's revolt) | Mexico | Initially a mass movement of Indigenous and mestizo peoples; later suppressed; independence achieved 1821 |
| 1815 | Congress of Vienna restores conservative order | Europe | **Continuity:** Great Powers attempt to reverse revolutionary changes; monarchy restored across Europe |
| 1815 | Simón Bolívar writes *Jamaica Letter* | Caribbean | Articulates vision of Latin American independence; acknowledges limits of Enlightenment democracy |
| 1821 | Greek independence revolt begins against Ottomans | Greece/Ottoman Empire | First successful European nationalist independence movement of the 19th century |
| 1821–1824 | Bolívar and San Martín liberate South America | South America | **Change:** Spanish colonial empire in South America ends |
| 1830 | Greek independence recognized by Great Powers | Europe | Nationalism achieves first European success; sets precedent for later movements |
| 1848 | Revolutions across Europe ("Springtime of Nations") | Europe | Nationalist and liberal revolts in France, Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy; mostly suppressed by year's end |
| 1861 | Italian unification completed | Italy | Nationalist movement (Risorgimento) achieves unified Italian state |
| 1871 | German unification under Bismarck; Franco-Prussian War | Germany | **Change:** Nationalism reshapes European map; new great power emerges |

---

> [!tip] Continuity vs. Change
> **Continuity:** Social hierarchies — especially race and gender — persisted across all Atlantic Revolutions; even the most radical (Haiti) did not immediately achieve economic equality.
> **Change:** Political legitimacy fundamentally shifted from divine right and hereditary succession to popular sovereignty and national identity — a transformation that is irreversible even when conservatives temporarily restore monarchies.
