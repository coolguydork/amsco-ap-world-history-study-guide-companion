# Unit 2.1: Practice Questions — The Silk Roads

> [!exam-tip]
> AP World History MCQs about trade networks almost always use a primary source stimulus — a traveler's account, a map, or a merchant document. Practice reading these quickly and identifying what claim the question is asking you to support or challenge.

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Question 1**

*"I found at Zaitun [Quanzhou, China] about a hundred vessels of the large kind called junks and innumerable small ones. Zaitun is the port of China, and is among the greatest ports of the world."*
— Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*, c. 1345

The passage best supports which of the following conclusions about the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Chinese merchants had established diasporic communities throughout the Indian Ocean region
B) Long-distance maritime trade integrated China into larger Eurasian exchange networks
C) The Mongol Yuan Dynasty restricted Chinese participation in overland trade
D) Islamic merchants had replaced Chinese merchants as the dominant force in East Asian trade

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Ibn Battuta's description of Quanzhou's massive port activity demonstrates China's deep integration into maritime trade networks — this supports the broader AP theme of expanding exchange networks.

---

**Question 2**

Which of the following best explains why the Mongol Empire initially *disrupted* but ultimately *enhanced* Silk Road trade?

A) The Mongols banned all foreign merchants from Central Asian cities but later reversed the policy
B) The Mongols destroyed key trading cities during conquest but then profited from taxing trade under the Pax Mongolica
C) The Mongols shifted trade to maritime routes, which proved more efficient than overland travel
D) The Mongols adopted Chinese paper money, which stabilized regional currencies

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This is the classic Mongol trade paradox: conquest was devastating, but Mongol rulers recognized that taxing trade was more profitable than raiding, so they actively rebuilt and protected Silk Road networks.

---

**Question 3**

The spread of the bubonic plague along Silk Road routes in the 14th century is best understood as evidence that:

A) Trade networks were primarily responsible for spreading religious ideas across Eurasia
B) The integration of trade networks could carry harmful as well as beneficial exchanges
C) The Mongol Empire deliberately used disease as a military weapon
D) Maritime trade routes were more dangerous than overland routes in the 14th century

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The plague is the AP exam's go-to example of the *unintended consequences* of trade network integration. The same connectivity that moved silk and ideas also moved disease.

---

**Question 4**

Which of the following most directly contributed to the decline of the overland Silk Road after the mid-14th century?

A) The Chinese Ming Dynasty's ban on all foreign contact
B) The European development of superior maritime navigation technology
C) The collapse of Mongol political unity that had maintained route security
D) The depletion of Chinese silk production due to plague mortality

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation:* The Pax Mongolica was the structural condition that made the overland Silk Road thrive. When Mongol unity fractured (Yuan collapse 1368, Ilkhanate collapse mid-14th century), the route became dangerous and expensive again.

---

**Question 5**

*Caravanserai* along the Silk Roads are best understood as evidence of:

A) State investment in trade infrastructure to generate tax revenue and prestige
B) The dominance of Islamic merchants over Buddhist and Christian traders
C) The military control that Mongol rulers exercised over Central Asian oasis cities
D) The failure of private merchants to organize long-distance trade independently

**Correct Answer: A**
*Explanation:* Caravanserai were typically built by rulers who understood that protecting merchants meant taxing merchants — they're evidence of states actively managing trade for revenue, not just allowing it.

---

**Question 6**

The travels of both Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta in the 13th–14th centuries most directly demonstrate which of the following?

A) That European and Islamic travelers had equivalent access to Asian trade goods
B) That the Pax Mongolica made long-distance overland travel feasible for elites
C) That merchants were more important agents of cultural diffusion than missionaries
D) That the Silk Roads primarily connected Europe and China, bypassing the Islamic world

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Both travelers explicitly benefited from Mongol political stability — Marco Polo traveled under Mongol protection; Ibn Battuta traveled through Mongol successor states. Their journeys are evidence of the Pax Mongolica's effect.

---

**Question 7**

The spread of Islam, Buddhism, and Nestorian Christianity along the Silk Roads in the period c. 600–1450 is best explained by:

A) The military conquests of missionaries who forced conversion on trading partners
B) The voluntary adoption of merchant religions by local rulers seeking commercial advantages
C) The role of merchants and missionaries who used trade routes as vectors for religious diffusion
D) The displacement of local religions by the Mongols, who promoted universal tolerance

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation:* Religious diffusion along the Silk Roads worked through human mobility — merchants carried their faiths, missionaries used merchant routes for protection and supply, and sustained contact over time led to voluntary conversion.

---

**Question 8**

Which of the following best explains why Muslim merchants came to dominate Silk Road trade by the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Islamic law forbade lending at interest, making Muslim merchants more trusted by partners
B) The geographic centrality of the Islamic world between China, India, Africa, and Europe gave Muslim merchants natural advantages as middlemen
C) Mongol rulers preferred Muslim merchants because they shared a common Turkic cultural heritage
D) Islamic states militarily suppressed competing Sogdian and Chinese merchant communities

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Islamic world's geographic position at the crossroads of Eurasian and African trade networks made Muslim merchants effective intermediaries — they were closest to every major trading zone simultaneously.

---

**Question 9**

The commercial innovations of the Silk Road period (bills of exchange, merchant partnerships, credit networks) most directly addressed which challenge of long-distance trade?

A) The cultural barriers between merchants of different religious backgrounds
B) The risk and impracticality of transporting large amounts of coin across great distances
C) The unwillingness of rulers to protect foreign merchants on their territory
D) The shortage of luxury goods available for exchange at Silk Road markets

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Bills of exchange and credit systems were practical solutions to the dangerous and heavy problem of coin transport — they enabled trust-based commerce across distances where legal enforcement was impossible.

---

**Question 10**

Which of the following pairs of outcomes best illustrates the *dual consequences* of Silk Road network integration in the period c. 1200–1450?

A) Expansion of Buddhist monasteries and decline of Confucian scholarship
B) Increase in long-distance trade volume and spread of the bubonic plague
C) Growth of Mongol political power and decline of maritime trade networks
D) Spread of Chinese paper money and decline of gold-based currencies in Europe

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This is the central paradox of Silk Road connectivity: the same routes that moved wealth also moved disease. The AP exam regularly asks students to analyze both positive and negative consequences of exchange.

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

*Use the following prompt to write a short answer response:*

**"The Mongol Empire was both the greatest threat and greatest benefit to Silk Road trade in the period c. 1200–1350."**

**Part A:** Briefly explain ONE piece of evidence that supports the claim that the Mongol Empire *disrupted* Silk Road trade.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE piece of evidence that supports the claim that the Mongol Empire *facilitated* Silk Road trade.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which the effects of Mongol rule on trade differed across different regions of Eurasia.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Focus on the initial conquests — destruction of Samarkand, Baghdad (1258), Zhongdu — and the disruption of existing merchant networks
> - Part B: Focus on the Pax Mongolica — standardized weights, protected routes, caravanserai, the journeys of Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta
> - Part C: Consider how trade effects differed in China (Yuan integration), the Middle East (Ilkhanate, partial recovery), and Europe (indirect benefits via access to Asian goods through Mongol-controlled intermediaries)

---

### SAQ 2

*Use the following prompt to write a short answer response:*

**Evaluate the extent to which the Silk Roads were primarily a vehicle for commercial exchange, as opposed to cultural and religious diffusion, in the period c. 1200–1450.**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE piece of evidence that the Silk Roads primarily functioned as a commercial network.

**Part B:** Briefly describe ONE piece of evidence that the Silk Roads were equally or more important for cultural/religious diffusion.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which commercial and cultural exchange along the Silk Roads were *mutually reinforcing* rather than separate phenomena.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Volume of goods, commercial innovations, caravanserai as trade infrastructure
> - Part B: Spread of Islam, Buddhism, Nestorian Christianity; diffusion of Chinese technologies; the Black Death as cultural/demographic disruption
> - Part C: Merchant diasporas — Muslim merchant communities spread Islam *while* they traded; religious conversion built trust networks that enhanced commerce

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline

### DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which trade along the Silk Roads transformed the societies it connected in the period c. 1200–1450."**

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**

**Thesis:** A strong thesis should make a historically defensible claim that goes beyond restating the prompt. Example: "While Silk Road trade created significant commercial and cultural transformations — spreading Islam, diffusing technology, and enabling new forms of credit — its most transformative impact on societies was unintentional: the spread of the bubonic plague, which caused demographic collapse that reshaped political and social structures more profoundly than any exchange of goods."

**Possible Groupings (for document analysis):**
- Group 1: Evidence of commercial transformation (documents showing trade volume, commercial innovations, merchant prosperity)
- Group 2: Evidence of cultural/religious transformation (documents showing spread of Islam, Buddhism, art, technology)
- Group 3: Evidence of negative/unintended transformation (documents showing plague consequences, social disruption)

**Outside Evidence to Incorporate:**
- *Pax Mongolica* and its role in enabling Silk Road commerce
- Black Death demographics (1/3 of Europe, destabilization of Yuan, Ilkhanate)
- Specific technologies that diffused: paper, printing, gunpowder, compass
- Specific religions that spread: Islam via merchant communities, Buddhism via monk travelers

**Complexity Point Options:**
- Argue transformation was *uneven* — regions closer to Silk Road centers transformed more than peripheries
- Argue transformation had *both positive and negative* dimensions — connectivity brought plague as well as prosperity
- Connect to Unit 3: Silk Road disruption after Mongol collapse → European search for maritime alternatives → Portuguese exploration
