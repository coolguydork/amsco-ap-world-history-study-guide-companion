# Unit 2.1: The Silk Roads (c. 1200–1450)

**AMSCO Unit:** Unit 2 — Networks of Exchange
**AP Exam Period:** Period 1 (c. 1200–1450)
**Key Theme:** Overland Trade & Cultural Diffusion

## Summary

The Silk Roads were a vast network of overland trade routes connecting East Asia, Central Asia, South Asia, the Middle East, and Europe from ancient times through the early modern era. (Note: maritime trade routes — including the Indian Ocean network — are technically distinct from the overland Silk Roads, though they were complementary and interconnected; this chapter focuses primarily on the overland routes.) During the period c. 1200–1450, the Mongol conquests temporarily disrupted the Silk Roads before the *Pax Mongolica* reopened and intensified trade across Eurasia. Merchants, missionaries, diplomats, and travelers moved along these routes carrying not only silk, spices, and precious metals but also religions, technologies, crops, and diseases — most devastatingly, the bubonic plague. The Silk Roads were never a single road but a web of interconnected routes that made sustained contact between distant civilizations possible and permanent.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how the Silk Roads facilitated the spread of goods, technologies, religions, and diseases across Eurasia
- Analyze the role of merchants, missionaries, and other travelers in cultural diffusion along the Silk Roads
- Explain how the Mongol Empire affected trade along the Silk Roads
- Evaluate continuity and change in Silk Road commerce across the period c. 1200–1450

## AP World History Key Concepts

- **KC-2.1.I** — Improved transportation technologies and commercial practices led to an increased volume of trade and expansion of trade networks
- **KC-2.1.II** — The Mongol Empire, though often destructive, created conditions for sustained overland trade (the *Pax Mongolica*)
- **KC-2.3.I** — Bubonic plague spread along the Silk Roads, causing massive demographic and social disruption
- **KC-2.2.I** — New belief systems spread through trade networks and missionary activity along the Silk Roads
