# Timeline — Unit 5.6: Industrialization: Government's Role

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** Government policies shaped industrialization across the 19th century — from Britain's laissez-faire model (the exception) to the various forms of state-directed development in Germany, Japan, Russia, and the USA (the norm for late industrializers). The spectrum of government roles illustrates that there is no "natural" path to industrialization.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|-------|--------|--------------|
| 1776 | Adam Smith publishes *The Wealth of Nations* | Britain | Intellectual foundation for laissez-faire; argues free markets produce maximum prosperity without government direction |
| 1815 | Corn Laws enacted | Britain | Landed interests use Parliament to protect grain prices; urban workers pay higher food costs; class conflict over tariff policy begins |
| 1834 | Zollverein (German Customs Union) established | Germany/Prussia | **Change:** Prussian-led market integration; economic precursor to German unification; government creates the market |
| 1833 | Factory Act (Britain) limits child labor; creates inspectors | Britain | First state regulation of factory conditions; laissez-faire ideology begins to crack under humanitarian pressure |
| 1842 | Coal Mines Act (Britain) bans women and boys under 10 underground | Britain | State intervention in labor market to protect workers; public opinion drives reform |
| 1846 | Repeal of Corn Laws (Britain) | Britain | **Change:** Britain adopts free trade; industrial bourgeoisie defeats landed aristocracy in policy battle |
| 1861 | Morrill Tariff (USA) — high protective duties | USA | Federal government protects Northern manufacturing; direct government role in industrial development |
| 1862 | Homestead Act (USA) — 160 acres free to settlers | USA | Government allocates land to encourage westward settlement; railroad land grants follow |
| 1863–1864 | National Banking Acts (USA) | USA | Creates national banking system and currency; financial infrastructure for industrial capitalism |
| 1868 | Meiji Restoration; state-directed industrialization begins | Japan | **Change:** Most comprehensive state direction of industrialization in 19th century; abolishes feudalism |
| 1869 | US Transcontinental Railroad completed; government-subsidized | USA | ~170 million acres in land grants; demonstrates active US government role despite laissez-faire ideology |
| 1871 | German unification; Bismarck as Chancellor | Germany | Unified state can now pursue national industrial policy |
| 1871 | Iwakura Mission sent abroad to study Western institutions | Japan | State invests in learning to accelerate industrialization; ~50 senior officials and 60 students study in USA and Europe |
| 1873 | Depression ("Long Depression") begins globally | World | Economic crisis drives European governments toward protective tariffs to shield domestic industries |
| 1879 | Bismarck's "iron and rye" tariff coalition in Germany | Germany | **Change:** Germany adopts high protective tariffs; coalition of heavy industry and Junker landowners |
| 1881–1889 | Meiji government sells state enterprises to zaibatsu | Japan | State initiates, then transfers to private conglomerates at favorable prices; creates close state-business ties |
| 1883 | Bismarck's Health Insurance Act (Germany) | Germany | **Change:** World's first national health insurance; government uses welfare to undercut socialist movement |
| 1884 | Bismarck's Accident Insurance Act (Germany) | Germany | Employer-funded; extends state protection to injured workers |
| 1889 | Bismarck's Old Age/Disability Pension Act (Germany) | Germany | **Change:** First national pension system; welfare state model established; workers 70+ eligible |
| 1891 | Trans-Siberian Railroad begins construction (Russia) | Russia | State-directed mega-project; opens Siberia; French capital used |
| 1892 | Witte becomes Russia's Finance Minister | Russia | Aggressive state-directed industrialization begins; Russia's industrial big push |
| 1906 | Pure Food and Drug Act, Hepburn Act (USA) | USA | **Change:** Progressive Era begins; US government expands regulatory role after decades of industrial abuses |

---

> [!tip] Continuity vs. Change
> **Continuity:** Property rights protection and contract enforcement (the basic rule of law) was provided by government in *all* industrializing nations; even the most laissez-faire states were not anarchies.
> **Change:** The *extent* of government direction — from Britain's minimal approach to Japan's comprehensive state capitalism — varied enormously and had lasting structural consequences for each nation's industrial character.
