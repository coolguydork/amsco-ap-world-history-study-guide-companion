## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–2.**

> *"It was my intention to bribe the working classes, or shall I say, to win them over to regard the state as a social institution existing for their sake and interested in their welfare."*
> — Otto von Bismarck, reflecting on his social welfare legislation, 1884

---

**1.** Bismarck's statement most directly reveals which of the following motivations for his social welfare legislation?

- A) Genuine humanitarian concern for the well-being of German workers
- B) A strategic political goal of reducing support for socialist and labor movements
- C) Economic theory suggesting that healthier workers would be more productive
- D) Pressure from German industrialists who wanted a healthier workforce

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Bismarck explicitly states his goal was to "win over" workers — to make them dependent on the state rather than attracted to socialist parties that promised more radical change.*

---

**2.** Bismarck's social welfare legislation is most significant in AP World History because it:

- A) Was adopted as a model by other European nations and eventually became the basis for modern welfare states
- B) Successfully eliminated socialist political movements in Germany
- C) Was the first time any government had provided economic assistance to citizens
- D) Proved that capitalism could not survive without significant redistribution of wealth

**✅ Correct Answer: A**
*Germany's social insurance model — health, accident, and old-age coverage — became the template for welfare states across Europe and eventually worldwide; it was the first systematic effort to use state institutions to manage the social consequences of industrial capitalism.*

---

**3.** Britain's repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846 most directly reflected:

- A) The government's recognition that agricultural tariffs were causing food shortages
- B) The political victory of the industrial bourgeoisie over landed aristocratic interests
- C) Britain's attempt to encourage agricultural imports from its colonial empire
- D) The influence of socialist ideology on British economic policy

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Corn Laws debate was a class conflict: landowners wanted protected grain prices; manufacturers wanted cheap food (lower wages) and open markets for their exports. The repeal marked the industrial middle class's decisive political victory.*

---

**4.** The American government's land grants to railroad corporations in the 1860s most directly illustrates which of the following?

- A) The United States government had fully embraced socialist economic principles by the Civil War era
- B) Despite laissez-faire ideology, the US government actively subsidized private industrial development
- C) Private railroads were unable to raise capital from investors without government guarantees
- D) Congress believed that public ownership of railroads was preferable to private ownership

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The land grant system — transferring public land worth billions to private railroad corporations — is a classic example of the gap between laissez-faire ideology and actual US government policy, which was deeply interventionist in facilitating industrial development.*

---

**5.** Which of the following best explains why Japan's Meiji government took a more active role in industrialization than Britain's government had a century earlier?

- A) Japan had a stronger socialist movement that demanded government economic leadership
- B) Japan lacked the private capital and faced foreign competition that made state initiation necessary
- C) The Meiji government believed that private enterprise was inherently inefficient
- D) Britain's laissez-faire model had already proven unsuccessful and Japan chose a different approach

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Late industrializers faced a qualitatively different problem than Britain: they had to compete with already-established British manufacturers. Without tariff protection and state subsidies, private Japanese capital could not have built competitive industries fast enough to prevent colonization.*

---

**6.** The concept of Social Darwinism was most commonly used in the late 19th century to justify which of the following?

- A) Government regulation of monopolistic corporations that were unfairly eliminating competition
- B) Opposition to labor unions, welfare legislation, and government regulation of business
- C) Nationalist movements in Europe seeking self-determination for ethnic minorities
- D) State-directed industrialization as a means of strengthening national competitive power

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Social Darwinists argued that economic competition naturally selected the "fittest" individuals and corporations; welfare and regulation interfered with this natural process and therefore harmed society by keeping "unfit" people alive or allowing "inferior" businesses to survive.*

---

**7.** The British government's imposition of free trade policies on colonial India most directly resulted in:

- A) India's rapid industrialization as cheap imported machines enabled factory construction
- B) The deindustrialization of India's textile sector as Lancashire cloth undercut local handloom weavers
- C) Increased revenue for the Indian colonial government from import tariffs
- D) Greater Indian political autonomy as British manufacturers no longer needed to control Indian trade

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Britain imposed free trade on India while maintaining protective tariffs for its own manufacturers when needed; the result was the destruction of India's world-leading textile industry as cheap Lancashire cotton flooded the Indian market.*

---

**8.** Sergei Witte's industrialization strategy for Russia in the 1890s most closely resembled which of the following?

- A) Britain's laissez-faire approach, relying on private entrepreneurs and minimal state direction
- B) Japan's Meiji model of state-directed industrial development using foreign expertise and investment
- C) Germany's Bismarckian approach of protective tariffs combined with social welfare legislation
- D) The American model of government land grants to private corporations

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Like the Meiji government, Witte used state power to direct industrial development, attracted foreign investment (French rather than Western broadly), built state railroads, and concentrated heavy industry — though without Japan's institutional reforms.*

---

**9.** Germany's "iron and rye" tariff coalition of 1879 was significant because it:

- A) United the interests of industrial manufacturers and agricultural landowners behind protective trade policy
- B) Demonstrated Bismarck's commitment to free trade principles inherited from Britain
- C) Primarily benefited German workers by reducing food prices
- D) Led to conflict with France over trade routes through Alsace-Lorraine

**✅ Correct Answer: A**
*The "iron and rye" coalition — steel manufacturers + Junker grain growers — was a political alliance of industrial and agricultural interests both seeking tariff protection; it dominated German economic policy for decades.*

---

**10.** The fundamental difference between Britain's and Germany's approaches to industrialization can best be summarized as:

- A) Britain used tariffs to protect domestic industry; Germany relied on free trade
- B) Britain industrialized through private entrepreneurship with minimal state direction; Germany used state investment, tariffs, and social welfare to manage industrial development
- C) Britain focused on heavy industry while Germany specialized in textiles and light manufacturing
- D) Britain's government owned the major industrial corporations while Germany's were privately held

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*This is the core comparative insight: Britain (first mover, laissez-faire, private enterprise) vs. Germany (late industrializer, state direction, social welfare, protective tariffs).*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Analyze how governments in TWO of the following countries shaped industrialization: Britain, Germany, Japan."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE specific government policy in each of your chosen countries that promoted industrialization.
**Part B:** Explain ONE similarity in how these two governments approached industrialization.
**Part C:** Explain ONE significant difference in their approaches and explain why the difference existed.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Best pair for contrast:** Britain and Japan — maximum difference in government role.
> - **Part A:** Britain: enclosure acts, patent law, repeal of Corn Laws. Japan: state-owned factories, Iwakura Mission, zaibatsu system. Germany: Zollverein, iron-rye tariff, Bismarck's welfare legislation.
> - **Part C:** Japan industrialized 100 years after Britain, under imperial threat, without private capital — state direction was a necessity, not a preference.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Evaluate the claim that 'free market' industrialization in the 19th century was actually dependent on significant government support."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE example of government policy that supported industrialization in Britain or the United States despite a laissez-faire ideology.
**Part B:** Explain how this government support contradicts the laissez-faire ideology that industrialists claimed to follow.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why industrialists continued to advocate for laissez-faire despite benefiting from government support.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Enclosure Acts (Britain) — government used Parliament to dispossess peasants for industrial labor supply. Land grants to railroads (USA) — government transferred 170M acres of public land to private corporations.
> - **Part B:** These were massive government interventions in favor of capital — not neutral market operation. Laissez-faire meant "no regulation of us" not "no help for us."
> - **Part C:** Industrialists wanted government help when it benefited capital (enclosure, land grants, patent protection) but opposed regulation when it cost them (Factory Acts, union rights, safety regulations). "Laissez-faire" was an ideology of convenience.
