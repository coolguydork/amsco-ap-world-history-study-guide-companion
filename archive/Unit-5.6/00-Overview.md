# Unit 5.6: Industrialization: Government's Role

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

Industrialization did not happen in a political vacuum. Governments shaped industrialization through their policies — and different states adopted dramatically different approaches, from Britain's minimal laissez-faire model to Japan's comprehensive state direction. At one end of the spectrum, Britain's early industrialization was driven almost entirely by private entrepreneurs, with the state providing only basic legal infrastructure (property rights, contract law, patents) and refusing to regulate factory conditions. At the other end, Japan's Meiji government built state-owned factories, banks, railroads, and schools; hired thousands of Western experts; sent students abroad; and then transferred state enterprises to private conglomerates. Between these poles, Germany used protective tariffs, state-linked investment banks, and state-owned railroads to accelerate private industrialization; the United States provided enormous land grants and tariff protection while leaving production to private enterprise; and Russia used state railroad construction and French investment to create industrial centers controlled by the autocracy. The key insight for the AP exam is that there is **no single "natural" path** to industrialization — government choice about tariffs, investment, property rights, and labor regulation shaped which nations industrialized, when, and in what form.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Compare the roles of government in industrialization across Britain, Germany, Japan, Russia, and the United States.
- Analyze the ideological debate between laissez-faire liberalism and state-directed industrialization.
- Explain how government policies shaped the timing, pace, and character of industrialization in different nations.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.1.II** — Governments played varying roles in industrialization, from laissez-faire to active state direction.
- **KC-5.6.I** — States developed policies to promote industrialization, including protective tariffs, railroad subsidies, and investment in infrastructure.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently asks you to **compare government roles** in industrialization. Know the spectrum: Britain (minimal) → USA (moderate) → Germany (significant) → Japan (dominant). Be ready to explain *why* each government took its particular approach — not just what it did.
