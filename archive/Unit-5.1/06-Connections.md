# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 5.1: The Enlightenment

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Scientific Revolution]]** (Unit 4 era) — Newton's rational laws of the physical universe provided the model Enlightenment thinkers applied to human society; Enlightenment *is* the Social Scientific Revolution.
- **[[Renaissance Humanism]]** (Unit 3–4) — The Renaissance's recovery of classical ideas about human potential and civic virtue was an intellectual precursor to Enlightenment secularism.
- **[[Protestant Reformation]]** (Unit 4) — Challenged papal authority and promoted individual interpretation of scripture; contributed to the fragmentation of religious authority that made Enlightenment secularism possible.
- **[[Print Culture and the Printing Press]]** ([[Unit-4.1]]) — Gutenberg's press made the *Encyclopédie*, pamphlets, and newspapers possible; Enlightenment ideas spread through the same print infrastructure.
- **[[Atlantic Trade Networks]]** ([[Unit-4.4]]) — The commercial prosperity of Atlantic trade created the educated merchant class and leisure time that sustained coffeehouse culture and salon debate.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Nationalism and Revolutions]]** ([[Unit-5.2]]) — Enlightenment ideas are the direct intellectual cause of the American, French, Haitian, and Latin American revolutions; 5.1 is the prerequisite for 5.2.
- **[[Industrial Revolution]]** ([[Unit-5.3]]) — Enlightenment empiricism and faith in rational progress created the intellectual environment for systematic technological experimentation; Adam Smith's free-market ideas justified industrial capitalism.
- **[[Reactions to the Industrial Economy]]** ([[Unit-5.8]]) — Marxism, socialism, and labor movements all engage (and often critique) Enlightenment liberalism; they cannot be understood without it.
- **[[New Imperialism]]** (Unit 6) — European powers justified colonial rule partly through Enlightenment-derived ideas of "civilizing missions" and racial hierarchy — a dark application of the same rationalism.
- **[[Feminism and Women's Suffrage]]** (Units 5–6) — Wollstonecraft's work initiated a tradition of feminist thought that built on and challenged Enlightenment universalism throughout the 19th century.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Enlightenment in Europe vs. Intellectual Traditions Elsewhere** — The Enlightenment was specifically a Western European movement; contrast with contemporaneous intellectual traditions in the Ottoman Empire (tanzimat reforms came later, partly in response to European power) and Qing China (which did not undergo an equivalent internal critique of imperial authority).
- **French vs. British Enlightenment** — The British Enlightenment (Locke, Smith, Hume) was more empiricist and politically moderate; the French Enlightenment (Voltaire, Rousseau, Diderot) was more radical in its critique of Church and monarchy — explaining why France had a revolution and Britain did not.
- **Male vs. Female Enlightenment figures** — Compare Rousseau's *Émile* (which prescribes a passive, domestic education for women) with Wollstonecraft's direct refutation: a classic AP comparison of Enlightenment contradictions.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam often pairs Enlightenment documents with revolutionary ones. Practice identifying which Enlightenment principle each revolutionary document most directly applies — and which groups it *still* excluded.
