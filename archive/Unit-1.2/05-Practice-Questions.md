# Unit 1.2: Practice Questions — Dar al-Islam (1200–1450)

## Multiple Choice Questions

**1.** *Stimulus:* "We entered Baghdad and found it a city of ruins. The canals were broken, the libraries burned. Of the scholars and scientists who had made this place the envy of the world, few remained."

This account most likely describes the aftermath of which event?

- A) The Crusader sack of Jerusalem in 1099
- B) The Mongol destruction of Baghdad in 1258
- C) The Black Death's arrival in the Middle East in the 1340s
- D) The Mamluk conquest of Syria in the 1260s

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258, which killed the last Abbasid caliph and destroyed the House of Wisdom, is described in contemporary sources using exactly this kind of language of civilizational destruction.*

---

**2.** The Mamluk Sultanate's defeat of the Mongols at Ain Jalut (1260) is historically significant primarily because it:

- A) Restored the Abbasid Caliphate in Baghdad
- B) Ended Mongol rule over Persia and Central Asia
- C) Halted the westward expansion of the Mongols into Africa and the Middle East
- D) United all Muslim rulers under a single political authority

**Correct Answer: C**
*Ain Jalut stopped the Mongol advance that had already reached Palestine. While it didn't undo Mongol rule in Persia, it prevented further expansion into Egypt and sub-Saharan Africa.*

---

**3.** Which of the following best explains why Sufism was particularly effective in spreading Islam to new regions?

- A) Sufi missionaries used military force to compel conversion in frontier regions
- B) Sufi practices adapted to local cultures, making Islam accessible without requiring complete rejection of pre-Islamic traditions
- C) Sufis controlled trade networks and required business partners to convert to Islam
- D) The Sufi message was identical to Buddhism, allowing easy conversion in Asian regions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Sufi adaptability — incorporating local music, language, and even some pre-Islamic customs — made conversion more accessible and less culturally disruptive than state-imposed Islam.*

---

**4.** The concept of Dar al-Islam is most useful for understanding which of the following about the Islamic world between 1200 and 1450?

- A) That Islamic states maintained strict political unity under the caliphate throughout this period
- B) That shared religious, legal, and commercial practices created cultural cohesion despite political fragmentation
- C) That Islam had spread to every inhabited continent by 1200
- D) That the Islamic world was isolated from other major civilizations by religious barriers

**Correct Answer: B**
*The Dar al-Islam is defined by shared religious practice, sharia law, and commercial networks — not by political unity, which had already fragmented well before 1200.*

---

**5.** *Stimulus:* "In every city I entered, I found mosques, madrasas, and lodges for Sufi travelers. Whether in Mali, India, or the lands of the Golden Horde, the learned men spoke Arabic and applied the same laws."

This passage, from a 14th-century traveler, best supports which of the following conclusions?

- A) Political unity of the Islamic world under the Mamluk sultanate
- B) The universality of the Sufi mystical tradition across all Muslim regions
- C) The cultural and religious cohesion of the Islamic world despite its political fragmentation
- D) The dominance of Arabic as the spoken language across all Muslim societies

**Correct Answer: C**
*Ibn Battuta's *Rihla* repeatedly emphasizes how Islamic legal, religious, and scholarly institutions created a coherent civilization across the politically fragmented Muslim world.*

---

**6.** The transmission of Islamic scientific knowledge to Europe during the medieval period most directly contributed to which of the following?

- A) The Protestant Reformation's challenge to Catholic authority
- B) The European Renaissance and Scientific Revolution by preserving and advancing Greek learning
- C) The Crusades by giving Europeans superior military technology
- D) The Black Death by spreading medical knowledge that helped Europeans survive

**Correct Answer: B**
*Islamic scholars preserved Greek texts and made original advances in mathematics, astronomy, and medicine; these were translated into Latin during the 12th century and became the foundation of European university education.*

---

**7.** The Mongol rulers of the Ilkhanate in Persia eventually converting to Islam best illustrates which historical pattern?

- A) Military conquest always leads to the destruction of conquered cultures
- B) Conquered peoples frequently adopt the religion of their conquerors
- C) Politically powerful minorities can be culturally absorbed by the populations they rule
- D) Islam was so militarily powerful that no empire could resist its spread

**Correct Answer: C**
*The Mongol conversion to Islam in Persia is a classic example of conquerors being absorbed by the cultures they conquered — the same pattern appears with the Yuan Dynasty in China.*

---

**8.** The trans-Saharan trade network between 1200 and 1450 primarily linked which commodities?

- A) Spices from Southeast Asia and textiles from the Byzantine Empire
- B) West African gold and salt, North African manufactured goods and Islamic scholarship
- C) Chinese silk and Indian cotton with Mediterranean wine and olive oil
- D) East African ivory and slaves with Middle Eastern horses and weapons

**Correct Answer: B**
*The trans-Saharan exchange was fundamentally about West African gold going north, Saharan salt going south, and manufactured goods and Islamic culture traveling in both directions.*

---

**9.** Which of the following best explains why the Abbasid Caliphate failed to mount an effective military resistance to the Mongol invasion?

- A) The caliph had converted to Christianity and lost the support of Muslim warriors
- B) The caliphate's real military power had been delegated to Turkish and Persian military commanders who did not coordinate effectively
- C) The caliphate had signed a peace treaty with the Mongols prior to the invasion
- D) Crusader armies from Europe had already destroyed the caliphate's military capacity

**Correct Answer: B**
*By 1258, the Abbasid caliph was a symbolic figurehead while real military power was held by fragmented Turkish and Persian forces that failed to unite against the Mongol threat.*

---

**10.** Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324–1325 best demonstrates which of the following?

- A) The political dominance of West African states over the Middle East
- B) The role of the hajj as a mechanism for economic and cultural exchange across the Islamic world
- C) That West African rulers had abandoned traditional religious practices for orthodox Sunni Islam
- D) The Mamluk Sultanate's control over all access to Mecca

**Correct Answer: B**
*Mansa Musa's pilgrimage carried enormous quantities of gold (disrupting Egyptian gold prices), brought Mali to the attention of Mediterranean cartographers, and returned with architects and scholars who transformed the Mali Empire — a perfect example of the hajj as global exchange.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1
*"The period 1200–1450 witnessed both the greatest catastrophe and unexpected resilience of Islamic civilization."*

**a)** Identify and explain ONE catastrophe that severely damaged Islamic civilization between 1200 and 1450.

**b)** Identify and explain ONE way Islamic civilization demonstrated resilience or recovery during this same period.

**c)** Explain ONE continuity in Islamic civilization that survived despite the catastrophes of this period.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Mongol sack of Baghdad (1258), destruction of the Abbasid Caliphate, and the House of Wisdom — with specific details
> - (b) Mamluk victory at Ain Jalut; emergence of new Islamic centers (Cairo, Delhi, Timurid Samarkand); Mongol rulers converting to Islam
> - (c) Sharia, the ulama, the Arabic scholarly language, Indian Ocean trade networks, or Sufi missionary activity

---

### SAQ 2
**a)** Explain ONE way in which Sufism contributed to the spread of Islam between 1200 and 1450.

**b)** Explain ONE way in which trade networks contributed to the spread of Islam between 1200 and 1450.

**c)** Explain ONE difference between the spread of Islam through Sufism versus through trade networks.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring response would include:**
> - (a) Sufi missionaries adapted to local cultures in sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, and Southeast Asia, making conversion accessible without requiring complete cultural transformation
> - (b) Muslim merchants established mosques and communities in port cities along the Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan routes; rulers converted to gain commercial advantages
> - (c) Sufi spread was often rural and involved personal religious conversion; trade spread was often urban and linked to commercial incentives — both could coexist in the same region

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Prompt

**Prompt:** Evaluate the extent to which the Mongol conquests of the 13th century destroyed Islamic civilization.

> [!tip]
> **Top-scoring DBQ outline would include:**
> - **Thesis:** A nuanced claim — e.g., "While the Mongol invasions caused catastrophic destruction of Islamic political institutions, urban infrastructure, and intellectual centers, Islamic civilization demonstrated remarkable resilience, spreading geographically even as its political heartland was devastated."
> - **Body paragraphs should address:**
>   - Evidence of destruction: Baghdad 1258, irrigation systems, House of Wisdom, population loss
>   - Evidence of continuity: sharia, ulama, trade networks, Arabic language, Sufi missionary activity
>   - Evidence of recovery/adaptation: Mamluk protection of Islamic civilization, Mongol conversions to Islam (Ilkhanate), Timurid cultural renaissance
>   - Geographic expansion: Islam spreads to Mali, Southeast Asia, and deeper into Central Asia during the same period
> - **Complexity point:** The Mongol destruction created conditions for the rise of new Islamic powers (Ottomans, Mughals) that would eventually create the gunpowder empires of the early modern period
> - **HAPP analysis:** Distinguish between sources written in the immediate aftermath of destruction vs. retrospective accounts; consider the bias of Islamic scholars mourning the loss of Baghdad
