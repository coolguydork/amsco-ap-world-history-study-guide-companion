# Unit 1.2: Timeline — Dar al-Islam (1200–1450)

> **Big Picture:** The Islamic world in 1200 is the most cosmopolitan civilization on Earth — stretching from Spain to Southeast Asia, leading in science, mathematics, and trade. By 1450, the Mongol catastrophe has reshuffled Islamic political power, destroyed the Abbasid center, and yet failed to destroy Islamic civilization itself. New centers (Cairo, Delhi, soon Istanbul) have emerged, and Islam has actually spread further geographically through Sufi missionary work and trade.

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| c. 1200 | Abbasid Caliphate functions as symbolic center of Islam | Baghdad | Political fragmentation already underway; sultans hold real power |
| 1206 | Delhi Sultanate founded | India | **Change:** First major Islamic political power in South Asia; begins Islamicization of northern India |
| 1206 | Genghis Khan unifies Mongols | Central Asia | Sets in motion the conquests that will devastate the Islamic heartland |
| c. 1220s | Mongols begin conquering Central Asian Islamic states | Central Asia | Destruction of Samarkand, Bukhara — major Islamic cultural centers |
| 1250 | Mamluks overthrow Ayyubid dynasty in Egypt | Egypt | **Change:** Slave-soldiers seize power; Egypt becomes the new center of Islamic political power |
| 1258 | **Mongols sack Baghdad; last Abbasid caliph executed** | Iraq | **Major turning point:** End of the Abbasid Caliphate; destruction of the House of Wisdom |
| 1260 | **Battle of Ain Jalut** — Mamluks defeat Mongols | Palestine | First major Mongol military defeat; halts Mongol expansion into Africa |
| c. 1260s | Ilkhanate established in Persia by Hulagu Khan | Persia | Mongol rule over Islamic heartland begins; eventual conversion to Islam |
| 1270s–1280s | Ilkhanate Mongols begin converting to Islam | Persia | **Continuity:** Shows Islam's power to absorb conquerors |
| 1295 | Ilkhan Ghazan officially converts to Islam | Persia | Marks Mongol assimilation into Islamic culture in Persia |
| c. 1300s | Sufi missionaries spread Islam to sub-Saharan Africa and SE Asia | Africa / Asia | **Continuity and expansion:** Islam grows geographically even as Abbasid center was destroyed |
| 1312–1337 | Reign of Mansa Musa of Mali | West Africa | 1324 hajj demonstrates wealth of Islamic West Africa; spreads Mali's fame |
| 1325–1354 | Ibn Battuta's travels across the Islamic world | Global | Documents the extraordinary connectivity and cultural unity of the Dar al-Islam |
| 1336–1405 | Timur (Tamerlane) builds Timurid Empire | Central Asia | Muslim Mongol successor state; destroys Delhi Sultanate briefly; Timurid cultural renaissance |
| c. 1350s | Ottoman Empire begins expanding in Anatolia | Anatolia | **Change:** New Islamic power rising; will eventually conquer Constantinople (1453) |
| 1398 | Timur sacks Delhi | India | Temporary devastation of Delhi Sultanate; sets up Mughal founding later |
| c. 1400s | Islam spreads to Malacca and Southeast Asian port cities | SE Asia | Trade-based Islamicization; will accelerate in 16th–17th centuries |
