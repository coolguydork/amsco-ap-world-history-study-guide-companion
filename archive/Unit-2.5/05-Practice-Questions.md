## Multiple Choice Questions

**Questions 1–2 refer to the following passage:**

> "I arrived in Mali after a journey of 24 days... The women there have no modesty in the presence of men... they are like all the other women in their country in this respect. The women go about with uncovered faces. I was astonished at the laxity of the blacks in religious matters."
>
> — Ibn Battuta, *Rihla*, c. 1352

**Question 1.** Ibn Battuta's surprise at practices in Mali best illustrates which historical concept?
- A) The complete failure of Islam to penetrate West Africa
- B) The tendency for Islam to undergo syncretism when spread via trade routes rather than conquest
- C) Evidence that Mali rulers did not practice Islam
- D) The uniformity of Islamic practice across all regions it reached

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Ibn Battuta's shock reveals that Mali's Islam was syncretic — genuinely Islamic in some respects but retaining local customs regarding gender that he found inconsistent with stricter Arabian norms. This is the classic pattern of Islam spread via trade: conversion is real but adaptation to local culture occurs.*

---

**Question 2.** The author's perspective in the passage above is best understood as reflecting:
- A) A European Christian misunderstanding of Islamic practice
- B) A North African Muslim scholar's view of proper Islamic conduct
- C) A Chinese Confucian diplomat's observation of foreign customs
- D) A West African ruler's defense of local traditions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Ibn Battuta was a Moroccan (North African) Muslim legal scholar traveling through the Islamic world. His perspective reflects educated Sunni Islamic norms, making him an insider-critic rather than an outside observer.*

**Questions 3–4 refer to the following excerpt:**

> "The Chinese used a mixture of charcoal, sulfur, and potassium nitrate, which when ignited produced a violent explosion... This knowledge was brought westward along caravan routes by the 13th century, where it was refined and adapted for military use."
>
> — Modern historian summarizing technology diffusion

**Question 3.** The process described above is best characterized as:
- A) Tributary exchange between states
- B) Cultural diffusion via trade networks
- C) Deliberate state-to-state technology transfer
- D) Religious missionary activity

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The passage describes technology (gunpowder) moving westward via trade routes — a classic example of cultural diffusion, in this case of technology rather than religion or art.*

---

**Question 4.** The most significant long-term consequence of the diffusion described in the passage was:
- A) The immediate collapse of the Mongol Empire
- B) The ability of rulers in West Asia, South Asia, and Europe to build gunpowder empires
- C) The decline of Buddhism along the Silk Roads
- D) The end of the caravanserai system

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Gunpowder technology, diffused westward, enabled the Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal Empires (and eventually European expansion) — a dramatic long-term consequence of Silk Road cultural transmission.*

**Questions 5–6 refer to the following description of Malacca, c. 1430:**

> "In Malacca, merchants from China, India, Arabia, and the islands of Southeast Asia traded together. The harbor was filled with ships of every kind. The ruler had converted to Islam and welcomed Muslim traders with favorable terms."

**Question 5.** The situation described in Malacca most directly reflects:
- A) The Mongol Empire's direct control of maritime Southeast Asia
- B) How rulers converted to Islam partly for economic and political advantage
- C) China's tributary system controlling Southeast Asian trade
- D) Buddhist missionaries spreading their faith via the Indian Ocean

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Malacca's ruler's conversion to Islam, combined with favorable treatment for Muslim merchants, shows the intertwined economic and religious logic of conversion along trade routes — political/commercial advantage reinforced religious change.*

---

**Question 6.** Malacca's role as described above is best described by which term?
- A) Tributary state
- B) Caravanserai
- C) Entrepôt
- D) Madrasa

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: An entrepôt is a commercial center where goods are collected and redistributed, serving merchants from multiple regions — exactly what Malacca was in the Indian Ocean network.*

**Questions 7–8 refer to this image prompt:** *[Imagine a photograph of the Djinguereber Mosque in Timbuktu, built by Abu Ishaq al-Sahili after accompanying Mansa Musa from his 1324 hajj.]*

**Question 7.** The Djinguereber Mosque best illustrates which historical pattern?
- A) Chinese architectural influence on West Africa via the Silk Roads
- B) The spread of Islamic architectural traditions along Trans-Saharan trade routes
- C) European influence on West African building styles
- D) The decline of indigenous African religious traditions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The mosque was built by an Andalusian architect who came to Mali with Mansa Musa from his hajj — a direct example of Islamic cultural influence traveling via the Trans-Saharan pilgrimage/trade route.*

**Question 8.** The construction of the Djinguereber Mosque most directly resulted from:
- A) Mongol military conquest of West Africa
- B) Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca and his patronage of Islamic scholars and artists
- C) Portuguese exploration of the West African coast
- D) The spread of Buddhist architecture from India

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Mansa Musa's hajj brought an architect (al-Sahili) back to Mali, along with Islamic scholars, and his patronage funded the mosque — showing how pilgrimage served as cultural transmission.*

**Questions 9–10 refer to the following passage:**

> "The Sufi teachers did not demand that converts abandon all their old customs. They allowed the local spirits, ancestors, and festivals to continue, giving them Islamic names and interpretations. In this way, Islam spread peacefully into regions that had never heard a Muslim call to prayer."

**Question 9.** The approach described above is best characterized as:
- A) Islamic legal scholarship (ulama tradition)
- B) Sufi missionary syncretism
- C) Forced conversion under jizya
- D) Tributary diplomacy

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: The passage describes Sufi missionaries allowing local customs to continue under Islamic reinterpretation — the definition of religious syncretism, and the key explanation for Islam's peaceful spread in many regions.*

**Question 10.** Which region's experience of Islamic conversion is BEST explained by the process described in the passage?
- A) North Africa in the 7th century
- B) Persia under the Umayyad Caliphate
- C) West Africa and Southeast Asia, c. 1200–1450
- D) Spain under Moorish rule

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: West Africa and Southeast Asia are the primary AP World History examples of Islam spreading via merchants and Sufi missionaries with significant syncretism, as opposed to military conquest.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1
**"Trade routes during c. 1200–1450 served as pathways for the spread of culture as much as commerce."**

**Part A:** Identify and explain ONE piece of evidence that supports the claim above using the Silk Roads.

**Part B:** Identify and explain ONE piece of evidence that supports the claim above using the Indian Ocean trade network.

**Part C:** Explain ONE way in which the cultural transmission described in Parts A and B resulted in syncretism rather than wholesale adoption of outside cultures.

*Strong answers for Part A might cite: Chinese technology diffusion, Buddhist pilgrimage, Persian literary culture.*
*Strong answers for Part B might cite: Islam in Southeast Asia or East Africa, Indian artistic influence in Southeast Asia, diasporic merchant communities.*
*Strong answers for Part C should define syncretism and give a specific example like Sufi adaptation in West Africa or Hindu-Islamic synthesis in South Asia.*

## DBQ Outline: Cultural Consequences of Trade Networks

**Prompt:** "Evaluate the extent to which trade networks caused significant cultural change across Afro-Eurasia in the period c. 1200–1450."

**Thesis Framework:** Trade networks were the primary engine of cultural change in this period, spreading religion (especially Islam), technologies, languages, and artistic traditions across vast distances — though the nature of change varied by region and the degree of syncretism vs. transformation differed based on local circumstances.

**Document Categories to Expect:**
1. Primary sources showing merchants as cultural agents (Ibn Battuta, Marco Polo)
2. Visual evidence of architectural/artistic syncretism (mosque styles in West Africa, Buddhist-Hindu temple art in SE Asia)
3. Evidence of technology diffusion (accounts of gunpowder, paper)
4. Evidence of limits/resistance to cultural change (rulers who converted but maintained local customs)

**Body Paragraph Structure:**
- **P1:** Islam spread via Indian Ocean merchants to East Africa and SE Asia — use evidence of coastal mosque-building, Swahili language, Malaccan conversion
- **P2:** Technology diffusion via Silk Roads — paper, gunpowder, compass transforming receiving societies
- **P3 (Complexity/Nuance):** Cultural change involved syncretism, not simple replacement — Sufi missionaries, local adaptation, Ibn Battuta's observations of "impure" Islam

**Required Complexity:** Discuss a counterargument OR compare Islamic vs. Buddhist spread OR discuss a region where trade networks produced minimal cultural change.

> [!exam-tip]
> For DBQ scoring, always identify the document's historical situation (HAPP: Historical context, Audience, Purpose, Point of view). For Ibn Battuta, his purpose was to document what he saw for an Islamic audience, and his point of view as a legal scholar made him sensitive to deviations from orthodox practice.
