# Key Terms — Unit 4.1: (4.1): Technological Innovations

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

1. **Caravel** — A small, maneuverable Portuguese sailing ship with lateen sails and a shallow draft, ideal for coastal exploration and ocean crossings. Portuguese shipbuilders eventually developed a larger variant (sometimes called *caravel-redonda*) that added square sails for speed on open-ocean runs — but the AP exam uses "caravel" as the umbrella term for all these designs. (Its design made long-distance Atlantic exploration feasible for the first time.)

2. **Lateen sail** — A triangular sail mounted at an angle on the mast, enabling ships to sail into the wind (i.e., "tack"). Adapted from Arab dhow technology; essential for return voyages from Africa and the Americas against prevailing winds. Key to navigating variable Atlantic wind patterns. (The AP exam sometimes calls this a "triangular lateen rig" — same concept, same term.)

3. **Astrolabe** — An instrument used to measure the altitude of celestial bodies, allowing sailors to calculate their latitude at sea. (Adapted from Islamic astronomers; critical for open-ocean navigation without landmarks.)

4. **Magnetic compass** — A navigational instrument that uses Earth's magnetic field to indicate direction. (Originated in China, diffused through the Islamic world to Europe; essential for oceanic navigation.)

5. **Portolan charts** — Detailed nautical maps showing coastlines, ports, and hazards, developed by Mediterranean sailors in the 13th–14th centuries. (Improved accuracy enabled sailors to chart new coasts and plan return voyages.)

6. **Gunpowder** — An explosive mixture of saltpeter, charcoal, and sulfur, originally invented in China and diffused to Europe via the Islamic world. (Enabled European powers to develop cannons and firearms that gave them military advantages over non-gunpowder civilizations.)

7. **Cannon** — A large gunpowder artillery piece that transformed both land and naval warfare. (Allowed European ships to bombard enemy ports and vessels from a distance, enabling trading-post empires in Asia and the Americas.)

8. **Printing press** — Gutenberg's movable-type press (c. 1440), which dramatically reduced the cost of producing books and maps. (Accelerated the spread of geographic and scientific knowledge, fueling exploration and the Scientific Revolution.)

9. **Joint-stock company** — A business structure in which many investors pool capital, share profits, and limit individual risk. (Allowed the enormous costs of colonial ventures to be distributed; key to the [[VOC]] and [[British East India Company]].)

10. **Double-entry bookkeeping** — An accounting system developed in Renaissance Italy in which every transaction is recorded as both a debit and a credit. (Made complex, large-scale commercial ventures financially trackable; foundational to modern capitalism.)

11. **Stern rudder** — A rudder mounted at the rear (stern) of the ship, providing more precise steering than the earlier side-mounted rudder. (Improved maneuverability for ocean-going vessels, especially in difficult conditions.)

> [!exam-tip]
> On the AP exam, questions about technology almost always ask about **diffusion** — which civilization originated a technology and how it spread. Practice tracing the path: China → Islamic world → Europe for gunpowder and the compass.
