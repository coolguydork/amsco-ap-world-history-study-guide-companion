# Unit 2.2: Practice Questions — The Mongol Empire

> [!exam-tip]
> Mongol questions on the AP exam almost always require you to evaluate *both* destructive and constructive consequences. One-sided answers that describe the Mongols as only destroyers or only trade facilitators will lose points. Always acknowledge the paradox.

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Question 1**

*"For [the Mongols] conquered vast territories. They spoiled the earth. They destroyed the foundations of civilization. They devastated much of the inhabited world, and caused the death of millions."*
— Ibn al-Athir, Arab historian, 13th century

A historian seeking to challenge the perspective expressed in the passage would most likely point to which of the following?

A) The Mongols' conversion to Islam, which made them protectors of Muslim civilization
B) The role of the Pax Mongolica in enabling unprecedented trade and exchange across Eurasia
C) The Mongol defeat at the Battle of Ain Jalut, which limited their destructive reach
D) The survival of the Abbasid Caliphate in Egypt after the sack of Baghdad

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Ibn al-Athir's account emphasizes destruction. A challenge to this view would focus on the constructive consequences of Mongol rule — specifically, the Pax Mongolica and the trade networks it enabled. Answer D is factually incorrect (the Abbasid Caliphate in Baghdad was destroyed; a symbolic Abbasid line continued in Cairo but held no real power).

---

**Question 2**

The Mongol rulers of Persia (Ilkhanate) and China (Yuan Dynasty) both eventually adopted significant elements of the cultures of the peoples they conquered. This pattern most directly supports which of the following historical arguments?

A) Military conquest is ultimately less powerful than cultural influence in determining political outcomes
B) Conquerors often adapt to the administrative and cultural needs of the populations they govern
C) The Mongols were unable to maintain their nomadic identity once they settled in cities
D) Religious conversion was the primary mechanism by which conquered peoples resisted Mongol authority

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* This is a classic CCOT/causation question about the limits of conquest. The Mongols were militarily dominant but administratively dependent on existing bureaucratic traditions — they adopted what worked (Chinese bureaucracy, Persian administration, Islam) to govern effectively.

---

**Question 3**

Which of the following best explains why the Mongol Empire's collapse in the mid-to-late 14th century led to the decline of overland Silk Road trade?

A) Ming Dynasty China banned all foreign merchants from Chinese ports
B) The Black Death killed so many Silk Road merchants that trade could not be sustained
C) The *Pax Mongolica* depended on Mongol political unity, which fragmented after 1350
D) Maritime trade proved cheaper and safer than overland trade even before the Mongol collapse

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation:* The Pax Mongolica was a political achievement — it required unified Mongol rule to maintain route security. When political unity fragmented (Yuan collapse 1368, Ilkhanate collapse 1335), the protection that made Silk Road travel safe disappeared, driving trade toward maritime alternatives.

---

**Question 4**

The Mongol conquests of the 13th century most directly caused which of the following long-term consequences for Russia?

A) Russia's conversion from Orthodox Christianity to Islam
B) The development of Russian autocratic political culture through centuries of tributary status to the Golden Horde
C) The destruction of Russian urban centers so complete that Russia did not recover until the 19th century
D) The integration of Russia into the Chinese-led economic system of the Yuan Dynasty

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Russian principalities paid tribute to the Golden Horde for over two centuries. Moscow's rise to power was partly based on its role as tribute collector. Historians debate the extent to which this experience shaped Russian political culture toward centralized autocracy — a classic AP-style interpretive question.

---

**Question 5**

The Mongol adoption of Chinese administrative practices under the Yuan Dynasty and Persian administrative practices under the Ilkhanate are best understood as examples of:

A) The failure of Mongol military power to maintain authority over conquered populations
B) The universal appeal of Confucian and Islamic governance models
C) Pragmatic adaptations by conquerors to the administrative realities of governing settled societies
D) The Mongols' deliberate policy of preserving conquered cultures as a form of tribute

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation:* The Mongols adopted existing administrative systems because they worked and because nomadic governance models were inadequate for managing millions of settled farmers and city-dwellers. This is pragmatism, not ideology.

---

**Question 6**

The Battle of Ain Jalut (1260), in which Mamluk forces from Egypt defeated the Mongols, is historically significant primarily because:

A) It proved that gunpowder weapons could defeat Mongol cavalry
B) It marked the western limit of Mongol expansion and preserved the Islamic world in Egypt
C) It demonstrated that the Mongol army was militarily weaker than previously believed
D) It allowed the Abbasid Caliphate to be restored in Baghdad

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* Ain Jalut set the permanent western boundary of Mongol expansion. Egypt and the Mamluk Sultanate remained outside Mongol control, preserving an Islamic political center after the destruction of Baghdad. Answer D is incorrect — the Baghdad caliphate was not restored; a symbolic line continued in Cairo.

---

**Question 7**

Which of the following most directly contributed to the unintended spread of the bubonic plague in the 14th century?

A) Mongol rulers deliberately used diseased corpses as biological weapons
B) The Pax Mongolica created trade networks and mobility that carried plague from Central Asia westward
C) The collapse of the Yuan Dynasty left Chinese ports unregulated, allowing plague ships to dock freely
D) The destruction of Silk Road cities forced travelers to use new routes through plague-infested areas

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Pax Mongolica is both the greatest achievement and greatest catastrophe of the Mongol Empire — the same mobility and connectivity that enabled trade also enabled plague transmission. Note: Answer A contains a kernel of truth (the Mongol siege of Caffa may have involved catapulting plague victims) but the primary mechanism was commercial network transmission.

---

**Question 8**

The forced relocation of skilled artisans and scholars by Mongol conquerors most directly contributed to:

A) The permanent decline of intellectual and artistic traditions in conquered territories
B) The spread of technologies and knowledge across Eurasia through involuntary migration
C) The Mongol adoption of Buddhism as their primary religious tradition
D) The development of a unified Mongol cultural identity that replaced regional traditions

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* When Mongols captured Chinese engineers, Persian mathematicians, and Central Asian craftspeople and moved them across the empire, they inadvertently created a massive technology transfer network. This is a form of cultural diffusion through forced migration — different from voluntary diffusion but equally consequential.

---

**Question 9**

Which of the following outcomes in East Asia most directly resulted from the Yuan Dynasty's collapse in 1368?

A) China's permanent withdrawal from maritime trade and exploration
B) The restoration of Chinese-led governance under the Ming Dynasty and the eventual sponsorship of Zheng He's voyages
C) The disintegration of the Chinese civil service examination system
D) The spread of Mongol cultural practices into Korean and Japanese societies

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation:* The Ming Dynasty emerged from Chinese resistance to Yuan Mongol rule. The early Ming period included ambitious maritime expeditions (Zheng He), demonstrating that China reasserted itself as a major power after Mongol rule — though the Ming eventually restricted foreign trade in a dramatic reversal.

---

**Question 10**

Historians debate whether the Mongol Empire's overall impact on world history was primarily destructive or primarily constructive. Which of the following best represents a nuanced resolution of this debate?

A) The Mongols were primarily constructive because the Pax Mongolica enabled centuries of sustained trade
B) The Mongols were primarily destructive because the Black Death killed more people than any of their military campaigns
C) The Mongols' impact was deeply contradictory — their conquests were catastrophically destructive, but the Pax Mongolica they created became the most powerful driver of Eurasian exchange in the pre-modern era, with consequences (including the plague) that neither they nor anyone else intended
D) The Mongols' impact was primarily felt in East Asia and Central Asia; their effect on Europe and Africa was minimal

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation:* This is the AP exam's "complexity" standard applied to the Mongols. The correct answer acknowledges both the destruction and the integration, recognizes the unintended consequences, and avoids one-sided conclusions.

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"The Mongol Empire affected different regions of Eurasia in fundamentally different ways."**

**Part A:** Briefly explain ONE similarity in how the Mongol Empire affected China and the Islamic Middle East.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE difference in how the Mongol Empire affected China versus Russia.

**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why the long-term legacy of Mongol rule differed between China (which recovered under the Ming) and Russia (which remained shaped by tribute relationships for centuries).

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Both China and the Middle East experienced massive destruction during conquest (Baghdad 1258; Song Dynasty 1279) followed by a period of integration into Mongol trade networks
> - Part B: China was directly ruled by Mongols (Yuan Dynasty, Kublai Khan adopted Chinese governance); Russia was tributary — the Golden Horde extracted tribute but did not administer Russia directly
> - Part C: China had a pre-existing bureaucratic tradition (civil service examination) that provided administrative continuity and a framework for resistance (the Ming rebellion); Russia had a more decentralized political structure that was shaped rather than replaced by Mongol overlordship

---

### SAQ 2

**Use the following image description to answer the questions below:**

*[Imagine a 14th-century Persian manuscript illustration showing the Mongol siege of Baghdad, with soldiers at city walls, flames, and a caption in Arabic noting the death of the Caliph.]*

**Part A:** Briefly explain the historical context of the event depicted.

**Part B:** Briefly explain ONE way in which the event depicted changed the political situation in the Islamic world.

**Part C:** Briefly explain ONE continuity in Islamic civilization that persisted despite the event depicted.

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**
> - Part A: Baghdad was the capital of the Abbasid Caliphate and the symbolic center of the Islamic world; the Mongols under Hulagu Khan sacked it in 1258, ending the Abbasid line there
> - Part B: The destruction of Baghdad eliminated the Abbasid Caliphate as a unified political/religious authority; the Islamic world fragmented into regional sultanates (Mamluk Egypt, Delhi Sultanate, etc.) with no central caliphate
> - Part C: Islam itself continued to spread and thrive despite the political destruction; the Mamluk Sultanate in Egypt preserved Islamic scholarship and political authority; later Mongol rulers in the Ilkhanate converted to Islam

## Document-Based Question (DBQ) Outline

### DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which the Mongol Empire was a force for exchange and cultural diffusion in Eurasia during the period c. 1200–1400."**

> [!tip] **Top-Score Guidance**

**Thesis:** "Although the Mongol Empire's initial conquests caused catastrophic destruction, the Pax Mongolica created conditions for the most sustained Eurasian cultural and commercial exchange in the pre-modern era — transmitting technologies, religions, and diseases across the continent with equally profound consequences for all."

**Possible Groupings:**
- Group 1: Commercial exchange (documents showing trade volume under Pax Mongolica, merchant activity, caravanserai)
- Group 2: Cultural/intellectual exchange (documents showing religious tolerance at Yuan court, technology transfer, Persian-Chinese artistic fusion)
- Group 3: Destructive exchange (documents showing plague transmission, destruction of cultural centers like Baghdad's House of Wisdom)

**Outside Evidence:**
- Specific technologies that spread (gunpowder, siege weapons, paper money)
- Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta as evidence of Pax Mongolica connectivity
- The Black Death as the most consequential unintended exchange
- Forced migration of artisans as technology transfer

**Complexity Point Options:**
- Argue exchange was **uneven by region**: China and Persia experienced substantial exchange; Russia's tributary relationship produced less cultural integration
- Argue exchange had **both positive and catastrophic dimensions**: same network that spread technology spread plague
- **Compare to Unit 3**: The end of Mongol-enabled exchange drove European maritime exploration — the Age of Exploration is a long-term consequence of Mongol Empire collapse
