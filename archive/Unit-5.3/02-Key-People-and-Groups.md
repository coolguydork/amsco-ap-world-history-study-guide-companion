# Key People and Groups — Unit 5.3: Industrial Revolution Begins

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## James Watt (1736–1819)
**Region:** Scotland/England | **Period:** 1760s–1800s

- Improved Thomas Newcomen's steam engine by adding a separate condenser (1769) and rotary motion (1782), transforming it from a mine pump into a general-purpose industrial power source.
- Partnered with entrepreneur Matthew Boulton to commercialize the engine — illustrating that invention alone is insufficient; business partnership and capital are equally necessary.
- The unit of power (the **watt**) is named after him; he also coined the term "horsepower" as a marketing tool for comparing engine power to familiar draft animal work.

---

## Richard Arkwright (1732–1792)
**Region:** England | **Period:** 1760s–1790s

- Invented the **water frame** (1769), which mechanized cotton spinning and required water power — and therefore a factory setting near rivers.
- Built the first modern factory at Cromford, Derbyshire (1771), establishing the organizational model of the factory: centralized machinery, supervised workers, strict schedules.
- Sometimes called "the father of the factory"; illustrates how industrialization required both technological and organizational innovation.

---

## James Hargreaves (c. 1720–1778)
**Region:** England | **Period:** 1760s

- Invented the **spinning jenny** (c. 1764), a multi-spindle device that allowed one worker to spin 8 (later 80+) threads simultaneously, dramatically increasing productivity.
- His invention was initially met with hostility by spinners who feared unemployment — an early Luddite-style reaction that illustrates the human cost of labor-saving technology.

---

## Abraham Darby (1678–1717) and Family
**Region:** England | **Period:** 1700s

- Abraham Darby I developed **coke smelting** (1709), using purified coal to smelt iron ore rather than scarce charcoal.
- Abraham Darby III used the resulting cheap iron to build the world's first **iron bridge** (1779) at Coalbrookdale — a symbol of the new industrial age.
- The Darby family's ironworks illustrate how industrial innovation was often multi-generational, with each generation building on the last.

---

## The Urban Working Class (Proletariat)
**Region:** England | **Period:** 1760s–1850s

- Formed by the enclosure movement and rural-to-urban migration; included former agricultural workers, artisans displaced by machines, women, and children.
- Worked 12–16 hour days in dangerous conditions for minimal wages; subject to strict factory discipline (fines for tardiness, talking, or leaving machines).
- Their experience is documented in reform literature (*Oliver Twist*, Dickens), government reports (Factory Commission, 1833), and early labor organizing — providing the evidence base for both reform legislation and Marxist theory.

---

## The Industrial Bourgeoisie
**Region:** Britain (and later broader Europe/Americas) | **Period:** Late 18th–19th century

- Factory owners, mine operators, railroad investors, and merchants who controlled the new means of industrial production.
- Distinguished from the older landed aristocracy by the source of their wealth (commerce and production vs. land) and their political ideology (liberalism, free trade, parliamentary reform).
- Their political rise — culminating in the British Reform Act of 1832, which gave manufacturing cities like Manchester parliamentary representation — illustrates how economic power generates political power.

---

## The Luddites (1811–1816)
**Region:** England (Nottingham, Yorkshire, Lancashire) | **Period:** 1811–1816

- Skilled textile workers (framework knitters, weavers) who organized to smash labor-saving machinery they believed threatened their livelihoods.
- Named after a possibly mythical leader "Ned Ludd"; organized secretly and effectively; the British government deployed more troops against them than against Napoleon in Spain.
- The violent suppression of the Luddites — including execution and transportation to Australia — illustrates the lengths to which the state would go to protect industrial property.

---

## Matthew Boulton (1728–1809)
**Region:** England | **Period:** 1770s–1800s

- Birmingham manufacturer who partnered with James Watt to produce and market the improved steam engine through the Boulton & Watt firm.
- His Soho Manufactory was a pioneering example of large-scale organized production and precision manufacturing.
- Illustrates the essential role of the **entrepreneur** — the person who mobilizes capital, organizes production, and takes on commercial risk — as distinct from the inventor.
