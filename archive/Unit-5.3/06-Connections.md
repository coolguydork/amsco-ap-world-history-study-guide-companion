# Cross-Topic Connections — Unit 5.3: Industrial Revolution Begins

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Atlantic Trade Networks and Colonial Wealth]]** ([[Unit-4.4]]) — Profits from the Atlantic slave trade and colonial commerce provided the capital invested in British factories; Indian cotton supplied British mills.
- **[[Joint-Stock Companies and Commercial Capitalism]]** ([[Unit-4.5]]) — The financial institutions of commercial capitalism (banking, credit, insurance) were the direct ancestors of the investment mechanisms that funded industrialization.
- **[[Enlightenment and Scientific Revolution]]** ([[Unit-5.1]]) — Enlightenment empiricism and rational problem-solving shaped the inventor-entrepreneur culture; Adam Smith's free-market ideas justified laissez-faire industrialization.
- **[[Changing Social Hierarchies]]** ([[Unit-4.7]]) — The enclosure movement transformed Britain's rural social order, creating the landless proletariat that factories required.
- **[[Atlantic Revolutions]]** ([[Unit-5.2]]) — The American Revolution temporarily disrupted British markets but ultimately accelerated domestic production; French revolutionary turmoil gave Britain's manufacturers a geopolitical advantage.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Industrialization Spreads]]** ([[Unit-5.4]]) — British techniques, capital, and machinery diffused to Belgium, France, Germany, USA, and Japan; 5.3 is the source from which 5.4 flows.
- **[[Technology in the Industrial Age]]** ([[Unit-5.5]]) — The steam engine of 5.3 powers the railroads, ships, and heavy industry of 5.5.
- **[[Reactions to the Industrial Economy]]** ([[Unit-5.8]]) — The Luddites, labor unions, and Marxism are all direct responses to the working conditions created by the factory system of 5.3.
- **[[New Imperialism]]** (Unit 6) — Britain's industrial advantage gave it the military and economic power to impose informal and formal empire on non-industrialized regions; cotton mill demand for raw material drove colonization of Egypt and India.
- **[[Environmental History]]** (Units 5–6) — Coal combustion beginning in 5.3 is the origin point of the fossil fuel era and its long-term environmental consequences.

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Britain vs. India textile industry** — Britain mechanized and undercut India's handloom textile production, which had been globally dominant before industrialization. A classic example of how industrial advantage translates into economic domination.
- **Watt's steam engine vs. earlier energy sources** — Compare wind, water, horse, and human power with coal-fired steam: the key difference is that steam power is scalable, mobile, and not dependent on geography (rivers) or weather.
- **Enclosure movement vs. other labor supply mechanisms** — Other industrializing nations used different mechanisms: Russia used serf labor (abolished 1861); the American South used enslaved labor; Japan used rural-to-urban migration organized by the Meiji state. Each mechanism shaped the character of industrialization in that country.

> [!exam-tip]
> For comparative essays, Britain's industrialization is the baseline against which all others are measured. When describing how Germany or Japan industrialized differently, always explain *why* — the different mechanisms reflect different resource endowments, political structures, and timing.
