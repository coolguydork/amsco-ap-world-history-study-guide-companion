# Unit 5.3: Industrial Revolution Begins

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

The Industrial Revolution — the shift from agrarian, hand-production economies to machine-powered factory production — began in Britain in the mid-18th century and was essentially complete there by 1850. Britain's industrial takeoff was the product of a unique convergence of factors: abundant coal and iron deposits located near navigable waterways; capital accumulated from Atlantic trade and the slave economy; colonial markets for manufactured goods and sources of raw materials (especially cotton from India and the American South); an **enclosure movement** that displaced rural workers and created a landless urban labor force; a relatively stable political environment; and a culture of practical tinkering that linked inventors with entrepreneurs. The key industries were **textiles** (spinning jenny, water frame, power loom) and **iron production** (coke smelting replacing charcoal); the key technology was **James Watt's steam engine** (1760s–80s), which provided a mobile, scalable power source applicable to pumping, spinning, weaving, and eventually transportation. The **factory system** replaced cottage industry and the putting-out system, concentrating workers in large buildings under strict discipline and clock-time schedules — transforming not just production but the social rhythms of daily life.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Identify the specific conditions in Britain that enabled the Industrial Revolution to begin there first.
- Explain how key technologies of the early Industrial Revolution — the steam engine, spinning jenny, and power loom — transformed textile and iron production.
- Analyze how the factory system changed labor, social organization, and daily life.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.1.I** — Industrialization fundamentally changed how goods were produced and transformed economies, social structures, and political systems.
- **KC-5.1.II** — The Industrial Revolution began in Britain and spread to other parts of Europe and the world.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently asks you to explain **why Britain industrialized first** — know the specific factors (coal, capital, empire, enclosure movement, navigable rivers, stable government) and be able to explain *why each mattered*. A list without explanation earns no credit.
