# Practice Questions — Unit 5.3: Industrial Revolution Begins

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–2.**

> *"The factory system, in its essential features, was one of the most momentous innovations ever introduced into the working life of man. It broke up the old framework of domestic and rural industry by which the workman had retained some control over his conditions of labor… [and] drew men, women, and children into cities to labor for wages under the direction of another."*
> — J.L. and B. Hammond, *The Rise of Modern Industry*, 1925

---

**1.** The authors' argument about the factory system most directly supports which of the following interpretations of the Industrial Revolution?

- A) Industrialization primarily benefited workers by providing stable employment and higher wages
- B) The factory system destroyed workers' economic autonomy and transferred control of production to capitalist owners
- C) Industrialization was largely driven by workers' desire for regular wages rather than irregular agricultural income
- D) The factory system was an evolution of the domestic putting-out system rather than a revolutionary change

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Hammonds emphasize the loss of worker control over conditions of labor — a classic argument about the factory system's transformation of work from artisan autonomy to wage dependency.*

---

**2.** Which of the following developments most directly created the conditions described in the passage?

- A) The repeal of the Corn Laws (1846), which lowered food prices for urban workers
- B) The enclosure movement, which displaced rural workers and drove them to industrial cities
- C) The invention of the power loom, which made cottage weaving less profitable
- D) The Factory Acts, which regulated working conditions and created factory inspectors

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The enclosure movement created the landless urban proletariat described in the passage by forcing agricultural workers off common lands and into factory cities.*

---

**3.** Britain industrialized before other European nations primarily because:

- A) Britain had the most advanced scientific institutions in Europe in the 18th century
- B) Britain's government actively planned and subsidized industrial development
- C) Britain possessed a unique combination of coal, capital, colonial markets, and an enclosure-driven labor supply
- D) Britain had the largest population in Europe and therefore the largest domestic market

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*Britain's industrial takeoff resulted from a convergence of specific factors; no other European nation had all of them simultaneously. Britain's government was actually less interventionist than France's — making active planning the wrong answer.*

---

**4.** The Luddite movement of 1811–1816 is best understood as:

- A) A revolutionary political movement seeking to overthrow the British monarchy
- B) A rational economic response by skilled workers to the destruction of their livelihoods by machinery
- C) A religious protest against the secularization of British society during industrialization
- D) A middle-class reform movement seeking to regulate factory conditions

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Luddites were skilled framework knitters and weavers whose craft expertise was being rendered obsolete by machines; their machine-breaking was a direct economic response, not random violence or political revolution.*

---

**5.** James Watt's improvements to the steam engine were most significant in AP World History because they:

- A) Were the first application of steam power to industrial production
- B) Converted the steam engine from a specialized mine pump into a general-purpose industrial power source applicable across industries
- C) Made Britain's coal reserves commercially viable for the first time
- D) Allowed factories to be built in cities rather than near rivers for the first time

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Newcomen's earlier engine existed, but Watt's rotary motion (1782) made steam power applicable to any machinery, not just mine pumps — turning it into the universal engine of the Industrial Revolution.*

---

**6.** The enclosure movement's most significant contribution to British industrialization was:

- A) Increasing agricultural productivity and lowering food prices for urban workers
- B) Creating a surplus of capital that could be invested in factory construction
- C) Displacing rural workers and creating a landless urban labor force available for factory employment
- D) Driving British farmers to develop new crop rotation methods that reduced agricultural labor needs

**✅ Correct Answer: C**
*The enclosure movement's primary industrial contribution was creating the proletariat — workers without land who had to sell their labor. Without this labor supply, factories could not have operated.*

---

**7.** The development of coke smelting by Abraham Darby (1709) most directly enabled which subsequent development?

- A) The construction of canals and waterways to transport industrial goods
- B) Large-scale iron production independent of forest resources, providing the structural material for industrial machinery and infrastructure
- C) The development of the steam engine, which required iron components and high-temperature furnaces
- D) The enclosure of common lands by wealthy landowners seeking to maximize agricultural yields

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Coke smelting freed iron production from charcoal (and therefore forest) dependence, enabling production at industrial scale and providing the iron for machines, rails, bridges, and building frames.*

---

**8.** Britain's colonial empire contributed to the early Industrial Revolution primarily by:

- A) Providing forced labor that could be transferred to British factories
- B) Supplying raw materials (especially cotton) and markets for manufactured goods
- C) Training British engineers and inventors in colonial workshops
- D) Generating tax revenue that the government used to subsidize industrial development

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The colonial relationship provided both supply (Indian cotton, American slave-grown cotton) and demand (colonial markets for cheap cotton cloth) — making the Lancashire textile industry viable at industrial scale.*

---

**9.** Which of the following represents the most significant social change produced by the factory system in Britain?

- A) The elimination of the traditional aristocracy and its replacement by a meritocratic elite
- B) The creation of a new urban working class (proletariat) dependent on wages rather than land
- C) The rapid increase in literacy as factory workers required reading skills for their jobs
- D) The equalization of wages between men and women for the first time in history

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The creation of the urban proletariat — a class of people with no productive property who must sell their labor — is the defining social transformation of early industrialization.*

---

**10.** The Factory Act of 1833, which limited child labor and created factory inspectors, is significant because it demonstrates:

- A) That the British government had fully embraced laissez-faire economic principles by the 1830s
- B) That industrial capitalism created social problems severe enough to eventually require state intervention
- C) That working-class political organizing had achieved complete control over the British Parliament by 1833
- D) That factory owners voluntarily agreed to limit child labor before government intervention

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The Factory Act marks the beginning of the state's grudging acknowledgment that the market alone could not protect workers — a crack in laissez-faire ideology that widened through the 19th century.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain why Britain was the first country to industrialize."**

**Part A:** Identify ONE specific factor that gave Britain an advantage in industrializing before other nations.
**Part B:** Explain how that factor directly contributed to industrial development.
**Part C:** Explain ONE way Britain's colonial empire contributed to early industrialization.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Parts A/B:** Don't just list a factor — explain the mechanism. Coal deposits near navigable rivers → cheap fuel transport → low production costs → competitive manufactured goods. Capital from the slave trade → investment in machines and factories → scaled-up production.
> - **Part C:** The Indian cotton trade is the best evidence — Britain imported raw cotton, manufactured it into cheap cloth in Lancashire, and sold it back to India (destroying Indian textile manufacturing in the process).

---

### SAQ 2

**"Analyze the social consequences of the early Industrial Revolution in Britain (c. 1760–1850)."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE social group that was negatively affected by industrialization and explain how.
**Part B:** Describe ONE social group that benefited from industrialization and explain how.
**Part C:** Explain ONE reason why industrialization's social consequences became a major political issue in Britain by the 1830s.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Child labor, urban working class, handloom weavers, or Luddites all work. Be specific about the mechanism of harm.
> - **Part B:** Industrial bourgeoisie, but also consumers (lower prices for manufactured goods) or the middle class more broadly.
> - **Part C:** The Reform Act of 1832 giving industrial cities parliamentary seats created a political voice for the industrial bourgeoisie — who then used Parliament to pass Factory Acts partly to discipline the working class and partly as genuine reform.
