# Timeline — Unit 5.3: Industrial Revolution Begins

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

> **Big Picture:** Britain's Industrial Revolution (c. 1760–1850) was the most rapid and complete economic transformation in human history to that point. It built on centuries of commercial development, colonial wealth, and scientific culture — but its human costs were immediate and severe. By the 1840s, Britain was the "workshop of the world," producing half of the world's iron and cotton cloth.

| c. 1733 | John Kay invents the flying shuttle | England | Speeds weaving; creates demand for faster spinning — sets up spinning jenny |

| 1769 | Richard Arkwright patents the water frame | England | Requires factory setting near rivers; Arkwright builds first modern factory at Cromford |
| 1769 | James Watt patents improved steam engine (separate condenser) | Scotland/England | **Change:** Steam engine becomes commercially viable for the first time |

| 1775–1783 | American Revolution; British lose colonial markets | North America | Short-term disruption; long-term increase in domestic production to replace American manufactures |
| 1779 | Iron Bridge completed at Coalbrookdale | England | Symbol of new industrial age; world's first cast-iron structure of its kind |
| 1782 | Watt adds rotary motion to steam engine | England | **Change:** Steam engine can now power spinning, weaving, and other rotary machinery — not just pumps |
| 1785 | Edmund Cartwright patents the power loom | England | Automates weaving; takes decades to improve to commercial viability; devastating to handloom weavers |
| 1800–1830 | Parliamentary Enclosure Acts peak | England | Rural workers displaced; urban labor force grows; accelerates migration to factory cities |
| 1801 | First census; Britain's population: ~10 million | Britain | **Continuity:** Most Britons still rural; urbanization is accelerating but not yet dominant |
| 1811–1816 | Luddite uprisings smash machinery | England | Worker resistance to industrialization; violently suppressed by state; illustrates class conflict |
| 1815 | Corn Laws protect landowners; raise food prices for workers | Britain | Political economy of early industrialization: landowners vs. manufacturers; later repealed 1846 |
| 1820s | Steam-powered factories become widespread in textiles | Britain | **Change:** Steam engine replaces water power; factories no longer limited to riversides |
| 1830 | Liverpool–Manchester Railway opens | Britain | **Change:** First intercity steam railroad; transforms transportation and industrialization's spatial reach |

| 1842 | Coal Mines Act bans women and boys under 10 from underground work | Britain | Reveals scale of child and female labor; produces middle-class reform response |
| c. 1850 | Britain produces ~half world's iron and cotton cloth | Britain | Peak of British industrial dominance; "workshop of the world" |

---

> [!tip] Continuity vs. Change
> **Change:** The pace and scale of production, the organization of labor (factory vs. cottage), and the energy source (coal/steam vs. human/animal/water) are genuinely unprecedented.
> **Continuity:** Merchant capitalism, credit systems, and long-distance trade networks that supported industrialization had existed for centuries; industrialization *intensified* rather than created capitalism.
