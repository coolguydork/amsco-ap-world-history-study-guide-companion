# Unit 5.7: Economic Developments and Innovations

#apwh #unit-5 #period-3

## AP Exam Period
**Period 3: c. 1750–1900**

## Summary

The Industrial Revolution did not just transform production — it transformed the financial and commercial systems needed to organize, fund, and distribute industrial output at continental and global scales. The 19th century saw capitalism evolve from the merchant and commercial forms of earlier centuries into a fully industrial and eventually corporate form. **Investment banking** mobilized the enormous capital required for factories, railroads, and mines. **Limited liability corporations** spread the risk of industrial investment among thousands of shareholders. **Global commodity markets** integrated raw material producers in Asia, Africa, and Latin America into industrial supply chains controlled from London, New York, and Berlin. **Free trade ideology** (championed by Britain after 1846) dismantled some mercantilist barriers while creating new forms of economic dependency. The **gold standard** provided a stable international monetary framework that facilitated global trade but constrained governments' ability to respond to economic downturns. The result by 1900 was a genuinely global capitalist economy — more integrated, more productive, and more unequal than any that had existed before.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how industrial capitalism evolved new financial and commercial institutions in the 19th century.
- Analyze how free trade ideology shaped global economic relations.
- Evaluate the global consequences of the gold standard and commodity markets.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-5.7.I** — Industrial capitalism created new economic institutions (corporations, investment banks, commodity markets) that integrated the global economy on an unprecedented scale.
- **KC-5.2.I** — Global trade networks and commodity markets transformed economic relations between industrialized and non-industrialized regions.
