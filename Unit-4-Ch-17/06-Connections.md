# Cross-Chapter Connections — Chapter 17 (4.4): Maritime Empires Established

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

## 1. Builds On (Earlier Units)

- **[[Exploration: Causes and Events]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-15]]) — Empire followed exploration; the routes found in 4.2 became the highways of empire in 4.4.
- **[[Technological Innovations]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-14]]) — Gunpowder and ships made military empire possible.
- **[[Columbian Exchange]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-16]]) — Demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans reshaped the labor needs of the empires established here.
- **[[Islamic Empires and Indian Ocean Trade]]** (Unit 3) — Portuguese deliberately targeted Muslim trading networks in the Indian Ocean; their empire was built on disrupting existing commerce.
- **[[Aztec and Inca Empires]]** (Unit 3) — Spanish conquest repurposed existing imperial administrative and tribute structures.

---

## 2. Sets Up (Later Topics and Units)

- **[[Maritime Empires Maintained]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-18]]) — 4.4 establishes; 4.5 sustains and develops. The systems created here are refined and challenged in the next topic.
- **[[Changing Social Hierarchies]]** ([[Unit-4-Ch-20]]) — The casta system and racial hierarchies born in colonial empire are analyzed in 4.7.
- **[[Atlantic Revolutions]]** (Unit 5) — The colonial structures established here are what creoles revolt against.
- **[[European Imperialism in Asia and Africa]]** (Unit 6) — The trading-post model established by Portugal evolves into full territorial imperialism in Unit 6.

---

## 3. Comparative Cases

- **Spanish territorial empire vs. Portuguese trading-post empire** — Most important comparison in 4.4.
- **Spanish empire vs. Aztec/Inca empires** — Both extracted tribute from subject peoples; what changed was the racial and religious character of the extraction.
- **European maritime empires vs. Ottoman land empire** — Both were expanding simultaneously; compare methods, resources, and long-term sustainability.

> [!exam-tip]
> The **feitoria vs. encomienda** contrast is one of the most commonly tested comparisons in AP World History. Practice stating it clearly in one or two sentences.
