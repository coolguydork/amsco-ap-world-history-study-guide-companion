# Key Terms — Chapter 17 (4.4): Maritime Empires Established

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

1. **Estado da India** — The Portuguese "State of India," the network of fortified trading posts and sea lanes controlled by Portugal in the Indian Ocean. (Enabled Portugal to dominate the spice trade for nearly a century through commercial coercion rather than territorial conquest.)

2. **Feitoria** — A Portuguese fortified trading post along coastlines in Africa and Asia. (The building block of the Portuguese trading-post empire; functioned as a commercial hub and military garrison combined.)

3. **Viceroy** — A royal representative governing a colonial territory on behalf of the Spanish or Portuguese Crown. (Viceroyalties of New Spain and Peru were the primary administrative units of Spanish America.)

4. **Encomienda** — A Spanish colonial grant giving settlers the right to Indigenous labor in exchange for "protection" and Christian instruction. (The primary mechanism of Indigenous exploitation in early Spanish colonies.)

5. **Mita** — An Andean rotational forced-labor draft used in Spanish silver mines, especially at [[Potosí]]. (Adaptation of a pre-Columbian Inca labor system repurposed for colonial extraction.)

6. **New Laws of 1542** — Spanish legislation limiting the power of encomenderos and theoretically protecting Indigenous peoples from the worst abuses of the encomienda system. (Largely unenforced; reflect the tension between Crown authority and colonial elite interests.)

7. **Requerimiento** — A legal document read (in Spanish) to Indigenous peoples before conquest, demanding their submission to the Spanish Crown and Catholic Church. (Provided theological justification for conquest regardless of Indigenous response; widely criticized as a legal fiction.)

8. **Hacienda** — A large Spanish colonial agricultural estate where Indigenous workers were tied through debt peonage. (Replaced the encomienda as the dominant labor system over time; persisted in Latin America into the 20th century.)

9. **Conquistador** — A Spanish military leader authorized to conquer territory in the Americas in exchange for a share of the spoils. (Operated under *capitulación* contracts with the Crown; often acted independently of royal authority.)

10. **Capitulación** — A contract between the Spanish Crown and a conquistador authorizing conquest in exchange for a share of revenues. (Legal framework that made conquest a public-private partnership rather than a purely state enterprise.)

11. **Council of the Indies** — The Spanish royal council that governed colonial policy in the Americas from 1524. (Represented the Crown's attempt to assert central control over colonial administrators and conquistadors.)

12. **Casa de Contratación** — The Spanish "House of Trade," the institution that regulated all commerce between Spain and its American colonies. (Enforced mercantilist policies by funneling all colonial trade through Seville.)

13. **Afonso de Albuquerque (1453–1515)** — Portuguese admiral who conquered Goa (1510), Malacca (1511), and Hormuz (1515), establishing Portuguese control of key Indian Ocean trade nodes. (The architect of the Portuguese Estado da India.)

> [!exam-tip]
> **Encomienda vs. mita**: the encomienda was a general labor grant; the mita was a specific rotational draft for mines. Both were coercive, but mita workers in silver mines faced especially deadly conditions.
