# Chapter 17 (Topic 4.4): Maritime Empires Established

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

## AP Exam Period
**Period 4: 1450–1750**

## Summary

Between 1450 and 1750, European maritime powers established two distinct models of overseas empire. Portugal built a trading-post empire (Estado da India) stringing fortified feitorias from West Africa to East Africa to India to Southeast Asia, controlling key commercial nodes without governing large interior populations. Spain, by contrast, conquered densely populated Indigenous empires in the Americas and built a territorial empire sustained by coerced Indigenous labor, silver mining, and plantation agriculture. Both models relied heavily on gunpowder weapons, and both generated enormous wealth — but through fundamentally different mechanisms. The Spanish model required governing millions of Indigenous and enslaved people and managing a vast continental territory; the Portuguese model required only enough force to control strategic harbors and straits. By the 17th century, the Dutch, English, and French began challenging Iberian dominance with their own hybrid models, combining elements of the trading-post and territorial approaches.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Compare the Portuguese trading-post empire with the Spanish territorial empire.
- Explain how European states used military force to establish commercial and political control.
- Analyze how the encomienda and mita systems functioned as tools of colonial governance.
- Evaluate the role of religion in legitimizing and administering maritime empires.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.2.I** — European states established maritime empires in the Americas and Asia.
- **KC-4.2.II** — European rulers and merchants collaborated in establishing overseas empires.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam frequently asks you to **compare** the Portuguese and Spanish imperial models. Know: trading-post (feitoria) vs. territorial; coastal control vs. interior governance; commercial extraction vs. tribute and mining.
