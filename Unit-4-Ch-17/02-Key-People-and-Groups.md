# Key People and Groups — Chapter 17 (4.4): Maritime Empires Established

#apwh #unit-4 #period-4

---

### Afonso de Albuquerque (1453–1515)
- **Region:** Portugal / Indian Ocean
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Portuguese admiral who established the Estado da India by seizing Goa, Malacca, and Hormuz — the three key choke points of Indian Ocean trade.
- His strategy of controlling straits rather than territories defined the Portuguese trading-post model.
- Brutal in conquest but strategically brilliant; his empire lasted over a century.

---

### Hernán Cortés (1485–1547)
- **Region:** Spanish Mesoamerica
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Conquered the Aztec Empire (1519–1521) via capitulación contract; established New Spain.
- His methods — allying with Tlaxcalans, exploiting Aztec political divisions, and spreading smallpox — became the template for Spanish territorial conquest.
- His *Cartas de Relación* are primary sources for AP DBQ analysis.

---

### Francisco Pizarro (c. 1471–1541)
- **Region:** Spanish South America
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Conquered the Inca Empire (1532), exploiting a civil war between Atahualpa and Huáscar.
- Opened the Andean silver mines; established the Viceroyalty of Peru.

---

### Atahualpa (c. 1502–1533)
- **Region:** Inca Empire (Andes)
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Last undisputed Inca emperor; captured by Pizarro at Cajamarca (1532) and executed despite paying a massive ransom.
- His capture and execution symbolize the destruction of the Inca state and the beginning of Spanish colonial rule in South America.

---

### Moctezuma II (c. 1467–1520)
- **Region:** Aztec Empire (Mesoamerica)
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Aztec emperor at the time of Spanish arrival; initially received Cortés as a guest and was later taken hostage.
- His death (disputed — killed by Spaniards or by his own people) marks the collapse of Aztec imperial authority.

---

### Tlaxcalan People
- **Region:** Mesoamerica
- **Period:** Early 16th century
- Indigenous people who allied with Cortés against the Aztecs, providing the majority of the military force that conquered Tenochtitlán.
- Their alliance demonstrates that the Spanish conquest was not a simple European victory over a unified Indigenous world, but a civil war in which Spanish forces played a crucial role.

---

### Spanish Franciscan and Dominican Missionaries
- **Region:** Spanish Americas
- **Period:** 16th–17th century
- Played complex roles in the colonial enterprise: some (like Las Casas) advocated for Indigenous rights; others actively suppressed Indigenous religions and destroyed cultural artifacts.
- Established missions, schools, and churches that were sites of both cultural destruction and, paradoxically, preservation (recording Indigenous languages and histories).

> [!exam-tip]
> The **Tlaxcalan alliance** is a sophisticated AP example that complicates the narrative of "Spanish conquest." Be ready to explain why Indigenous peoples allied with Spain and what this tells us about the limits of a simple colonizer/colonized framework.
