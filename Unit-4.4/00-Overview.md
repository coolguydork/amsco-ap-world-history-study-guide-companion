# Unit 4.4: Maritime Empires Link Regions

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

## AP Exam Period
**Period 2: 1450–1750**

## Summary

Topic 4.4's organizing question is not simply *how* European maritime empires were built, but *what they connected* — and what those connections changed. For the first time in history, all major inhabited regions of the globe were brought into sustained contact through European maritime networks. The **Atlantic system** linked Western Europe, West Africa, and the Americas in a triangular exchange of manufactured goods, enslaved people, and colonial commodities (sugar, silver, tobacco). The **Indian Ocean system**, already ancient, was partly restructured as Portugal inserted itself into existing networks and forced trade through its fortified feitorias; later the Dutch VOC and English EIC competed for nodes in the same network. The **Pacific** was crossed for the first time by Magellan's circumnavigation (1519–1522) and then regularly by the Manila Galleons carrying American silver to China in exchange for silk and porcelain — connecting the Americas to Asia for the first time.

The critical AP frame for this topic is **continuity and change in economic and labor systems**. Trade networks themselves showed significant continuity — the Indian Ocean had been operating for millennia, and the Silk Roads did not vanish. What changed was *who controlled key nodes*, *what commodities moved at what scale*, and *what labor systems extracted those commodities*. The encomienda and mita in Spanish America, the plantation system in the Caribbean and Brazil, and the trading-post model in Asia all represented different answers to the question of how to extract colonial wealth — and each had profoundly different consequences for the populations subjected to them.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain how maritime empires linked previously separate regions across the Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and Pacific.
- Analyze continuity and change in economic systems as maritime trade networks expanded.
- Compare how different labor systems (encomienda, mita, chattel slavery, trading-post extraction) were used across maritime empires.
- Evaluate how political and commercial interests shaped the organization of maritime empires.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.2.I** — European states established maritime empires that linked regions across the Atlantic, Indian Ocean, and Pacific.
- **KC-4.2.II** — The economic systems of maritime empires showed both continuity (long-distance trade, coerced labor) and change (new commodities, new routes, new scales).
- **KC-4.2.III** — Labor systems in maritime empires ranged from modified indigenous coercion (encomienda, mita) to chattel slavery — representing a spectrum of responses to the problem of extracting colonial wealth.

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam tests this topic through **CCOT questions on labor systems and trade networks**. Know: (1) what connected which regions (Atlantic triangle, Indian Ocean feitorias, Manila Galleons); (2) how labor systems varied by region and commodity; (3) what was *continuous* from pre-1450 patterns vs. what was genuinely new.
