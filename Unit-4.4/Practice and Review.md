# Topic 4.4 – Practice and Review

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Multiple Choice Practice

**1.** The encomienda system established by Spain in the Americas in the early 1500s is best described as a

- A) system of free land grants to encourage European settlement
- B) coercive labor arrangement compelling indigenous peoples to work for Spanish landowners
- C) form of chattel slavery identical to the Atlantic slave trade
- D) cooperative economic arrangement between Spanish colonizers and indigenous leaders

**Answer: B** — The encomienda compelled indigenous people to work for Spanish landowners in exchange for food, shelter, and Christian instruction. It was coercive and brutal, but legally distinct from chattel slavery (the workers were not technically property). In practice, the distinction often mattered little.

---

**2.** Spain's adaptation of the Incan mit'a system in Peru was most significant because it

- A) improved the economic situation of indigenous communities
- B) created a voluntary labor market for silver mining
- C) used an existing indigenous labor obligation to coerce village populations into dangerous mine work
- D) abolished earlier encomienda labor arrangements

**Answer: C** — The Spanish converted the Incan communal labor obligation into a colonial extraction system, compelling villages to send quotas of male workers to silver mines. This shows how colonizers adapted existing indigenous systems for their own purposes.

---

**3.** The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) most directly resulted in

- A) the Spanish monopoly over the entire Americas
- B) Portugal claiming Brazil while Spain controlled the rest of the Americas
- C) France and England agreeing to stay out of the Americas
- D) the permanent division of the African coastline between Spain and Portugal

**Answer: B** — The Treaty drew a line through eastern South America; Portugal claimed lands to the east (including Brazil), Spain claimed lands to the west. This explains Brazil's Portuguese language and culture today.

---

**4.** Which of the following best explains why African captives became the primary labor force on American plantations?

- A) Africans voluntarily emigrated to the Americas seeking economic opportunity
- B) Indigenous American populations had been devastated by disease and could escape colonial labor; European indentured servants gained freedom after seven years; Africans were unfamiliar with the territory and could not easily escape
- C) Africans possessed agricultural skills unavailable in Europe or among indigenous Americans
- D) International agreements between African kingdoms and European powers required the provision of labor

**Answer: B** — Multiple factors converged: indigenous populations had been devastated by disease; survivors could escape into familiar territory; European indentured servants only worked 7 years; Africans were in unfamiliar territory without social networks for escape. This combination made enslaved Africans the "preferred" (from a colonizer's perspective) labor force.

---

**5.** Japan's gradual expulsion of European traders and missionaries in the 17th century most directly resulted from

- A) military defeats suffered by Japan in conflicts with European naval forces
- B) Japanese rulers' concerns about Christian converts' intolerance of other religions and the political destabilization Europeans caused
- C) the economic failure of Portuguese and Dutch trade in Japan
- D) pressure from China for Japan to limit contact with Europeans

**Answer: B** — Japanese Christians (converted by missionaries) had destroyed Buddhist shrines, alarming the government. Japanese rulers saw Christianity as a threat to social order and eventually expelled missionaries and closed Japan to most foreign contact (sakoku policy).

## Short Answer Question (SAQ) Practice

**Question**: Explain how ONE continuity and ONE change characterized labor systems in the Americas between 1500 and 1750.

**Model Response**:

**Continuity**: Throughout the period 1500–1750, coerced labor remained the foundation of colonial economic production in Spanish and Portuguese America. Whether through the encomienda (which compelled indigenous labor from the early 1500s), the mit'a system (adapted for silver mining), the hacienda system (for agriculture), or chattel slavery (for sugar and tobacco), colonial economies consistently depended on forced labor rather than free wage labor. This continuity reflects the fundamental logic of colonial extraction: European colonizers sought to maximize profit by minimizing labor costs.

**Change**: The primary source of coerced labor shifted dramatically during this period, from indigenous peoples to enslaved Africans. In the early 16th century, the encomienda relied primarily on indigenous labor. However, epidemic diseases killed 50–90% of indigenous populations, making this labor source increasingly unavailable. By the mid-16th century, enslaved Africans had become the dominant labor force in sugar-producing regions of Brazil and the Caribbean. This shift to African chattel slavery — people treated as inheritable property — represented a qualitative change in the nature of colonial labor systems.

## Comparison: Trading Post Empire vs. Colonial Empire

| Feature | Portuguese Trading Post Empire | Spanish Colonial Empire |
|---------|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| Territory controlled | Small coastal forts at strategic chokepoints | Large inland territories (New Spain, Peru) |
| Population governed | Minimal; focused on trade control | Millions of indigenous and enslaved peoples |
| Primary resource extracted | Spices, silk, and other trade goods | Silver, gold, sugar |
| Labor system | Local labor used for trade; some slavery | Encomienda, mit'a, hacienda, chattel slavery |
| Cultural impact | Limited local cultural imposition | Mass conversion to Christianity; destruction of indigenous cultures |

> [!exam-tip]
> This comparison appears on the AP exam regularly. The key distinction: the Portuguese trading post empire controlled *routes and ports*, while the Spanish colonial empire controlled *territory and people*. Both were forms of imperial extraction, but they operated very differently.

## LEQ and DBQ Connections

**Common LEQ prompts for Topic 4.4:**
- "Evaluate the extent to which European maritime empires changed existing economic systems in the period 1450–1750."
- "Compare the labor systems used by European colonial powers in the Americas in the period 1500–1750."
- "Explain the causes of the transatlantic slave trade's expansion in the period 1500–1700."

**Key argument: Why did the slave trade expand?**
1. Indigenous population collapse (disease) eliminated the primary labor supply
2. Sugar (and later tobacco) cultivation required enormous, year-round labor
3. Enslaved Africans could not easily escape in unfamiliar territory without social networks
4. Some African states participated, providing captives in exchange for goods
5. European commercial profit motive drove further expansion of existing slave-raiding networks

> [!tip]
> When asked about the causes of the slave trade, don't just say "Europeans needed labor." Explain *why* Africans specifically became the preferred labor force — indigenous population collapse, indentured servants' freedom after 7 years, unfamiliarity with American territory preventing escape. These specifics are what distinguish a strong response.
