## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the map description below for Questions 1–2.**
> *[Stimulus: A map showing Portuguese feitorias (trading posts) along the coasts of West Africa, East Africa, India, and Southeast Asia, connected by dotted sea lanes, c. 1540.]*

**1.** The pattern shown in the map most directly reflects which of the following Portuguese imperial strategies?

- A) Territorial conquest and administration of large interior populations
- B) Control of key coastal trade nodes to dominate commercial exchange
- C) Religious conversion of African and Asian populations as a primary goal
- D) Settlement colonies populated by Portuguese emigrants

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The map shows coastal feitorias connected by sea lanes — the classic Portuguese trading-post empire model focused on commercial control, not territorial governance.*

---

**2.** Which of the following most directly contributed to Portugal's ability to establish the network shown in the map?

- A) Diplomatic agreements with Ottoman and Mughal rulers
- B) Superior navigational technology and cannon-equipped ships
- C) A large population base that supplied soldiers and settlers
- D) Financial support from Dutch and English investors

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Cannon-equipped caravels allowed Portugal to bombard resistant ports and force trading terms; navigational tools enabled the long voyages required to maintain the network.*

---

**3.** The Spanish encomienda system differed most significantly from the Portuguese feitoria system in that the encomienda:

- A) Was less profitable than Portuguese commercial trading posts
- B) Required governing and exploiting large subject populations through coerced labor
- C) Was administered directly by the Catholic Church rather than the Crown
- D) Applied only to African enslaved workers, not Indigenous peoples

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The encomienda was a labor grant over Indigenous populations; Portugal's feitorias were commercial posts that didn't require governing large subject populations.*

---

**4.** The New Laws of 1542 are best understood as evidence of which of the following?

- A) The Spanish Crown's genuine commitment to protecting Indigenous peoples
- B) Tension between the Crown's desire for control and colonial elite resistance to oversight
- C) The success of Bartolomé de las Casas in ending the encomienda system
- D) Spain's economic weakness and inability to enforce colonial regulations

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The New Laws were largely unenforced because encomenderos resisted them; they show the Crown wanted reform but lacked the power to impose it against colonial elite interests.*

---

**5.** Which of the following best explains why Cortés was able to conquer the Aztec Empire with only ~600 Spanish soldiers?

- A) The Aztec military was technologically inferior to Spanish forces in every respect
- B) A combination of smallpox, Indigenous alliances, and exploitation of political divisions
- C) The Spanish had secretly negotiated with Aztec priests before the conquest
- D) The Aztec population had already been severely reduced by earlier European diseases

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*No single factor explains the conquest; smallpox weakened resistance, Tlaxcalan allies provided most of the military force, and Aztec political tensions (resentment of Aztec dominance) made widespread alliance-building possible.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Compare the methods used by Spain and Portugal to establish maritime empires in the period 1450–1600."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE method Spain used to establish its empire in the Americas.
**Part B:** Briefly describe ONE method Portugal used to establish its empire in Asia or Africa.
**Part C:** Explain ONE significant difference in the long-term consequences of these methods.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Conquest via conquistadors, encomienda, military force + disease + Indigenous alliance.
> - **Part B:** Feitoria model: gunboat diplomacy, fortified trading posts, control of straits.
> - **Part C:** Spain created a densely governed colonial society with millions of subjects; Portugal maintained a thin commercial presence. Long-term: Spain's model produced Latin American colonial culture; Portugal's model was more easily displaced by later European competitors.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Evaluate the role of religion in the establishment of Spanish maritime empire in the Americas."**

**Part A:** Describe ONE way religion was used to justify Spanish conquest.
**Part B:** Describe ONE way religious institutions shaped colonial governance.
**Part C:** Explain whether religion or economic motives better explain Spanish imperial expansion.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Requerimiento, papal bulls, conversion obligation.
> - **Part B:** Missions, church courts (Inquisition), encomienda "protection" requirement.
> - **Part C:** Strong essays argue religion and economics were *inseparable* — religion justified extraction, but gold was the goal. Avoid either/or framings.
