# Topic 4.3 – Key Terms and People

**Unit 4: Transoceanic Interconnections | Period 2 (c. 1450–c. 1750)**

## Core Terms

**Columbian Exchange**
The transfer of crops, animals, diseases, people, and ideas between the Eastern Hemisphere (Afro-Eurasia) and Western Hemisphere (the Americas) following Columbus's 1492 voyage. Named by historian Alfred Crosby. *Significance*: One of the most transformative events in human history; reshaped demographics, economies, environments, and cultures on every continent.

**Transatlantic Slave Trade**
The forced transportation of African people — primarily from West and Central Africa — to the Americas to work as enslaved laborers, primarily on sugar, tobacco, and later cotton plantations. Lasted from the early 1500s to the mid-19th century; approximately 10–15 million Africans were transported. *Significance*: The largest forced human migration in history; created the African Diaspora; profoundly shaped cultures across the Americas.

**Cash Crop**
A crop grown for sale on the market rather than for subsistence. Key cash crops of the period: sugar, tobacco, cacao. *Significance*: The profitability of cash crops drove demand for enslaved labor, fueling the transatlantic slave trade.

**Engenhos**
Portuguese word meaning "engines"; refers to large Brazilian sugar plantations. Used because they processed so much sugar that they operated like industrial machines. *Significance*: Represents the brutal efficiency of plantation slavery; plantation owners lost 5–10% of enslaved workers per year and constantly needed replacements, driving slave importation.

**African Diaspora**
The dispersion of Africans out of Africa, primarily through the transatlantic slave trade. *Significance*: Despite forced migration, Africans retained and transformed their cultures in the Americas, contributing to languages (creole), music (jazz, blues, samba), food, and religion across the Western Hemisphere.

**Creole Languages**
New languages that developed when enslaved Africans combined European colonial languages (English, Spanish, French, Portuguese) with West African languages and grammatical patterns. *Examples*: Haitian Creole; Gullah (coastal South Carolina and Georgia). *Significance*: Evidence of African cultural resilience despite forced assimilation.

**Smallpox**
The deadliest disease introduced by Europeans to the Americas. Spread through respiratory contact; indigenous Americans had no immunity. *Significance*: Responsible for the majority of indigenous American deaths — more than guns or military conquest. Some regions lost 90% of their populations.

**Conquistadores**
Spanish soldier-explorers who led the military conquest of indigenous American empires. Key examples: **Hernán Cortés** (conquered Aztecs, 1521) and **Francisco Pizarro** (conquered Inca, 1533). *Significance*: Their conquests brought smallpox and other diseases into the Americas and destroyed the two largest indigenous empires.

> [!warning]
> Smallpox was spread unintentionally by conquistadores, not as a deliberate weapon. European immunity came from centuries of exposure to animal-borne diseases, not from any deliberate protection program. Don't overstate European intentionality here.

**Sugarcane**
The most economically important crop of the Columbian Exchange. Grown in the Caribbean, Brazil, and other tropical American regions. Required enormous amounts of labor, driving African slave importation. *Significance*: "Sugar eclipsed silver as the main moneymaker for the European empires" by the 17th century.

## Key People

**Hernán Cortés**
Spanish conquistador who overthrew the Aztec Empire by 1521 with a small force, aided by indigenous allies who resented Aztec rule, smallpox, and superior weaponry. Founded the colony of New Spain with Mexico City built on the ruins of Tenochtitlan. *Exam relevance*: Example of how disease + indigenous alliances + European weapons combined to enable conquest.

**Francisco Pizarro**
Spanish conquistador who captured Inca ruler Atahualpa in 1533, demanded a roomful of gold as ransom, received it, and then killed Atahualpa anyway. Completed the conquest of the Inca Empire by 1572. *Exam relevance*: Shows the duplicity and violence of Spanish conquest; also example of how disease had already weakened the Inca before military attack.

**Alfred Crosby**
Modern historian who coined the term "Columbian Exchange." His work (The Columbian Exchange, 1972) drew attention to the biological dimensions of the encounter between hemispheres that previous historians had overlooked. *Exam relevance*: May appear as an author of a secondary source in DBQs.

## Environmental Terms

**Deforestation**
Cutting down forests, especially to clear land for agriculture. *Impact in the Americas*: European colonists cleared land far more intensively than indigenous peoples had, leading to large-scale forest loss.

**Soil Depletion**
Exhaustion of soil nutrients from growing the same crop year after year on the same land (monoculture). Common on sugar and tobacco plantations. *Significance*: Created environmental problems that persist today in formerly colonial plantation regions.
