# Timeline — Unit 4.3: (4.3): The Columbian Exchange

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

> [!tip] Big Picture
> The Columbian Exchange unfolded over centuries, not years. Its most dramatic effects (disease, demographic collapse) were felt almost immediately after 1492; its slower effects (crop adoption, population growth in Eurasia) played out over the 17th–18th centuries. Both timelines matter for AP essays.

---

| Date | Event | Region | Significance |
|------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1492 | Columbus lands in Caribbean; first sustained transatlantic contact | Caribbean | Biological exchange begins immediately — pathogens travel with the first ships |
| 1493–1496 | Smallpox and other diseases begin killing Taino people | Caribbean | First documented mass die-off; Taino population of ~250,000 nearly extinct by 1550 |
| 1519–1520 | Smallpox epidemic sweeps Aztec Empire ahead of Cortés | Mesoamerica | Disease weakens Aztec resistance; kills emperor Cuitláhuac; accelerates Spanish conquest |
| 1520s–1530s | European livestock (cattle, pigs, horses) multiply rapidly across Americas | Americas | Transform Indigenous landscapes; disrupt agricultural systems |
| 1530s–1540s | Epidemic diseases reach Inca Empire before Pizarro | Andes | Kill emperor Huayna Capac; trigger civil war that weakens Inca resistance to Spanish conquest |
| 1545–1548 | Catastrophic epidemic (likely typhus) kills ~80% of remaining Mexican Indigenous population | Mesoamerica | One of the deadliest events in human history; accelerates Spanish labor crisis |
| 1550s | Potato and maize begin spreading to Europe and Africa | Eurasia / Africa | Slow adoption initially; becomes dietary staple by 17th–18th centuries |
| 1570s | Tobacco cultivation spreads to Europe as luxury good | Europe | Creates demand that drives English and Spanish plantation agriculture in Americas |
| 1600s | Sweet potato adopted widely in China; contributes to population growth | China | By 1700, China's population growing rapidly partly due to American crop calories |
| 1650–1850 | European and Chinese populations roughly double | Eurasia | Partly attributable to caloric boost from American crops (potato, maize, sweet potato) |
| 1700s | Horses spread across North American Great Plains | North America | Comanche, Lakota, and other nations build mobile, horse-centered cultures |
| 1700s–1800s | Cassava (manioc) becomes staple crop across Sub-Saharan Africa | Africa | American crop feeds growing populations even as slave trade drains them |

---

> [!warning] Continuity Note
> Long-distance biological exchange had happened before (Black Death traveled the Silk Roads). What made the Columbian Exchange unprecedented was its **scale** and the **complete biological isolation** of the Americas before 1492 — unlike Eurasian peoples, Indigenous Americans had no prior exposure to Old World pathogens whatsoever.
