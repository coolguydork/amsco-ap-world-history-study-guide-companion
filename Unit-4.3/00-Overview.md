# Unit 4.3: The Columbian Exchange

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

## AP Exam Period
**Period 2: 1450–1750**

## Summary

The Columbian Exchange — the massive transfer of plants, animals, diseases, and peoples between the Eastern and Western Hemispheres following 1492 — was the most consequential biological event in human history since the Ice Age. European ships inadvertently transported pathogens (smallpox, measles, typhus) to the Americas, where Indigenous peoples had no acquired immunity; the resulting demographic collapse killed an estimated 50–90% of the pre-contact population, perhaps 50–70 million people. In the other direction, American crops — the potato, maize, sweet potato, tomatoes, cacao, and tobacco — spread to Eurasia and Africa, dramatically expanding caloric availability and driving population growth over the following centuries. Animals also crossed: horses and cattle transformed Indigenous American societies, while Native American peoples had no domestic animals of comparable scale to contribute. The Columbian Exchange was not a balanced exchange — it devastated the Americas while ultimately benefiting Eurasia and Africa — and its consequences reshaped global demography, ecology, and economy for centuries.

## AMSCO Learning Objectives

- Explain the causes and biological mechanisms of the Columbian Exchange.
- Analyze the demographic consequences of the exchange for the Americas, Eurasia, and Africa.
- Evaluate the long-term economic and environmental consequences of crop and animal transfers.
- Connect the demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans to the intensification of the Atlantic slave trade.

## AP Key Concepts

- **KC-4.1.VII** — The Columbian Exchange of plants, animals, and diseases resulted in population growth in Afro-Eurasia and population decline in the Americas.

> [!exam-tip]
> The Columbian Exchange is one of the most-tested topics in AP World History. Know it in *both directions* (Americas → Eurasia AND Eurasia → Americas) and always connect demographic collapse to its downstream consequence: demand for enslaved African labor.
