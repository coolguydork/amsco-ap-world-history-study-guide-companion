# Practice Questions — Unit 4.3: (4.3): The Columbian Exchange

#apwh #unit-4 #period-2

---

## Multiple-Choice Questions

**Use the passage below to answer Questions 1–2.**

> *"In the year 1520… a sick Spaniard arrived at [Cempoala]. He was covered with smallpox, and with this pestilence there occurred a great dying throughout all the land… The Indians, having never known this sickness and not knowing what it was, washed themselves very often, and that was a great cause of their death."*
> — Francisco de Aguilar, Spanish soldier, memoir written c. 1560

---

**1.** The passage best supports which of the following conclusions about the Spanish conquest of the Americas?

- A) Spanish military strategy was the primary cause of Indigenous population decline
- B) Epidemic disease was a decisive factor in the Spanish conquest, spreading ahead of military forces
- C) Indigenous peoples were unable to adapt medically to new diseases
- D) The Spanish deliberately used smallpox as a weapon of biological warfare

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The passage describes smallpox arriving before military conquest and killing large numbers — demonstrating that disease was a key factor independent of direct military action.*

---

**2.** Which of the following best explains why Indigenous Americans were so vulnerable to European diseases?

- A) Indigenous Americans had weaker immune systems due to their diet
- B) Indigenous Americans had no prior exposure to Old World pathogens and thus no acquired immunity
- C) Indigenous Americans lived in unsanitary conditions that made disease spread easier
- D) European diseases were more deadly in tropical climates than in Europe

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The biological mechanism was lack of prior exposure (virgin soil epidemic), not inherent weakness. This is an important distinction — framing it as inherent weakness reflects colonial bias.*

---

**3.** The spread of American crops (potato, maize) to Eurasia and Africa most directly resulted in which of the following?

- A) The decline of traditional Eurasian agricultural systems
- B) Population growth in Europe, Africa, and Asia during the 17th and 18th centuries
- C) A decrease in the demand for enslaved African labor in the Americas
- D) Political instability in Europe as elites competed for control of crop trade

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*American crops produced more calories per acre than many existing staples and could grow in marginal soils — contributing directly to population growth across Eurasia and Africa.*

---

**4.** Which of the following most directly resulted from the demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans?

- A) The establishment of the encomienda system in Spanish colonies
- B) Increased demand for enslaved African workers to replace the lost labor force
- C) Spanish abandonment of colonial silver mining operations
- D) The Treaty of Tordesillas reassigning colonial territories

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The causal chain: epidemic disease → demographic collapse → labor vacuum → Atlantic slave trade. This is the most important causal connection in Unit 4.*

---

**5.** The reintroduction of horses to the Americas via the Columbian Exchange most directly resulted in which of the following?

- A) Indigenous peoples in the Amazon developing new forms of agriculture
- B) Great Plains peoples developing mobile, bison-hunting cultures
- C) European settlers gaining a decisive military advantage over all Indigenous peoples
- D) The decline of existing Indigenous trade networks in the Southwest

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Horses enabled the Comanche, Lakota, Cheyenne, and others to become highly mobile hunters and warriors — transforming Great Plains cultures within a century of horses spreading northward from Spanish settlements.*

---

**6.** Which of the following best describes how the Columbian Exchange affected West African societies?

- A) West Africa was largely isolated from the Columbian Exchange due to its distance from the Americas
- B) American crops supplemented West African diets while the slave trade removed millions from African societies
- C) West African rulers used newly introduced American crops to strengthen their militaries and resist European colonization
- D) The Columbian Exchange caused a demographic collapse in West Africa comparable to that in the Americas

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*West Africa received American crops (maize, cassava) that contributed to population growth while simultaneously losing millions to the Atlantic slave trade — a complex, contradictory demographic impact.*

---

**7.** The Columbian Exchange is best understood as an example of which broader historical process?

- A) Cultural diffusion through voluntary trade and diplomatic exchange
- B) The unintended environmental and biological consequences of human migration and commercial contact
- C) Deliberate state policy to transform colonial territories through biological means
- D) The spread of European technological superiority to other world regions

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The exchange was largely unintentional — no one planned to transmit smallpox or introduce horses; it was an emergent consequence of sustained transatlantic contact.*

---

**8.** Which of the following represents the most significant long-term global consequence of the Columbian Exchange?

- A) The extinction of several European domesticated animal species
- B) A fundamental demographic shift: population collapse in the Americas, growth in Eurasia and Africa
- C) The complete replacement of traditional Eurasian diets with American foods
- D) The decline of the Atlantic slave trade as American crops made plantation agriculture less profitable

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The demographic consequences were global and long-lasting: Americas lost 50–90% of population; Eurasia and Africa grew substantially partly due to American crops.*

---

**9.** Which of the following best explains why the Columbian Exchange's demographic effects were so asymmetric — devastating for the Americas but ultimately beneficial for Eurasia?

- A) European diseases were more lethal than American diseases
- B) Americans had no acquired immunity to Old World diseases, while Eurasians had already survived centuries of epidemic disease exposure
- C) American crops were more nutritious than Eurasian crops
- D) The Americas had a smaller population base and were therefore more vulnerable to disease

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*Eurasia had experienced repeated epidemics (Black Death, etc.) that selected for some immunity in surviving populations. The Americas had been biologically isolated for ~15,000 years with no exposure to these pathogens.*

---

**10.** A historian studying the long-term economic consequences of the Columbian Exchange would most likely emphasize which of the following?

- A) The immediate enrichment of Indigenous peoples through trade with Europeans
- B) The connection between demographic collapse, the slave trade, and the plantation economy
- C) The failure of American crops to gain popularity in European markets
- D) The decline of Spanish imperial power caused by the influx of American silver

**✅ Correct Answer: B**
*The economic story of the Columbian Exchange runs: demographic collapse → labor vacuum → Atlantic slave trade → plantation economy → Atlantic commercial capitalism. Each step connects.*

## Short Answer Questions (SAQ)

### SAQ 1

**"Explain the causes and effects of the demographic collapse of Indigenous Americans in the period 1492–1600."**

**Part A:** Identify the primary cause of Indigenous American demographic collapse.
**Part B:** Explain ONE political or economic consequence of the demographic collapse for Spanish colonial rule.
**Part C:** Explain ONE long-term global consequence of the demographic collapse.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - **Part A:** Epidemic disease — be specific (smallpox, measles, typhus) and explain the mechanism (virgin soil epidemic / no prior immunity).
> - **Part B:** Labor crisis → encomienda modification → shift to African slavery. Or: disruption of tribute-based colonial economy.
> - **Part C:** Atlantic slave trade / African diaspora. Show the *causal connection*, not just a list of facts.

---

### SAQ 2

**"Compare the effects of the Columbian Exchange on the Americas with its effects on ONE other world region in the period 1492–1750."**

**Part A:** Briefly describe ONE effect of the Columbian Exchange on the Americas.
**Part B:** Briefly describe ONE effect of the Columbian Exchange on your chosen region.
**Part C:** Explain ONE significant difference in how the Columbian Exchange affected the two regions.

> [!tip] Top-Score Guidance
> - Good comparison regions: Eurasia (population growth from crops) or Africa (crop adoption + slave trade).
> - **Part C:** The key difference is *direction of impact*: devastating for the Americas (disease), ultimately beneficial demographically for Eurasia/Africa (crops). Explain WHY the effects differed.

## DBQ Prompt

**"Evaluate the extent to which the Columbian Exchange transformed global societies in the period 1492–1750."**

> [!tip] DBQ Outline Guidance
> **Thesis:** Make a specific claim about the *degree* of transformation — strong essays will note that transformation was profound but uneven (devastating for some, beneficial for others).
>
> **Complexity:** Explain both the immediate effects (demographic collapse) and longer-term effects (crop diffusion, population growth), showing that "transformation" played out on different timescales in different places.
>
> **Documents to expect:** Accounts of epidemic disease from Spanish observers; Indigenous accounts of conquest; population data tables; maps of crop diffusion; accounts of the slave trade.
>
> **Outside evidence:** Specific population estimates (pre-contact Americas ~50–70M, post-1600 ~5–10M); specific crops and their destinations; horse culture among Plains peoples.
