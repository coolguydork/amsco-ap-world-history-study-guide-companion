# Timeline & Chronology — Topic 2.1: The Silk Roads

---

## Deep Background (Pre-1200)

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| c. 500 BCE | Caravanserais first appear (Persian Empire) | Physical infrastructure for overland trade established |
| c. 200 BCE | Bill of exchange developed (China) | Commercial credit instrument predates the Silk Roads revival |
| c. 200 BCE | Zhang Qian opens original Silk Roads | Han Dynasty establishes the first overland routes to Central Asia |
| c. 700 CE | Bill of exchange in wide use (China) | Precursor to modern banking instruments |
| c. 800 CE | Paper money introduced (China) | Replaces cumbersome copper coins for large transactions |
| 8th–9th c. | Abbasid merchants revive Silk Roads | First revival after collapse of Han/Roman empires; second golden age begins |

---

## Core Period: c. 1200–c. 1450

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1200–1450 | Peak era of Silk Roads under study | Unit 2 covers this period; trade at highest historical volume |
| 1235 | Sundiata establishes the Mali Empire | Connects West African gold to trans-Saharan network feeding into Silk Roads |
| 1258 | Mongols conquer the Abbasid Caliphate | Unifies Silk Roads corridor under Mongol authority; Pax Mongolica begins |
| c. 1271–1295 | Marco Polo travels to China | Venetian traveler's journey possible due to Pax Mongolica security |
| c. 1296 CE | Hanseatic League formalized (Germany) | Western European commercial alliance linking Baltic trade to Mediterranean routes |
| c. 1300s | European banking houses established | Bills of exchange and credit systems spread westward from China via Silk Roads |
| 1324 | Mansa Musa's pilgrimage to Mecca | Demonstrates West African wealth connected to broader Afro-Eurasian networks |
| 1325 | Ibn Battuta begins his travels | Muslim traveler uses Silk Road networks; journeys to Asia, Europe, and Africa |
| 1347 | Black Death begins ravaging Europe | Plague travels westward along Silk Roads from Central Asia; kills ~1/3 of Europe |
| 1417 | Samarkand builds grand Islamic school | Silk Roads city at height of Islamic cultural influence |

## After the Period

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1405–1433 | Zheng He's voyages | Chinese sea power reflects shift toward Indian Ocean routes |
| c. 1450 onwards | Overland Silk Roads decline | Ottoman expansion disrupts routes; European maritime routes around Africa emerge |
| 1603 | Later commercial instruments fully developed | Long legacy of Chinese commercial innovations in European finance |

---

## Chronological Thinking

> [!exam-tip]
> The AP exam may ask you to identify a **turning point** in the growth of exchange networks. The correct answer for this period is almost always the **Mongol conquest and the establishment of the [[Concept Notes/Pax Mongolica\|Pax Mongolica]] (1258 onward)** — it unified fragmented routes, improved security, and enabled a dramatic increase in trade volume.

> [!tip]
> **Memory scaffold for the key sequence:**
> Abbasid revival (8th–9th c.) → Mongol conquests (1200s) → Pax Mongolica peak trade (1258–1350s) → [[Concept Notes/Black Death\|Black Death]] via trade routes (1347) → Ottoman disruption + European sea routes (post-1450)
