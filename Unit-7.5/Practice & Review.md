# Practice & Review

**AMSCO pp. 500–502**

## Multiple-Choice Questions

*Source: Ho Chi Minh, speech at the founding of the Indo-Chinese Communist Party, Hong Kong, 1930*

> "After [World War I], the world was divided into two camps: one is the revolutionary camp which includes the oppressed colonial peoples and the exploited working class throughout the world. Its vanguard is the Soviet Union. The other is the counter-revolutionary camp of international capitalism and imperialism, whose general staff is the League of Nations. . . . French imperialism was the hardest hit. Therefore, in order to restore the forces of capitalism in France, the French imperialists have . . . built new factories to exploit the workers by paying them starvation wages. They have plundered the peasants' land to establish plantations and drive them to destitution. . . . World War Two will break out. When it does the French imperialists will certainly drive our people to an even more horrible slaughter."

**Question 1.** Which statement best reflects the relationship between conditions before and after World War I as described by Ho Chi Minh?

(A) They changed significantly.
(B) They were remarkably similar.
(C) They became slightly worse.
(D) They became slightly better.

> [!tip] Answer: **B**
> Ho Chi Minh's central argument is that French imperialism *continued and intensified* after WWI — new factories, same exploitation; land seizure continued; military forces maintained. The continuity of colonial exploitation is his point. (A) is wrong because he argues little has changed in *structure*; (C) and (D) both imply slight changes but miss his main argument about fundamental continuity.

---

**Question 2.** Which statement most accurately corresponds to the claim in the first paragraph that World War I divided the world into two camps?

(A) The world was divided into those camps, with the Soviet Union standing for one and the League of Nations for the other.
(B) The war divided colonized people between those allied with capitalism and those allied with imperialism.
(C) The "oppressed colonial peoples" and "exploited working class" were not united enough to represent one camp.
(D) The Soviet Union actually reintroduced private property and capitalism.

> [!tip] Answer: **A**
> Ho explicitly identifies the Soviet Union as the vanguard of the revolutionary camp and the League of Nations as the "general staff" of the counter-revolutionary camp. (B) misreads the passage — he does not divide colonized people, he unites them in one camp. (C) contradicts his argument. (D) is a factual error — the Soviet Union did not reintroduce capitalism in 1930.

---

**Question 3.** Because of Ho Chi Minh's point of view, which is the best way to evaluate the accuracy of his account?

(A) Because he was a revolutionary, assume that he used exaggerations.
(B) Because he was educated, trust that he reported events accurately.
(C) Because imperialism was common, see what others wrote about revolutions in their lands.
(D) Because many people wrote about these events, compare his statements with theirs.

> [!tip] Answer: **D**
> The correct AP approach to evaluating a primary source with a known point of view is to compare it with other accounts — not to assume exaggeration (A, which is unwarranted), nor to trust uncritically (B, which ignores bias), nor to look at vague "others" without a clear comparison standard (C is close but less precise than D). Corroboration through comparison is the historian's method.

## Short-Answer Questions

### SAQ 1 — Korean and Chinese Declarations (1919)

*Use both passages below:*

**Korean Declaration of Independence, March 1, 1919:** "We hereby declare that Korea is an independent state and that Koreans are a self-governing people. We proclaim it to the nations of the world in affirmation of the principle of the equality of all nations. . . ."

**Chen Duxiu, "Our Final Awakening," 1916:** "We, having been living in one corner of the world for several decades, must ask ourselves what is the level of our national strength and our civilization. . . . if we open our eyes and take a hard look at the situation within our country and abroad, what place does our country and our people occupy, and what actions should we take?"

**(A) Identify ONE way World War I influenced colonized states.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Causation
> World War I raised and then dashed expectations of self-determination for colonized peoples. Colonial troops fought for the Allied cause believing Wilson's Fourteen Points promised self-governance, but the Paris Peace Conference denied independence to Asian and African peoples, applying self-determination only to white European nations. This betrayal intensified nationalist movements in Korea, China, India, and Africa — as demonstrated by both the Korean Declaration of Independence and the Chinese intellectual soul-searching in Duxiu's "Final Awakening."

**(B) Explain ONE way that the ideas in the Korean declaration are similar to the ideas in Duxiu's statement.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Comparison
> Both documents reflect a nationalist awakening rooted in a demand for self-assessment and agency. The Korean Declaration invokes "the principle of the equality of all nations" — insisting Korea has the right to self-governance. Duxiu calls for a "final awakening" in which Chinese people honestly evaluate their national strength and determine what actions to take. Both authors frame their people as capable of self-determination and demand that they — not foreign powers — define their nation's future. The common thread is the rejection of colonial or imperial paternalism and a call to active political consciousness.

**(C) Explain ONE historical result after 1900 of League of Nations mandates.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Consequence
> The League of Nations mandate system, which placed former Ottoman and German territories under British and French control, generated intense anti-colonial nationalism in the Middle East. Arab populations in Palestine, Transjordan, Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq had expected independence after fighting alongside the Allies in WWI, but instead found themselves under new European masters. In Palestine, the mandate combined with the Balfour Declaration's promise of a Jewish homeland to create a situation of irreconcilable competing nationalisms. The conflict between Arab and Jewish claims to Palestine, rooted in the mandate system, produced decades of violence that persists today in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

### SAQ 2 — Post-WWI Nationalism and Imperial Expansion

**(A) Identify ONE example of new forms of nationalism after World War I.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Evidence
> The May Fourth Movement (1919) represents a new form of Chinese nationalism that rejected Western liberalism in favor of Marxism after China was betrayed at the Paris Peace Conference. When the Allies awarded Germany's Shandong concessions to Japan rather than returning them to China, Chinese intellectuals and students concluded that Western democratic ideals were a facade for imperial self-interest. Many May Fourth leaders joined the newly founded Chinese Communist Party (1921), redirecting Chinese nationalism toward a Soviet-inspired revolutionary model rather than liberal democracy.

**(B) Explain ONE example of an imperial state that gained territory through conquest after World War I.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Evidence
> Japan gained territory through conquest when it invaded Manchuria in September 1931, seizing the resource-rich northeastern Chinese region. When the League of Nations condemned Japan's actions, Japan simply withdrew from the League and continued its expansion, creating the puppet state of Manchukuo in 1932. Japan's conquest demonstrated that the post-WWI international order could not restrain a determined imperial power, emboldening further Japanese aggression and undermining the collective security the League was designed to provide.

**(C) Explain ONE example of an imperial state that gained territory through a treaty settlement after World War I.**

> [!tip] Model Response — Evidence
> Japan also gained territory through the Treaty of Versailles: the Paris Peace Conference awarded Japan control over Germany's former holdings in the Shandong Peninsula of China and the German-held islands of the Western Pacific. This treaty settlement rewarded Japan for its participation in the Allied war effort and reflected European powers' willingness to trade Chinese and Pacific interests for Japanese cooperation. The Shandong decision directly triggered the May Fourth Movement, showing how treaty settlements created as many tensions as they resolved.

## Key Comparisons for Essay Practice

| Feature | Indian Movement | Chinese Movement | African Movement |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Primary method** | Nonviolent civil disobedience | Political/military (CCP vs. KMT) | Intellectual assertion; labor organizing |
| **Key leader** | Gandhi | Mao Zedong / Chiang Kai-shek | Kenyatta, Senghor |
| **Colonial power** | Britain | Japan (spheres of influence) | France, Britain |
| **Ideology** | Hindu spirituality + liberalism | Marxism-Leninism | Pan-Africanism, Négritude |
| **Result by 1947** | Independence + partition | Civil war won by CCP (1949) | Independence movements maturing |

> [!exam-tip]
> AP DBQs on this era frequently include Ho Chi Minh's speech, Korean/Chinese declarations, Gandhi's writings, and documents from African intellectuals. Practice identifying: (1) the author's purpose, (2) the intended audience, (3) what the document reveals about anti-colonial ideology, and (4) what it omits or distorts.
