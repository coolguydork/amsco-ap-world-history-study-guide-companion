# Connections

**AMSCO pp. 493–502**

## Builds On

**[[Unit-7.3/Conducting World War I Overview\|Conducting World War I Overview]]**
WWI created the conditions for Topic 7.5 on three levels: (1) colonial troops' frontline service radicalized them politically, (2) the Allies' wartime promises of self-determination created expectations, and (3) the devastation of European powers undermined their credibility as "superior" civilizations. Without WWI, the Paris betrayal and its consequences don't happen.

**Topic 5.6 — Industrialization Spreading (Japan's modernization)**
Japan's rise as an industrial and military power — [[Concept Notes/Meiji Restoration\|Meiji Restoration]], victory in Russo-Japanese War — is the prerequisite for understanding why Japan could seize Manchuria in 1931 and why the Paris Conference gave Shandong to Japan in 1919. Japan's expansionism in Topic 7.5 is the logical extension of its 19th-century industrialization and imperialism.

**[[Unit-6.3/Indigenous Responses to State Expansion Overview\|Indigenous Responses to State Expansion Overview]]**
Earlier anti-colonial resistance strategies set the stage for interwar movements. The shift from 19th-century armed resistance (often crushed) to 20th-century mass civil disobedience and intellectual organizing represents a change in *strategy*, not in the underlying drive for self-determination.

## Leads To

**Causes of World War II [[Unit-7.6/Causes of World War II Overview|Causes of World War II Overview]]**
Japanese imperial expansion — from Manchukuo (1932) through the full Pacific War — flows directly from the unresolved tensions of Topic 7.5. The Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere ideology and Japan's withdrawal from the League of Nations after Manchuria condemned are direct bridges to WWII.

**Decolonization After 1900 [[Unit-8.5/Decolonization after 1900 Overview|Decolonization after 1900 Overview]]**
The interwar nationalist movements built the organizations, ideologies, and leaders that achieved independence after WWII. Gandhi's [[Concept Notes/Indian National Congress\|Indian National Congress]], the CCP, the African leaders educated in Europe — all are products of the 1919–1945 period. Decolonization is the fulfillment of what Topic 7.5 began.

## Compare With

**[[Unit-6.3/Indigenous Responses to State Expansion Overview\|Indigenous Responses to State Expansion Overview]]**
AP comparison question: How did anti-colonial resistance strategies change from the 19th century (armed resistance, accommodation) to the interwar 20th century (mass civil disobedience, intellectual nationalism, Marxist organizing)? The change reflects both the failure of armed resistance and the new political tools available (mass media, international public opinion, ideological frameworks).

**[[Unit-7.2/Causes of World War I Overview\|Russian Revolution / Soviet Communism]]**
The Soviet model influenced multiple anti-colonial movements in Topic 7.5: the CCP was directly inspired by the Bolshevik revolution, Ho Chi Minh founded the Indo-Chinese Communist Party on the Soviet model, and the Comintern actively supported anti-colonial movements. Compare: both Marxist revolution and nonviolent nationalism were responses to imperialism, but they offered very different visions of the post-colonial future.

## AP Essay Opportunities

**Causation LEQ options:**
- Explain the causes of the growth of anti-colonial nationalism in Asia and/or Africa between 1919 and 1945.
- Explain the causes of increased political instability in Asia between the World Wars.

**Comparison LEQ options:**
- Compare the methods of anti-colonial resistance in TWO regions during the interwar period.
- Compare Chinese and Indian nationalist movements in the early 20th century.

**Continuity and Change-Over-Time:**
- Explain the continuities and changes in territorial control of colonial empires from 1900 to 1975.
- Explain how the mandate system represented both change and continuity in European imperialism.
